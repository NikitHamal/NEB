::: note Answers — Zoology (1–40)
**1.** (c) — one ancestral finch species gave rise to many species, each adapted to a different niche (seeds, insects, cactus) — adaptive (divergent) radiation.
**2.** (d) — count alleles: a = 320 + 2 × 40 = 400 out of 2 × 1000 = 2000, so q = 400/2000 = 0.2 (and p = 0.8).
**3.** (d) — a small group of colonists carries a random, unrepresentative sample of alleles — the founder effect, a form of genetic drift. A bottleneck is a sharp fall in an existing population.
**4.** (a) — ctenophores (comb jellies, e.g. Pleurobrachia) swim by ciliary comb plates (ctenes) and have colloblasts, not cnidoblasts.
**5.** (c) — cnidocytes with nematocysts are the hallmark of Cnidaria (Hydra). Sycon is a sponge, Pleurobrachia a ctenophore (with colloblasts) and Fasciola a flatworm.
**6.** (b) — Balanoglossus has a proboscis, collar and trunk, gill slits and a buccal diverticulum (stomochord); it is now kept in the separate phylum Hemichordata.
**7.** (c) — the generic name begins with a capital letter and the specific epithet with a small letter; both are italicised in print (or underlined separately when handwritten).
**8.** (c) — goblet cells are flask-shaped mucus-secreting cells among columnar epithelial cells. Paneth cells secrete lysozyme; oxyntic cells HCl; chief cells pepsinogen.
**9.** (a) — after birth, erythropoiesis occurs in red marrow (ends of long bones, sternum, ribs, vertebrae). The liver and spleen form RBCs in the foetus; yellow marrow is mostly fat.
**10.** (c) — serum is the clear fluid left after blood clots; fibrinogen has been used up as fibrin, so serum = plasma − clotting factors. Albumin, antibodies and glucose remain.
**11.** (b) — Ca²⁺ binds troponin, which shifts tropomyosin off the myosin-binding sites on actin, so cross-bridges can form.
**12.** (d) — the sporozoite grows into a liver schizont whose merozoites are cryptozoites (cryptomerozoites); merozoites of later liver cycles are metacryptozoites.
**13.** (b) — within minutes of the blood meal, the male gametocyte throws out 4–8 whip-like microgametes; one fertilises a macrogamete to form the zygote.
**14.** (a) — as in other non-chordates, the cord runs ventrally and is solid; it is formed of two fused cords, with a ganglion in each segment.
**15.** (b) — two pairs of lateral hearts (segments 7 and 9) and two pairs of lateral-oesophageal hearts (12 and 13) pump blood from the dorsal to the ventral vessel.
**16.** (a) — the sticky, bilobed tongue is fixed to the front of the lower jaw, so its free hind end can be flicked out to catch prey.
**17.** (d) — renal portal veins bring blood from the hind limbs and posterior body into capillaries of the kidneys. The hepatic portal system drains the intestine to the liver.
**18.** (a) — bile has no digestive enzyme; its bile salts break fat into fine droplets, increasing the surface on which pancreatic lipase acts.
**19.** (c) — per half jaw: 2 incisors, 1 canine, 2 premolars, 3 molars = 8; 8 × 4 = 32 teeth. (3143 is the formula of the pig.)
**20.** (b) — VC = TV + IRV + ERV = 500 + 3000 + 1100 = 4600 mL. Residual volume cannot be breathed out, so it is not part of VC (adding it gives total lung capacity, 5800 mL).
**21.** (d) — Bohr effect: more H⁺ (lower pH), more CO₂, higher temperature or more 2,3-BPG lower haemoglobin's affinity for O₂, so more O₂ is unloaded in active tissues.
**22.** (b) — P wave = atrial depolarisation; QRS = ventricular depolarisation (atrial repolarisation is hidden in it); T wave = ventricular repolarisation.
**23.** (c) — the first Rh⁺ baby sensitises the Rh⁻ mother at delivery; her anti-Rh IgG crosses the placenta in the next Rh⁺ pregnancy and destroys foetal RBCs. Anti-D given after delivery prevents this.
**24.** (a) — pulse pressure = 120 − 84 = 36 mmHg; MAP = 84 + 36/3 = 84 + 12 = 96 mmHg.
**25.** (b) — liver cells make urea through the ornithine (Krebs–Henseleit) cycle; the kidneys only filter and excrete it.
**26.** (c) — 125 mL/min × 1440 min = 180 000 mL = 180 L per day; about 99 % is reabsorbed, leaving roughly 1.5 L of urine.
**27.** (d) — the cerebellum maintains posture and equilibrium and co-ordinates muscular activity; the medulla controls breathing and heart rate; the hypothalamus regulates temperature, thirst and hunger.
**28.** (b) — in short-sightedness (myopia) the eyeball is too long or the lens too convex; a diverging (concave) lens moves the image back onto the retina.
**29.** (b) — adrenaline (an amine) binds a surface receptor and works via cAMP. Steroids such as cortisol, oestrogen and testosterone pass through the membrane and act on intracellular receptors.
**30.** (d) — α-cells secrete glucagon (raises blood glucose); β-cells insulin; δ-cells somatostatin; PP (F) cells pancreatic polypeptide.
**31.** (a) — during spermiogenesis Golgi vesicles fuse to form the acrosome, whose enzymes (hyaluronidase, acrosin) help the sperm penetrate the ovum. Mitochondria form the middle-piece spiral.
**32.** (c) — hCG is secreted by the trophoblast of the implanted embryo (later the placenta); it keeps the corpus luteum active and appears in urine within about two weeks of conception.
**33.** (c) — BCG (Bacillus Calmette–Guérin) is a live attenuated strain of Mycobacterium bovis that protects mainly against severe childhood tuberculosis.
**34.** (d) — the dengue virus is carried by day-biting Aedes aegypti and Ae. albopictus. Anopheles spreads malaria, Culex filariasis and Japanese encephalitis, sandflies kala-azar.
**35.** (a) — ready-made maternal antibodies (mainly IgA) are received, not produced by the baby (passive), through a natural process (feeding). It is short-lived.
**36.** (a) — IgM, the largest antibody, appears first; IgG dominates the later secondary response. Secretory IgA is a dimer; IgE (monomer) mediates allergy.
**37.** (d) — MRI detects radio signals from hydrogen nuclei aligned in a magnetic field. CT and X-ray use X-rays; PET uses positron-emitting radioisotopes.
**38.** (b) — the larva's paired silk glands (modified salivary glands) secrete fibroin and sericin, which harden in air into the cocoon thread; cocoons are boiled before the moth emerges.
**39.** (d) — Sagarmatha National Park (established 1976) was listed in 1979 and Chitwan in 1984; they are Nepal's two natural World Heritage Sites.
**40.** (a) — ex situ means outside the natural habitat — seed banks, botanical gardens, zoos, gene banks. The other three protect species in their own habitat (in situ).

::: note Answers — Botany (41–80)
**41.** (b) — nucleoside = base + pentose (e.g. adenosine); adding phosphate makes a nucleotide (e.g. AMP), the monomer of nucleic acids.
**42.** (c) — it removes a fraction of active enzyme whatever the substrate level, so Vmax falls; the substrate still binds the unaffected enzyme normally, so Km is unchanged. A competitive inhibitor raises Km instead.
**43.** (d) — the prophage is copied along with the host chromosome at each division; only when induced does it enter the lytic cycle and burst the cell.
**44.** (c) — in conjugation (Lederberg and Tatum, 1946) an F⁺ donor passes DNA through a pilus to a recipient. Transformation is uptake of naked DNA; transduction uses a phage.
**45.** (d) — a heteroecious rust needs two unrelated hosts: uredo- and teleutospores form on wheat, pycnia and aecia on barberry. An autoecious rust completes its cycle on one host.
**46.** (c) — fucoxanthin (a xanthophyll) masks the chlorophylls a and c of brown algae. Phycoerythrin colours red algae and phycocyanin blue-greens; chlorophyll b is absent in brown algae.
**47.** (a) — two rings of peristome teeth bend in and out with humidity, releasing spores gradually in dry weather. Elaters occur in Marchantia and Equisetum; the annulus and indusium belong to fern sporangia and sori.
**48.** (d) — Equisetum has hollow, jointed aerial stems with whorls of scale leaves and silica in the epidermis (hence 'scouring rush'), and bears cones (strobili).
**49.** (d) — the thin epicarp is the skin, the juicy mesocarp is eaten and the hard stony endocarp encloses the seed. (In coconut the edible part is endosperm.)
**50.** (a) — the curd is a mass of thick, branched, prefloral inflorescence axes with arrested flower buds. In cabbage the edible part is the terminal bud of leaves.
**51.** (d) — the male fern Dryopteris filix-mas yields filicin (oleoresin of male fern) from its rhizome.
**52.** (b) — producers → primary (2000 J) → secondary (200 J) → tertiary (20 J): 20 000 × 0.1³ = 20 J.
**53.** (a) — phytoplankton (diatoms, blue-green and green algae) colonise the water first; rooted submerged, floating, reed-swamp and sedge-meadow stages follow. Lichens pioneer a xerosere on rock.
**54.** (b) — DDT is fat-soluble and not excreted, so it builds up at each trophic level (biomagnification); top carnivores carry the highest load, causing thin egg shells.
**55.** (b) — mangroves grow in waterlogged, oxygen-poor saline mud; pneumatophores grow upward and take in air through lenticels. Vanda has velamen-covered aerial roots.
**56.** (c) — mitochondria and chloroplasts are semi-autonomous; their own DNA and prokaryote-type 70S ribosomes support the endosymbiotic theory.
**57.** (d) — cilia and flagella have the 9 + 2 doublet axoneme; centrioles and basal bodies have nine triplets with no central tubule (9 + 0).
**58.** (a) — at metaphase the chromosomes are maximally condensed and spread on the equatorial plate, so a karyotype is prepared from colchicine-arrested metaphase cells.
**59.** (d) — in anaphase I the homologues of each bivalent separate while sister chromatids stay joined at the centromere; sister chromatids separate only in anaphase II.
**60.** (b) — G₁ = 2C = 16 pg; after S phase 4C = 32 pg; meiosis I halves it to 2C = 16 pg per cell (n chromosomes, each with two chromatids); each gamete then has C = 8 pg.
**61.** (d) — DNA polymerase works only 5′→3′, so the strand running away from the replication fork is made discontinuously in short Okazaki fragments, later joined by DNA ligase.
**62.** (a) — each amino acid needs one codon of three bases: 50 × 3 = 150, plus 3 for the stop codon = 153.
**63.** (d) — AABB, AAbb, aaBB and aabb are each 1/16, so 4/16 = 1/4 (equivalently ½ homozygous at A × ½ at B).
**64.** (a) — every W_ plant is white (9 + 3 = 12/16); wwY_ is yellow (3/16); wwyy is green (1/16). 9:3:4 is recessive epistasis, 9:7 complementary genes, 15:1 duplicate genes.
**65.** (b) — 20 map units = 20 % recombinants, shared equally between the two recombinant classes Ab and aB: 10 % each (parentals AB and ab are 40 % each).
**66.** (c) — adding or removing 1 or 2 bases shifts the reading frame, changing every codon downstream. A purine-for-purine change is a transition (point substitution).
**67.** (a) — the suberised (and lignified) band blocks the apoplast path, forcing water and ions to pass through the living endodermal cells — a selective checkpoint.
**68.** (a) — cambium forms wide, light early (spring) wood and narrow, dense late (autumn) wood each year; together they make one ring, so counting rings gives the age.
**69.** (b) — radial bundles mark a root; polyarch xylem with a large pith and no cambium mark a monocot root such as maize. A dicot root is di- to hexarch with a small or no pith.
**70.** (c) — proton pumps drive K⁺ (with Cl⁻/malate) into guard cells; water follows by osmosis, and their unevenly thickened walls bow apart to open the pore.
**71.** (c) — Dixon and Joly (1894): transpiration creates tension in continuous water columns held by cohesion and adhesion. Godlewski (relay pump) and Bose (pulsation) proposed vital theories.
**72.** (b) — RQ = CO₂ evolved / O₂ used = 102/145 ≈ 0.70, typical of fats, which are poor in oxygen. Carbohydrates give 1.0; organic acids more than 1.
**73.** (c) — RuBisCO oxygenates RuBP in the chloroplast; glycolate is converted to glycine in the peroxisome; two glycines give serine and CO₂ in the mitochondrion.
**74.** (c) — each CO₂ needs 3 ATP and 2 NADPH: 6 × 3 = 18 ATP and 6 × 2 = 12 NADPH.
**75.** (a) — ABA, the 'stress hormone', induces and maintains dormancy of seeds and buds; gibberellins break dormancy and trigger germination.
**76.** (c) — leaf = 2n = 24, so n = 12; the endosperm forms from the fusion of two polar nuclei with one male gamete (triple fusion): 3n = 36.
**77.** (b) — nucellar cells grow into the embryo sac and form adventive embryos (sporophytic budding); these are genetically identical to the mother plant.
**78.** (a) — the dividing cells of the shoot apex are usually free of virus because vascular tissue (through which viruses spread) is not yet formed there.
**79.** (b) — genes for β-carotene synthesis (from daffodil/maize and a bacterium) were added to the rice endosperm to fight vitamin A deficiency; the carotene gives the golden colour.
**80.** (d) — its Ti (tumour-inducing) plasmid transfers T-DNA into the plant genome; disarmed Ti plasmids carry the desired gene instead of tumour genes.
