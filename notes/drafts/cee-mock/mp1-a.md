::: note Answers — Zoology (1–40)
**1.** (b) — CH₄, NH₃, H₂ and water vapour (boiling water) were sparked; amino acids formed. Free oxygen was deliberately absent: the early atmosphere was reducing.
**2.** (c) — insect and bird wings do the same job but have different origins (convergent evolution). The other pairs are built on one plan (homologous).
**3.** (d) — Ramapithecus remains (now referred to Sivapithecus) were found at Tinau Khola, Butwal, in 1980; the first find was in the Siwaliks of India (Lewis, 1932).
**4.** (a) — the water vascular (ambulacral) system with tube feet is unique to echinoderms (starfish, sea urchin).
**5.** (c) — notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord and pharyngeal gill slits are the three chordate features; a vertebral column exists only in vertebrates, not in Amphioxus or tunicates.
**6.** (b) — flame cells are the protonephridia of flatworms (Planaria, Taenia, Fasciola). Annelids use nephridia.
**7.** (c) — Ascaris (Aschelminthes) has a pseudocoelom; Taenia is acoelomate, Pheretima is coelomate, Hydra is diploblastic.
**8.** (b) — microvilli multiply the absorptive surface of the intestine. The trachea has ciliated pseudostratified epithelium, the skin keratinized stratified squamous.
**9.** (a) — transitional epithelium lines the urinary bladder and ureters; alveoli and Bowman's capsule are simple squamous.
**10.** (d) — intercalated discs join cardiac fibres so the impulse spreads as one wave.
**11.** (b) — Haversian systems (osteons) with lamellae and lacunae are typical of mammalian compact bone; cartilage has no blood canals.
**12.** (c) — sporozoites from the mosquito's salivary glands enter the blood and go to the liver.
**13.** (d) — P. falciparum causes malignant tertian (cerebral) malaria. P. malariae causes quartan malaria; vivax and ovale cause benign tertian.
**14.** (a) — segments 14, 15 and 16 form the clitellum. The male genital pores are on segment 18.
**15.** (b) — the typhlosole is a mid-dorsal fold of the intestinal wall (from segment 26) that increases the absorbing area. Grinding is done by the gizzard.
**16.** (a) — the frog has 10 pairs of cranial nerves; man has 12.
**17.** (c) — the venae cavae open into the sinus venosus, which empties into the right auricle.
**18.** (a) — enterokinase activates trypsinogen to trypsin; pepsinogen is activated by HCl in the stomach.
**19.** (c) — fatty acids and monoglycerides are resynthesized into fats, packed as chylomicrons and enter the lacteals (lymph).
**20.** (d) — about 70 % travels as HCO₃⁻ (made by carbonic anhydrase in RBCs), about 20–23 % as carbaminohaemoglobin and about 7 % dissolved.
**21.** (a) — the respiratory rhythm centre is in the medulla; the pons fine-tunes it.
**22.** (b) — cardiac output = stroke volume × heart rate = 70 mL × 72 = 5040 mL ≈ 5.0 L min⁻¹.
**23.** (d) — the SA node in the right atrium generates the impulse; the AV node delays and relays it.
**24.** (b) — O is IᴼIᴼ, AB is IᴬIᴮ; every child gets Iᴼ from the father and Iᴬ or Iᴮ from the mother, so A (IᴬIᴼ) or B (IᴮIᴼ).
**25.** (c) — ADH (vasopressin) makes the distal tubule and collecting duct permeable to water via aquaporins.
**26.** (a) — all the glucose and amino acids and about two-thirds of the water and salt are reabsorbed in the PCT.
**27.** (d) — about −70 mV, inside negative, kept by the Na⁺–K⁺ pump and the high resting K⁺ permeability. +35 mV is near the peak of an action potential.
**28.** (b) — the fovea has only cones, packed densely; the blind spot has no photoreceptors at all.
**29.** (a) — without ADH the kidney cannot concentrate urine: large volumes of dilute urine. Insulin deficiency causes diabetes mellitus.
**30.** (c) — cretinism: stunted growth and mental retardation. Thyroxine deficiency in the adult is myxoedema.
**31.** (d) — each primary spermatocyte gives four spermatids by meiosis, each becoming one sperm: 25 × 4 = 100.
**32.** (d) — the high oestrogen of the late follicular phase triggers an LH surge, and ovulation follows about a day later.
**33.** (a) — the Widal test detects antibodies against Salmonella typhi antigens.
**34.** (b) — HIV binds CD4 on helper T cells; their fall below about 200 per mm³ marks AIDS.
**35.** (c) — IgG is about 75–80 % of serum antibody and the only class that crosses the placenta.
**36.** (d) — tetanus toxoid is inactivated toxin. BCG, OPV and measles vaccines are live attenuated.
**37.** (a) — amniotic fluid with fetal cells, taken at about 15–20 weeks, is cultured and karyotyped (e.g. Down's syndrome).
**38.** (c) — Bt makes a crystal (Cry) protein toxic to insect larvae; it is the bio-control agent behind Bt crops.
**39.** (d) — movement of a whole organism towards light is positive phototaxis; tropism is a growth movement of a fixed organ.
**40.** (b) — Chitwan National Park was established in 1973 (a World Heritage Site since 1984).

::: note Answers — Botany (41–80)
**41.** (c) — in sucrose the glucose C-1 and fructose C-2 (both anomeric carbons) are joined, so no free carbonyl reduces Fehling's solution. Maltose and lactose are reducing.
**42.** (b) — the inhibitor competes for the active site; raising the substrate concentration overcomes it.
**43.** (d) — Whittaker (1969): Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia. Haeckel added Protista (three kingdoms), Copeland proposed four.
**44.** (a) — TMV is a rod of protein around single-stranded RNA (about 6400 nucleotides).
**45.** (c) — gametangial copulation of + and − strains gives a thick-walled zygospore; Mucor is a zygomycete (phycomycete).
**46.** (b) — one or more spiral, ribbon-shaped chloroplasts with pyrenoids. Cup-shaped is Chlamydomonas, star-shaped Zygnema, reticulate Oedogonium.
**47.** (a) — in bryophytes the gametophyte is dominant and free-living; the sporophyte (sporogonium) depends on it.
**48.** (c) — in gymnosperms the endosperm is the female gametophyte, formed before fertilization: haploid. Triploid endosperm is the angiosperm product of double fertilization.
**49.** (a) — six stamens, four long and two short (tetradynamous), cruciform corolla and siliqua: mustard family.
**50.** (d) — Liliaceae: P₃₊₃, A₃₊₃, G₍₃₎ superior, trilocular with axile placentation.
**51.** (b) — sarpagandha (Rauvolfia serpentina) roots yield reserpine, an antihypertensive. Neem is Azadirachta, tulasi Ocimum, ginger Zingiber.
**52.** (b) — the standing crop of phytoplankton is small at any moment though it reproduces fast, so the biomass of consumers can exceed it. The energy pyramid is always upright.
**53.** (d) — Nitrosomonas oxidizes NH₃ to NO₂⁻, Nitrobacter oxidizes NO₂⁻ to NO₃⁻. Pseudomonas denitrifies.
**54.** (c) — 1 DU is a layer of pure ozone 0.01 mm thick at STP; about 300 DU is normal.
**55.** (a) — crustose lichens secrete acids that weather rock and begin soil formation; mosses follow.
**56.** (b) — 70S (50S + 30S); eukaryotic cytoplasmic ribosomes are 80S (60S + 40S). Svedberg units do not add.
**57.** (d) — lysosomes hold acid hydrolases; if the membrane ruptures, the enzymes digest the cell (autolysis).
**58.** (d) — Singer and Nicolson (1972). Danielli–Davson gave the earlier sandwich model; Robertson the unit membrane.
**59.** (c) — crossing over happens at pachytene; synapsis starts at zygotene; chiasmata become visible at diplotene.
**60.** (b) — meiosis I separates homologues: chromosome number halves to 12, but each chromosome still has two sister chromatids until anaphase II.
**61.** (a) — A = T = 20 %, so G + C = 60 % and C = 30 %.
**62.** (d) — AUG is the start codon (methionine). UAA, UAG and UGA are stop codons.
**63.** (c) — AaBb gives four gametes in equal numbers; ab from the recessive parent lets each show: 1 : 1 : 1 : 1.
**64.** (a) — incomplete dominance: the genotype ratio 1 RR : 2 Rr : 1 rr appears directly as phenotypes.
**65.** (c) — her father gave her his X carrying the allele, so she is a carrier (XᶜX). Each son gets one of her X chromosomes: probability 1/2.
**66.** (d) — Klinefelter's (47, XXY): sterile male with some female traits. Turner's is 45, XO; Down's trisomy 21; Edwards' trisomy 18.
**67.** (b) — monocot stems have many scattered, closed (no cambium) bundles with a bundle sheath; dicot stems have a ring of open bundles.
**68.** (a) — radial bundles (xylem and phloem on alternate radii) are typical of roots.
**69.** (d) — large, thin-walled bulliform cells in the upper epidermis of grass leaves make the leaf roll in water stress.
**70.** (b) — Ψcell = −0.8 + 0.3 = −0.5 MPa, which is higher than −0.6 MPa outside; water moves from higher to lower water potential, out of the cell.
**71.** (c) — hydathodes at the leaf margins release liquid water under root pressure, at night when transpiration is low.
**72.** (a) — the oxygen-evolving complex of PS II splits water and replaces P680's electrons.
**73.** (c) — PEP carboxylase fixes CO₂ on PEP in the mesophyll to give oxaloacetate (the first stable product). RuBP is the acceptor of the Calvin cycle in the bundle sheath.
**74.** (d) — acetyl-CoA (2C) + oxaloacetate (4C) → citric acid (6C), hence "citric acid cycle".
**75.** (a) — gibberellins cause bolting in rosette plants and can replace a cold or long-day requirement.
**76.** (b) — 8 nuclei in 7 cells: egg, 2 synergids, 3 antipodals and the central cell with 2 polar nuclei.
**77.** (c) — Vallisneria (and Zostera) are pollinated by water. Salvia is insect-pollinated, maize wind-pollinated, Ficus by fig wasps.
**78.** (d) — totipotency is the basis of plant tissue culture. Heterosis is hybrid vigour.
**79.** (a) — restriction endonucleases cut DNA at specific recognition sites; ligase is the "molecular glue".
**80.** (b) — the cyanobacterium Anabaena azollae in Azolla's leaf cavities fixes atmospheric nitrogen. Rhizobium lives in legume nodules.
