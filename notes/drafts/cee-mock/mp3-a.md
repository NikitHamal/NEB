::: note Answers — Zoology (1–40)
**1.** (c) — the RNA-world idea: RNA carries information and can act as an enzyme (ribozyme), so it could copy itself before proteins and DNA existed.
**2.** (a) — one ancestral finch from the mainland diversified into many species filling different niches on the islands: adaptive radiation (divergent evolution).
**3.** (b) — Java man (*Pithecanthropus*) and Peking man (*Sinanthropus*) are both *Homo erectus*, cranial capacity about 900–1100 cm³.
**4.** (d) — ctenophores (comb jellies such as *Pleurobrachia*) have eight comb rows and no nematocysts, which separates them from cnidarians.
**5.** (b) — the salivary glands of the leech (Annelida: Hirudinea) secrete hirudin, which blocks thrombin and keeps sucked blood from clotting.
**6.** (a) — a keel-less (raft-like) sternum marks a flightless ratite such as the ostrich; flying birds like pigeons, peafowl and parakeets have a keeled sternum.
**7.** (c) — the male sea horse broods eggs in a ventral pouch until they hatch.
**8.** (d) — despite its name, the germinal epithelium of the ovary is a single layer of cuboidal cells; it does not form the ova.
**9.** (a) — reticular tissue is a network of reticular fibres and cells that supports lymphoid organs and bone marrow.
**10.** (c) — eosinophils rise (eosinophilia) in allergic reactions and in helminth infections.
**11.** (b) — dendrites carry impulses towards the cyton; the single axon carries them away.
**12.** (d) — sporozoites in hepatocytes form cryptozoites (pre-erythrocytic schizogony); later liver generations are metacryptozoites.
**13.** (b) — sporozoites released from the oocysts migrate through the haemocoel to the salivary glands and enter man with saliva at the bite.
**14.** (a) — the peristomium (buccal segment) is segment 1; the prostomium is only a lobe overhanging the mouth, not a segment.
**15.** (c) — like liver cells, chloragogen cells store glycogen and fat and carry out deamination to form urea.
**16.** (d) — the frog's middle ear has one rod-like ossicle, the columella auris; mammals have three (malleus, incus, stapes).
**17.** (a) — frog RBCs are oval, biconvex and nucleated; mammalian RBCs are circular, biconcave and enucleate.
**18.** (c) — milk dental formula is 2102/2102 (20 teeth); premolars appear only in the permanent set (2123/2123).
**19.** (b) — parietal (oxyntic) cells secrete both HCl and intrinsic factor; its lack causes pernicious anaemia.
**20.** (d) — VC = TV + IRV + ERV = 500 + 3000 + 1100 = 4600 mL; 3500 is inspiratory capacity, 5800 is total lung capacity, 2300 is FRC.
**21.** (b) — the Bohr effect shifts the O₂–Hb dissociation curve to the right. The Haldane effect is the reverse: O₂ binding releases CO₂.
**22.** (a) — pulse pressure = 130 − 85 = 45; MAP ≈ diastolic + ⅓ pulse pressure = 85 + 15 = 100 mm Hg (diastole lasts longer, so a simple average overestimates).
**23.** (c) — "lubb" marks closure of the tricuspid and bicuspid valves as ventricles start contracting; "dub" is closure of the semilunar valves.
**24.** (d) — an Rh⁻ mother is sensitised by Rh⁺ foetal cells at the first delivery; her anti-Rh IgG can cross the placenta and destroy RBCs of a later Rh⁺ foetus.
**25.** (a) — modified smooth-muscle (JG) cells of the afferent arteriole secrete renin; the macula densa only senses NaCl in the distal tubule.
**26.** (c) — filtrate = 125 mL × 1440 min = 180 L/day; reabsorbed = (180 − 1.5)/180 = 99.2 %.
**27.** (d) — in a sagittal section the cerebellum's white matter branches like a tree inside the grey cortex: arbor vitae.
**28.** (b) — hair cells of the organ of Corti sit on the basilar membrane; their hairs touch the overhanging tectorial membrane.
**29.** (a) — oxytocin from the posterior pituitary contracts myoepithelial cells around the alveoli; prolactin drives milk production, not ejection.
**30.** (c) — Addison's disease is hyposecretion of cortisol and aldosterone; excess ACTH (from the same precursor as MSH) darkens the skin.
**31.** (b) — interstitial (Leydig) cells between the seminiferous tubules secrete testosterone under LH; Sertoli cells nourish developing sperm.
**32.** (d) — meiosis I finishes just before ovulation; the secondary oocyte stays in metaphase II until a sperm enters.
**33.** (a) — HBV (a hepadnavirus) is the DNA virus; A and E are RNA viruses spread faeco-orally, and C is an RNA virus spread mainly by blood.
**34.** (c) — *Vibrio cholerae* toxin makes the gut secrete large volumes of watery stool; treatment is rapid rehydration with ORS.
**35.** (d) — ready-made antibodies given by injection give immediate, short-lived, artificially acquired passive immunity.
**36.** (b) — IgM, a pentamer, appears first in a primary response; IgG dominates later and in secondary responses.
**37.** (a) — zygote intrafallopian transfer; embryos with more than eight cells are placed in the uterus (IUT), and GIFT transfers gametes, not embryos.
**38.** (c) — identical twins share all histocompatibility genes, so an isograft (syngraft) is not rejected.
**39.** (d) — Koshi Tappu was listed in December 1987; Nepal now has ten Ramsar sites, including Ghodaghodi, Rara and Gokyo.
**40.** (b) — persistent, fat-soluble DDT becomes more concentrated at each higher trophic level (biomagnification), thinning the eggshells of birds of prey.

::: note Answers — Botany (41–80)
**41.** (b) — fructose is a six-carbon sugar with a keto group at C-2; glucose and galactose are aldohexoses, ribose an aldopentose.
**42.** (d) — the end product binds an allosteric (regulatory) site and changes the enzyme's shape; it is reversible and not overcome by more substrate.
**43.** (a) — Protista holds unicellular eukaryotes: diatoms (chrysophytes), dinoflagellates, euglenoids, slime moulds and protozoans.
**44.** (c) — a prophage replicates with the host chromosome until induction switches it to the lytic cycle.
**45.** (a) — *Puccinia graminis tritici* (Basidiomycetes) causes black stem rust; *Albugo* causes white rust of crucifers, *Phytophthora* late blight of potato, *Ustilago* smut.
**46.** (d) — algin (alginic acid) comes from brown algae such as *Laminaria* and kelps; agar and carrageenan come from red algae.
**47.** (b) — the moss spore forms a branched filamentous protonema that bears buds for the leafy gametophores; a prothallus is the fern gametophyte.
**48.** (c) — gymnosperm xylem (except Gnetales) has tracheids with bordered pits but no vessels; its phloem also lacks companion cells.
**49.** (d) — a caryopsis is a one-seeded dry fruit of grasses with the pericarp fused to the seed coat; an achene has them free.
**50.** (a) — Fabaceae have a single carpel with ovules along the ventral suture (pod/legume); Brassicaceae are parietal, Solanaceae and Liliaceae axile.
**51.** (b) — tulasi (*Ocimum tenuiflorum*, syn. *O. sanctum*) is a mint (Lamiaceae); neem is Meliaceae, ginger Zingiberaceae, sarpagandha Apocynaceae.
**52.** (c) — the epiphytic orchid gains support and light while the mango is neither helped nor harmed: commensalism.
**53.** (a) — denitrifying bacteria use nitrate as an electron acceptor in the absence of O₂, returning N₂ to the air.
**54.** (d) — tropical forests below about 1000 m are dominated by Sal; subtropical forests have *Schima–Castanopsis* and chir pine.
**55.** (b) — phytoplankton → rooted submerged → rooted floating → reed-swamp (amphibious) → sedge-meadow → woodland → climax forest.
**56.** (c) — Virchow (1855) added that new cells arise only by division of existing cells; Schleiden and Schwann stated the original theory (1838–39).
**57.** (a) — Golgi-derived vesicles collect at the phragmoplast and fuse to form the cell plate, which becomes the middle lamella.
**58.** (b) — the 9 + 2 axoneme: nine peripheral doublets around two central singlet microtubules; the basal body has nine triplets (9 + 0).
**59.** (d) — colchicine stops spindle formation, so condensed chromosomes cannot move apart and cells are held at metaphase.
**60.** (c) — S phase doubles 8 pg to 16 pg (seen at prophase I); two divisions without replication leave 4 pg in each haploid cell.
**61.** (b) — semiconservative replication: generation 1 is all hybrid; generation 2 gives 2 hybrid and 2 light molecules from each original duplex.
**62.** (a) — 61 sense codons code for 20 amino acids, so most amino acids have several synonymous codons (e.g. leucine has six).
**63.** (c) — number of gamete types = 2ⁿ, where n is the number of heterozygous pairs (Aa, Cc, Dd): 2³ = 8.
**64.** (d) — 20 map units = 20 % recombinants, shared equally between Ab and aB, so Ab = 10 %.
**65.** (a) — XʷXʷ × XᴿY gives XᴿXʷ (red-eyed) daughters and XʷY (white-eyed) sons: criss-cross inheritance.
**66.** (b) — Edwards' is trisomy 18; Patau's is trisomy 13 and Down's trisomy 21.
**67.** (c) — sclereids (stone cells) with thick lignified walls are scattered in the pulp of pear and guava.
**68.** (d) — Cucurbitaceae stems have bicollateral bundles; maize has closed collateral bundles, sunflower open collateral and *Dracaena* amphivasal.
**69.** (b) — radial, exarch, polyarch bundles with a large pith mark a monocot root; a dicot root has 2–6 xylem groups and little or no pith.
**70.** (a) — at incipient plasmolysis the protoplast just begins to leave the wall, so TP = 0 and DPD equals osmotic pressure.
**71.** (c) — light-driven proton pumping draws K⁺ into guard cells, lowering their water potential; water enters and the turgid cells bow apart.
**72.** (d) — PSI's reaction centre is P700; PSII's is P680.
**73.** (a) — each CO₂ fixed needs 3 ATP and 2 NADPH, so 6 CO₂ need 18 ATP and 12 NADPH.
**74.** (b) — RQ = CO₂/O₂ = 102/145 ≈ 0.70; carbohydrates give 1.0 and organic acids such as malic acid about 1.33.
**75.** (c) — auxin moving down from the apical bud maintains apical dominance; cytokinin actually promotes lateral-bud growth.
**76.** (d) — coconut water is free-nuclear endosperm; the white "meat" is cellular endosperm formed later.
**77.** (a) — 100 megaspore mother cells need 100 meioses; 100 pollen grains need 100 ÷ 4 = 25 meioses; total 125.
**78.** (c) — apical meristem cells divide faster than viruses spread into them, so meristem culture yields virus-free stock.
**79.** (b) — golden rice carries daffodil and bacterial genes for the β-carotene (provitamin A) pathway, to combat vitamin A deficiency.
**80.** (d) — leguminous crops ploughed in green add organic matter and fixed nitrogen: green manuring. Biofertilizers are living microbes.
