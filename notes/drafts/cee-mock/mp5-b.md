::: note Answers — Chemistry (81–130)
**81.** (b) — H₂ = 5 mol and O₂ = 2 mol; 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, so O₂ is limiting and gives 4 mol H₂O = 72 g, with 1 mol H₂ left over.
**82.** (c) — Since 1961 the atomic mass unit is defined as 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom (1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg).
**83.** (d) — n + l = 6 for 5p, 6s and 4d, but 7 for 4f; among equal sums the lower n (4d) fills first.
**84.** (a) — Cr³⁺ is [Ar] 3d³ with 3 unpaired electrons; μ = √(n(n + 2)) = √15 ≈ 3.87 BM.
**85.** (c) — All have 10 electrons; N³⁻ has the fewest protons (7), so the weakest pull and the largest radius.
**86.** (b) — Cl has 3 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs (sp³d); the lone pairs take equatorial positions, leaving a T-shape.
**87.** (a) — Single bonds give 2 C–H + 1 C–H + 1 C–C = 4 σ; C=C adds 1 σ + 1 π and C≡N adds 1 σ + 2 π: 6 σ, 3 π.
**88.** (d) — Cl goes from 0 to −1 in NaCl and to +5 in NaClO₃: a disproportionation reaction.
**89.** (d) — At equilibrium the vapour pressure depends only on the nature of the liquid and the temperature, not on the amount or vessel size.
**90.** (b) — 8 corners × 1/8 + 6 face centres × 1/2 = 4 atoms; each atom touches 12 neighbours (cubic close packing).
**91.** (a) — Reversing gives 1/100; halving the coefficients takes the square root: (0.01)^½ = 0.1.
**92.** (c) — Ksp = s(2s)² = 4s³; s³ = 8 × 10⁻¹², s = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ M.
**93.** (b) — Mn goes from +7 to +4 (3 electrons): equivalent weight = 158/3 = 52.7.
**94.** (c) — Units of k are (mol L⁻¹)^(1 − n) s⁻¹; mol⁻¹ L s⁻¹ means 1 − n = −1, so n = 2.
**95.** (a) — 9650 C = 0.1 F; Al³⁺ needs 3 F per mole, so 0.1/3 mol × 27 g mol⁻¹ = 0.9 g.
**96.** (d) — ΔG = ΔH − TΔS < 0 when T > ΔH/ΔS = 30 000/100 = 300 K.
**97.** (b) — α lowers Z by 2 and A by 4; two β⁻ raise Z by 2. Same Z, A smaller by 4: an isotope.
**98.** (a) — D₂O slows fast fission neutrons (deuterium absorbs few of them), so natural uranium can sustain a chain reaction.
**99.** (c) — UV splits Cl atoms off CFCs; Cl· + O₃ → ClO· + O₂ and ClO· + O → Cl· + O₂, so one Cl destroys many O₃.
**100.** (d) — Cu + 4HNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NO₂ + 2H₂O; dilute acid gives mainly NO instead.
**101.** (b) — SO₂ + 2H₂O → H₂SO₄ + 2[H]; nascent hydrogen reduces the dye, and air later reoxidizes it, so the colour returns (Cl₂ bleaches permanently by oxidation).
**102.** (a) — Impurities are more soluble in the melt, so a moving molten zone sweeps them to one end; used for Si, Ge and Ga.
**103.** (c) — HgCl₂ is corrosive sublimate; Hg₂Cl₂ is calomel and ZnSO₄·7H₂O is white vitriol.
**104.** (d) — dx²−y² and dz² point straight at the six ligands, so they are raised (eg); dxy, dyz, dxz lie between them (t₂g).
**105.** (b) — Zn (E° = −0.76 V) is oxidized before Fe (−0.44 V), so it corrodes sacrificially and protects exposed iron.
**106.** (c) — Vitamin B₁₂ (cobalamin) contains Co; chlorophyll has Mg and haemoglobin Fe.
**107.** (a) — Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase exports 3 Na⁺ and imports 2 K⁺ per ATP, making the cell interior negative.
**108.** (d) — The nitronium ion is electron-deficient and accepts an electron pair; NH₃, H₂O and CN⁻ donate pairs (nucleophiles).
**109.** (a) — The positively polarized N of –NO₂ withdraws electrons more strongly than the halogens or –COOH.
**110.** (c) — Butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-1-ol and 2-methylpropan-2-ol; the other three C₄H₁₀O isomers are ethers.
**111.** (b) — Iso-octane (2,2,4-trimethylpentane) is rated 100 and n-heptane 0; cetane belongs to the diesel scale.
**112.** (d) — Peroxide effect: a Br· radical adds to the terminal carbon to form the more stable secondary radical, giving anti-Markovnikov 1-bromopropane.
**113.** (a) — C₅H₅⁻ is planar, cyclic and fully conjugated with 6 π electrons (4n + 2, n = 1); the others have an sp³ carbon or 8 π electrons in a non-planar ring.
**114.** (c) — The planar carbocation is attacked almost equally from both faces, so the product is mainly racemic.
**115.** (b) — 2CHCl₃ + O₂ → 2COCl₂ + 2HCl; filling to the brim excludes air, and dark glass excludes light.
**116.** (d) — Kolbe's reaction places –COO⁻ ortho to –O⁻, and acidification gives salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid).
**117.** (a) — C₂H₅OC₂H₅ + 2HI → 2C₂H₅I + H₂O; with excess hot HI the ethanol first formed is also converted to ethyl iodide.
**118.** (c) — Benzaldehyde has no α-hydrogen, so it disproportionates to benzyl alcohol and sodium benzoate; the others undergo aldol condensation.
**119.** (b) — Zn–Hg with conc. HCl reduces C=O to CH₂; hydrazine/KOH is the Wolff–Kishner method.
**120.** (d) — Reactivity is acid chloride > anhydride > ester > amide; Cl⁻ is the best leaving group.
**121.** (c) — HCOOCH(CH₃)₂ gives HCOOH (reduces Tollens') and propan-2-ol (has CH₃CH(OH)–, iodoform positive); ethyl acetate gives acetic acid, which does not.
**122.** (a) — CH₃CONH₂ + Br₂ + 4KOH → CH₃NH₂ + K₂CO₃ + 2KBr + 2H₂O: the amine has one carbon fewer than the amide.
**123.** (b) — In water, inductive effect, solvation of the cation and steric crowding together make 2° amine > 1° > 3° > NH₃ ≫ aniline.
**124.** (d) — CH₃MgBr adds to (CH₃)₂C=O; hydrolysis of the alkoxide gives the tertiary alcohol (CH₃)₃COH.
**125.** (c) — Iron promoted with K₂O and Al₂O₃ (molybdenum in older plants); V₂O₅ is the contact-process catalyst and Pt–Rh the Ostwald one.
**126.** (a) — Gypsum (about 2–3 %) retards the rapid setting of tricalcium aluminate, giving time to work the concrete.
**127.** (b) — Nylon-6,6 comes from hexamethylenediamine and adipic acid (6 C each); caprolactam gives nylon-6 and glycol + terephthalic acid gives Dacron.
**128.** (d) — NaCN from fusion gives Na₄[Fe(CN)₆] with FeSO₄; Fe³⁺ then forms ferric ferrocyanide, Fe₄[Fe(CN)₆]₃ (Prussian blue).
**129.** (a) — Lighter gases diffuse faster through a porous partition (Graham's law), so the mixture is enriched stepwise.
**130.** (b) — 2MnO₄⁻ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O; 2.5 mmol oxalate needs 1.0 mmol MnO₄⁻, V = 1.0/0.02 = 50 mL.
