::: note Answers — Zoology (1–40)
**1.** (c) — Sidney Fox (1950s) made proteinoid microspheres, membrane-bounded protobiont models; Oparin's protobionts were coacervates.
**2.** (a) — similar proteins and DNA sequences in related species are biochemical (molecular) evidence of common descent.
**3.** (a) — Neanderthals had a brain as large as ours and practised burial; H. erectus was about 900–1100 cm³, Australopithecus about 500 cm³.
**4.** (c) — flatworms are the first bilateral, triploblastic animals, though still acoelomate; cnidarians are radial and diploblastic.
**5.** (c) — feathers are unique to Aves; crocodiles and mammals have four-chambered hearts, reptiles lay shelled eggs, turtles have horny beaks.
**6.** (a) — cyclostomes are jawless (Agnatha), with a round suctorial mouth and no paired fins.
**7.** (b) — ostia, an osculum, a canal system and spicules mark sponges such as Sycon.
**8.** (b) — in holocrine secretion the entire cell disintegrates into the secretion (sebum); merocrine glands secrete by exocytosis.
**9.** (c) — osteoclasts are large multinucleate cells that resorb bone; osteoblasts lay down matrix and become osteocytes.
**10.** (a) — myoglobin stores O₂ and many mitochondria make ATP aerobically, so red fibres resist fatigue; white fibres rely on anaerobic glycolysis.
**11.** (d) — neuroglia (e.g. astrocytes, Schwann cells) far outnumber neurons and form the supporting framework of nervous tissue.
**12.** (c) — the vacuole makes the trophozoite look like a ring with a stone; it later grows into an amoeboid trophozoite and a schizont.
**13.** (b) — Ross linked malaria to mosquitoes (Nobel Prize 1902); Laveran discovered the parasite in human blood in 1880.
**14.** (c) — the earthworm breathes through its skin, which must stay moist; dorsal pores occur mid-dorsally from the 12/13 groove backwards.
**15.** (b) — the cerebral ganglia lie in segment 3 and join the subpharyngeal ganglia through the circumpharyngeal connectives.
**16.** (a) — fat bodies are food reserves drawn on when the frog does not feed in winter and when gonads mature.
**17.** (b) — the frog's teeth are only on the upper jaw and palate (none on the lower jaw); they grip the prey, not masticate it.
**18.** (d) — Brunner's glands are a feature of the duodenal submucosa, where acid chyme first enters the intestine.
**19.** (b) — undigested lactose is fermented by colon bacteria (lactose intolerance); in curd much lactose is already fermented to lactic acid.
**20.** (c) — alveolar pO₂ is about 104 mm Hg; atmospheric air is about 159, venous blood about 40, and venous pCO₂ about 45 mm Hg.
**21.** (a) — CO forms stable carboxyhaemoglobin (affinity about 200 times that of O₂), so the blood cannot carry oxygen.
**22.** (b) — cycle time = 60 s ÷ 80 = 0.75 s; 0.8 s corresponds to 75 beats per minute.
**23.** (c) — albumin, the most abundant plasma protein, keeps water in the vessels; antibodies are globulins.
**24.** (d) — angina is temporary ischaemia of heart muscle; infarction is death of heart muscle when supply is cut off.
**25.** (c) — the countercurrent multiplier of the loop makes the medulla hypertonic so the collecting duct can withdraw water.
**26.** (d) — glucose is normally all reabsorbed in the PCT, but its carriers saturate above the renal threshold (about 180 mg/dL).
**27.** (b) — the corpus callosum is a broad band of nerve fibres joining the hemispheres; corpora quadrigemina are in the midbrain.
**28.** (a) — the sympathetic system prepares for 'fight or flight'; the other three are parasympathetic effects.
**29.** (d) — the pineal gland secretes melatonin mainly in darkness.
**30.** (a) — this is acromegaly; excess GH before the epiphyses fuse causes gigantism, and excess cortisol causes Cushing's syndrome.
**31.** (d) — Sertoli (sustentacular) cells nourish spermatids; Leydig cells between the tubules secrete testosterone.
**32.** (c) — ovulation ≈ cycle length − 14 = 32 − 14 = day 18; only the follicular phase varies with cycle length.
**33.** (a) — PPD injected into the skin gives a delayed hypersensitivity swelling in people sensitised to TB bacilli.
**34.** (a) — H and N are the two surface glycoprotein spikes of the influenza virus envelope.
**35.** (d) — the child's own immune system made antibodies and memory cells after natural infection; a vaccine would be artificial.
**36.** (b) — cytotoxic T cells recognise foreign antigen on a cell's surface and release perforins that kill the cell; helper T cells only coordinate.
**37.** (d) — Rosie's milk carried human α-lactalbumin, more balanced for babies; α-1-antitrypsin came from the transgenic sheep Tracy.
**38.** (b) — aerated flocs of bacteria and fungi consume organic matter; grit removal is primary and biogas forms in the anaerobic digester.
**39.** (d) — its numbers recovered in Nepal and India, so IUCN moved it from Endangered to Vulnerable (2008).
**40.** (d) — nitrate causes methaemoglobinaemia ('blue baby syndrome'); fluoride causes fluorosis, arsenic skin lesions, mercury Minamata disease.

::: note Answers — Botany (41–80)
**41.** (c) — Mo is the metal cofactor of nitrogenase and nitrate reductase, so it is essential for nitrogen metabolism.
**42.** (a) — isozymes (isoenzymes) differ in structure and kinetics but catalyse one reaction; zymogens are inactive precursors.
**43.** (b) — Species Plantarum (1753) used binomials consistently; 1735 is the first Systema Naturae, 1758 its 10th edition (zoology), 1859 Darwin's Origin.
**44.** (a) — fimbriae are adhesive appendages; bacteria swim with flagella, which are longer and fewer.
**45.** (c) — Claviceps (Ascomycetes) replaces rye grains with sclerotia containing ergot alkaloids such as ergometrine.
**46.** (d) — Chlorella (Chlorophyceae) grows fast and is about half protein; Gelidium is red, Fucus brown, Spirogyra filamentous.
**47.** (b) — the sacs make the pollen light and buoyant for anemophily, and in the pollination drop they float it towards the ovule.
**48.** (a) — all its spores are alike and grow into bisexual prothalli; heterosporous plants like Selaginella have micro- and megaspores.
**49.** (c) — the papilionaceous flower of pea and bean has a large standard petal overlapping two wings, and nine fused stamens plus one free.
**50.** (d) — in Nepenthes the leaf blade forms the pitcher and its lid; the petiole is flattened and tendril-like.
**51.** (b) — Sphagnum (peat moss) has large dead hyaline cells that hold many times its weight of water; Riccia and Marchantia are liverworts.
**52.** (b) — Cuscuta is a total stem parasite: it gains and the host is harmed.
**53.** (d) — UV light splits CFCs; each Cl atom can destroy thousands of O₃ molecules before it is removed.
**54.** (d) — chilaune–katus (Schima–Castanopsis) and chir pine forests are the typical subtropical forests of Nepal.
**55.** (b) — in salty, waterlogged mud a seedling that is already growing can root quickly when it drops.
**56.** (c) — mesosomes carry respiratory enzymes and take part in DNA segregation and septum formation.
**57.** (d) — they are ATP synthase; the F₁ head makes ATP as protons pass through the F₀ base.
**58.** (d) — it carries the nucleolar organiser (rRNA genes); spindle fibres attach at the centromere and telomeres seal the ends.
**59.** (c) — after S phase each of the 20 chromosomes has 2 sister chromatids: 20 × 2 = 40.
**60.** (d) — pairing (synapsis) begins in zygotene; the complex dissolves in diplotene, when chiasmata become visible.
**61.** (b) — 680 ÷ 0.34 = 2000 bp (200 turns of 10 bp); 4000 is the number of nucleotides, not base pairs.
**62.** (d) — reverse transcriptase (Temin and Baltimore, 1970) carries information from RNA to DNA, an exception to the central dogma.
**63.** (a) — TT : Tt : tt = 1 : 2 : 1, so TT = 400 × 1/4 = 100; total tall is 300.
**64.** (d) — more chiasmata can form in a longer stretch of chromosome, which is the basis of genetic map distances.
**65.** (b) — the father passes Xʰ to every daughter (carriers) and Y to every son; sons get a normal X from the mother.
**66.** (a) — it combines the A, B and D genomes (7 chromosomes each), 6x = 42, from different species, so it is allopolyploid.
**67.** (c) — collenchyma gives flexible support to young stems and petioles; sclerenchyma is dead with lignified walls.
**68.** (a) — amphivasal (leptocentric) bundles have central phloem; amphicribral (hadrocentric) bundles, common in ferns, have central xylem.
**69.** (b) — pericycle cells opposite the protoxylem divide and push the new root out through the cortex and epidermis.
**70.** (a) — DPD = OP − TP: A = 10 − 6 = 4 atm, B = 8 − 2 = 6 atm; water moves towards the higher DPD.
**71.** (c) — active absorption of ions makes water enter the root and pushes it up, so sap exudes even without leaves.
**72.** (a) — photosynthesis fell in far-red light (>680 nm) but rose above the sum when red and far-red light were given together.
**73.** (b) — mesophyll cells fix CO₂ into C₄ acids, which release CO₂ in the bundle sheath of the Kranz anatomy for RuBisCO.
**74.** (b) — link reaction 1 NADH + Krebs cycle 3 NADH = 4 (plus 1 FADH₂ and 1 ATP/GTP).
**75.** (d) — cytokinins delay leaf senescence (Richmond–Lang effect); ABA and ethylene hasten yellowing and abscission.
**76.** (c) — Nawaschin saw it in Lilium and Fritillaria; Strasburger described syngamy and Amici the pollen tube.
**77.** (c) — the scutellum is the grass cotyledon; the coleoptile sheathes the plumule and the coleorhiza the radicle.
**78.** (a) — Skoog and Miller showed high cytokinin : auxin favours shoots and high auxin : cytokinin favours roots.
**79.** (a) — they are selectable markers: only transformed cells grow on medium containing the antibiotic.
**80.** (c) — mycorrhizal hyphae extend the absorbing area and take up phosphate; the other three supply nitrogen.
