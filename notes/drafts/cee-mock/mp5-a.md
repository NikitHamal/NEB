::: note Answers — Zoology (1–40)
**1.** (c) — Fox heated dry amino acids into proteinoids (thermal polypeptides); in cooling water these formed microspheres with a selectively permeable boundary. Oparin's protobionts were coacervates.
**2.** (a) — Malthus (1798) argued that populations grow geometrically but food only arithmetically, so many must perish; Darwin applied this to all organisms. Lyell shaped his view of geological time.
**3.** (d) — Neanderthals had about 1300–1600 cm³ brains, heavy brow ridges and no chin, made Mousterian tools and buried their dead. *H. erectus* was about 900–1100 cm³; Cro-Magnon man had a chin.
**4.** (b) — Cyclostomata (Agnatha) are jawless, with a round sucking mouth and no paired fins. Cephalochordates have no cranium or vertebrae at all.
**5.** (b) — insects such as the cockroach remove uric acid through Malpighian tubules. The earthworm uses nephridia, *Pila* a kidney, and *Hydra* simple diffusion.
**6.** (c) — in *Amphioxus* the notochord is lifelong and extends into the snout (hence Cephalo-chordata). In urochordates it is in the larval tail only; in vertebrates it is replaced by the vertebral column.
**7.** (a) — *Ascaris* has separate sexes; the male is smaller, with a ventrally curved tail and two penial spicules. *Taenia*, *Fasciola* and *Pheretima* are hermaphrodite.
**8.** (d) — goblet cells are single-celled mucous glands. Paneth cells (crypts) secrete lysozyme, chief cells secrete pepsinogen, and mast cells (connective tissue) release histamine and heparin.
**9.** (a) — microglia are the phagocytes of the brain and spinal cord. Oligodendrocytes myelinate CNS fibres, Schwann cells myelinate peripheral fibres, astrocytes support neurons.
**10.** (c) — the cilia beat towards the uterus and, with peristalsis, carry the ovum or early embryo along the tube.
**11.** (b) — like all connective tissues, blood is mesodermal and its cells lie in a matrix (plasma). It has no collagen bundles, and the vessel lining (endothelium) is an epithelium.
**12.** (d) — on 20 August 1897 Ross found oocysts on the stomach wall of an *Anopheles* fed on a malaria patient, proving the mosquito is the vector; he received the 1902 Nobel Prize.
**13.** (c) — each macrogametocyte matures into only one macrogamete, and one microgamete fertilises one macrogamete, so zygotes ≤ 50; the other 350 microgametes are wasted.
**14.** (a) — blood glands on the septa of segments 4–6 form blood cells and haemoglobin. Hearts are in 7, 9, 12, 13; the clitellum covers 14–16; the typhlosole runs in the intestine from about segment 26 onwards.
**15.** (b) — fertilisation occurs inside the cocoon, and a small worm hatches directly; earthworms have no larva. A trochophore occurs in marine polychaetes.
**16.** (d) — the pulmocutaneous arch divides into pulmonary and cutaneous arteries; both lungs and skin are respiratory surfaces in the frog.
**17.** (a) — paired vocal sacs (with nuptial pads) are male secondary sexual characters used for the mating call.
**18.** (c) — Brunner's glands lie in the submucosa of the duodenum only; their alkaline mucus protects the wall from acid chyme.
**19.** (b) — HCl from the oxyntic cells activates pepsinogen; pepsin then activates more pepsinogen (autocatalysis). Enterokinase activates trypsinogen.
**20.** (d) — the chloride shift (Hamburger phenomenon) keeps RBCs electrically neutral as HCO₃⁻ formed by carbonic anhydrase leaves the cell.
**21.** (a) — 104 − 40 = 64 mm Hg. The CO₂ gradient is much smaller (45 − 40 ≈ 5 mm Hg), but CO₂ is about 20 times more soluble.
**22.** (c) — one cycle = 60/75 = 0.8 s; diastole = 0.8 − 0.3 = 0.5 s.
**23.** (b) — the AV nodal delay lets atrial systole complete ventricular filling before the ventricles contract.
**24.** (d) — angina pectoris is reversible ischaemic pain. Myocardial infarction is the death of heart muscle from prolonged blockage.
**25.** (a) — the loop of Henle (descending limb permeable to water, ascending limb pumping out NaCl) multiplies the medullary concentration; the vasa recta act as countercurrent exchangers.
**26.** (b) — urea diffuses down its concentration gradient into urea-free fluid; glucose and salts at plasma levels prevent their loss.
**27.** (c) — the vagus (cranial nerve X) supplies parasympathetic fibres to the heart, lungs and gut.
**28.** (d) — synaptic vesicles occur only in the presynaptic knob, and receptors only on the postsynaptic membrane, so the impulse crosses one way.
**29.** (b) — zona glomerulosa → mineralocorticoids (aldosterone); fasciculata → glucocorticoids (cortisol); reticularis → sex steroids; medulla → adrenaline and noradrenaline.
**30.** (a) — these are signs of Cushing's syndrome (glucocorticoid excess). Excess thyroxine causes weight loss; insulin deficiency raises sugar but does not give a moon face.
**31.** (c) — Sertoli cells nourish developing sperms and secrete inhibin (and androgen-binding protein). Leydig cells secrete testosterone.
**32.** (d) — the luteal phase is fairly constant at about 14 days, so ovulation ≈ 32 − 14 = day 18. The follicular phase varies with cycle length.
**33.** (a) — Sabin OPV contains live, weakened virus. Salk IPV is inactivated, tetanus vaccine is a toxoid, and hepatitis B vaccine is a recombinant HBsAg subunit.
**34.** (b) — the influenza genome has eight RNA segments; in a host infected by two strains, segments mix (reassortment), giving a new antigenic type.
**35.** (c) — IgG is a Y-shaped monomer of two heavy and two light chains held by disulphide bonds. Five units with a J chain is IgM.
**36.** (d) — *Salmonella* Typhi multiplies in the lymphoid Peyer's patches of the ileum; they ulcerate and may perforate in the third week.
**37.** (b) — Rosie's milk had about 2.4 g/L of human α-lactalbumin, making it nutritionally closer to human milk.
**38.** (c) — *Propionibacterium shermanii* ferments lactate to propionic acid (flavour) and CO₂ (holes). *P. roqueforti* is the blue-cheese mould.
**39.** (a) — anadromous fish run from sea to fresh water to breed; catadromous fish (freshwater eels) go from fresh water to the sea.
**40.** (d) — the gharial is Critically Endangered; Nepal breeds and releases it from the Gharial Breeding Centre at Kasara, Chitwan National Park.

::: note Answers — Botany (41–80)
**41.** (b) — steroids such as cholesterol are derived lipids. Fats and waxes are simple lipids; phospholipids and glycolipids are compound lipids.
**42.** (d) — zinc is needed to make tryptophan, from which IAA is formed; low auxin gives short internodes and small leaves.
**43.** (a) — *Species Plantarum* (1 May 1753) is the starting point of plant names. 1735 is *Systema Naturae*, 1859 the *Origin of Species*, 1866 Mendel's paper.
**44.** (c) — mesosomes are membrane infoldings linked with respiration, wall formation and DNA separation. Plasmids are extra-chromosomal DNA; heterocysts are N₂-fixing cells of cyanobacteria.
**45.** (c) — *Aspergillus flavus* (and *A. parasiticus*) produce aflatoxins, a food-safety concern in groundnuts and maize; they damage the liver and can cause liver cancer.
**46.** (a) — red algae store floridean starch. Brown algae store laminarin and mannitol, green algae true starch, and blue-green algae cyanophycean starch.
**47.** (b) — the archegoniophore (carpocephalum) has a rayed disc with archegonia hanging below; the antheridiophore has a lobed disc with antheridia on top.
**48.** (d) — ephedrine comes from *Ephedra*, a gymnosperm (Gnetales); *Ephedra gerardiana* grows in the dry inner Himalaya of Nepal.
**49.** (a) — a hesperidium (*Citrus*) is a berry whose edible juicy hairs come from the endocarp. Pepo is cucumber, pome apple, drupe mango.
**50.** (c) — a persistent calyx (very enlarged in *Physalis*) is typical of Solanaceae, along with a bicarpellary, obliquely placed ovary.
**51.** (d) — nodes, internodes, scale leaves and axillary buds mark a rhizome as a stem; storage, horizontal growth and taste are not diagnostic.
**52.** (b) — below the compensation depth there is too little light for photosynthesis, so heterotrophs and decomposers dominate the profundal zone.
**53.** (a) — amensalism (− / 0): one species is harmed while the other is unaffected. Antibiotic secretion (antibiosis) is the standard example.
**54.** (c) — subtropical forests (about 1000–2000 m) have chir pine and *Schima–Castanopsis*. Sal is tropical; oak–rhododendron temperate; fir–birch subalpine.
**55.** (d) — hydrosere: phytoplankton → submerged → floating → reed swamp → sedge meadow → woodland → climax forest.
**56.** (b) — the botanist Schleiden (1838) and zoologist Schwann (1839) proposed the cell theory; Virchow (1855) added that cells arise from pre-existing cells.
**57.** (b) — *Ficus* leaves have CaCO₃ cystoliths in lithocysts. *Pistia* and *Colocasia* have calcium oxalate raphides; *Opuntia* has oxalate druses.
**58.** (a) — the secondary constriction (NOR) carries rRNA genes and organises the nucleolus; beyond it lies the satellite.
**59.** (d) — chiasmata begin to move towards the chromosome ends in diplotene and terminalisation is completed in diakinesis.
**60.** (c) — fraction in M × cycle time = (50/1000) × 24 h = 1.2 h = 72 minutes.
**61.** (a) — 1.02 µm = 1020 nm; 1020 ÷ 0.34 = 3000 base pairs (6000 nucleotides).
**62.** (b) — reverse transcriptase (Temin and Baltimore, 1970) copies RNA into DNA, an exception to the central dogma.
**63.** (d) — aa, bb and cc each have probability 1/4; (1/4)³ = 1/64.
**64.** (c) — both alleles are fully expressed side by side (codominance). In incomplete dominance the hybrid is an intermediate blend (pink four-o'clock).
**65.** (a) — male *Drosophila* show no crossing over, so linked genes are always inherited together. Maize, sweet pea and female *Drosophila* show incomplete linkage.
**66.** (b) — the daughter must be a carrier (XᴴXʰ); each of her sons receives Xʰ or Xᴴ from her, so the chance is 1/2.
**67.** (c) — collenchyma is living, with pectocellulose thickening at the corners, giving flexible support. Sclerenchyma is dead and lignified.
**68.** (d) — *Dracaena* and *Yucca* have amphivasal (leptocentric) bundles. Fern rhizomes have amphicribral bundles; *Cucurbita* has bicollateral bundles.
**69.** (b) — a ring of conjoint, collateral, open bundles with endarch xylem and a starch sheath is a dicot stem. Roots have radial bundles with exarch xylem.
**70.** (a) — DPD = OP − TP = 12 − 4 = 8 atm.
**71.** (c) — bleeding (exudation) from a cut stump is caused by root pressure, which pushes water up from the root.
**72.** (d) — light beyond 680 nm alone works poorly (red drop) but adding shorter-wavelength light raises the rate above the sum of both (enhancement), showing that PS I and PS II work together.
**73.** (a) — in Kranz anatomy, mesophyll cells fix CO₂ with PEP carboxylase; RuBisCO and the Calvin cycle are found only in bundle sheath chloroplasts.
**74.** (b) — cyanide blocks cytochrome c oxidase (cytochrome a₃), so electrons cannot pass to O₂ and oxidative phosphorylation stops.
