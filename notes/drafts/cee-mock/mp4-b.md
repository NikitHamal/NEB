::: note Answers — Chemistry (81–130)
**81.** (c) — Gay-Lussac (1808): e.g. 2 vol H₂ + 1 vol O₂ → 2 vol steam. Avogadro later explained it with equal numbers of molecules in equal volumes.
**82.** (d) — 3 g H₂ = 1.5 mol needs only 0.5 mol N₂ (1 mol present), so H₂ limits: N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ gives 1.0 mol NH₃ = 17 g.
**83.** (c) — A shell holds n² orbitals: 4s (1) + 4p (3) + 4d (5) + 4f (7) = 16, holding up to 2n² = 32 electrons.
**84.** (b) — Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π ≈ 5 × 10⁻³⁵ J s; for a 0.16 kg ball the resulting uncertainties are far below measurable limits, but not for an electron of mass 9.1 × 10⁻³¹ kg.
**85.** (b) — Highest n = 3 gives period 3; s² p³ = five valence electrons in a p-block element, so group 10 + 5 = 15 (phosphorus).
**86.** (d) — NaOH is Na⁺ OH⁻ (ionic), while O–H inside the hydroxide ion is covalent. NaCl is purely ionic; CH₄ and HCl are covalent.
**87.** (b) — Small, highly electronegative O forms strong intermolecular H-bonds; S cannot, so H₂S has only weak dipole and dispersion forces.
**88.** (c) — Each Cr falls from +6 to +3, so Cr₂O₇²⁻ gains 6 e⁻; each Fe²⁺ loses 1 e⁻. Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 6Fe²⁺ + 14H⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 6Fe³⁺ + 7H₂O.
**89.** (c) — Graham's law: r/r(He) = √(4/M) = 1/2, so M = 4 × 2² = 16 g mol⁻¹ (e.g. methane).
**90.** (a) — Surface tension falls as temperature rises; at the critical temperature the liquid–vapour boundary vanishes, so there is no surface.
**91.** (b) — Δn(gas) = 2 − 2 = 0, so pressure has no effect on the position; Kc changes only with temperature.
**92.** (d) — Henderson equation: pH = pKa + log([salt]/[acid]) = 4.74 + log(1.0/0.1) = 4.74 + 1 = 5.74.
**93.** (a) — 1 L = 1000 g of water; 1000/18 = 55.6 mol in 1 L, so about 55.6 mol L⁻¹.
**94.** (b) — Zero order: [A] = [A]₀ − kt, so t½ = [A]₀/2k. Only a first-order half-life (0.693/k) is independent of [A]₀.
**95.** (b) — E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode = 0.80 − (−2.37) = 3.17 V. E° is intensive, so it is not doubled for 2Ag⁺.
**96.** (d) — ΔG° = −2.303RT log K = −2.303 × 8.314 × 298 × 1 ≈ −5705 J ≈ −5.71 kJ mol⁻¹; K > 1 means ΔG° < 0.
**97.** (d) — Mass number: 27 + 1 − 4 = 24; atomic number: 13 + 0 − 2 = 11, which is sodium-24.
**98.** (b) — deuterium, ²₁H (D), has one neutron; protium has none and tritium has two.
**99.** (a) — Basic NH₃ reacts with acidic H₂SO₄ and P₂O₅ and forms an adduct (CaCl₂·8NH₃) with CaCl₂; basic CaO absorbs only the water.
**100.** (d) — Oxidizing power is F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂; a halogen displaces only those below it, so I₂ cannot oxidize Cl⁻.
**101.** (c) — In graphite each C forms three σ bonds within a layer; the fourth electron is delocalized over the layer and carries charge. Diamond's four sp³ σ bonds leave none free.
**102.** (c) — Al is a stronger reducing agent with a very negative ΔHf of Al₂O₃: Cr₂O₃ + 2Al → Al₂O₃ + 2Cr, strongly exothermic.
**103.** (b) — Sodium tarnishes in air and reacts violently with water vapour (2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂); inert kerosene keeps air out.
**104.** (d) — Ti³⁺ is 3d¹. Absorbing yellow-green light (about 500 nm) promotes the electron across the octahedral splitting Δ₀; the complementary colour is seen.
**105.** (a) — In the converter, Cu₂S + 2Cu₂O → 6Cu + SO₂; dissolved SO₂ escapes while the copper cools, blistering its surface (about 98 % Cu).
**106.** (a) — Chlorophyll is a magnesium porphyrin; Fe²⁺ is in haem and cobalt in vitamin B₁₂.
**107.** (c) — Cd from mining waste entered rice fields; Cd²⁺ damages the kidneys and disturbs calcium metabolism. Mercury caused Minamata disease.
**108.** (d) — An O lone pair of –OH delocalizes into the ring (+R), activating it; –NO₂, –CHO and –COOH withdraw electrons by resonance (−R).
**109.** (c) — Metamers have the same functional group but different alkyl groups on either side of it (C₂H₅–O–C₂H₅ vs CH₃–O–C₃H₇). The others are functional, chain and position isomers.
**110.** (b) — Number from the CHO carbon (C-1); the C=C starts at C-2, and the aldehyde suffix -al is added to but-2-ene.
**111.** (a) — MTBE raises octane number without lead. n-Heptane has octane number 0; cetane rates diesel; ethylene dibromide was only a lead scavenger used with TEL.
**112.** (d) — 2C₂H₅COO⁻ → C₂H₅–C₂H₅ + 2CO₂ + 2e⁻; two ethyl radicals join, so the alkane has twice the alkyl group: butane.
**113.** (d) — Wurtz coupling joins any two alkyl groups: CH₃–CH₃, CH₃–C₂H₅ and C₂H₅–C₂H₅. This is why Wurtz reaction is poor for odd-carbon alkanes.
**114.** (c) — A Cl lone pair delocalizes into the ring, shortening and strengthening the C–Cl bond (sp² carbon); the phenyl cation is also unstable.
**115.** (c) — CH₂=CH₂ + CO + H₂ → CH₃CH₂CHO; the aldehyde is then hydrogenated to propan-1-ol, so the chain grows by one carbon.
**116.** (a) — This is the Reimer–Tiemann reaction: dichlorocarbene attacks the ortho position of phenoxide, giving o-hydroxybenzaldehyde. With CCl₄, salicylic acid forms.
**117.** (a) — Secondary alcohols give ketones (propan-2-ol → acetone); primary give aldehydes then acids; tertiary resist oxidation.
**118.** (d) — Aldol needs an α-hydrogen to form the enolate; ethanal has three. The others have no α-H and undergo the Cannizzaro reaction instead.
**119.** (d) — Ketones have two alkyl groups, which crowd the carbonyl carbon and reduce its positive charge by +I effect; aldehydes have only one.
**120.** (a) — P₂O₅ removes water from the amide: CH₃CONH₂ → CH₃C≡N + H₂O. Br₂/KOH would instead give methylamine (Hofmann).
**121.** (b) — Moles NaOH = 0.010 = moles of acid; M = 0.60/0.010 = 60 g mol⁻¹ = CH₃COOH (HCOOH 46, C₂H₅COOH 74, C₆H₅COOH 122).
**122.** (a) — In acid the nitro group is fully reduced to –NH₂: C₆H₅NO₂ + 6[H] → C₆H₅NH₂ + 2H₂O (as the anilinium salt, freed by alkali).
**123.** (b) — Resonance makes aniline's lone pair less available to H⁺ (pKb about 9.4), whereas the +I methyl group makes CH₃NH₂ more basic (pKb about 3.4).
**124.** (c) — (CH₃)₂Cd + 2CH₃COCl → 2CH₃COCH₃ + CdCl₂. Organocadmium reagents are too weak to attack the ketone further, so it is a clean ketone synthesis.
**125.** (b) — Anode: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻. Cathode: 2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻; NaOH collects in the cathode compartment, kept apart from Cl₂ by the diaphragm.
**126.** (a) — Phenol and HCHO form methylene-bridged, highly cross-linked networks; once set, Bakelite cannot be remelted.
**127.** (a) — M = 60 g mol⁻¹, with 28 g N: 28/60 × 100 = 46.7 %. (35 % is NH₄NO₃, 21.2 % is (NH₄)₂SO₄.) This makes urea the richest solid N fertilizer.
**128.** (b) — NO₃⁻ is reduced to NO, which forms brown [Fe(H₂O)₅NO]²⁺ at the junction of the two liquids.
**129.** (a) — Starch–I₂ is deep blue; added early with much I₂ it binds iodine firmly and gives a sluggish, overshot end point. Added when the solution is pale yellow, the blue vanishes sharply.
**130.** (c) — EBT forms a wine-red complex with Mg²⁺/Ca²⁺; when EDTA has taken all the metal, free EBT turns blue at pH 10 (NH₃–NH₄Cl buffer).
