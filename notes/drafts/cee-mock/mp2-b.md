::: note Answers — Chemistry (81–130)
**81.** (b) — C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O; 4.4 g = 0.1 mol propane needs 0.5 mol O₂ = 0.5 × 22.4 = 11.2 L.
**82.** (a) — 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O: 2 mol H₂ needs 1 mol O₂ but only 0.5 mol is present, so O₂ limits and gives 1 mol (18 g) water.
**83.** (d) — radial nodes = n − l − 1 = 3 − 1 − 1 = 1.
**84.** (c) — l can only take values 0 to n − 1, so l = 2 is impossible when n = 2.
**85.** (c) — N (half-filled 2p³) exceeds O, which loses a paired electron easily; B (2p¹) is lowest. Values ≈ 1402, 1314, 1086, 801 kJ mol⁻¹.
**86.** (a) — Cl has 3 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs (sp³d); the lone pairs take equatorial positions, leaving a T shape.
**87.** (b) — the central carbon forms two σ bonds and two π bonds (to two different carbons), so it is sp-hybridized and linear.
**88.** (d) — 2(+1) + 4x + 6(−2) = 0 gives x = +2.5 (an average: two S at +5 and two at 0).
**89.** (a) — Graham's law: t ∝ √M, so t(H₂) = 20 × √(2/32) = 5 s.
**90.** (c) — orthorhombic: three unequal axes, all at right angles. Tetragonal has a = b ≠ c.
**91.** (d) — Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn with Δn = 2 − 3 = −1, so Kp/Kc = (RT)⁻¹.
**92.** (b) — [OH⁻] = 2 × 0.001 = 0.002 M; pOH = 2.70, so pH = 14 − 2.70 = 11.3.
**93.** (b) — 4.9/98 = 0.05 mol in 0.5 L = 0.1 M; H₂SO₄ is dibasic, so N = 2 × 0.1 = 0.2 N.
**94.** (d) — t½ = 0.693/k = 10 min; 75% reacted means two half-lives = 20 min (t = ln 4/k).
**95.** (c) — Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al; 9/27 = 1/3 mol Al needs 1 mol electrons = 96 500 C.
**96.** (a) — ΔG = ΔH − TΔS < 0 when T > ΔH/ΔS = 40 000/100 = 400 K.
**97.** (d) — α count = (238 − 206)/4 = 8, lowering Z to 92 − 16 = 76; six β⁻ raise it to 82.
**98.** (b) — UV light frees Cl atoms from CFCs; Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂ and ClO + O → Cl + O₂ regenerate Cl, so one atom destroys many O₃ molecules.
**99.** (a) — P₄ + 3NaOH + 3H₂O → PH₃ + 3NaH₂PO₂ (sodium hypophosphite); phosphorus disproportionates.
**100.** (c) — the H–X bond weakens down the group, so HI ionizes most easily; HF is a weak acid.
**101.** (a) — SO₂ + 2H₂O → H₂SO₄ + 2[H]; the nascent hydrogen reduces the dye, and air slowly re-oxidizes it, restoring the colour.
**102.** (c) — roasting converts sulphide ores to oxides, e.g. 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂; carbonates are calcined.
**103.** (b) — solubility of Group 2 hydroxides increases down the group (lattice energy falls faster than hydration energy).
**104.** (d) — Fe²⁺ is d⁶; strong-field CN⁻ pairs all six electrons in t₂g (t₂g⁶ eg⁰), giving zero unpaired electrons.
**105.** (c) — white vitriol is ZnSO₄·7H₂O; blue vitriol is CuSO₄·5H₂O and green vitriol is FeSO₄·7H₂O.
**106.** (d) — vitamin B₁₂ (cobalamin) contains a Co ion in a corrin ring; haemoglobin has Fe, chlorophyll Mg and carbonic anhydrase Zn.
**107.** (a) — Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pumps 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in per ATP, making the cell interior negative.
**108.** (b) — number the five-carbon chain from the end nearer the OH: OH on C-2, methyl on C-4.
**109.** (b) — four alcohols (butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-1-ol, 2-methylpropan-2-ol) and three ethers (methyl propyl, methyl isopropyl, diethyl).
**110.** (a) — electron-withdrawing Cl disperses the negative charge of the carboxylate ion, stabilizing it.
**111.** (c) — isooctane (2,2,4-trimethylpentane) is rated 100 and n-heptane 0.
**112.** (d) — peroxide effect (Kharasch): free-radical addition gives the anti-Markovnikov product, 1-bromopropane.
**113.** (d) — propyne has an acidic terminal ≡C–H that forms silver propynide; but-2-yne has no terminal hydrogen.
**114.** (c) — SN2 attack from the back is hindered by bulky groups, so methyl > 1° > 2° > 3°.
**115.** (a) — 2CHCl₃ + O₂ → 2COCl₂ + 2HCl; phosgene (COCl₂) is highly toxic.
**116.** (b) — boron adds to the less substituted carbon, so oxidation gives the anti-Markovnikov alcohol, propan-1-ol.
**117.** (c) — the halide must be primary (here methyl) for SN2; a tertiary halide with an alkoxide undergoes elimination to an alkene.
**118.** (b) — pentan-3-one (CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₃) has no CH₃CO– group; the others have CH₃CO– or CH₃CH(OH)–.
**119.** (d) — benzaldehyde has no α-hydrogen; 2-methylpropanal still has one α-H and gives the aldol reaction.
**120.** (a) — the better the leaving group (Cl⁻ > RCOO⁻ > RO⁻ > NH₂⁻), the more reactive the derivative.
**121.** (a) — HVZ replaces an α-hydrogen: CH₃COOH + Cl₂ → ClCH₂COOH + HCl (red P as catalyst).
**122.** (d) — in water, the +I effect, solvation of the cation by H-bonding and steric hindrance combine to make the secondary amine strongest and NH₃ weakest.
**123.** (b) — in neutral medium reduction stops at C₆H₅NHOH; Sn/HCl (acidic) goes all the way to aniline.
**124.** (c) — CH₃⁻ adds to the C=O of (CH₃)₂CO; hydrolysis gives (CH₃)₃COH, a tertiary alcohol.
**125.** (d) — N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ over iron promoted with molybdenum (or K₂O/Al₂O₃) at about 450 °C and 200 atm.
**126.** (a) — nylon-6,6 is from H₂N(CH₂)₆NH₂ and HOOC(CH₂)₄COOH; caprolactam gives nylon-6.
**127.** (c) — the thyroid takes up iodine, so I-131 concentrates there; Co-60 is for external radiotherapy and C-14 for dating.
**128.** (b) — when N and S are both present, NaSCN forms in the fusion and gives blood-red Fe(SCN)²⁺.
**129.** (a) — NH₄Cl sublimes on heating (NH₄Cl ⇌ NH₃ + HCl) while NaCl stays behind; both dissolve in water, so filtration fails.
**130.** (d) — weak acid–strong base titration has its equivalence point at pH ≈ 8–9, inside phenolphthalein's range (8.2–10).
