::: note Answers — Chemistry (81–130)
**81.** (a) — Masses of O (16 and 32 per 2 g H) are in a simple whole-number ratio 1 : 2: the law of multiple proportions (Dalton).
**82.** (c) — 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O: 2 mol H₂ (4 g) needs only 1 mol O₂ (32 g), so H₂ is limiting and 64 − 32 = 32 g O₂ remains.
**83.** (b) — An l = 3 (f) subshell has 2l + 1 = 7 orbitals, each holding 2 electrons: 14.
**84.** (a) — ν̄ = R(1/2² − 1/3²) = R(9 − 4)/36 = 5R/36 (the first Balmer line, Hα).
**85.** (d) — Cl (−349 kJ mol⁻¹) exceeds F (−328): the small, crowded 2p shell of F repels the incoming electron.
**86.** (b) — Cl in ClF₃ has 3 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs (sp³d); the lone pairs sit equatorially, leaving a T shape. BF₃ is trigonal planar; NH₃ and PCl₃ are pyramidal.
**87.** (c) — The CH₃ carbon has four σ bonds (sp³); both doubly bonded carbons have three σ bonds and one π bond (sp²).
**88.** (b) — 3(+1) + x + 2(−2) = 0 gives x = +1 (only one H is acidic; two are bonded to P).
**89.** (a) — Graham's law: r ∝ 1/√M, so r₁/r₂ = √(64/M) = 2 needs M = 16, i.e. CH₄.
**90.** (d) — 8 corners × 1/8 + 1 body centre × 1 = 2. Simple cubic has 1, face-centred cubic 4.
**91.** (b) — The forward reaction is endothermic, so heating favours NO₂. Raising pressure favours fewer moles (N₂O₄); inert gas at constant volume has no effect.
**92.** (a) — With [Cl⁻] ≈ 0.01 M from NaCl, s = Ksp/[Cl⁻] = 10⁻¹⁰/10⁻² = 10⁻⁸ mol L⁻¹: the common-ion effect lowers solubility from 10⁻⁵ M.
**93.** (d) — 0.1 mol NaOH per 0.5 kg solvent = 0.2 mol kg⁻¹. Molality uses kilograms of solvent, not litres of solution.
**94.** (a) — Unit of k = (mol L⁻¹)¹⁻ⁿ s⁻¹; mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹ requires n = 0.
**95.** (b) — Q = 9.65 × 600 = 5790 C = 0.06 F; Cu²⁺ needs 2 F per mol, so 0.03 mol × 63.5 = 1.905 g.
**96.** (c) — ΔG = ΔH − TΔS < 0 when T < ΔH/ΔS = 10000/50 = 200 K (both negative: enthalpy-driven, favoured at low T).
**97.** (d) — 1/4 = (½)², so two half-lives have passed: 2 × 5730 = 11460 years.
**98.** (c) — D₂O slows fast neutrons by collisions while absorbing very few of them, so it is an efficient moderator.
**99.** (a) — 2KI + O₃ + H₂O → 2KOH + I₂ + O₂; the liberated iodine turns starch blue (test for ozone).
**100.** (b) — Concentrated HNO₃ oxidizes the metal surface to a thin, adherent oxide layer that stops further attack (passivity).
**101.** (d) — HI is a strong reducing agent; hot concentrated H₂SO₄ oxidizes it to I₂ (being reduced to SO₂, S or H₂S), so H₃PO₄ is used instead.
**102.** (c) — Impurities are more soluble in the melt, so a moving molten zone sweeps them to one end; used for Si, Ge, Ga of semiconductor purity. Copper is electro-refined.
**103.** (d) — Washing soda is the decahydrate Na₂CO₃·10H₂O; NaHCO₃ is baking soda, anhydrous Na₂CO₃ soda ash and Na₂SO₄·10H₂O Glauber's salt.
**104.** (a) — Fe²⁺ is 3d⁶; the strong field forces all six electrons into t₂g (low spin, t₂g⁶ e_g⁰), so there are no unpaired electrons (diamagnetic).
**105.** (b) — Corrosive sublimate is HgCl₂ (highly poisonous); calomel is Hg₂Cl₂.
**106.** (a) — Carbonic anhydrase (Zn²⁺) interconverts CO₂ and HCO₃⁻ in red cells; catalase contains Fe, nitrogenase Mo–Fe, cytochrome oxidase Fe and Cu.
**107.** (d) — Methylmercury discharged into Minamata Bay (Japan) accumulated in fish; cadmium causes itai-itai disease.
**108.** (c) — Number the 4-carbon chain to give the C=O the lowest locant (C-2); the methyl is then on C-3.
**109.** (b) — Each doubly bonded carbon in CH₃CH=CHCH₃ has two different groups; the others have two H atoms (or two CH₃) on one carbon.
**110.** (d) — The nitronium ion is electron-deficient and attacks benzene in nitration; NH₃, H₂O and CN⁻ donate electron pairs (nucleophiles).
**111.** (a) — Octane number = % isooctane (2,2,4-trimethylpentane, 100) in an isooctane–n-heptane (0) blend with the same knocking.
**112.** (c) — The C=C of CH₃CH=CHCH₃ is cleaved and each half becomes C=O: two molecules of CH₃CHO.
**113.** (b) — C₅H₅⁻ is planar, cyclic and fully conjugated with 6 π electrons (n = 1). Cyclobutadiene (4) and cyclooctatetraene (8) are not; cyclopentadiene has an sp³ carbon.
**114.** (d) — SN2 involves back-side attack and complete Walden inversion; OH and Br have the same priority rank, so R becomes S.
**115.** (a) — A lone pair of Cl is delocalized into the ring, shortening and strengthening the C–Cl bond (sp² carbon), so it is hard to break.
**116.** (b) — Denatured (methylated) spirit is supplied tax-free for industry. Rectified spirit is 95.6% ethanol, absolute alcohol 100%, power alcohol is ethanol blended with petrol.
**117.** (c) — The negative charge of C₆H₅O⁻ is delocalized into the ring by resonance; ethoxide has no resonance and +I of ethyl destabilizes it.
**118.** (a) — Only the aldehyde reduces Tollens' reagent (silver mirror). Both give 2,4-DNP derivatives, bisulphite adducts and iodoform.
**119.** (d) — Cannizzaro reaction (no α-H): 2C₆H₅CHO + NaOH → C₆H₅CH₂OH + C₆H₅COONa.
**120.** (b) — HCOOH contains an aldehyde-like H–C=O group, so it reduces Tollens' and Fehling's reagents; other acids do not.
**121.** (c) — Saponification (soap making: fats + NaOH → soap + glycerol) is irreversible because the acid is removed as its salt.
**122.** (a) — Hofmann bromamide degradation: CH₃CONH₂ + Br₂ + 4KOH → CH₃NH₂ + K₂CO₃ + 2KBr + 2H₂O; the amine has one carbon fewer.
**123.** (d) — The –NH₂ group strongly activates the ortho and para positions, so all three are brominated at once (white precipitate).
**124.** (c) — CH₃MgI adds to CO₂ giving CH₃COOMgI; acid hydrolysis gives CH₃COOH, with one carbon more than the alkyl group.
**125.** (b) — About 2–3% gypsum retards the rapid setting of tricalcium aluminate, giving time to place the concrete.
**126.** (a) — 2NH₃ + CO₂ → NH₂COONH₄ (ammonium carbamate), which loses water to give NH₂CONH₂.
**127.** (d) — Aspirin is made by acetylating salicylic acid with acetic anhydride; methyl salicylate is oil of wintergreen.
**128.** (c) — NaCN from fusion forms Na₄[Fe(CN)₆] with FeSO₄; Fe³⁺ then gives ferric ferrocyanide, Fe₄[Fe(CN)₆]₃ (Prussian blue).
**129.** (a) — Rf = distance moved by solute / distance moved by solvent = 3.0/7.5 = 0.40 (Rf is always less than 1).
**130.** (d) — Starch gives an intense blue with I₂; the blue disappears when thiosulphate has reduced all the iodine.
