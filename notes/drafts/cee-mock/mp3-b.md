::: note Answers — Chemistry (81–130)
**81.** (a) — Masses of O (16 and 32 per 2 g H) are in a simple whole-number ratio 1 : 2: the law of multiple proportions (Dalton).
**82.** (c) — 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O: 2 mol H₂ (4 g) needs only 1 mol O₂ (32 g), so H₂ is limiting and 64 − 32 = 32 g O₂ remains.
**83.** (b) — Radial nodes = n − l − 1 = 3 − 1 − 1 = 1 (3p also has one angular node).
**84.** (a) — λ = h/√(2meV) = 12.27/√150 Å ≈ 1.00 Å = 0.1 nm.
**85.** (d) — All have 10 electrons; the fewer the protons, the weaker the pull, so O²⁻ (8p) > F⁻ (9p) > Na⁺ (11p) > Mg²⁺ (12p).
**86.** (b) — Cl in ClF₃ has 3 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs (sp³d); the lone pairs sit equatorially, leaving a T shape. BF₃ is trigonal planar; NH₃ and PCl₃ are pyramidal.
**87.** (c) — σ: three C–H, one C=C, one C–C, one C≡N = 6; π: one in C=C and two in C≡N = 3.
**88.** (b) — Mn goes from +7 in MnO₄⁻ to +2 in Mn²⁺, a gain of 5 electrons per ion (10 for two, matching 5 C₂O₄²⁻ losing 2 each).
**89.** (a) — V₂ = 600 × (400/300) × (760/380) = 1600 mL.
**90.** (d) — Orthorhombic: three unequal axes, all angles 90° (e.g. rhombic sulphur). Tetragonal has a = b ≠ c.
**91.** (b) — The forward reaction is endothermic, so heating favours NO₂. Raising pressure favours fewer moles (N₂O₄); inert gas at constant volume has no effect.
**92.** (a) — With [Cl⁻] ≈ 0.01 M from NaCl, s = Ksp/[Cl⁻] = 10⁻¹⁰/10⁻² = 10⁻⁸ mol L⁻¹: the common-ion effect lowers solubility from 10⁻⁵ M.
**93.** (d) — 0.1 M H₂SO₄ = 0.2 N; N₁V₁ = N₂V₂ gives 0.2 × 25 = 0.2 × V, so V = 25 mL.
**94.** (a) — Unit of k = (mol L⁻¹)¹⁻ⁿ s⁻¹; mol L⁻¹ s⁻¹ requires n = 0.
**95.** (b) — Q = 9.65 × 600 = 5790 C = 0.06 F; Cu²⁺ needs 2 F per mol, so 0.03 mol × 63.5 = 1.905 g.
**96.** (c) — ΔG = ΔH − TΔS < 0 when T < ΔH/ΔS = 10000/50 = 200 K (both negative: enthalpy-driven, favoured at low T).
**97.** (d) — α removes A 4 and Z 2 (²³⁴Th, Z = 90); each β⁻ adds 1 to Z, giving Z = 92, A = 234: an isotope of the parent (²³⁴U).
**98.** (c) — D₂O slows fast neutrons by collisions while absorbing very few of them, so it is an efficient moderator.
**99.** (a) — 2KI + O₃ + H₂O → 2KOH + I₂ + O₂; the liberated iodine turns starch blue (test for ozone).
**100.** (b) — Concentrated HNO₃ oxidizes the metal surface to a thin, adherent oxide layer that stops further attack (passivity).
**101.** (d) — HI is a strong reducing agent; hot concentrated H₂SO₄ oxidizes it to I₂ (being reduced to SO₂, S or H₂S), so H₃PO₄ is used instead.
**102.** (c) — Impurities are more soluble in the melt, so a moving molten zone sweeps them to one end; used for Si, Ge, Ga of semiconductor purity. Copper is electro-refined.
**103.** (d) — Solubility of the group-2 hydroxides increases down the group (lattice energy falls faster than hydration energy): Ba(OH)₂ is the most soluble.
**104.** (a) — Fe²⁺ is 3d⁶; the strong field forces all six electrons into t₂g (low spin, t₂g⁶ e_g⁰), so there are no unpaired electrons (diamagnetic).
**105.** (b) — Corrosive sublimate is HgCl₂ (highly poisonous); calomel is Hg₂Cl₂.
**106.** (a) — Vitamin B₁₂ (cobalamin) has Co at the centre of a corrin ring; Mg is in chlorophyll, Fe in haemoglobin, I in thyroxine.
**107.** (d) — Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase expels 3 Na⁺ and takes in 2 K⁺ per ATP, keeping the cell interior negative.
**108.** (c) — Longest chain including CHO has 4 C (C-1 = CHO); the methyl is on C-3: 3-methylbutanal.
**109.** (b) — Butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, 2-methylpropan-1-ol and 2-methylpropan-2-ol; the 3 ethers make 7 isomers in all.
**110.** (d) — Electronegative Cl withdraws electrons (−I), dispersing the negative charge of ClCH₂COO⁻ and stabilizing it (pKa 2.86 vs 4.76).
**111.** (a) — Octane number = % isooctane (2,2,4-trimethylpentane, 100) in an isooctane–n-heptane (0) blend with the same knocking.
**112.** (c) — Peroxide effect (Kharasch): free-radical addition puts Br on the terminal carbon, giving anti-Markovnikov CH₃CH₂CH₂Br.
**113.** (b) — Only terminal alkynes (≡C–H) have an acidic H that forms silver acetylide; propyne gives CH₃C≡CAg (white).
**114.** (d) — SN2 involves back-side attack and complete Walden inversion; OH and Br have the same priority rank, so R becomes S.
**115.** (a) — 2CHCl₃ + O₂ → 2COCl₂ + 2HCl; 1% ethanol is added to convert any phosgene into harmless diethyl carbonate.
**116.** (b) — BH₃ adds with B on the terminal carbon; H₂O₂/OH⁻ replaces B by OH, giving anti-Markovnikov propan-1-ol.
**117.** (c) — The negative charge of C₆H₅O⁻ is delocalized into the ring by resonance; ethoxide has no resonance and +I of ethyl destabilizes it.
**118.** (a) — Iodoform needs CH₃CO– or CH₃CH(OH)–; ethanol is oxidized to CH₃CHO and gives yellow CHI₃. Propanal, pentan-3-one and methanol lack the group.
**119.** (d) — Cannizzaro reaction (no α-H): 2C₆H₅CHO + NaOH → C₆H₅CH₂OH + C₆H₅COONa.
**120.** (b) — Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky reaction replaces an α-hydrogen by Cl, giving ClCH₂COOH.
**121.** (c) — Reactivity follows leaving-group ability: Cl⁻ > RCOO⁻ > RO⁻ > NH₂⁻; amides are least reactive because of strong resonance.
**122.** (a) — Hofmann bromamide degradation: CH₃CONH₂ + Br₂ + 4KOH → CH₃NH₂ + K₂CO₃ + 2KBr + 2H₂O; the amine has one carbon fewer.
**123.** (d) — In water, +I effect and solvation together make (CH₃)₂NH strongest (pKb ≈ 3.3); (CH₃)₃N is less solvated, NH₃ weaker, aniline weakest.
**124.** (c) — CH₃⁻ adds to the C=O carbon of (CH₃)₂CO; hydrolysis gives the tertiary alcohol (CH₃)₃COH, 2-methylpropan-2-ol.
**125.** (b) — About 2–3% gypsum retards the rapid setting of tricalcium aluminate, giving time to place the concrete.
**126.** (a) — n H₂N(CH₂)₆NH₂ + n HOOC(CH₂)₄COOH → nylon-6,6 + water; both monomers have six carbons. Caprolactam gives nylon-6.
**127.** (d) — The thyroid takes up iodine, so I-131 is used for thyroid scans and to treat hyperthyroidism and thyroid cancer. Co-60 is for external cancer therapy.
**128.** (c) — NaCN from fusion forms Na₄[Fe(CN)₆] with FeSO₄; Fe³⁺ then gives ferric ferrocyanide, Fe₄[Fe(CN)₆]₃ (Prussian blue).
**129.** (a) — Rf = distance moved by solute / distance moved by solvent = 3.0/7.5 = 0.40 (Rf is always less than 1).
**130.** (d) — Weak acid–strong base titration has its equivalence point near pH 8–9, inside the phenolphthalein range (8.3–10).
