Unit 3 is about **giving information to many people at once** and **giving
instructions**. Reading I has two announcements: a pre-flight announcement by
Nepal Airlines and a Parents Day announcement by Dibya Jyoti Secondary School.
Reading II is a set of seven steps on how to wash clothes in a washing machine.
The grammar is **reported speech of yes/no questions** (*She asked if…*). In
writing you prepare an announcement and a set of instructions.

::: key What the exam asks from this unit
(1) Find facts in an announcement: what, when, where, who. (2) Report yes/no
questions with **if/whether**, changing tense, pronouns and time words.
(3) Write an announcement that answers *what, when, where, who, how* and invites
people. (4) Write numbered instructions with imperative verbs.
:::

## 3.1 Reading I: Public Announcements

An **announcement** is a short public message, spoken or written, that tells
people about an event, a change or a rule. It is made at airports, bus parks,
schools, offices and on radio and TV.

### Text 1: Pre-flight announcement (Nepal Airlines)

The crew welcome passengers to **Flight RA-402 from Kathmandu to New Delhi**.
The plane is **third in line** for take-off and should be in the air in about
**five minutes**. Passengers are asked to:

1. fasten their seatbelts;
2. keep their baggage under the seat or in the overhead compartments;
3. put their seats in the **upright** position for take-off;
4. turn off personal gadgets such as laptops and mobile phones.

Smoking is strictly prohibited. The announcement ends by thanking passengers for
choosing Nepal Airlines.

### Text 2: Parents Day announcement (Dibya Jyoti Secondary School)

| Question | Answer from the text |
|---|---|
| What? | Parents Day, on the school's **42nd anniversary** |
| When? | **22nd of March**, at **11:00 a.m.** |
| Where? | the **Assembly Hall** |
| Who is the chief guest? | the **Mayor of Bardaghat Municipality**, an ex-student of the school |
| What will happen? | the mayor confers prizes for academic, extra-curricular and co-curricular activities; a cultural performance by the school's talented students; a short photo session with the chief guest **at the end** |

## 3.2 Writing I: Writing an announcement

### Format

1. **Heading** — the name of the organisation and/or *Announcement*.
2. **Opening** — *We are pleased to announce that…* / *Attention, please!*
3. **What, when, where, who, how** — the event, date, time, place, chief guest
   or organiser, and the programme.
4. **Request** — *All are requested to attend / to be on time.*
5. **Closing** — *Thank you!* and the name of the person or office announcing.

::: example Model answer — Children's Day announcement (Writing I)
**Problem.** As class captain, write an announcement about celebrating an event
at your school.

**Solution.**

**Shree Janata Secondary School, Butwal — Announcement**

Dear friends and teachers, I am pleased to announce that our school is going
to celebrate **Children's Day** on **Bhadra 29** (14 September). The programme
will be held in the school ground from **10:30 a.m.** The ward chairperson will
be the chief guest. There will be a quiz contest, a drawing competition, songs,
dances and a short speech on children's rights by grade 8 students. Prizes will
be given to the winners, and snacks will be served at the end. All students,
teachers and parents are kindly requested to attend the programme on time and
make it a success. Thank you!

Ramesh Thapa
Class Captain, Grade 8
:::

::: caution Common mistakes in announcements
- Missing the **date, time or place** — these carry the marks.
- Writing it like a story in the past tense. Use **will / is going to** for the
  event and the present for requests.
- No request to attend at the end.
:::

## 3.3 Speaking and pronunciation: requests and /r/

| Making a request | Responding |
|---|---|
| Can I ask you to…? | Yes, what do you need? |
| I was wondering if you could… | I'll see what I can do. |
| Do you think you could…? | Let me check and get back to you. |
| Could you please…? | Sure, no problem. / I'm sorry, I've injured my arm. |
| Would you mind if I…? | Of course not. Go ahead. |

Example from the clues: *Could you please help push my wheelchair across the
road? — Sure, no problem.* / *I was wondering if you could lend me your bicycle
for a few days. — I'm sorry, I need it myself.*

**Pronunciation of r.** In standard British English, *r* is pronounced only
before a vowel: in *compartment, turn off, your, confer, winner* the *r* at the
end of a syllable is silent and the vowel is lengthened (*turn* /tɜːn/). In
*overhead, anniversary, entertainment, guardian* — check whether a vowel
follows. American English pronounces every *r*.

## 3.4 Grammar: Reported speech — yes/no questions

When we report a yes/no question:

1. Use **asked / wanted to know / inquired / wondered** as the reporting verb.
2. Join with **if** or **whether**.
3. Change the question into **statement word order** (subject + verb) and drop
   the question mark.
4. Remove *do/does/did* and change the tense one step back (if the reporting
   verb is past).
5. Change pronouns and time/place words.

| Direct | Reported (past reporting verb) |
|---|---|
| is/are | was/were |
| do/does + verb | past simple (*likes → liked*) |
| did + verb / past simple | had + past participle |
| has/have + pp | had + pp |
| can / will / may | could / would / might |
| today, tomorrow, this | that day, the next day, that |

*"Does he like singing?"* → *She asked **if he liked** singing.*

If the reporting verb is **present** (*says, asks*), do **not** change the tense:
*"Is your sister coming?" says Pallawi.* → *Pallawi **asks** if my sister **is**
coming.*

a. Pawan asked if I was going to the store. b. Pallawi asks if my sister is

::: caution Common mistakes
- *She asked **did I finish** my homework.* ✗ → *She asked **if I had finished**…* ✓
- *He asked if was I ready?* ✗ → *He asked **if I was** ready.* ✓ (no question order, no ?)
- *She said me if…* ✗ → *She **asked** me if…* ✓
- Forgetting pronoun change: *"Are you a foreigner?" she asked* → *She asked if **I** was…*
:::

## 3.5 Reading II: How to Wash Clothes in a Washing Machine

A washing machine is a home **appliance** that washes clothes automatically. The
text gives seven steps:

| Step | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Separate your laundry | sort by fabric and colour; read the care label; wash delicate items by hand or on the delicate cycle in a mesh bag | to avoid damaging fabrics |
| 2. Load the machine | put the clothes in; **do not overload** | an overloaded machine does not wash properly |
| 3. Add detergent and fabric softener | read the manual to see which goes where; check the amount on the detergent box | the amount depends on load size and machine type |
| 4. Turn on the washer | close the door and press start | some machines warn if the door is open, some do not |
| 5. Choose the washing cycle | follow the care label; cycles: normal, permanent press, delicate, quick | to clean well and protect clothes |
| 6. Set the water temperature | **hot** for towels, bedding, sturdy and very dirty items; **warm** for moderately dirty loads, dark colours, permanent press; **cold** is gentlest and saves energy | hot water kills germs but can shrink and fade clothes and uses more energy |
| 7. Clean the machine | use a washing machine cleaner; leave the door open after use | to air it out and prevent mould |

### Vocabulary (Exercise A)

a–v (appliance: a device for a specific task), b–i (agitate: make something
move around by shaking), c–ii (shrink), d–vi (detergent: a water-soluble
cleaning substance), e–iv (dump: put down carelessly), f–iii (mould: a fungus).

### Comprehension (Exercise B) — model answers

**a. What is a washing machine?** It is a home appliance that does the laundry
automatically, without anyone having to supervise it.

**b. Why is it necessary to separate the laundry?** Different fabrics and
colours need different cycles and temperatures; sorting stops colours running
and protects delicate clothes from damage.

**c. How can the user maximise the cleanliness of clothes?** By choosing the
correct washing cycle and water temperature according to the fabric care label.

**d. Mention the effects of overloading.** The clothes are not washed as well as
they should be.

**e. Why should we clean a washing machine regularly?** To keep it fresh and to
prevent mould from growing inside it.

**f. Is the text useful for you? How?** Yes. It teaches me to use a washing
machine safely and correctly, so that clothes get clean without shrinking or
fading and energy is saved.

## 3.6 Writing II: Preparing a set of instructions

- Give a **title** (*How to…*) and, if needed, a list of things required.
- **Number** the steps in the right order.
- Begin each step with an **imperative verb**: *Pour, Add, Stir, Press*.
- Keep each step short; add warnings with *Make sure…* / *Do not…*.

::: example Model answer — How to prepare sel roti (project work)
**Things needed:** 2 cups of rice soaked overnight, ½ cup sugar, 2 tablespoons
ghee, a little water or milk, oil for frying.

1. Drain the soaked rice and grind it into a smooth, thick paste.
2. Add the sugar and ghee and mix well; add a little water so that the batter
   flows slowly.
3. Leave the batter to rest for about half an hour.
4. Heat the oil in a deep pan on a medium flame.
5. Pour the batter into the oil in a ring shape, by hand or from a cone.
6. Turn the roti with a stick when the lower side is golden brown.
7. Take it out, let the extra oil drain, and serve warm.

*Make sure the oil is hot enough before you pour; otherwise the roti will soak
up oil.*
:::

## 3.7 Punctuation (Extra bit): comma, semicolon, colon

| Mark | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| comma (,) | items in a list; after linking phrases; before quoted speech; after *yes/no* | *On the other hand, phones have some disadvantages.* |
| semicolon (;) | joins two closely related complete sentences; separates list items that already have commas | *I am going abroad this year; I will study there.* |
| colon (:) | introduces a list or an explanation | *I need to buy: milk, bread and vegetables.* |

## Chapter summary

- Announcements give *what, when, where, who* and end with a request and thanks.
- Flight RA-402, Kathmandu → New Delhi: seatbelts, baggage under seat or
  overhead, seat upright, gadgets off, no smoking.
- Parents Day: 22nd March, 11 a.m., Assembly Hall, 42nd anniversary, chief guest
  the Mayor of Bardaghat (an ex-student).
- Reported yes/no question: *asked + if/whether + subject + verb*, tense one
  step back, no question mark.
- Present reporting verb (*says/asks*) → no tense change.
- Instructions: title, numbered steps, imperative verbs, warnings.
- Washing: separate → load → detergent → turn on → cycle → temperature → clean.
