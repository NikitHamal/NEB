Unit 15 is about **giving reasons and explanations**. Reading I, *Conservation
of Earth*, explains why every creature needs its natural habitat, why saving
single species is not enough, and why we must look after the biosphere. Reading
II is a real **official notice** from Nepal's Ministry of Education, Science and
Technology about closing schools during the COVID-19 spread in 2078 BS. The
grammar is **connectives of reason, purpose and contrast** (*because, so that,
although, however…*). In writing you write an argumentative (opinion) essay
and a notice.

::: key What the exam asks from this unit
(1) Explain cause and effect from an expository text. (2) Pick out the key facts
of a notice: who, what, when, why, what to do. (3) Join sentences with the
correct connective and correct grammar after it (*because* + clause, *because
of* + noun). (4) Write an argumentative essay with a clear opinion and reasons.
(5) Write a notice in the proper format.
:::

## 15.1 Reading I: Conservation of Earth

### Summary, paragraph by paragraph

1. **Natural habitat.** Every creature has a place where it feels at home —
   polar bears in the Arctic (protected by **thick layers of fat**), lions in
   hot African grasslands (thinner coats). This place is its **natural
   habitat**, where it and its **ancestors** have lived for perhaps millions of
   years. Habitats differ in temperature and vegetation; wild creatures are used
   to one kind and **cannot survive long in a different one**.
2. **Early conservation.** Several **decades** ago, **conservationists** began
   saving specific endangered species by banning some **hunting, poaching and
   overfishing**.
3. **Saving habitats.** Saving one species was not enough; its home had to be
   saved too. Habitats are in danger because people cut forests for firewood
   and timber, and **global warming** is melting the ice caps.
4. **Extinction.** Thousands of species are in danger of becoming **extinct**,
   and once gone they never come back. Thousands more are still **undiscovered**,
   in hard-to-reach places — isolated rainforests, deep oceans, the Arctic and
   Antarctic.
5. **The biosphere.** Other creatures give us **food, building materials, fuel
   and medicine**. If we destroy the biosphere through greed or foolishness, we
   will die out ourselves. Conserving it means looking after ourselves.
6. **Looking ahead.** Some scientists are searching for other **habitable**
   planets; the **James Webb Space Telescope** looks for signs of oxygen and
   water vapour. This will take hundreds of years, so we must look after the
   Earth now.

**Main idea:** protect habitats and the whole biosphere, not just single
species, because human survival depends on it.

### Comprehension (Exercise C) — model answers

**a. How can polar bears live in the Arctic?** Their thick layers of fat protect
them from the freezing temperatures.

**b. Why can't wild creatures survive long in a different habitat?** They are
adapted to the climate, vegetation and other species of their own habitat,
where they and their ancestors have lived for millions of years.

**c. What have conservationists done to save specific species?** They banned
some hunting, poaching and overfishing.

**d. What is the impact of cutting down trees?** The creatures living in the
forest lose their home and may have nowhere else to go.

**e. Where are many undiscovered species found?** In places that are difficult to
reach, such as isolated rainforests, deep oceans, the Arctic and the Antarctic.

**f. What do other creatures contribute to humans?** Food, building materials,
fuel and medicine.

**g. Why are scientists looking for oxygen and water vapour on other planets?**
Because living things need them; their presence would suggest that a planet
might be habitable.

## 15.2 Grammar: Connectives of reason, purpose and contrast

| Function | Connective | Followed by | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reason** | because, as, since | a clause | They survive **because** they have thick fat. |
| | because of, due to | a **noun / noun phrase** | He couldn't join the army **because of** his visual impairment. |
| **Result** | so, therefore | a clause | It rained; **therefore**, we stayed in. |
| **Purpose** | so that | clause (often with *can/could*) | I went to the bank **so that** I could withdraw money. |
| | to, in order to | **base verb** | She spoke clearly **in order to** impress the headteacher. |
| **Contrast** | but | clause (joins two) | I work a lot, **but** I don't get good grades. |
| | although, even though | clause | **Although** she was sick, she came to school. |
| | in spite of, despite | **noun / -ing** | **In spite of** being ill, he wants to meet me. |
| | however | new sentence, comma after | It was a great start; **however**, it was not enough. |

**Rule to remember:** *although* and *but* are **not** used together:
*~~Although~~ she was rich, ~~but~~ she lived simply* — use only one.

### Textbook answers

**Grammar I A:** a. because b. Although c. because d. however e. to f. so that

**Grammar I B:** a. because b. because of c. because d. Although e. so that
f. Because of g. Although h. in order to i. but

**Grammar II:** a. Although she is very rich, she lives a simple life. b. In
spite of being ill (his illness), he wants to meet me. c. Although she works
carefully, she makes many mistakes. d. I went to the grocery so that I could buy
some rice. e. She has got good grades; however, she has not passed the entrance
test. f. He ran away because he was afraid. g. We did not go out because of the
heavy rain. h. Although it was hot, we went to play football. i. She bought a
book and a pencil. j. He likes dancing, but he doesn't like singing.

::: caution Common mistakes
- *because of it was raining* ✗ → *because it was raining* / *because of the
  rain* ✓
- *in spite of he was ill* ✗ → *in spite of being ill* / *although he was ill* ✓
- *in order to buying* ✗ → *in order to **buy*** ✓
- *Although … but …* ✗ — use one connective only.
:::

## 15.3 Writing I: An argumentative (opinion) essay

**Structure:** title → introduction with your **opinion** → 2–3 body paragraphs,
each with one reason and an example → a paragraph answering the other side →
conclusion restating your view. Use connectives: *firstly, moreover, because,
however, on the other hand, therefore, in conclusion*.

::: example Model answer — Radio and Television: Good or Bad for Students?
**Radio and Television: Good or Bad for Students?**

Some people think that radio and television waste students' time, while others
believe they are an important source of learning. In my opinion, radio and
television are useful for students if they are used wisely.

Firstly, they give knowledge. News programmes keep students informed about
events in Nepal and the world, and educational channels and radio lessons teach
science, history and languages in an interesting way. During the COVID-19
school closures, many Nepali students continued learning through radio and TV
lessons.

Secondly, they improve language skills. Listening to English news or
documentaries helps students learn new words and correct pronunciation.
Moreover, quiz shows and debates encourage them to think.

On the other hand, it is true that too much television harms students.
Some students watch films and serials for hours, neglect their homework and
damage their eyes, and some programmes show violence. However, this is a
problem of overuse, not of the media themselves.

In conclusion, radio and television are good teachers if students choose
useful programmes and limit their viewing time. Parents should guide them so
that these media help, not harm, their studies.
:::

## 15.4 Reading II: An Official Notice

### The notice in brief

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Issuing office | Government of Nepal, **Ministry of Education, Science and Technology**, Singhadurbar, Kathmandu |
| Date | **26th Poush 2078** |
| Title | Notice about School Closure |
| What and why | all schools across the country closed **because of the rapid spread of COVID-19** |
| When | from **27th Poush to 15th Magh** |
| Other decisions | children aged **12–17** to be vaccinated at local health posts during the vacation; parents to take their children on the specified dates; schools to continue learning through **alternative methods**; universities may hold pre-scheduled exams following health and safety standards |

**Format of a notice:** name of the organisation → date → the word *Notice* and
a title → the message (what, when, where, why, who) → request/instructions →
name and post of the person issuing it (signature).

### Vocabulary (Exercise A)

a–iii (rapid), b–v (infection), c–iv (vaccination), d–i (arrangement),
e–ii (alternative)

### Comprehension (Exercise B) — model answers

**a. What is the notice about?** The closure of all schools in Nepal.
**b. Why did the government close all schools?** Because of the rapid spread of
COVID-19 infection.
**c. How long will schools remain closed?** From 27th Poush to 15th Magh, about
three weeks.
**d. What are parents asked to do?** To contact schools and take their children
aged 12–17 for vaccination on the specified dates.
**e. What are universities told about exams?** They can hold their
pre-scheduled examinations following public health and safety standards.

**Exercise C (message to an absent friend):** *Hi Suman, the Ministry of
Education has closed all schools from 27th Poush to 15th Magh because COVID-19
is spreading fast. Children aged 12 to 17 will get vaccines at the local health
post during the holiday, so ask your parents to contact the school for the date.
Our teachers will send lessons online. Take care! — Bibek*

## 15.5 Writing II: A notice

::: example Model answer — one-day school closure
**Shree Saraswati Secondary School**
Pokhara-8, Kaski

Date: 5 Bhadra 2082

**NOTICE**

**School Closed on Thursday**

This is to inform all students, teachers and parents that the school will
remain closed on **Thursday, 7 Bhadra 2082**, because the school building will be
used as a polling centre for the by-election. Regular classes will resume on
Friday, 8 Bhadra, at the usual time.

All students are requested to complete the homework given for the day.

Principal
:::

::: tip The examiner is looking for
The organisation's name, the date, the heading *NOTICE* and a title, the exact
day/date of the closure, the **reason**, what happens next, and the issuer's
post. Keep it short, formal and impersonal (no *Dear…*, no *Yours…*).
:::

## 15.6 Speaking, pronunciation and project work

**Giving explanations (speaking):** *Why are bicycles popular these days? —
Because they are cheap and eco-friendly.* / *Why did you go to the bank? — I
went there in order to withdraw some money.*

**English consonant sounds** in the word list: /k/ creature, /t/ temperature,
/l/ layer, /h/ habitat, /s/ ancestors, /w/ warming, /f/ freezing, /ʃ/ bush,
/ʒ/ treasure and decision, /dʒ/ danger, /v/ living, /ɡ/ great, /θ/ thousands,
/ð/ another, /j/ you. English has 24 consonant sounds.

**Listening (zoos):** the speaker is against keeping animals in zoos; he says
they look sad, children can learn from television and the internet, and most zoo
animals are not endangered.

**Project work:** make a class bulletin board from local materials and pin
notices on it.

## Chapter summary

- Natural habitat = where a creature and its ancestors have always lived; wild
  creatures cannot survive long elsewhere.
- Conservation moved from banning hunting/poaching/overfishing to saving whole
  habitats; threats: deforestation, global warming; extinction is permanent.
- The biosphere gives food, building materials, fuel, medicine; destroying it
  threatens humans.
- Notice (26 Poush 2078): schools closed 27 Poush–15 Magh due to COVID-19;
  vaccination for 12–17-year-olds.
- *because* + clause / *because of* + noun; *so that* + clause / *(in order) to* +
  verb; *although* + clause / *in spite of* + noun or -ing; *however* starts a
  new sentence.
- Opinion essay: opinion → reasons with examples → other side → conclusion.
