Unit 7 is about **the future** — predicting the weather and thinking about where
people will live. Reading I is a weather report for seven stations in Nepal
with a forecast for today and tonight. Reading II is an essay on **migration**:
why people move, push and pull factors, and its benefits and drawbacks. The
grammar is the **future simple** (*will*), the **going to future** and the
**future perfect** (*will have done*). In writing you punctuate a paragraph and
write paragraphs on the environment.

::: key What the exam asks from this unit
(1) Read a data table: highest, lowest, zero values. (2) Use *will* for
decisions made now and uncertain predictions, *going to* for plans and
predictions from present evidence. (3) Use *will have + past participle* with
*by* (*by next week*). (4) Explain push and pull factors. (5) Write a
well-organised essay on the environment.
:::

## 7.2 Reading II: Migration

### Summary

- People have always moved. Those who visit a place for a short time are
  **tourists**. Moving to another place **to live there** is **migration**;
  people who move in search of work or shelter are **migrants**. Because more
  and more people move for better opportunities, the number of migrants will
  rise.
- **Pull factors** attract people to the destination: better jobs, higher
  wages, facilities and working conditions.
- **Push factors** drive people out: economic, social or political problems and
  natural disasters.
- **Benefits:** the text says the place of origin may gain economic
  opportunities (for example, more jobs and land for those who stay, especially
  in rural areas short of labour). The destination fills labour shortages,
  gains new businesses and consumer spending, new skills, knowledge and ideas
  that boost productivity, and a richer culture.
- **Drawbacks:** loss of skilled people from the home country ("brain drain");
  jobs become hard to find and pay low where too many migrants arrive;
  population growth harms the environment; migrants may face **cultural
  displacement** and struggle to adapt.


### Vocabulary (Exercise A — word search)

a. **destination** b. **intention** c. **overrun** d. **drawbacks**
e. **disaster** f. **prospect** g. **drive**

### Comprehension (Exercise B) — model answers

**a. Who are called tourists?** People who go to a new place only for a short
visit.

**b. Why do people move from one place to another?** In search of work, shelter
and better opportunities, or because problems or disasters push them out of
their place.

**c. List any two push factors.** Economic problems (such as poverty and
unemployment) and natural disasters. (Social and political problems are also
correct.)

**d. When does it become difficult for migrants to find a job?** When a place
is overrun by migrants.

**e. List any two benefits of migration to the destination.** It fills labour
shortages and helps start new businesses; migrants bring new skills, knowledge
and ideas that increase productivity and enrich the culture.

## 7.3 Grammar: Future simple, going to future and future perfect

### will vs be going to

| Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **will** + base verb | a decision made **at the moment of speaking** | I forgot the bread! I'**ll** go back and buy some. |
| | a **prediction** based on opinion (less certain) | I think he **will** win the election. |
| | offers, promises, conditions | If I meet him, I **will** tell him. |
| **am/is/are going to** + base verb | a **plan** already decided | I'**m going to** meet her next week. |
| | a prediction from **present evidence** (more certain) | Look at those dark clouds! It'**s going to** rain. |

### Future perfect

**will have + past participle** — an action that will be **finished before** a
point in the future. It usually comes with **by** (*by 6 o'clock, by next year,
by the time…*). Negative: **won't have + pp**.

| Future simple | Future perfect |
|---|---|
| He will leave home at 8 a.m. (he leaves *at* 8) | He will have left home by 8 a.m. (he leaves *before* 8) |
| I will eat lunch at 12 noon. | I will have eaten lunch by 12 noon. |

### Textbook answers

**Grammar I B:** a. 'll (decision now) b. am going to (plan; boots already on)
c. will (condition) d. am going to (plan) e. is going to (evidence: dark clouds)
f. am going to (long-time plan) g. B: 'll cancel (decision now); C: am going to
spend (already planned after hearing the news).

**Grammar II B:** a. won't have finished b. will have grown c. will have mended
d. will have closed e. will have finished f. won't have been g. will have
ironed h. will have left i. will have completed j. will have saved

**Grammar II C:** a. will have found (*in twenty years*) b. will come c. will
have finished (*by next month*) d. 'll drop (offer) e. will have left (*by the
time*) f. Will … open (request) g. Will … complete h. Will … have completed
(*by the deadline*) i. won't pass j. will have finished (*in two hours' time*)

::: caution Common mistakes
- *It will rain — look at those clouds!* → use ***is going to*** with visible
  evidence.
- *I will have finish it by Monday.* ✗ → *will have **finished*** ✓
- *By next year she will pass.* → *By next year she **will have passed**.* ✓
- *I am going to answer the phone* — for a sudden decision say *I'**ll** answer
  it.*
:::

to stay there?* – g; *Where will I go to study?* – c; *Will I have good

Board-game prompts: *It's very cloudy today.* → *It's going to rain.* / *He is

dance competition.* → *I'm going to practise every day.*

## 7.5 Writing: punctuation and paragraphs on the environment

### Punctuation (Writing A) — corrected

*Nitin is one of the most laid-back students I know. He is tall and slim with
black hair, and he always wears a shirt and black jeans. His jeans have holes
in them, and his baseball boots are scruffy too. He usually sits at the back of
the class, and he often seems to be asleep. However, when the exam results are
given out, he always gets an "A". I don't think he's as lazy as he appears to
be.*

Points checked: capitals for *Nitin* and *I*; full stops at sentence ends;
comma after *However*; apostrophes in *don't* and *he's*.

### Paragraphs on the environment (Writing B)

Plan four paragraphs, one per question: definition → importance → causes of
degradation → protection.

::: example Model answer — Our Environment
**Problem.** Write a couple of paragraphs on the environment.

**Solution.** The environment is everything around us — air, water, soil,
plants, animals and the people and buildings of our surroundings. Living and
non-living things depend on one another, and together they make our natural
surroundings.

The environment is important because it gives us the air we breathe, the water
we drink, food, medicine and materials for shelter. Forests control floods and
landslides, and clean surroundings keep us healthy.

However, our environment is being degraded. Forests are cut down for timber and
settlements; rivers such as the Bagmati are polluted by sewage and waste;
vehicles and factories pollute the air; and plastic is thrown everywhere. The
growing population increases all these problems.

We can protect the environment in simple ways. We should plant trees, reduce,
reuse and recycle waste, avoid plastic bags, use public transport and save
water and electricity. Community clean-up campaigns and strict laws are also
needed. If each of us acts responsibly, we will hand over a healthy earth to
the next generation.
:::
