Unit 12 is about **hobbies, likes and dislikes**. Reading I, *Nepal's Bird
Man*, is a tribute by Rajendra N. Suwal to Hari Sharan Nepali, "Kazi Dai", a
self-taught ornithologist who turned birdwatching into a lifelong mission for
bird conservation in Nepal. Reading II is a funny poem by Kenn Nesbitt, *My
Teacher Ate my Homework*. The grammar is **verb + -ing or infinitive**
(*enjoy reading, want to read*) and **yes/no and wh-questions**. In writing you
write an essay on your hobbies and interests.

::: key What the exam asks from this unit
(1) Answer questions on a profile: who, what he did, which organisations.
(2) Explain the joke and the rhymes of the poem. (3) Choose *-ing* or *to +
verb* after a given verb. (4) Form wh- and yes/no questions from statements.
(5) Write an essay on hobbies answering the five textbook questions.
:::

## 12.1 Reading I: Nepal's Bird Man

### Summary

- **Hari Sharan Nepali** was called **Kazi** by his friends because of his smart
  dress and genteel manners. "Kazi Dai" was a famous **birder and
  ornithologist** (a scientist who studies birds).
- He was a friend of the writer's father, **Parasar Narayan Suwal**, who had a
  Ph.D.; Kazi Dai was a school dropout. They were bonded by their shared love
  of the wilderness and explored the jungles of the Terai together.
- **1972:** the writer joined his father's students of Ananda Kuti Science
  College on a week-long camp to Pokhara. Kazi Dai taught bird ecology, field
  identification and taxidermy. They trekked from Bagar to Hyangja and were hit
  by a hailstorm; at Suinkhet he identified a Lesser Racquet-tailed Drongo, a
  Green Magpie and an Egyptian Vulture; on the way back he pointed out a Wall
  Creeper. This mentoring was a **turning point** in the writer's interest in
  birds.
- **Late 1970s:** Kazi Dai and **Karna Shakya** (then with the IUCN Commission
  on Education) founded the **Nepal Bird Watching Club**, later renamed **Bird
  Conservation Nepal (BCN)**. BCN now trains young Nepalis in bird
  documentation, photography, research and conservation; ornithology has moved
  on to satellite tracking, and a digital record of **more than 880 bird
  species** of Nepal has been made.
- **1995:** the writer and Kazi Dai documented his knowledge in the Biodiversity
  Profiles Project (Department of National Parks and Wildlife Conservation);
  both served on the Environment Protection Council chaired by the Prime
  Minister.
- They went birding with **Jimmy Carter** (a former US President) and set up the
  **Lumbini Crane Sanctuary**, now a nesting home for **Sarus cranes**,
  supported by WWF Nepal with the International Crane Foundation and Lumbini
  Development Trust.
- He led the Ornithological Survey of Nepal; Tribhuvan University's **Natural
  History Museum** is an outcome of his passion; he identified **thirteen new
  bird species** for Nepal beyond those listed by the Flemings and Lain Singh
  Bangdel (1976).
- He died on **19 May 2021**; his passion lives on in the young naturalists he
  mentored.

(The textbook misprints IUCN as "International Union for the *Conversation* of
Nature"; it is the International Union for **Conservation** of Nature.)

### Vocabulary (Exercise A)

a. **diversity** b. **genteel** c. **enhanced** d. **mentoring** e. **nurturing**

### Multiple choice (Exercise B)

a. **ii. birder** b. **iii. Parasar N. Suwal** c. **iii.** while they were on the
trip to Pokhara ("his mentoring during this trip was a turning point")
d. **ii** is false — he worked with foreigners such as Jimmy Carter.
e. **i. IUCN** — it was Karna Shakya who was with the IUCN; Kazi Dai co-founded
BCN and worked on the WWF-supported crane sanctuary.

### Comprehension (Exercise C) — model answers

**a. What made Kazi Dai a friend of Parasar Narayan Suwal?** (The question
misprints the surname as Shrestha.) Their shared love of the wilderness; they
often explored the jungles of the Terai together.

**b. Was the journey to Pokhara memorable for the writer? How?** Yes. They
faced a hailstorm and landslide debris, saw rare birds such as the Wall Creeper,
and Kazi Dai's mentoring on the trip shaped his lifelong interest in birds.

**c. Name a person the writer worked with besides Kazi Dai.** Karna Shakya (he
also went birding with Jimmy Carter).

**d. Mention the areas BCN works in at present.** Bird documentation,
photography, research and conservation; also satellite tracking of migration
and digital recording of Nepal's bird species.

**e. Who supports the Lumbini Crane Sanctuary?** WWF Nepal, in partnership with
the International Crane Foundation and the Lumbini Development Trust.

**f. Do you like Kazi Dai? Why?** Yes. Though a school dropout, he became an
expert through passion and hard work, and he freely shared his knowledge with
young people and worked to protect Nepal's birds.

## 12.2 Speaking: likes, dislikes and preferences

| Like | Dislike | Prefer |
|---|---|---|
| I love / like / enjoy …ing | I don't like / dislike …ing | I prefer X **to** Y |
| I'm keen on / crazy about / fond of …ing | I hate / can't stand …ing | I'd rather + verb |

Speaking A: a. Like b. Dislike c. Like d. Dislike e. Dislike f. Like g. Like.
Speaking B: don't like playing · love playing · do … like · like riding (a
bicycle) · like (to dance) · singing.

## 12.3 Grammar I: Verb + -ing and infinitive

Some verbs are followed by **-ing**, some by **to + verb**, and some by both.

| Followed by -ing | Followed by to + verb | Both (little change in meaning) |
|---|---|---|
| enjoy, mind, avoid, finish, keep (on), admit, dislike, can't stand, practise, *go* + activity (*go fishing*) | want, would like, learn, agree, decide, hope, plan, promise, refuse, wait | like, love, hate, start, begin, prefer |

- After **prepositions** use **-ing**: *keen on gardening, good at drawing*.
- **help** + object + base verb (or *to*): *Munal helped me **carry** this load.*
- *I like swimming* (I enjoy it in general) / *I'd like to swim* (I want to now).

### Textbook answers

**Grammar I B:** a. going b. to tell c. to come d. asking e. talking f. to
speak g. giving h. carry i. waiting j. preparing

**Grammar I C:** a. listening (or *to listen*) b. fishing c. swimming
d. writing (or *to write*) e. to talk f. to pay g. calling (or *to call*)
h. talking i. to pay j. stealing — and the correct sentence is *He admitted
stealing the money* or *He admitted **to** stealing* (not *admitted of*).

::: caution Common mistakes
- *I enjoy to read.* ✗ → *I enjoy **reading**.* ✓
- *I want going home.* ✗ → *I want **to go** home.* ✓
- *Would you mind to give me a hand?* ✗ → *mind **giving*** ✓
- *We go to swim on Saturdays* → *We **go swimming*** ✓
:::

## 12.4 Writing: Essay on hobbies and interests

::: example Model answer — My Hobbies and Interests
**My Hobbies and Interests**

A hobby is an activity we do for pleasure in our free time. My main hobbies are
birdwatching and drawing, and I am also interested in reading story books.

I chose birdwatching because there is a small forest and a pond near my house
in Chitwan. Every morning I hear different birds, and after reading about Hari
Sharan Nepali, I wanted to learn their names. Drawing helps me record the birds
I see, so the two hobbies go together.

To develop my hobbies, I plan to keep a notebook of every bird I identify, with
a drawing, the date and the place. I want to join a bird-watching club and
learn to use a field guide. Next year I hope to take part in a school drawing
competition.

I need some support. A pair of binoculars and a bird guidebook are expensive,
and my parents sometimes worry that hobbies take time away from studies. I
will show them that I can manage my time — study first, hobby after.

My hobbies have already changed my life. I wake up early, I spend less time on
the mobile phone, and I have learnt patience. In future, I would like to become
a wildlife researcher and help protect Nepal's birds.
:::

The textbook's five questions give the five paragraphs: *what* → *why* → *how
you will achieve them* → *support needed / barriers* → *changes in your life*.

## 12.5 Reading II: Poem — My Teacher Ate my Homework

### What happens in the poem

The speaker is surprised ("rather odd") to see his teacher eating his homework.
The teacher **sniffs** it, smiles and nods approvingly, takes a little
**nibble**, then a larger **bite**, and **chews** thoughtfully. He clearly likes
it: he "really went to town", **gobbled** it "with gusto" and **wolfed** it all
down. He **licks** his fingers, burps and says, "You pass." Only in the last two
lines do we understand: this is a **cooking class**, and the homework was food.

**Humour:** the title makes us think of the old excuse "my dog ate my
homework"; the poem turns it around. The joke is kept for the last line
(a **surprise ending**), and the eating words are **exaggerated** for comic
effect.

**Form:** four stanzas of four lines; lines 2 and 4 rhyme (*odd/nod,
true/chew, town/down, pass/class*).

### Exercises

**A (rhymes):** odd – nod (god, rod); true – chew (blue, new); town – down
(brown, crown); pass – class (glass, grass).

**B (eating words):**

| Word | Meaning | Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| nibble | take very small bites | The rabbit nibbled a carrot. |
| bite | cut with the teeth | He took a big bite of the apple. |
| chew | crush food with the teeth | Chew your food well. |
| gobble | eat fast and greedily | The hungry boys gobbled the momo. |
| wolf (down) | eat very fast in large amounts | She wolfed down her lunch and ran out. |
| lick | move the tongue over | The child licked the ice cream. |

**C (summary):** strange · approval (box: *approved*) · tasted · his fingers ·
cooking class

## 12.6 Grammar II: Yes/no and wh-questions

**Yes/no question:** helping verb + subject + verb: *Does she sing English
songs?*

**Wh-question:** wh-word + helping verb + subject + verb: *When will Madan buy a
computer?* If the wh-word **is the subject** (*who, what*), there is no
*do/does/did*: *Who is in class eight? Who doesn't want to help you?*

| Wh-word | Asks about |
|---|---|
| who | person | 
| what | thing / action |
| where | place |
| when | time |
| why | reason |
| how | manner |

### Textbook answers

**Grammar II A:** a–iv, b–v, c–vi, d–i, e–iii, f–vii, g–ii

**Grammar II B:** a. Who is in class eight? b. What is the dog chasing? c. Were
they practising English yesterday? d. Why did he go to the market? e. When did
you come to see me? f. Did Anuska buy a new computer? g. Where did William see a
snake? h. Will they have to do the work? i. How did you pay? j. Does she have a
beautiful painting?

::: caution Common mistakes
- *Why he went to the market?* ✗ → *Why **did** he **go**…?* ✓
- *Who did break the glass?* ✗ (subject question) → *Who **broke** the glass?* ✓
- *Where William saw a snake?* ✗ → *Where **did** William **see**…?* ✓
:::
