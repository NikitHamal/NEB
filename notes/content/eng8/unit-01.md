Unit 1 of your textbook is about **information you have to use**. Reading I is
a school **notice with a timetable** for a tour to the Central Zoo; you must
find times in it and work out how long things take. Reading II is a **request
letter** from grade 8 students asking their headteacher for a better computer
lab. The grammar is the modal verbs **can, could, would** and the **present
simple tense**. In writing you interpret a sports timetable and fill in a hostel
registration form.

::: key What the exam asks from this unit
(1) Answer questions from a timetable, including "how long" questions that need
subtraction. (2) Make yes/no questions with *can, could, would* and fill blanks
with them, using the negative where the meaning needs it. (3) Put verbs in the
present simple, with **-s/-es** for *he, she, it*. (4) Write a request letter in
the correct layout. (5) Turn a timetable into a short paragraph.
:::

## 1.1 Reading I: A Tour to Central Zoo (timetable)

### What the text is

The text is a **notice** from Saraswati Niketan Secondary School, Teku,
Kathmandu, dated 20th June. It tells grade 8 students that the school is taking
them on an **educational tour** to the Central Zoo, Jawalakhel, Lalitpur, on
**24th June**. Most of the notice is a **timetable**: a list of activities on
the left and the time of each on the right.

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 9:00 a.m. | Arrive at school and arrange groups |
| 9:15 a.m. | Board the bus |
| 9:20 a.m. | Attendance |
| 9:45 a.m. | Reach Central Zoo |
| 10:00 a.m. | Form small animal groups |
| 10:05 a.m. | Enter the zoo |
| 10:05 a.m. – 2:00 p.m. | Explore the animals in the zoo |
| 2:10 p.m. | Board the bus |
| 2:15 p.m. | Attendance |
| 2:40 p.m. | Arrive back at school |
| 2:50 p.m. | Return to classroom |
| 2:50 p.m. – 4:00 p.m. | Grand discussion |

The students explore in four **animal groups**: birds, reptiles, mammals and
amphibians. A note at the end says that **Mrs. Karki, the English teacher**,
will lead the tour, and students must follow the timetable and her guidelines.

### How to read a timetable

1. **Find the activity** in the left column (e.g. *Reach Central Zoo*).
2. **Read the time** next to it (9:45 a.m.).
3. For a **"how long"** question, take two times and subtract:
   bus leaves 9:15, reaches zoo 9:45 → **30 minutes**.

### Vocabulary (Exercise A)

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| tour | a visit to a place |
| board | to get on or into a vehicle |
| explore | to travel to or around an area to learn about it |
| grand | large and important |
| amphibian | an animal that can live both on land and in water (e.g. a frog) |

Answer key: **a–iii, b–iv, c–i, d–v, e–ii.**

### Comprehension (Exercise B) — model answers

**a. What time do the students need to arrive at school?**
They need to arrive at school at 9:00 a.m.

**b. How long will it take them to reach the zoo from their school?**
They board the bus at 9:15 a.m. and reach the zoo at 9:45 a.m., so the journey
takes about 30 minutes. (Counting from the attendance at 9:20, it is 25 minutes.)

**c. Why do you think the teacher will take the students' attendance twice?**
To make sure that every student is on the bus — once before leaving school and
once before leaving the zoo — so that nobody is lost or left behind.

**d. Why do you think the students will explore the zoo in animal groups?**
Small groups are easier to manage, and each group can study one kind of animal
(birds, reptiles, mammals or amphibians) closely and report on it in the grand
discussion.

**e. Who should the students contact if they have any problem during the tour?**
They should contact Mrs. Karki, the English teacher, who is leading the tour.

::: caution Timetable traps
- Read **a.m.** and **p.m.** carefully: 2:00 p.m. is in the afternoon.
- Exploring lasts from 10:05 a.m. to 2:00 p.m. = **3 hours 55 minutes**, not 4
  hours and not 8 hours.
- Answer in a full sentence and give the exact time from the table.
:::

## 1.2 Reading II: A Request Letter

### What the letter says

The letter is written from Kushma-9, Parbat, on 27 April 2023, by the
**students of Grade 8** to **the Headteacher of Adarsha Secondary School,
Kushma, Parbat**. Its subject is *A request to increase computer lab
facilities*.

- **Paragraph 1 – thanks.** The students thank the headteacher for setting up a
  computer lab. It gives them up-to-date learning devices and technological
  knowledge.
- **Paragraph 2 – the problem.** The effort is "not quite sufficient". The lab
  lacks equipment: (1) there are not enough computers, (2) only a few computers
  have the required software, and (3) there is no internet connection, so they
  cannot reach the "vast world of knowledge and information".
- **Paragraph 3 – the request.** They would be thankful if the headteacher
  provided everything a modern lab needs, and they await a reply.

Notice the polite structure: **thank first, explain the problem with
reasons, then request.** The request uses *would*: "We **would** be thankful if
you provided…".

### Vocabulary (Exercise A — unscrambled)

| Meaning | Word |
|---|---|
| having qualities that you admire and respect | **admirable** |
| not having enough of something | **lacking** |
| tools needed for a particular purpose | **equipment** |
| large in area, amount, etc. | **vast** |
| the programmes used by a computer | **software** |

### Comprehension (Exercise B) — model answers

**a. Who wrote the letter?** The students of Grade 8 of Adarsha Secondary
School, Kushma, Parbat, wrote the letter.

**b. Who is the letter addressed to?** It is addressed to the Headteacher of
Adarsha Secondary School.

**c. Why are the students grateful to the headteacher?** Because he/she has
succeeded in establishing a computer lab at their school.

**d. Why are the students very happy about the computer lab?** Because it gives
them the most up-to-date learning devices and helps them gain technological
knowledge.

**e. List the three issues the students talk about.** (i) There are not enough
computers. (ii) Only a few computers have the required software installed.
(iii) The lab has no internet connection.

## 1.3 Grammar I: Modal verbs — can, could, would

Modal verbs are helping verbs. They never take *-s*, *-ing* or *-ed*, and they
are always followed by the **base form** of the verb (*can swim*, not *can
swims* or *can to swim*).

| Modal | Main uses | Example |
|---|---|---|
| can | present ability; informal request; permission | Ganesh **can** cook Thakali food. **Can** I sit here? |
| could | past ability; polite request; possibility | I **couldn't** go to the party last night. **Could** you tell me the day of the visit? |
| would | very polite request; imagined result (*if…*); wish; *would rather* (preference) | **Would** you tell the time again, please? If I had a car, I **would** drive it. I'**d rather** go to the cinema. |

**Negatives:** cannot/can't, could not/couldn't, would not/wouldn't.

**Yes/no questions:** move the modal to the front.
*They could rescue the victims.* → ***Could** they rescue the victims?*

### Textbook answers

**Exercise A (yes/no questions):** a. Could they rescue the victims? b. Can Linda
win the match? c. Can your mother drive a car? d. Could you help me? (In a
question *you* stays; *me* stays.) e. Would he suggest a solution to a problem?
f. Would they do social work? g. Would your father take us to the park?
h. Could you come to meet me?

**Exercise B (blanks):** a. couldn't (past + sick) b. Can (answer is "Yes, he
can") c. couldn't … can (last year not able, now able) d. would (imagined
result after *If I had*) e. could (some were able to fly) f. would (after *I
wish they*) g. can't (glasses are missing) h. would (*would rather* =
prefer).

::: caution Common mistakes with modals
- *She cans drive.* ✗ → *She **can** drive.* ✓
- *Could you to help me?* ✗ → *Could you **help** me?* ✓
- *Last year I can't swim.* ✗ → *Last year I **couldn't** swim.* ✓ (past ability = could)
- *Can you lend me your pen, sir?* is acceptable but plain; to a teacher or in a
  letter, *Could/Would you…, please?* is more polite.
:::

## 1.4 Grammar II: The present simple tense

Use the present simple for **habits** (*Peter starts his day at 7:00*),
**facts** (*an apple a day keeps the doctor away*) and **timetables** (*the
bus to Dharan leaves at 9:00 a.m.*).

| Subject | Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they / plural nouns | work | don't work | Do they work? |
| he / she / it / singular nouns | work**s** | doesn't work | Does he work? |

**Spelling of the -s form:** add **-es** after *-s, -sh, -ch, -x, -o* (*goes,
watches*); consonant + *y* → **-ies** (*tidy → tidies*); *have → has*;
*be → am/is/are*. After *does/doesn't* use the base form (*Does she work?*
not *Does she works?*).

**Word order with frequency adverbs:** *always, never, usually* go **before** the
main verb but **after** *be*: *She **always** drives too fast.* / *He is
**always** late.*

### Textbook answers

**Exercise A:** a. are b. provides c. helps d. have e. limits

**Exercise B (jumbled sentences):** a. He works in a department store.
b. Suman does his laundry at the weekend. c. My friends and I go to the cinema
once a month. d. My neighbour never gives me a nice gift. e. Akash always loves
playing the guitar. f. My mother is a doctor. g. She always drives her scooter
too fast. h. A lot of people work during the day. i. Where does your brother
work? j. What time does your sister get up every day?

**Exercise C (Peter Hanson):** starts, has, likes, has, believes, keeps, makes,
tidies, gets, is; start, have, love, rehearse, are, want; don't have, doesn't
like, are.

::: caution Common mistakes with the present simple
- *My friends goes to school.* ✗ → *My friends **go**…* (plural subject, no -s)
- *She don't like it.* ✗ → *She **doesn't** like it.* ✓
- *Where your brother works?* ✗ → *Where **does** your brother **work**?* ✓
:::

## 1.5 Writing: interpreting a timetable and filling in a form

### Interpreting a timetable

To **interpret** a timetable, turn the table into connected sentences. Start
with what the event is, where and when; then go through the days in order;
use the present simple or the future (*will*), and linking words (*first, then,
on the following day, finally*).

::: example Model answer — Inter-house Football Tournament (textbook Writing A)
**Problem.** Study the sports timetable of Janata Secondary School and
interpret it.

**Solution.** Janata Secondary School is organising a three-day Inter-house
Football Tournament from May 3 to May 5. On May 3, the morning session, from
10:00 to 12:00, is for the opening ceremony and the registration of teams and
players. From 1:00 p.m. onwards, Sagarmatha House plays against Manaslu House.
On May 4, grade 8 students give a cultural performance in the morning, and in
the afternoon Machhapuchhre House plays against Annapurna House. On the last
day, May 5, the final match is played in the morning, and the tournament ends
in the afternoon with the prize distribution and the closing ceremony.
:::

(Note: the textbook prints "10.00–12.00 am"; strictly, 12:00 is noon, so write
"from 10 a.m. to 12 noon".)

### Filling in a form (Writing B)

The hostel registration form of Mero Janmabhumi Secondary School asks for the
date of registration, full name, date of birth, gender, roll number, grade,
parent's and local guardian's names, e-mails and contact numbers, permanent and
current address, and three signatures (student, parent/guardian, warden).

- Write names in **BLOCK letters** when the form says so.
- Write dates in the boxes given: **DD / MM / YYYY**.
- Tick (✓) only one gender box.
- Do not leave any required box empty; do not sign in the warden's box.

## 1.6 Speaking, listening, pronunciation and project work

**Word stress.** In a word of two or more syllables, one syllable is said more
strongly. The textbook marks it with ' before the stressed syllable:
a'ttendance, a'rrange, 'mammal, 'reptile, a'rrival, ex'plore, a'rrive,
am'phibian, re'turn, in'form. Many two-syllable **verbs** are stressed on the
second syllable (ar'rive, ex'plore); many two-syllable **nouns** on the first
('mammal, 'reptile).

**Speaking.** Raima asks her teacher about a museum visit: "**Could** you tell me
the day of the visit?" and "Sorry, **would** you tell the time again, please?"
These are polite ways of *asking for information* and *asking for repetition*.
For the textbook situations: *Could you tell me your mobile number, please?* /
*Can you tell me how much a cup of coffee costs?* / *Could you tell me what time
the next bus for Pokhara leaves?* / *Would you tell me how far the hospital is?*

**Project work.** In groups, design an ideal weekly class timetable (subjects,
times, days) and display it.

## Chapter summary

- A timetable lists activities and times; for "how long", subtract one time
  from another (9:15 → 9:45 = 30 minutes).
- The zoo notice: tour on 24th June, led by Mrs. Karki; four animal groups —
  birds, reptiles, mammals, amphibians; attendance twice.
- The request letter: thanks → problem (too few computers, little software, no
  internet) → polite request with *would*; closes *Yours faithfully*.
- *can* = present ability/informal request; *could* = past ability/polite
  request; *would* = very polite request, imagined result, wish, *would
  rather*.
- Modal + base verb; no *-s*; question = modal first.
- Present simple: habits, facts, timetables; *he/she/it* + **-s/-es/-ies**;
  *does/doesn't* + base verb.
- Interpreting a timetable = turning it into connected sentences in order.
