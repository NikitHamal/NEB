Plant Anatomy carries about **3 of the 40 Botany questions**, and it is the
most diagram-driven chapter of the paper: the setter describes (or draws) a
transverse section and asks what organ, what plant group, or what tissue you
are looking at. The content falls into three blocks — the **tissues**
themselves (meristems and the simple and complex permanent tissues), the
**vocabulary of vascular bundles**, and the six standard sections:
**dicot and monocot root, stem and leaf**. Master the small set of
diagnostic features that separate those six slides and this chapter is
free marks.

::: key What the paper asks from this chapter
- **Meristems** — characters (dense cytoplasm, thin walls, no big vacuole); apical (length), intercalary (grasses), lateral (thickness: vascular and cork cambium); primary vs secondary (cork cambium by dedifferentiation); protoderm, procambium, ground meristem.
- **Simple tissues** — parenchyma (chlorenchyma, aerenchyma), collenchyma (living, pectin corners, flexible), sclerenchyma (dead, lignified; fibres and sclereids/stone cells).
- **Complex tissues** — xylem (tracheids, vessels, fibres dead; parenchyma living) and phloem (enucleate sieve tubes + companion cells, bast fibres, parenchyma); proto-/metaxylem; exarch root, endarch stem.
- **Bundle words** — radial (roots); conjoint collateral; bicollateral (Cucurbitaceae); amphivasal = xylem outside (*Dracaena*); amphicribral = phloem outside (ferns); open (cambium present) vs closed.
- **Root sections** — epiblema with root hairs, endodermis with **Casparian strips** and passage cells, radial exarch bundles; dicot diarch–hexarch, pith absent; monocot polyarch, big pith.
- **Stem sections** — dicot: ring of open bundles, collenchyma hypodermis, medullary rays; monocot: scattered closed bundles with sclerenchyma sheath, lysigenous cavity, undifferentiated ground tissue.
- **Leaf sections** — dorsiventral dicot (palisade above, spongy below, hypostomatic) vs isobilateral monocot (uniform mesophyll, bulliform/motor cells, dumb-bell guard cells).
:::

## 10.1 Plant tissues: characters, classification, structure and functions

A **tissue** is a group of cells of common origin working together.
Plant tissues divide first by one question — **can the cells still
divide?** If yes, they are **meristematic**; if they have matured and
stopped, they are **permanent**.

### Meristematic tissues

Meristem cells are small and isodiametric, with **thin cellulose walls,
dense cytoplasm, a large central nucleus and no large vacuole**, and they
are packed together without intercellular spaces. They divide repeatedly
by mitosis. Three classifications are asked:

**By position.**
- **Apical meristem** — at root and shoot tips; causes **primary growth**
  in length.
- **Intercalary meristem** — pieces of apical meristem left at the bases
  of internodes and leaves, famous in **grasses** (it is why a lawn
  regrows after mowing and grazing).
- **Lateral meristem** — cylinders along the axis, the **vascular
  cambium** and **cork cambium (phellogen)**; causes growth in thickness
  (secondary growth) in dicots and gymnosperms. Monocots generally lack
  lateral meristems.

**By origin.** The **promeristem** is the few initials at the very tip;
**primary meristems** descend from them directly (apical, intercalary and
the intrafascicular cambium); **secondary meristems** arise later when
permanent cells regain the power to divide — **dedifferentiation** — as
the cork cambium and interfascicular cambium do.

**By function (Haberlandt).** At an apex, **protoderm** makes the
epidermis, **procambium** makes primary xylem, phloem and vascular
cambium, and **ground meristem** makes cortex, endodermis, pericycle,
pith and medullary rays.

### Simple permanent tissues

Permanent cells arise from meristems by **differentiation**. Simple
tissues are built of one cell type.

| Tissue | Cells | Walls | Function and notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Parenchyma** | living, isodiametric | thin, cellulose | the packing/storage tissue; with chloroplasts = **chlorenchyma** (photosynthesis); with big air spaces = **aerenchyma** (hydrophytes, buoyancy and aeration) |
| **Collenchyma** | living, elongated | **unevenly thickened with pectin** (angular, lamellar, lacunar types) | flexible mechanical support in still-growing organs — petioles, young stems, leaf midribs |
| **Sclerenchyma** | **dead** at maturity | thick, uniformly **lignified**, simple pits, narrow lumen | rigid mechanical support; long **fibres** (bundles) and short **sclereids (stone cells)** — grit in pear, hard seed coats and nutshells |

The collenchyma–sclerenchyma contrast is a perennial MCQ: collenchyma is
**living and plastic** (grows with the organ), sclerenchyma is **dead and
rigid** (only in organs that have finished elongating).

### Complex permanent tissues

**Xylem** carries water and minerals **upwards** from root to leaf.

| Element | Living? | Structure and function |
|---|---|---|
| **Tracheids** | dead | elongated and tapering, lignified, water passes through **bordered pits**; the only conducting element of pteridophytes and gymnosperms; conduction + support |
| **Vessels** | dead | rows of cells with **perforated end walls** forming continuous wide pipes; the efficient conductors characteristic of **angiosperms** |
| **Xylem fibres** | dead | sclerenchymatous; support |
| **Xylem parenchyma** | **living** | storage and sideways conduction — the only living xylem element |

**Phloem** translocates prepared food (mainly sucrose) from source to
sink, in either direction as needed.

| Element | Living? | Structure and function |
|---|---|---|
| **Sieve tube elements** | living but **enucleate** | end-to-end tubes joined by perforated **sieve plates**; the conducting channel |
| **Companion cells** | living | narrow, dense cytoplasm, large nucleus; keep the sieve tube alive and load its sugar; confined to angiosperms |
| **Phloem fibres (bast fibres)** | dead | sclerenchymatous; support — jute, flax and hemp of commerce |
| **Phloem parenchyma** | living | storage; **absent in most monocots** — a standard named exception |

Xylem formed first (**protoxylem**, narrow annular/spiral elements that
can stretch) differs from later **metaxylem** (wide, pitted). Their
arrangement names the organ: **exarch** — protoxylem towards the
periphery — in **roots**; **endarch** — protoxylem towards the centre —
in **stems**. ("Roots are EXtroverts, stems are ENDoverts.")

Special **secretory tissues** — laticifers carrying latex (*Euphorbia*,
*Ficus*, rubber), resin ducts (pines), oil glands and nectaries — complete
the list.

## 10.2 Types of vascular bundles

A **vascular bundle** is a strand of xylem and phloem. The paper asks you
to *name* bundles, so learn the vocabulary exactly:

- **Radial** — xylem and phloem in separate groups on **different radii**,
  alternating. The root arrangement.
- **Conjoint** — xylem and phloem together in **one bundle** (stems and
  leaves):
  - **Collateral** — phloem on the **outer side only**, xylem inside
    (the usual stem bundle).
  - **Bicollateral** — phloem on **both sides** of the xylem
    (Cucurbitaceae — *Cucurbita*, *Luffa*; also *Solanum*).
- **Concentric** — one tissue completely surrounds the other:
  - **Amphivasal (leptocentric)** — **xylem surrounds phloem**
    (*Dracaena*, *Yucca*).
  - **Amphicribral (hadrocentric)** — **phloem surrounds xylem**
    (ferns).
- **Open** — **cambium present** between xylem and phloem: secondary
  growth possible (dicot stems).
- **Closed** — **no cambium**: no secondary growth (monocot stems, all
  leaves).

::: memory The one-syllable trap
Amphi-**VAS**-al: **vas**cular tissue — wood, **xylem** — outside.
Amphi-**CRIB**-ral: *cribrum* = a **sieve** — sieve tubes, **phloem** —
outside.
:::

## 10.3 T.S. of monocot and dicot root, stem and leaf

### Roots — the shared ground plan

Work from outside in. **Epiblema (rhizodermis)**: one layer of thin-walled
cells, many extended into **root hairs**; no cuticle, no stomata — it
absorbs. **Cortex**: broad parenchyma with air spaces. **Endodermis**:
one ring of barrel-shaped cells whose radial and transverse walls carry
a waterproof band of suberin and lignin, the **Casparian strip**, forcing
water and minerals to cross a living membrane (the cell checks what
enters the stele). A few endodermal cells opposite the protoxylem stay
thin-walled — **passage cells**, letting water through quickly.
**Pericycle**: one layer inside the endodermis; in roots it is the layer
that **gives rise to lateral roots** (endogenous origin). **Stele**:
xylem and phloem in separate **alternating radial groups**, xylem
**exarch**, with conjunctive parenchyma between them.

|---|---|---|

| Pith | **absent** (xylem meets at the centre) | **large**, well-developed parenchyma |
| Secondary growth | occurs (cambium forms) | does not occur |
| Shared signs | both: root hairs, Casparian strips and passage cells, radial and exarch bundles | |

### Stems

**Dicot stem (*Helianthus*).** Epidermis with cuticle, stomata and
multicellular hairs; **hypodermis of collenchyma**; general cortex;
endodermis full of starch grains (the **starch sheath**); pericycle with
semilunar sclerenchyma caps over the bundles; then a **single ring of
vascular bundles** (a eustele), uniform in size, each **conjoint,
collateral, open and endarch**, separated by **medullary rays**; large
central **pith**. The open bundles and ring layout are exactly what
secondary growth needs: the cambium completes itself into a ring.

**Monocot stem (*Zea mays*).** Epidermis with thick cuticle; **hypodermis
of sclerenchyma**; inside it, **no cortex/endodermis/pith at all** — one
continuous parenchymatous **ground tissue**; vascular bundles
**scattered** through it (an atactostele), numerous, **smaller and
denser towards the periphery**, each **conjoint, collateral and closed**,
wrapped in a **sclerenchymatous bundle sheath**, and carrying a
water-filled **lysigenous cavity** (protoxylem lacuna) where the earliest
protoxylem vessel broke down. Scattered closed bundles are why a maize
stem can never thicken into a trunk.

### Leaves

A horizontal dicot leaf is two-faced — **dorsiventral**. The upper
epidermis has a thick cuticle and few or no stomata; the lower has many
stomata (**hypostomatic**). The mesophyll is differentiated: **palisade
parenchyma** above — elongated cells crammed with chloroplasts in the
best light, the main photosynthetic layer — and **spongy parenchyma**
below, loose cells and air spaces leading to the stomata. Vein bundles
are conjoint, collateral and closed, with **xylem towards the upper side
and phloem towards the lower**.

A vertical monocot (grass/maize) leaf is alike on both sides —
**isobilateral**: stomata on **both** epidermes with **dumb-bell-shaped
guard cells** (dicot guard cells are kidney-shaped); **bulliform
(motor) cells** — big, thin-walled, watery cells in the upper epidermis
that lose turgor in dry weather and roll the leaf inwards to cut water
loss; mesophyll **undifferentiated** (no palisade/spongy split); bundles
in a parallel row between the veins.

    if all((x-px)**2 + (y-py)**2 > 0.34 for px, py in pts):
        pts.append((x, y))
        s = 0.58 - 0.20*(r/2.0)
        bundle(ax2, x, y, 0.34*s/0.45, 0.52*s/0.45, ang=np.degrees(a)+90)
ax2.text(0, -2.85, 'scattered closed bundles', fontsize=6.4, ha='center', color=MUTED)
```

::: example Worked example — naming an unknown slide
**Problem.** A stained transverse section shows: a cuticularised epidermis
with stomata and hairs; a collenchymatous hypodermis; conjoint, collateral,
endarch vascular bundles of nearly equal size arranged in one ring, each
with cambium; medullary rays between the bundles; and a large central
pith. Identify the section.

**Solution.** Eliminate in order. Root hairs and radial, exarch bundles
are absent, and a cuticle with stomata is present — so it is **not a
root**. The bundles are **in a ring**, not scattered — so it is **not a
monocot stem**. Open bundles (cambium present), collenchyma hypodermis,
medullary rays and a large pith complete the picture: a **young dicot
stem**. Each decision used one feature the alternatives cannot have —
that is how these marks are earned quickly.
:::

monocot** — then confirm with open vs closed. Leaf? Palisade present =

## Formula and fact sheet

- **Meristem characters**: thin walls, dense cytoplasm, big nucleus, tiny vacuoles, no spaces. Apical = length; intercalary = grasses; lateral = thickness (vascular + cork cambium).
- **Origins**: protoderm → epidermis; procambium → vascular tissues; ground meristem → cortex, pith etc. Cork cambium = secondary meristem by dedifferentiation.
- **Parenchyma**: living, thin; chlorenchyma photosynthesises; aerenchyma aerates hydrophytes.
- **Collenchyma**: living, pectin-thickened corners, flexible. **Sclerenchyma**: dead, lignified — fibres + sclereids (pear grit, seed coats).
- **Xylem**: tracheids, vessels, fibres dead; **parenchyma the only living element**. Vessels = angiosperms; gymnosperms conduct by tracheids alone.
- **Phloem**: sieve tubes **enucleate** + companion cells; bast fibres = jute/flax; phloem parenchyma absent in most monocots.
- **Exarch = root, endarch = stem**; protoxylem narrow and early, metaxylem wide and later.
- **Bundles**: radial (root); collateral; bicollateral (Cucurbitaceae); amphivasal — xylem outside (*Dracaena*); amphicribral — phloem outside (ferns); open = cambium, closed = none.
- **Root**: epiblema + root hairs; **Casparian strip** in endodermis; passage cells opposite protoxylem; **lateral roots from pericycle**.
- **Dicot root**: 2–6 xylem groups, pith absent. **Monocot root**: polyarch, big pith.
- **Dicot stem**: collenchyma hypodermis, starch-sheath endodermis, ring of open bundles, medullary rays, pith. **Monocot stem**: sclerenchyma hypodermis, ground tissue, scattered closed bundles + sheath + lysigenous cavity.
- **Leaves**: dorsiventral — palisade up, spongy down, hypostomatic, kidney guard cells; isobilateral — uniform mesophyll, bulliform cells, dumb-bell guard cells, stomata both sides.

## Chapter summary

- Tissues are meristematic (still dividing: apical, intercalary and lateral; primary and, by dedifferentiation, secondary) or permanent (simple and complex).
- Parenchyma packs and stores (aerenchyma in hydrophytes), living pectin-rich collenchyma supports growing organs flexibly, and dead lignified sclerenchyma (fibres and stone cells) supports finished ones rigidly.
- Xylem moves water up through dead tracheids and vessels, its parenchyma alone alive; phloem moves food through enucleate sieve tubes serviced by companion cells, with bast fibres for strength.
- Bundle names encode geometry: radial in roots; collateral and bicollateral conjoint bundles in stems; concentric amphivasal (xylem out) and amphicribral (phloem out); open bundles carry cambium, closed do not.
- Roots show epiblema with hairs, a Casparian-striped endodermis with passage cells, pericycle that births lateral roots, and exarch radial groups — few and pith-less in dicots, polyarch with a big pith in monocots.
- Dicot stems ring their open bundles round a pith (ready for secondary growth); monocot stems scatter closed, sheathed bundles with a lysigenous cavity through undifferentiated ground tissue.
- Dorsiventral dicot leaves split mesophyll into palisade and spongy layers and hide stomata below; isobilateral grass leaves keep mesophyll uniform, bear stomata and dumb-bell guard cells on both faces, and roll up by their bulliform cells.
