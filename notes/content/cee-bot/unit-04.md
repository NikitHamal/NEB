This chapter of the Biodiversity unit carries about **2 of the 40 Botany
questions**. The paper tests the land plants without flowers as one story —
from the bryophytes, where the haploid gametophyte is the plant you see, through
the ferns, where both generations live on their own, to the gymnosperms, where
the gametophytes shrink inside the sporophyte and the seed appears. Expect a
comparison question (which group has vascular tissue, seeds, motile sperm,
heterospory), a type-organism question on *Marchantia*, *Dryopteris* or *Pinus*,
and often a ploidy question (n or 2n of a named structure).

::: key What the paper asks from this chapter
- **Which generation dominates** — gametophyte in bryophytes; sporophyte in pteridophytes and gymnosperms; both independent only in pteridophytes.
- **Three bryophyte classes** — liverworts (*Marchantia*: thalloid, unicellular rhizoids, scales, elaters), hornworts (*Anthoceros*: horn-like capsule, one chloroplast per cell), mosses (*Funaria*: leafy, multicellular rhizoids, protonema, peristome).
- ***Marchantia*** — dioecious; gemma cups; barrel-shaped air pores; antheridiophore and archegoniophore; capsule with spores (n) and elaters (2n).
- **Pteridophytes** — first vascular plants; no seeds; homosporous (*Dryopteris*) or heterosporous (*Selaginella*).
- ***Dryopteris*** — rhizome with ramenta, circinate vernation, sori with reniform indusium, annulus and stomium, heart-shaped prothallus, multiflagellate sperm.
- **Gymnosperms** — naked ovules, no fruit, wind pollination, haploid endosperm, no double fertilisation, no vessels (except Gnetales).
- ***Pinus*** — long and dwarf shoots, needles in fascicles, monoecious cones, winged pollen, 2 ovules per ovuliferous scale, polyembryony, seed with 2n coat, n endosperm and 2n embryo.
:::

## 4.1 Bryophytes: liverworts, hornworts and mosses; *Marchantia*

### General characters

Bryophytes are small, non-vascular land plants of moist, shady places — the
**amphibians of the plant kingdom**, because they live on land but need water
for fertilisation. Their study is bryology.

```photo id="bio11/funaria" caption="*Funaria hygrometrica* growing on soil. The small green leafy gametophyte is the plant you see; each slender yellow seta lifts a capsule, the sporophyte, which stays attached to the gametophyte." width="82"
```


- The plant body is the **gametophyte (n)** — thalloid or leafy, attached by
  **rhizoids**; there are no true roots, stems, leaves, xylem or phloem.
- Sex organs are **multicellular and jacketed**: the **antheridium** makes
  **biflagellate antherozoids**; the flask-shaped **archegonium** (neck with
  neck canal cells, swollen venter with a venter canal cell and one egg) holds
  the egg.
- Antherozoids **swim** in a film of water to the archegonium.
- The zygote grows into a multicellular **embryo** — bryophytes are the first
  embryophytes — and then into the **sporophyte (2n)**: foot, seta and
  capsule. It stays **attached to and dependent on** the gametophyte.
- Spore mother cells in the capsule undergo **meiosis**; the haploid spores
  grow into a new gametophyte, directly (liverworts) or through a filamentous
  **protonema** (mosses).

| Feature | **Liverworts** (Hepaticopsida) | **Hornworts** (Anthocerotopsida) | **Mosses** (Bryopsida) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gametophyte | thalloid and dorsiventral (*Marchantia*, *Riccia*) or leafy (*Porella*) | thalloid, lobed | **leafy**, erect, radial ("stem" with spirally arranged "leaves") |
| Rhizoids | **unicellular**, smooth and tuberculate | unicellular, smooth | **multicellular**, branched, with oblique septa |
| Scales | present on the ventral surface | absent | absent |
| Chloroplasts | many per cell, no pyrenoid | usually **one large chloroplast with a pyrenoid** per cell | many per cell |
| Protonema | absent | absent | **well developed** |
| Sporophyte | simple: foot, short seta, capsule (*Riccia*: capsule only) | **long, horn-like capsule** growing from a basal (intercalary) meristem; has stomata and chlorophyll | foot, long seta, complex capsule with operculum and **peristome teeth** |
| Columella / elaters | no columella; **elaters** present | **columella** present; pseudo-elaters | columella present; no elaters |
| Examples | *Marchantia*, *Riccia*, *Pellia*, *Porella* | *Anthoceros*, *Notothylas* | *Funaria*, *Polytrichum*, *Sphagnum* (peat moss) |

*Sphagnum* forms peat, which is dried and burnt as fuel and used as packing
material because it holds many times its weight of water; mosses and lichens
together colonise bare rock in succession.

### *Marchantia* — structure

*Marchantia* (*M. polymorpha*) is a liverwort of wet soil, rocks near streams
and old walls, common in Nepal's hills in the monsoon.

```photo id="cee-bot/marchantia" caption="Thallus of the liverwort *Marchantia polymorpha*, the gametophyte. The cup-shaped gemma cups hold green gemmae that splash out in rain and grow into new plants - asexual reproduction." width="76"
call 0.20 0.92 0.35 0.62 "Gemma cup"
call 0.80 0.12 0.68 0.46 "Gemma cup (cut open)"
call 0.40 0.08 0.50 0.28 "Dorsal thallus; pale dots are air pores"
```


- **Thallus**: flat, green, **dorsiventral**, **dichotomously branched**, with a
  midrib and a notch (growing point) at each tip.
- **Dorsal surface**: divided into polygonal **areolae**, each with a central
  **barrel-shaped air pore** (a ring of 4–5 tiers of cells) that **cannot
  close** — it is not a stoma. Along the midrib sit cup-like **gemma cups**.
- **Ventral surface**: **scales** of two kinds (appendiculate and ligulate)
  and **rhizoids** of two kinds — **smooth-walled** and **tuberculate** (with
  peg-like inward thickenings).
- **Internal structure** (vertical section): upper epidermis with air pores;
  **air chambers** containing branched **photosynthetic filaments**; a
  colourless **storage zone** of parenchyma (starch, oil bodies, mucilage
  cells); lower epidermis bearing rhizoids and scales.

### *Marchantia* — reproduction

**Vegetative.** (1) **Gemmae**: green, multicellular, biconvex discs with two
notches, on a one-celled stalk inside the gemma cup; rain splashes them out and
each grows into a new thallus identical to the parent. (2) **Fragmentation**: the
older parts of the thallus die and the branches become separate plants.
(3) Adventitious branches from the ventral surface.

**Sexual.** *Marchantia polymorpha* is **dioecious** (male and female thalli
are separate; sex is fixed by sex chromosomes in the spores).
- **Male thallus**: stalked **antheridiophores** whose top is a lobed (usually
  8-lobed) disc; **antheridia** sit in flask-shaped cavities on its upper
  surface. Each antheridium has a one-layered jacket and produces many
  **biflagellate antherozoids**.
- **Female thallus**: stalked **archegoniophores** whose top bears 8–9
  finger-like **rays**. The **archegonia** hang **neck downwards** in rows on the
  underside, between the rays, protected by a fringed **perichaetium**.
- **Fertilisation**: rain splashes the antherozoids to the female receptacle;
  they swim down the neck to the egg. The zygote (2n) is the first cell of the
  sporophyte.

**Sporophyte.** The embryo grows into a **foot** (anchored in the
receptacle, absorbing food), a short **seta** and a **capsule**, enclosed by the
**calyptra** (from the venter) and a **pseudoperianth**. In the capsule, the
sporogenous tissue forms **spore mother cells**, which divide by **meiosis**
into tetrads of haploid **spores**, and **elater mother cells**, which do not
divide by meiosis but elongate into **elaters** — long cells with **spiral
thickenings**, hygroscopic, which twist as they dry and scatter the spores.
The capsule splits from the apex into 4–6 teeth. A spore germinates into a short
germ tube and then a new thallus — half the spores give male thalli, half
female.

## 4.2 Pteridophytes; *Dryopteris*

### General characters

Pteridophytes are the **vascular cryptogams** — the **first land plants with
xylem and phloem**, but still without seeds or flowers.

- The plant you see is the **sporophyte (2n)**, divided into **true roots,
  stem** (often an underground **rhizome**) **and leaves** — small
  **microphylls** (*Selaginella*, *Equisetum*) or large **megaphylls** (ferns).
- Xylem has **tracheids** (vessels only in a few, e.g. *Selaginella*); phloem
  has sieve cells and no companion cells.
- **Sporangia** are borne on leaves called **sporophylls**, which in some form
  compact cones (**strobili**: *Selaginella*, *Equisetum*).
- Most are **homosporous** — one kind of spore (*Dryopteris*, *Pteris*,
  *Lycopodium*, *Equisetum*). A few are **heterosporous**, with small
  microspores and large megaspores (*Selaginella*, *Salvinia*, *Marsilea*,
  *Azolla*). Heterospory, with the female gametophyte developing inside the
  megaspore, is the **precursor of the seed habit**.
- The spore grows into a small, green, free-living, thalloid gametophyte, the
  **prothallus**, which bears antheridia and archegonia. Water is needed for
  fertilisation. The zygote grows into an embryo and then the new sporophyte.

| Class | Example | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Psilopsida | *Psilotum* (whisk fern) | no true roots; a "living fossil" |
| Lycopsida | *Selaginella* (spike moss), *Lycopodium* (club moss) | microphylls; *Selaginella* is heterosporous and has a ligule |
| Sphenopsida | *Equisetum* (horsetail) | jointed, ribbed stem with silica; whorled scale leaves |
| Pteropsida | *Dryopteris*, *Pteris*, *Adiantum* (maidenhair fern), *Marsilea*, *Azolla* | large compound fronds in most; sori on the underside or inside sporocarps |

In Nepal the young fronds of *Diplazium esculentum* (*niuro*) are a popular
vegetable, tree ferns (*Cyathea*) grow in moist hill forests, and *Azolla*
(with *Anabaena*) is used as a biofertiliser in rice fields.

### *Dryopteris* — structure

*Dryopteris* (wood fern, male shield fern; *D. filix-mas*) grows in moist,
shady forests.

- **Stem**: a short, stout, underground **rhizome**, covered with persistent
  leaf bases and brown, chaffy, multicellular scales called **ramenta**;
  **adventitious roots** arise from it. Its vascular cylinder is a
  **dictyostele** (a ring of separate strands, the meristeles, each with xylem
  surrounded by phloem).
- **Leaves (fronds)**: large, petiolate, **pinnately compound** (bipinnate), in
  a crown at the rhizome tip, with ramenta on the petiole. Young leaves are
  coiled like a watch spring — **circinate vernation** — and uncoil as they grow.

### *Dryopteris* — reproduction

**Sporophyte → spores.** On the **underside** of fertile pinnules, along the
veins, are rounded **sori** (singular sorus). Each sorus is a cluster of
stalked **sporangia** on a cushion (the placenta), covered by a
**kidney-shaped (reniform) indusium**. Each sporangium (a
**leptosporangium**, developing from one surface cell) has a stalk and a
biconvex capsule with a one-cell-thick wall. Running two-thirds round its edge
is the **annulus** — a row of cells with thick inner and radial walls; the rest
is the thin-walled **stomium**. Usually **16 spore mother cells** undergo
**meiosis** to give **64 haploid spores**, all alike (**homosporous**).

**Dehiscence.** As the annulus dries, its cells lose water and pull the
annulus straight; the wall tears at the stomium, then the annulus springs back
and **flings the spores out** like a catapult.

**Gametophyte (prothallus).** A spore germinates into a small (a few mm), green,
**heart-shaped**, dorsiventral **prothallus**, one cell thick except for a
central cushion. It is **free-living and photosynthetic**, attached by
rhizoids. On its **ventral** surface it bears **antheridia** (among the
rhizoids, towards the base) and **archegonia** (on the cushion near the apical
notch). It is **monoecious** but the antheridia mature first (**protandry**),
which favours cross-fertilisation.

**Fertilisation.** **Multiflagellate**, spirally coiled antherozoids swim in
dew or rain water, attracted chemically (by malic acid) to the archegonium neck,
and one fuses with the egg. The **zygote (2n)** divides into an **embryo** with
a foot, primary root, stem apex and first leaf; the young sporophyte at first
depends on the prothallus, then becomes independent, and the prothallus dies.

**Unusual cycles.** *Dryopteris* also shows **apogamy** (a sporophyte grows
from a prothallus cell without fertilisation) and **apospory** (a prothallus
grows from a sporophyte cell without meiosis).

## 4.3 Gymnosperms; *Pinus*

### General characters

Gymnosperms (Greek *gymnos*, naked; *sperma*, seed) are seed plants whose
**ovules are not enclosed in an ovary**; they lie exposed on
**megasporophylls** (cone scales), so the seeds are naked and **no fruit** is
formed.

- Woody trees and shrubs (many evergreen conifers), with a tap root; *Cycas*
  has **coralloid roots** containing N₂-fixing cyanobacteria; *Pinus* roots
  carry **mycorrhizal** fungi.
- Xylem has **tracheids but no vessels** (vessels occur only in the Gnetales —
  *Gnetum*, *Ephedra*); phloem has **albuminous cells instead of companion
  cells**. The wood is "soft wood".
- **Heterosporous**: microsporophylls in **male cones** make microspores
  (pollen grains); megasporophylls bear **ovules**, usually in **female cones**.
- **Pollination is by wind**; pollen lands directly on the ovule's micropyle
  (caught in a pollination drop). A **pollen tube** carries the male gametes
  (siphonogamy), so water is no longer needed — though *Cycas* and *Ginkgo*
  still have **motile, multiflagellate sperms**.
- The **female gametophyte** (the **endosperm**) is **haploid** and forms
  **before fertilisation**; it bears **archegonia**. There is **no double
  fertilisation**.
- **Polyembryony** is common.

| Gymnosperm | Fact worth knowing |
|---|---|
| *Cycas* | dioecious; palm-like; coralloid roots; largest ovules and sperms among plants; no female cone (loose megasporophylls); sago from the pith |
| *Pinus* | monoecious; needles on dwarf shoots; resin, turpentine |
| *Cedrus deodara* (deodar) | durable timber |
| *Abies*, *Picea* | firs, spruces of high Himalaya |
| *Taxus wallichiana* (Himalayan yew, *lauth salla*) | **taxol** (paclitaxel) — anticancer drug |
| *Ginkgo biloba* | maidenhair tree; a "living fossil"; motile sperms |
| *Ephedra* | **ephedrine** for asthma and colds; vessels in xylem |
| *Sequoia* | redwoods — the tallest trees |

### *Pinus* — structure

*Pinus* is a tall, evergreen, **monoecious** tree with a conical (excurrent)
crown. Nepal has *Pinus roxburghii* (chir pine, *khote salla*; 3 needles per
spur) in the lower hills and *P. wallichiana* (blue pine, *gobre salla*;
5 needles) higher up.

- **Root**: tap root without root hairs; the young roots carry
  **ectomycorrhizae** (fungi that absorb water and minerals for the tree).
- **Stem**: two kinds of branches — **long shoots** of unlimited growth, bearing
  only brown **scale leaves**, and **dwarf shoots** (spurs) of limited growth,
  each carrying a cluster (**fascicle**) of **needles** wrapped at the base by
  scale leaves. The dwarf shoot falls with its needles.
- **Needle anatomy (xerophytic)**: thick cuticle, **sunken stomata**,
  sclerenchymatous hypodermis, mesophyll with peg-like infoldings,
  **resin ducts**, a clear endodermis, transfusion tissue and one or two
  vascular bundles.
- **Number of needles per spur**: 1 (*P. monophylla*), 2 (*P. merkusii*,
  *P. sylvestris*), 3 (*P. roxburghii*, *P. gerardiana*), 5 (*P. wallichiana*).

### *Pinus* — reproduction

**Male cone.** Small cones in clusters at the base of the current year's long
shoot. A central axis bears spirally arranged **microsporophylls**, each with
**two microsporangia (pollen sacs)** on its lower surface. Microspore mother
cells undergo **meiosis** to give **microspores** — the **winged pollen grains**
(two air sacs). The pollen grain is shed at the **four-celled stage**: two
(degenerating) prothallial cells, a generative cell and a tube cell.

**Female cone.** Larger cones, one to a few near the tip of a long shoot. The
axis bears spirally arranged **bract scales**; in the axil of each is a woody
**ovuliferous scale** carrying **two ovules** on its upper surface, micropyles
facing the axis. Each ovule has **one integument** and a nucellus. The
**megaspore mother cell** divides by **meiosis** into a **linear tetrad**; the
**lowest (chalazal) megaspore is functional** and the other three degenerate.
It grows into the **female gametophyte (endosperm, n)**, which bears **2–5
archegonia** at its micropylar end.

**Pollination and fertilisation.** Wind carries the pollen to the micropyle,
where it is drawn in by a pollination drop. The generative cell divides into a
stalk cell and a body cell; the body cell divides into **two non-motile male
gametes**. The pollen tube grows slowly through the nucellus — fertilisation
takes place about **a year after pollination**. One male gamete fuses with the
egg; the other degenerates — there is **no double fertilisation and no triple
fusion**.

**Embryo and seed.** The zygote forms a proembryo; several archegonia may be
fertilised (**simple polyembryony**) and one proembryo may split into four
(**cleavage polyembryony**), but usually only one embryo survives. The embryo
has a radicle, a hypocotyl and **many cotyledons** (polycotyledonous). The
mature seed is **winged** (the wing comes from the ovuliferous scale) and
germinates **epigeally**.

::: key A *Pinus* seed holds three generations
- **Seed coat** — from the integument of the parent sporophyte: **2n**
- **Endosperm** — the female gametophyte: **n**
- **Embryo** — the new sporophyte: **2n**

Compare angiosperms, where the endosperm is **triploid (3n)** and forms only
after double fertilisation.
:::

| Dominant generation | **gametophyte (n)** | sporophyte (2n) | sporophyte (2n) |

sporangium wall, pine needle, seed coat, embryo). Spores are the first cells of
the gametophyte; the zygote is the first cell of the sporophyte.
:::

::: example Chromosome numbers across the Marchantia and Dryopteris life cycles
**Problem.** In *Dryopteris*, a young coiled leaf (circinate vernation) has $2n = 82$ chromosomes. Give the chromosome number in (a) a prothallus cell, (b) a neck-canal cell, (c) a spore mother cell, and (d) an indusium cell.

**Solution.** The leafy fern is the diploid sporophyte ($2n = 82$), so the spore mother cell and indusium cell are diploid (**82**). The prothallus is the haploid gametophyte ($n = 41$), and the archegonial neck-canal cell arises on it by mitosis, so both have **41** chromosomes.
:::

::: example Pinus ploidy contrast: gymnosperm vs angiosperm endosperm
**Problem.** A root-tip cell of *Pinus roxburghii* contains $2n = 24$ chromosomes. How many chromosomes are present in (a) a cell of the female prothallus (endosperm), (b) a cell of the seed wing, and (c) a cotyledon cell of the embryo?

**Solution.** Gymnosperm endosperm is pre-fertilisation female gametophyte tissue, so it is haploid ($n = \mathbf{12}$). The seed wing develops from the ovuliferous scale of the parent sporophyte ($2n = \mathbf{24}$), and the cotyledon belongs to the new sporophyte embryo ($2n = \mathbf{24}$).
:::

## Chapter summary

- Bryophytes are non-vascular embryophytes with a dominant haploid gametophyte and a dependent diploid sporophyte; water is needed for their biflagellate sperm.
- Liverworts (thalloid, unicellular rhizoids, scales, elaters), hornworts (horn-like capsule, one chloroplast per cell) and mosses (leafy, multicellular rhizoids, protonema, peristome) are the three classes.
- *Marchantia* is a dioecious, dichotomous thallus with barrel-shaped air pores and gemma cups; it bears stalked antheridiophores and archegoniophores; its capsule holds haploid spores and diploid elaters.
- Pteridophytes are the first vascular plants; their dominant sporophyte has roots, stems and leaves; most are homosporous, but *Selaginella*, *Marsilea*, *Salvinia* and *Azolla* are heterosporous.
- *Dryopteris* has a rhizome with ramenta, circinate fronds, and sori with a reniform indusium; its sporangium opens by the annulus; the spore grows into a free-living, heart-shaped, protandrous prothallus; sperm are multiflagellate.
- Gymnosperms bear naked ovules, are wind-pollinated, have a haploid endosperm formed before fertilisation and no double fertilisation, and lack vessels and fruits.
- *Pinus* is monoecious with long and dwarf shoots, needles in fascicles, winged pollen, and two ovules on each ovuliferous scale; fertilisation follows pollination by about a year; polyembryony is common.
- A *Pinus* seed holds three generations: seed coat 2n, endosperm n, embryo 2n.

**1.** (c) — Each spore mother cell gives a tetrad of four spores by meiosis: 64 × 4 = 256.
**2.** (a) — Moss leaves are gametophyte (n = 10); the capsule wall is sporophyte (2n = 20); spores are made by meiosis (n = 10).

**4.** (b) — Ramenta on a rhizome, circinate vernation and sori with reniform indusia identify *Dryopteris*.
**5.** (b) — The blue pine (*P. wallichiana*) of the higher hills has five needles per spur; chir pine (*P. roxburghii*) has three, *P. merkusii* two.

### Model answer D1: bryophytes and alternation of generations (10 marks)

**Introduction.** **Bryophytes** (liverworts, hornworts and mosses) are the "amphibians of the plant kingdom": small, non-vascular plants that need water for fertilisation. The dominant plant is the haploid **gametophyte**.

**Features.** No true roots (rhizoids instead), no xylem or phloem, a cuticle that is thin, and a multicellular **jacketed** sex organ. The sporophyte depends on the gametophyte.

**Life cycle of *Marchantia*.**

1. **Gametophyte:** a flat, dichotomously branched, dorsiventral thallus with rhizoids and **gemma cups** for vegetative reproduction. It is dioecious.
2. **Male plant:** an **antheridiophore** (disc-shaped) with antheridia, producing biflagellate antherozoids.
3. **Female plant:** an **archegoniophore** (star-shaped, with finger-like rays) with archegonia, each with an egg.
4. **Fertilisation:** the antherozoids swim in a film of water (rain splash) to the egg. The zygote forms.
5. **Sporophyte:** a diploid **sporogonium** with a foot, a seta and a capsule, which remains attached to the female gametophyte. Meiosis in the capsule gives haploid **spores** (with **elaters** that help dispersal).
6. A spore germinates into a protonema-like stage and grows into a new thallus.

**Alternation of generations.** A cycle in which a haploid gametophyte (gamete-producing) alternates with a diploid sporophyte (spore-producing).

| Group | Dominant stage | Sporophyte |
|---|---|---|
| Bryophyta | gametophyte | dependent on the gametophyte |
| Pteridophyta | sporophyte | independent |
| Gymnosperms, angiosperms | sporophyte | gametophyte reduced and dependent |

**Importance.** *Sphagnum* peat moss is used as fuel and for soil moisture and packing plants, mosses are pioneers of **primary succession** on rock, they prevent soil erosion and they are indicators of acid rain. *Funaria* is a common moss.

**Numerical.** If a sporangium makes $10$ spore mother cells, then meiosis gives $4\times10 = 40$ haploid spores. For a plant with $n = 9$ in the gametophyte, the sporophyte has $2n = 18$.

**Conclusion.** In bryophytes, the visible plant is the haploid gametophyte, and water is required for the swimming antherozoids to reach the egg.

### Model answer D2: pteridophytes and gymnosperms (10 marks)

**Introduction.** **Pteridophytes** are the first vascular land plants, with true roots, stems and leaves, xylem and phloem, but no seeds. **Gymnosperms** are seed plants with **naked seeds**.

**Fern (*Dryopteris*).**

1. **Sporophyte:** a dominant plant with an underground rhizome, adventitious roots and large compound leaves (fronds), with circinate vernation. The stele is a dictyostele.
2. **Sori** on the underside of fronds hold **sporangia**, each with an **annulus** that rips it open to release haploid spores. The sorus is covered by an indusium.
3. **Gametophyte:** a small, green, heart-shaped, free-living **prothallus** with rhizoids, antheridia and archegonia. It is monoecious.
4. **Fertilisation** needs water and gives a zygote which grows into the new sporophyte.

**Pine (*Pinus*).**

1. **Sporophyte:** a tall tree with a tap root, a woody stem, needle leaves in clusters of $2$ ($3$ or $5$ in some), **resin canals** and a xerophytic structure (sunken stomata, thick cuticle).
2. **Male cones** in clusters bear microsporophylls with microsporangia producing **winged pollen grains**.
3. **Female cones** (woody) bear megasporophylls with ovules, and the megaspore forms a female gametophyte with archegonia.
4. **Pollination** is by wind. There is no need for water, and a **pollen tube** carries the male nuclei (**siphonogamy**).
5. **Seeds** are naked and winged. They form two years after pollination.

| Feature | Fern | Pine |
|---|---|---|
| Dominant stage | sporophyte | sporophyte |
| Spores | homosporous | heterosporous |
| Gametophyte | free-living | retained in the sporophyte |
| Water for fertilisation | needed | not needed |
| Seed | absent | naked seed |
| Vascular tissue | no vessels | tracheids, no vessels |

**Examples and uses.** Pteridophytes: *Selaginella*, *Equisetum*, *Lycopodium*, *Azolla* (a biofertiliser). Gymnosperms: Cycas, Pinus, Ginkgo, Ephedra (ephedrine), and the redwood. Pine gives timber, resin, turpentine and paper pulp, and the Himalayan Pinus roxburghii is common in Nepal's mid hills.

**Numerical.** If a fern frond has $20$ sori with $40$ sporangia each, and each holds $48$ spores, then $20\times40\times48 = 38\,400$ spores per frond. A pine pollen grain is only $60\ \mu\text{m}$ but may reach thousands of kilometres by wind.

**Conclusion.** The sporophyte dominates in both, but the pine does not need water for fertilisation and its ovule becomes a seed.
