Angiosperms and economic botany close the Biodiversity unit and carry about
**3 of the 40 Botany questions** — the most of any Biodiversity chapter. The
questions come from four places: plant **morphology** (which organ a potato,
an onion or a pea tendril really is; inflorescence, placentation, fruit types);
the four prescribed **families** (read a floral formula, name the family, give
its fruit); the **economic importance** of every plant group in Nepal; and the
five **medicinal plants** named in the syllabus — neem, sarpagandha,
yarsagumba, tulasi and ginger — with their scientific names, parts used and
active compounds.

::: key What the paper asks from this chapter
- **Modified organs by origin** — potato, ginger, onion, *Colocasia* (stems); sweet potato, carrot, radish (roots); pea tendril, *Opuntia* spine (leaves); *Opuntia* pad, *Asparagus* "leaf", *Citrus* thorn (stems).
- **Inflorescence** — racemose (acropetal) vs cymose (basipetal); special types: verticillaster (tulasi), cyathium (*Euphorbia*), hypanthodium (fig), capitulum (sunflower).
- **Flower terms** — symmetry, ovary position, aestivation, cohesion of stamens (monadelphous, diadelphous, syngenesious), tetradynamous and didynamous, placentation.
- **Fruits** — simple, aggregate, composite; true and false; legume, siliqua, berry, capsule, pome.
- **Four families** — Brassicaceae K₂₊₂ C₄ A₂₊₄ G₍₂₎; Fabaceae K₍₅₎ C₁₊₂₊₍₂₎ A₍₉₎₊₁ G₁; Solanaceae K₍₅₎ C₍₅₎ A₅ G₍₂₎; Liliaceae P₃₊₃ A₃₊₃ G₍₃₎ — with one diagnostic character each.
- **Economic botany of Nepal** — biofertilisers, fermented foods (gundruk, kinema), antibiotics, lokta paper, sal timber, pine resin, invasive weeds.
- **Medicinal plants** — *Azadirachta indica*, *Rauwolfia serpentina*, *Ophiocordyceps sinensis*, *Ocimum sanctum*, *Zingiber officinale*.
:::

## 5.1 Morphology of root, stem, leaf, inflorescence, flower and fruit

### Root

The **tap root** system grows from the radicle (dicots); the **fibrous** root
system of monocots replaces the short-lived primary root with many roots from
the base of the stem; **adventitious** roots arise from any part other than the
radicle.

| Modification | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Conical / fusiform / napiform tap roots | storage | carrot / radish / turnip and beet |
| Tuberous (adventitious) root | storage | **sweet potato** |
| Fasciculated roots | storage | *Dahlia*, *Asparagus* |
| **Prop roots** (from branches) | support | **banyan** (*Ficus benghalensis*) |
| **Stilt roots** (from lower nodes) | support | **maize**, sugarcane, *Pandanus* |
| Climbing roots | climbing | betel, money plant (*Pothos*) |
| **Pneumatophores** (negatively geotropic) | breathing in swamps | mangroves — *Rhizophora*, *Avicennia* |
| **Velamen** roots | absorbing moisture from air | epiphytic orchids (*Vanda*) |
| Haustoria (sucking roots) | parasitism | dodder (*Cuscuta*, total stem parasite); mistletoes (*Loranthus*, *Viscum*, partial) |
| Root nodules | nitrogen fixation | legumes (*Rhizobium*); alder, *uttis* (*Frankia*) |

### Stem

The stem bears **nodes, internodes and buds**, grows towards light, and in
its modified forms can always be recognised by these — **a structure with nodes,
buds or scale leaves is a stem**, however root-like it looks.

| Modification | Recognise it by | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Rhizome** | horizontal underground stem with nodes, scale leaves, buds | **ginger**, turmeric, banana |
| **Stem tuber** | swollen tip of an underground branch; "eyes" are nodes with buds | **potato** |
| **Corm** | short, upright, swollen underground stem | *Colocasia* (pindalu), *Amorphophallus*, *Crocus*, *Gladiolus* |
| **Bulb** | reduced disc-like stem with fleshy scale leaves | **onion** (tunicated), garlic (compound bulb of cloves), lily (scaly) |
| Runner, stolon, offset, sucker | sub-aerial branches that root and form new plants | *Cynodon* (dubo grass), strawberry (runners); jasmine, mint (stolons); *Pistia*, water hyacinth (offsets); *Chrysanthemum*, banana, pineapple (suckers) |
| **Stem tendril** | from an axillary or terminal bud | grape vine, cucurbits |
| **Thorn** | from an axillary bud; has vascular supply | *Citrus*, *Bougainvillea*, pomegranate |
| **Phylloclade** | flattened green stem of many internodes; leaves reduced to spines | *Opuntia*, *Euphorbia* |
| **Cladode** | green stem only one internode long | *Asparagus*, *Ruscus* |
| Bulbil | fleshy axillary bud that drops and grows | *Dioscorea*, *Agave* |

::: caution Thorn, spine and prickle
A **thorn** is a modified **stem** (axillary branch — *Citrus*, *Bougainvillea*).
A **spine** is a modified **leaf** or part of a leaf (*Opuntia*, *Berberis*;
stipular spines of *Zizyphus*). A **prickle** is only an **epidermal
outgrowth** with no vascular tissue (**rose**). Thorn and spine have the same
function but different origins — they are **analogous**.
:::

### Leaf

A leaf has a **leaf base** (swollen into a **pulvinus** in legumes, sheathing in
grasses), a **petiole** and a **lamina**; many have a pair of **stipules**.

- **Venation**: **reticulate** in dicots and **parallel** in monocots
  (exceptions: *Smilax* and *Dioscorea* are monocots with reticulate venation;
  *Calophyllum* is a dicot with parallel venation).
- **Compound leaves**: **pinnate** — leaflets on a rachis (**neem** and rose,
  imparipinnate; tamarind, paripinnate; *Mimosa*, bipinnate; *Moringa*,
  tripinnate); **palmate** — leaflets from one point (unifoliate *Citrus*,
  trifoliate *Aegle* (bel) and *Oxalis*, quadrifoliate *Marsilea*, multifoliate
  silk cotton, *simal*). A compound leaf has a bud in its axil; a leaflet has
  none.
- **Phyllotaxy**: **alternate** (one leaf per node — mustard, China rose),
  **opposite** (two per node — guava, *Calotropis* (decussate)), **whorled**
  (more than two — *Alstonia* (chhatiwan), *Nerium*).

| Leaf modification | Examples |
|---|---|
| Leaf tendril | **pea** (terminal leaflets); *Lathyrus aphaca* (whole leaf; the stipules become leaf-like) |
| Leaf spines | *Opuntia*, cacti, *Berberis* |
| **Phyllode** (flattened green petiole) | Australian *Acacia* |
| Pitcher / bladder (insect traps) | *Nepenthes* / *Utricularia* |
| Fleshy storage leaves | onion bulb scales, *Aloe* |
| **Heterophylly** (different leaves on one plant) | *Sagittaria*, *Ranunculus aquatilis*, *Limnophila* |

### Inflorescence

| Feature | **Racemose** (indefinite) | **Cymose** (definite) |
|---|---|---|
| Main axis | grows on indefinitely | ends in a flower |
| Order of flowers | **acropetal** — oldest at the base, youngest at the tip | **basipetal** — oldest at the tip |
| Opening | centripetal (outside → in) | centrifugal (inside → out) |

| Racemose type | Character | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Raceme** | long axis, **pedicellate** flowers | mustard, radish |
| **Spike** | long axis, **sessile** flowers | *Achyranthes*, *Amaranthus* |
| Spikelet | small spikes with glumes | wheat, rice (grasses) |
| **Catkin** | pendulous spike of unisexual flowers | mulberry, willow, birch |
| **Spadix** | fleshy axis enclosed by a large bract, the **spathe** | *Colocasia*, banana, maize (female) |
| **Corymb** | lower pedicels longer, flowers at one level | candytuft (*Iberis*) |
| **Umbel** | pedicels from one point, with an involucre of bracts | coriander, carrot (compound umbel), *Centella* |
| **Capitulum (head)** | flat receptacle with sessile **ray and disc florets**, involucre | sunflower, marigold, *Tridax* |

**Cymose types**: monochasial (one branch below the terminal flower —
helicoid or scorpioid), dichasial (two — jasmine), polychasial (several —
*Calotropis*). **Special types**: **cyathium** (a cup of bracts with one female
flower surrounded by male flowers — *Euphorbia*, poinsettia); **verticillaster**
(a pair of cymes at each node, looking like a whorl — **tulasi**, *Leucas*,
Lamiaceae); **hypanthodium** (a hollow, flask-shaped receptacle with flowers
inside and a small pore, the ostiole — fig, peepal, banyan).

### Flower

The flower is a modified shoot for sexual reproduction. On the **thalamus**
(receptacle) sit four whorls: **calyx** (sepals) and **corolla** (petals) are
the accessory whorls; **androecium** (stamens) and **gynoecium** (carpels) are the
essential whorls. When calyx and corolla look alike they are called a
**perianth** of tepals (lily, onion).

| Term | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Actinomorphic** ⊕ | can be cut into equal halves in any vertical plane | mustard, *Datura*, chilli |
| **Zygomorphic** % | only one plane gives equal halves | pea, bean, gulmohur, *Cassia* |
| Monoecious / dioecious | male and female flowers on one plant / on separate plants | maize, cucurbits / papaya, date palm |
| **Hypogynous** (superior ovary) | other whorls arise below the ovary | mustard, China rose, brinjal |
| **Perigynous** (half-inferior) | other whorls on the rim of a cup-shaped thalamus | plum, rose, peach |
| **Epigynous** (inferior ovary) | thalamus fuses with the ovary wall; other whorls above it | guava, cucumber, sunflower ray florets, apple |

**Aestivation** (arrangement of sepals or petals in the bud): **valvate** —
margins only touch (*Calotropis*); **twisted** — each overlaps the next
(China rose, cotton, lady's finger); **imbricate** — irregular overlapping
(*Cassia*, gulmohur); **vexillary** (descending imbricate) — the large standard
covers the two wings, which cover the keel (**pea, bean**).

| Androecium term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Monadelphous** | filaments united into one bundle | China rose (*Hibiscus*) |
| **Diadelphous** | two bundles | pea, (9) + 1 |
| **Polyadelphous** | more than two bundles | *Citrus*, castor |
| **Syngenesious** | anthers united, filaments free | sunflower (Asteraceae) |
| **Epipetalous** | stamens attached to petals | brinjal, *Datura* (Solanaceae) |
| **Epiphyllous** | stamens attached to the perianth | lily |
| **Didynamous** | 4 stamens, 2 long + 2 short | tulasi (Lamiaceae) |
| **Tetradynamous** | 6 stamens, 4 long + 2 short | mustard (Brassicaceae) |

**Gynoecium.** Carpels free (**apocarpous** — lotus, rose, *Michelia*) or fused
(**syncarpous** — mustard, tomato). **Placentation** is the arrangement of
ovules inside the ovary:

Marginal — along one suture of a single carpel (pea, legumes). **Axile** — on the
central axis of a multilocular ovary (tomato, China rose, lemon, Liliaceae).
**Parietal** — on the ovary wall of a unilocular ovary (cucumber, *Argemone*;
mustard, where a false septum, the **replum**, later divides it). **Free
central** — on a central column with no septa (*Dianthus*, *Primula*). **Basal** —
one ovule at the base (sunflower, marigold).

### Fruit

A fruit is a ripened ovary. A **true fruit** develops from the ovary alone
(mango, tomato); a **false fruit** has other parts too — the **thalamus** in apple
and pear (pome), strawberry and cashew. A fruit formed without fertilisation is
**parthenocarpic** (seedless banana, pineapple).

| Kind | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple** fruit | from one ovary of one flower | mango, tomato, pea |
| **Aggregate** (etaerio) | from **many free carpels of one flower** | strawberry, custard apple (*Annona*), raspberry, lotus |
| **Composite** (multiple) | from a **whole inflorescence** | jackfruit and pineapple (sorosis), mulberry (sorosis), fig (syconus) |

| Simple fruit | Type | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Legume** (pod) | dry, monocarpellary, splits along both sutures | pea, bean, gram |
| **Siliqua** / silicula | dry, bicarpellary, long / short, splits from below leaving the replum | mustard / *Capsella* |
| **Capsule** | dry, multicarpellary, splits by many valves | cotton, lady's finger, *Datura* |
| Caryopsis | dry, one seed, seed coat fused with fruit wall | wheat, rice, maize |
| Cypsela | dry, one seed, from inferior ovary, often with pappus | sunflower, marigold |
| Nut | dry, hard, one-seeded | litchi, cashew |
| **Drupe** | fleshy; stony endocarp | mango, plum, coconut (fibrous mesocarp) |
| **Berry** | fleshy throughout, many seeds | tomato, brinjal, chilli, grape, banana, guava |
| Hesperidium | berry with juicy hairs from the endocarp | orange, lemon |
| **Pome** | false fruit; fleshy thalamus | apple, pear |
| Pepo | berry from an inferior ovary, hard rind | cucumber, pumpkin |

## 5.2 Brassicaceae, Solanaceae, Fabaceae and Liliaceae

### Writing a floral formula

Br = bracteate, Ebr = ebracteate; ⊕ actinomorphic, % zygomorphic; ⚥ bisexual,
♂ male, ♀ female; K calyx, C corolla, P perianth, A androecium, G gynoecium.
The number after the letter is the number of members; **brackets** mean the
members are **united**; **+** separates whorls or groups. A line under G means a
superior ovary, a line over it an inferior ovary — in this book the ovary
position is written in words. All four families below have a **superior
ovary** (hypogynous flowers).

| Character | **Brassicaceae** (Cruciferae) | **Fabaceae** (Papilionaceae) | **Solanaceae** | **Liliaceae** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Habit | herbs, pungent juice | herbs, shrubs, trees, climbers; **root nodules** | herbs, shrubs; rich in alkaloids | **monocot** herbs with bulbs, rhizomes or corms |
| Leaves | simple, alternate, exstipulate | **pinnately compound**, stipulate, pulvinate base; leaflets may be tendrils | simple, alternate, exstipulate | mostly basal, linear, **parallel venation** |
| Inflorescence | **raceme** (corymbose raceme) | raceme | solitary or axillary cymes | raceme, spike or umbellate cyme (onion) |
| Flower | ebracteate, ⊕, ⚥, **tetramerous** | bracteate, **%**, ⚥, pentamerous | ⊕, ⚥, pentamerous | bracteate, ⊕, ⚥, **trimerous** |
| Calyx | 4, free, in two whorls (2 + 2) | 5, **united**, odd sepal anterior | 5, **united**, **persistent** | — |
| Corolla | 4, free, **cruciform** (cross-shaped, clawed) | 5, **papilionaceous**: 1 standard, 2 wings, 2 united keel petals; **vexillary** | 5, **united**, rotate or funnel-shaped, valvate | perianth of **6 tepals in two whorls** (3 + 3), free or united, petaloid |
| Androecium | 6, **tetradynamous** (2 short outer + 4 long inner) | 10, **diadelphous** (9) + 1 | 5, **epipetalous**, alternate with the petals | 6 in two whorls (3 + 3), **epiphyllous** |
| Gynoecium | **bicarpellary**, syncarpous; **parietal** placentation; unilocular, made bilocular by the **replum** | **monocarpellary**, unilocular, **marginal** placentation | **bicarpellary**, syncarpous, **ovary oblique** (septum at 45°); bilocular; **axile**, swollen placentae | **tricarpellary**, syncarpous, **trilocular**, **axile** |
| Fruit | **siliqua** or silicula | **legume** (pod) | **berry** (tomato, brinjal, chilli) or **capsule** (*Datura*, tobacco) | **capsule** (berry in *Asparagus*) |
| Seed | non-endospermic | non-endospermic | endospermic | endospermic |
| **Floral formula** | Ebr ⊕ ⚥ K₂₊₂ C₄ A₂₊₄ G₍₂₎ | Br % ⚥ K₍₅₎ C₁₊₂₊₍₂₎ A₍₉₎₊₁ G₁ | ⊕ ⚥ K₍₅₎ C₍₅₎ A₅ G₍₂₎ (stamens epipetalous) | Br ⊕ ⚥ P₃₊₃ A₃₊₃ G₍₃₎ — P₍₃₊₃₎ when the tepals are united (onion) |
| Diagnostic character | **cruciform corolla + tetradynamous stamens + siliqua** | **papilionaceous corolla + diadelphous stamens + legume** | **epipetalous stamens + oblique bicarpellary ovary** | **trimerous perianth (3 + 3) + six stamens + trilocular ovary** |

::: caution Traps in the family questions
- **Tetradynamous** (6: 4 long + 2 short) is Brassicaceae; **didynamous**
  (4: 2 long + 2 short) is Lamiaceae (tulasi). Do not swap them.
- In Fabaceae the **standard is posterior** and the **keel anterior**; the
  **free stamen** is the one opposite the standard. The odd sepal is
  **anterior** — the reverse of most families.
- The Solanaceae ovary is **oblique** — its septum lies at 45° to the median
  plane — and the placentae are **swollen**. The calyx is **persistent** on the
  fruit (brinjal, tomato) and enlarges into a bladder in *Physalis*.
- Liliaceae is the only **monocot** of the four; "perianth" (not calyx and
  corolla) and the number **three** identify it. Modern classification moves
  onion and garlic to Amaryllidaceae and *Aloe* and *Asparagus* to other
  families, but the CEE syllabus keeps them in Liliaceae.
- Pea, gram and lentil are **Fabaceae**, but this family has two older
  names — **Papilionaceae** and **Leguminosae**; mustard's old family name is
  **Cruciferae**.
:::

### Notes on each plant

**Neem (*Azadirachta indica*).** A large evergreen tree with **imparipinnate**
compound leaves. **Azadirachtin**, a limonoid concentrated in the seeds,
repels insects and stops them feeding and moulting — the basis of neem
biopesticides and neem-coated urea. Leaves are stored with grain and clothes
to keep insects away.

**Sarpagandha (*Rauwolfia serpentina*).** A small undershrub with whorled
leaves and white-pink flowers. Its root alkaloid **reserpine** depletes
noradrenaline at nerve endings, lowering blood pressure and calming the mind;
it was one of the first modern antihypertensive drugs (1950s). Over-collection
for its roots has made it rare in the wild.

**Yarsagumba (*Ophiocordyceps sinensis*).** An **ascomycete fungus** that
**parasitises the caterpillars of ghost moths** (*Thitarodes*) living in the soil
of high alpine meadows. Spores infect the larva in late summer; the fungus fills
and **mummifies** it during winter (the "winter worm"); in spring a slender,
dark, club-shaped **stroma** grows from the larva's head above the ground (the
"summer grass") and bears perithecia with ascospores. Its Tibetan name,
*yartsa gunbu*, means "summer grass, winter worm". Collection in May–June
is a major cash income for mountain households; the price runs to millions of
rupees per kilogram, and over-harvesting has made the species **Vulnerable**
on the IUCN Red List.

**Tulasi (*Ocimum sanctum*).** An aromatic herb of the mint family with a
**square stem**, opposite decussate leaves, **verticillaster** inflorescence,
**bilabiate** corolla and **didynamous** stamens. Its leaf oil is rich in
**eugenol** (also in clove). It is worshipped in Hindu homes and planted in a
*tulasi math* in the courtyard.

**Ginger (*Zingiber officinale*).** A monocot herb whose edible part is a
**rhizome — a modified underground stem** with nodes, scale leaves and buds,
not a root. Fresh ginger owes its pungency to **gingerols**; on drying (sutho)
they turn into the even more pungent **shogaols**. Nepal is among the world's
largest ginger producers and exports much of it to India.

::: caution Name traps
- Yarsagumba is a **fungus** (Ascomycetes), not a plant or an insect — the
  "worm" is the dead host larva. Its current genus is *Ophiocordyceps*.
- Ginger is a **stem** (rhizome); sweet potato and carrot are **roots**.
- Reserpine is from **sarpagandha**; azadirachtin is from **neem** — the
  two are often swapped in options.
- Tulasi is in **Lamiaceae** (didynamous, verticillaster), not Solanaceae.
:::

## Chapter summary

- A modified organ is identified by its origin, not its looks: nodes, buds or scale leaves mean stem (potato, ginger, onion, *Colocasia*, *Opuntia* pad, *Asparagus* cladode, *Citrus* thorn); sweet potato, carrot and radish are roots; pea tendrils and *Opuntia* spines are leaves.
- Racemose inflorescences grow on and open acropetally; cymose ones end in a flower and open basipetally; tulasi has a verticillaster, *Euphorbia* a cyathium, the fig a hypanthodium and the sunflower a capitulum.
- Flower terms the paper uses: actinomorphic or zygomorphic; hypo-, peri- or epigynous; valvate, twisted, imbricate or vexillary aestivation; monadelphous, diadelphous, syngenesious, epipetalous, tetradynamous and didynamous stamens.
- Placentation is marginal (pea), axile (tomato, lily), parietal (mustard), free central (*Dianthus*) or basal (sunflower).
- Fruits are simple, aggregate (many free carpels of one flower) or composite (a whole inflorescence); false fruits such as the apple include the thalamus.
- Brassicaceae: Ebr ⊕ ⚥ K₂₊₂ C₄ A₂₊₄ G₍₂₎, siliqua. Fabaceae: Br % ⚥ K₍₅₎ C₁₊₂₊₍₂₎ A₍₉₎₊₁ G₁, legume. Solanaceae: ⊕ ⚥ K₍₅₎ C₍₅₎ A₅ G₍₂₎ with epipetalous stamens and an oblique ovary, berry. Liliaceae: Br ⊕ ⚥ P₃₊₃ A₃₊₃ G₍₃₎, capsule.
- Every group matters in Nepal: nitrogen-fixing bacteria and blue-green algae, gundruk and kinema, mushrooms and yeast, agar, *Sphagnum*, *Azolla*, pine resin and timber, taxol, lokta paper and sal timber — and the harm done by plant viruses, late blight, aflatoxin, poisonous mushrooms and invasive weeds.
- The five named medicinal plants: neem (*Azadirachta indica*, azadirachtin), sarpagandha (*Rauwolfia serpentina*, reserpine, roots), yarsagumba (*Ophiocordyceps sinensis*, an ascomycete on ghost-moth larvae, cordycepin), tulasi (*Ocimum sanctum*, eugenol, leaves) and ginger (*Zingiber officinale*, rhizome, gingerols).

::: note Answers and explanations — Set A
**1.** (a) — Banyan's prop roots hang from horizontal branches like pillars. Maize has stilt roots from the lower nodes; *Rhizophora* has pneumatophores; carrot is a storage tap root.
**2.** (c) — The potato is a stem tuber: its "eyes" are nodes with axillary buds. Sweet potato is a tuberous root.
**3.** (b) — *Opuntia* spines are modified leaves (its green pad is the stem); pea tendrils are modified terminal leaflets.
**4.** (d) — A phylloclade has many nodes and internodes. A cladode is only one internode long (*Asparagus*); a phyllode is a flattened petiole.
**5.** (b) — Racemose = indefinite growth with acropetal succession. Option (a) describes a cyme; (c) and (d) are cyathium and hypanthodium.
**6.** (a) — Tulasi (Lamiaceae) has a pair of small cymes at each node that look like a whorl — a verticillaster. The cyathium belongs to *Euphorbia*, the hypanthodium to the fig.
**7.** (d) — Hypogynous means the other whorls arise below the ovary, so the ovary is superior.
**8.** (c) — One bundle = monadelphous (Malvaceae). Diadelphous is two bundles (pea); syngenesious means anthers fused (sunflower).
**9.** (c) — Tomato has ovules on a central axis in a multilocular ovary. Pea is marginal, mustard parietal, sunflower basal.

**14.** (a) — The papilionaceous corolla has one posterior standard, two lateral wings and two anterior petals fused into the keel. Option (b) is cruciform (mustard).

   (a) yeasts produce alcohol (b) moulds produce citric acid (c) lactic acid bacteria ferment its sugars (d) *Acetobacter* makes vinegar

   (a) repels insects and blocks their feeding and moulting (b) kills bacteria in the soil (c) fixes nitrogen (d) is an antibiotic for humans

   (a) reserpine (b) eugenol (c) gingerols (d) cordycepin

::: note Answers and explanations — Set B
**1.** (b) — Stems carry nodes, buds and scale leaves (the potato's eyes); the sweet potato tuber has none, so it is a swollen adventitious root. Storage and position do not decide the organ.
**2.** (d) — Both protect the plant, but the thorn is a modified branch and the spine a modified leaf: same function, different origin = analogous.
**3.** (a) — The true leaves of *Asparagus* are tiny scales; in their axils arise green cladodes, each one internode long. A phyllode is a flattened petiole (*Acacia*).
**4.** (c) — The flat disc at the base of the bulb is the true stem; the onion layers are fleshy leaf bases. Growing underground or storing food does not make an organ a stem.
**5.** (a) — The capitulum has outer ray florets and inner disc florets on a flattened receptacle, ringed by an involucre of bracts. Each "petal" is a ray floret.
**6.** (b) — Vexillary (descending imbricate) aestivation is the mark of the papilionaceous corolla.
**7.** (d) — Brassicaceae ovaries have parietal placentation; a false septum (replum) grows across between the two placentae.
**8.** (c) — Epipetalous stamens have their filaments fused to the corolla tube. Stamens attached to tepals are epiphyllous (lily).
**9.** (b) — Only Fabaceae has a single carpel with ovules along one margin. The other three have superior ovaries and bisexual flowers too, and Solanaceae also has five sepals.
**10.** (a) — Trimerous flowers with an undifferentiated perianth are typical monocot features. A superior ovary, bisexuality and axile placentation also occur in dicots.
**11.** (d) — *Rhizobium* fixes N₂ in legume nodules; *Anabaena* fixes it in rice fields and inside *Azolla*. Both reduce the need for urea.
**12.** (c) — Gundruk is made by natural lactic acid fermentation (*Lactobacillus*, *Pediococcus*), which also preserves the greens. No alcohol or vinegar is involved.
**13.** (a) — Azadirachtin is an antifeedant and insect growth regulator. It is not an antibiotic for humans and does not fix nitrogen.
**14.** (c) — Gingerols give fresh ginger its bite; drying converts them to shogaols. Eugenol is tulasi's oil; reserpine and cordycepin belong to sarpagandha and yarsagumba.
**15.** (b) — *Sphagnum* holds many times its weight of water, so it is used for packing and as dressings, and it accumulates into peat used as fuel. It has no timber or fruit.

::: note Answers and explanations — Set C
**1.** (c) — Five united sepals and petals, five stamens and an oblique bicarpellary ovary is Solanaceae. The formula cannot show epipetaly, but the oblique ovary settles it.
**2.** (a) — Cruciform corolla, tetradynamous stamens and siliqua are the three marks of Brassicaceae — mustard, radish, cabbage.
**3.** (d) — Standard, wings and keel with (9) + 1 stamens mean Fabaceae, whose monocarpellary ovary ripens into a legume (pod).

**7.** (d) — Reserpine from the roots of sarpagandha lowers blood pressure; the plant's name ("snake-smell") recalls its folk use for snake bite.
**8.** (b) — The stalk is the stroma of *Ophiocordyceps sinensis*, which bears perithecia with asci; the larva is the dead host filled with fungal mycelium.
**9.** (d) — Neem twigs are stems, tulasi is used for its leaves, and ginger's rhizome is an underground stem.
**10.** (a) — Sessile flowers in acropetal order on an elongated axis make a spike. A raceme has stalked flowers; a cyme has the oldest flower at the tip.
