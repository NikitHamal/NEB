## 3.1 Fungi: the four classes; yeast and Mucor

### General characters

Fungi are **eukaryotic, achlorophyllous, heterotrophic** thallophytes. They
digest food outside the body and **absorb** it — as **saprophytes** (on dead
matter), **parasites** (on living hosts) or **symbionts** (in lichens and
mycorrhizae). The body is a **mycelium**, a mass of thread-like **hyphae** that
are either **aseptate and coenocytic** (multinucleate, no cross walls) or
**septate**. The wall contains **chitin** (fungal cellulose) with glucans, and the reserve food
is **glycogen and oil** — never starch. The study of fungi is **mycology**.

```photo id="cee-bot/mucor" caption="*Mucor* growing on bread: each black globe is a sporangium full of sporangiospores, held up on a fine, unbranched sporangiophore. The hypha beneath is coenocytic (aseptate)." width="70"
call 0.16 0.90 0.49 0.76 "Sporangium"
call 0.76 0.18 0.58 0.40 "Sporangiophore"
```


Sexual reproduction has three steps: **plasmogamy** (fusion of protoplasts),
**karyogamy** (fusion of nuclei) and **meiosis**. In Ascomycetes and
Basidiomycetes karyogamy is delayed, so the hyphae pass through a
**dikaryotic** (n + n) stage with two nuclei per cell.

| Feature | **Phycomycetes** (algal fungi) | **Ascomycetes** (sac fungi) | **Basidiomycetes** (club fungi) | **Deuteromycetes** (fungi imperfecti) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mycelium | **aseptate, coenocytic** | septate, branched (yeast unicellular) | septate, with clamp connections; long dikaryotic phase | septate, branched |
| Asexual spores | endogenous **sporangiospores** (*Mucor*) or motile **zoospores** (*Albugo*, *Phytophthora*) | exogenous **conidia** on conidiophores; budding in yeast | rare (conidia, oidia); fragmentation common | **conidia** only |
| Sexual spores | **zygospore** (isogamous fusion of gametangia) or **oospore** (oogamy) | **ascospores**, usually **8**, formed **endogenously** in a sac, the **ascus** | **basidiospores**, **4**, formed **exogenously** on a club-shaped **basidium**; no sex organs — two vegetative hyphae fuse (somatogamy) | **not known** |
| Fruiting body | none | **ascocarp** — cleistothecium (closed), perithecium (flask), apothecium (cup) | **basidiocarp** — mushroom, bracket fungus, puffball | none |
| Examples | *Mucor*, *Rhizopus*, *Albugo*, *Phytophthora*, *Pythium* | *Saccharomyces*, *Penicillium*, *Aspergillus*, *Claviceps*, *Neurospora*, *Morchella*, *Ophiocordyceps* | *Agaricus*, *Pleurotus*, *Ganoderma*, *Puccinia*, *Ustilago*, *Amanita*, *Lycoperdon* | *Alternaria*, *Colletotrichum*, *Trichoderma*, *Fusarium*, *Cercospora*, *Helminthosporium* |
| Diseases | **late blight of potato** (*Phytophthora infestans* — the Irish famine), **white rust of crucifers** (*Albugo candida*, on mustard and radish), damping-off (*Pythium*) | **ergot of rye** (*Claviceps purpurea*), powdery mildew (*Erysiphe*) | **black stem rust of wheat** (*Puccinia graminis*), **loose smut of wheat** (*Ustilago*) | **early blight of potato** (*Alternaria solani*), **red rot of sugarcane** (*Colletotrichum falcatum*), tikka of groundnut (*Cercospora*), brown spot of rice (*Helminthosporium*), ringworm |

**Z**ygo-**A**sco-**B**asidio-**N**one: Phyco**Z**, **A**sco = sac with 8 inside,

### Yeast (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*)

**Structure.** Yeast is a **unicellular** ascomycete (no mycelium), with oval
cells about 5–10 μm long. Each cell has a thin wall (glucans and mannans with a
little chitin), a plasma membrane, a large central **vacuole** with volutin
granules, one **nucleus** beside it, mitochondria, and reserve food as
**glycogen and oil droplets**. It is a **facultative anaerobe**: with oxygen it
respires completely; without oxygen it ferments sugar with the enzyme complex
**zymase** (Buchner, 1897):

```photo id="bio11/rhizopus" caption="The mycelium of *Rhizopus stolonifer* under the microscope. The long, unbranched-looking hyphae carry upright sporangiophores topped by dark, round sporangia. The scale bar is 0.1 mm." width="80"
```


C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ + energy (2 ATP per glucose)

**Reproduction.**

| Method | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Budding** (main vegetative method) | a small outgrowth (bud) appears; the nucleus divides and one daughter nucleus enters the bud, which enlarges and breaks off, leaving a **bud scar**. In rich sugar medium buds stay attached in chains — a **pseudomycelium** |
| **Fission** | the cell divides into two by a septum — in fission yeast, *Schizosaccharomyces* |
| **Ascospore formation** (sexual) | two haploid cells of opposite mating type fuse (plasmogamy + karyogamy) → a diploid cell; it may bud as a diploid, and later turns into an **ascus**: meiosis gives **4 haploid ascospores**, which are released and bud as haploid cells |

Because both haploid and diploid cells of *S. cerevisiae* bud and grow, its
life cycle is **haplo-diplontic**. (*Schizosaccharomyces octosporus* is
haplontic, with 8 ascospores; *Saccharomycodes ludwigii* is diplontic.)

**Uses.** Baking (CO₂ raises the dough), brewing of beer and wine, and in Nepal
the *murcha* (marcha) starter used for *jaand* and *raksi*, in which moulds
such as *Mucor* and *Rhizopus* break starch into sugar and yeasts ferment it;
vitamin B complex and single-cell protein; recombinant hepatitis B vaccine;
and the model eukaryote of genetics (first eukaryotic genome sequenced, 1996).
*Candida albicans*, a yeast-like fungus, causes candidiasis.

```photo id="bio11/agaricus" caption="Fruiting bodies (basidiocarps) of the button mushroom *Agaricus bisporus*. What we eat is the compact, tissue-like mass of hyphae; the mycelium itself stays hidden in the compost." width="72"
```


### *Mucor* (pin mould)

**Structure.** *Mucor* (Phycomycetes, class Zygomycetes in newer books) grows
as a white cottony **mycelium** on moist bread, cow dung, rotting fruit and
vegetables. The hyphae are branched, **aseptate and coenocytic** (septa form
only to cut off reproductive organs), with chitin walls and glycogen and oil as
food. Unlike *Rhizopus*, *Mucor* has **no rhizoids and no stolons**; its
sporangiophores arise singly from the mycelium.

**Asexual reproduction.** Erect, aerial **sporangiophores** each end in a round,
black **sporangium**. Its central sterile dome is the **columella**; around it
are many **non-motile sporangiospores** (aplanospores). The sporangial wall
dissolves or breaks, the spores are blown away and each germinates into a new
mycelium. In poor conditions *Mucor* also forms thick-walled, intercalary
**chlamydospores**; in sugary liquid it buds into yeast-like **oidia**.

**Sexual reproduction (conjugation).** Most species are **heterothallic** —
zygospores form only when hyphae of two compatible strains, **+ and −**, meet
(Blakeslee, 1904, discovered heterothallism in *Mucor mucedo*). The steps:

1. Short side branches of + and − hyphae grow towards each other and touch —
   **progametangia**.
2. Each tip is cut off by a septum as a multinucleate **gametangium**; the
   stalk behind it is the **suspensor**.
3. The wall between the two gametangia dissolves and their contents fuse
   (**plasmogamy**); + and − nuclei pair and fuse (**karyogamy**).
4. The fused cell becomes a thick, black, warty-walled resting **zygospore**
   (2n) — the **only diploid stage**.
5. On germination, meiosis occurs and the zygospore puts out a **germ
   sporangium** (promycelium + sporangium). In *M. mucedo* only one meiotic
   nucleus survives, so all spores of one germ sporangium are of one strain
   (+ or −).

A zygospore that forms without fusion is an **azygospore** (parthenospore).
Since the only diploid structure is the zygote, the life cycle is
**haplontic** with **zygotic meiosis**.

## 3.2 Lichens: introduction and types

A **lichen** is a **composite organism**: a stable, self-supporting **symbiotic
association** between a **fungus** (the **mycobiont**) and an **alga or a
cyanobacterium** (the **phycobiont** or photobiont). The fungus forms most of
the body, absorbs water and minerals and shelters the alga; the alga
photosynthesises and feeds both. (Because the fungus gains more and can
penetrate the algal cells, some authors call it controlled parasitism, or
**helotism**.) The mycobiont is usually an **ascomycete**; the phycobiont is
usually a green alga (*Trebouxia*, *Trentepohlia*) or a cyanobacterium
(*Nostoc*). Lichenology is the study of lichens.

```photo id="bio11/usnea" caption="*Usnea*, a fruticose ("beard") lichen: pale green, branched and hanging like tiny shrubs from the bark." width="72"
```


| Basis | Types | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Growth form** | **crustose** — a thin crust tightly glued to rock or bark, cannot be removed whole | *Graphis*, *Lecanora*, *Rhizocarpon* |
| | **foliose** — flat, lobed, leaf-like; attached loosely by **rhizines** | *Parmelia*, *Peltigera*, *Physcia* |
| | **fruticose** — shrubby, branched, erect or hanging; attached by a basal disc | *Usnea* (old man's beard), *Cladonia* (reindeer moss), *Ramalina* |
| **Internal structure** | **homoiomerous** — algal cells scattered evenly among hyphae | *Collema*, *Leptogium* |
| | **heteromerous** — algal cells in a distinct layer: upper cortex, algal zone, medulla, lower cortex | *Parmelia*, *Physcia*, *Usnea* |
| **Fungal partner** | ascolichens (most), basidiolichens, deuterolichens | *Parmelia*; *Cora* |

**Reproduction.** Vegetatively by **fragmentation**, by **soredia** (tiny
granules of a few algal cells wrapped in hyphae, dispersed by wind) and by
**isidia** (coral-like outgrowths of the thallus that break off); the fungus
alone reproduces sexually, forming apothecia or perithecia, and its spores
must find a suitable alga to form a new lichen.

**Importance.**
- **Pioneers**: crustose lichens are the first colonisers of bare rock in a
  xerosere; their acids (oxalic acid, lichen acids) weather the rock and start
  soil formation.
- **Air-pollution indicators**: they are very sensitive to **SO₂**, so they
  disappear from polluted city air.
- **Food and trade**: reindeer moss (*Cladonia rangiferina*) feeds reindeer; in
  Nepal, lichens (*jhyau*, mostly of the family Parmeliaceae) are collected in
  the hills and exported to India for spice mixes, dyes and perfume resins.
- **Dyes and chemicals**: **litmus** (from *Roccella*), orchil dye; oak moss
  (*Evernia*) in perfumes; usnic acid of *Usnea* is antibacterial.

## 3.3 Algae: green, red and brown; Spirogyra

### General characters

Algae are simple, chlorophyll-bearing, **thalloid**, autotrophic and mostly
**aquatic** eukaryotes. They have no roots, stems, leaves or vascular tissue;
their sex organs are usually **unicellular and not covered by a jacket of
sterile cells**, and the zygote does not grow into an embryo. They range from
unicellular (*Chlamydomonas*, *Chlorella*) and colonial (*Volvox*) to
filamentous (*Spirogyra*, *Ulothrix*) and large parenchymatous thalli
(*Ulva*, *Laminaria*). Sexual reproduction is **isogamous** (gametes alike),
**anisogamous** (unlike in size) or **oogamous** (a large non-motile egg and a
small motile sperm — *Volvox*, *Fucus*). Their study is **phycology**.

| Feature | **Chlorophyceae** (green algae) | **Phaeophyceae** (brown algae) | **Rhodophyceae** (red algae) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main pigments | **chlorophyll *a*, *b***, carotenoids | chlorophyll *a*, *c*, **fucoxanthin** | chlorophyll *a* (*d* in some), **r-phycoerythrin**, phycocyanin |
| Stored food | **starch** (around pyrenoids) | **laminarin and mannitol** | **floridean starch** |
| Cell wall | cellulose | cellulose + **algin** (alginic acid) | cellulose, pectin and sulphated polysaccharides (**agar**, carrageenan) |
| Flagella | 2–8, **equal, apical** | **2, unequal, lateral** | **none** — no motile cells |
| Habitat | fresh water, brackish and sea water, moist soil | mostly **marine**, cool seas | mostly marine, often in **deep water** |
| Body | unicellular to parenchymatous | filaments to giant kelps with **holdfast, stipe and frond**; air bladders | filaments to complex thalli |
| Sexual reproduction | iso-, aniso- or oogamous | iso-, aniso- or oogamous | **oogamous**, with complex post-fertilisation stages |
| Examples | *Chlamydomonas*, *Volvox*, *Ulothrix*, *Spirogyra*, *Chara*, *Ulva*, *Chlorella* | *Ectocarpus*, *Dictyota*, *Laminaria*, *Fucus*, *Sargassum* | *Polysiphonia*, *Porphyra*, *Gelidium*, *Gracilaria*, *Batrachospermum* (fresh water) |

::: memory Pigment and food in one line
**Green — a, b, starch. Brown — a, c, fucoxanthin; laminarin, mannitol;
algin. Red — a, phycoerythrin; floridean starch; agar; no flagella.**
Red algae live deepest because **phycoerythrin absorbs the blue-green light**
that penetrates furthest into the sea.
:::

Products to remember: **agar** (*Gelidium*, *Gracilaria*) for culture media and
jellies; **carrageenan** (*Chondrus*) and **algin** (*Laminaria*, *Macrocystis*)
as thickeners; *Porphyra* (nori) and *Laminaria* (kombu) as food; *Chlorella*
and *Spirulina* (a cyanobacterium) as protein supplements; kelps as a source of
iodine and potash.

### *Spirogyra* — structure

*Spirogyra* ("water silk", "pond scum") is a free-floating, **unbranched,
filamentous green alga** of still fresh water — ponds, ditches and paddy
fields. Its slimy feel comes from a **mucilage sheath** of pectin outside the
**cellulose** wall. All cells of the filament are alike and each can divide:
there is no division of labour.

Each **cylindrical cell** has:
- a two-layered wall (inner cellulose, outer pectin);
- a thin lining layer of cytoplasm and a large central **vacuole**;
- a single **nucleus** suspended in the centre of the vacuole by
  **cytoplasmic strands**;
- one to several **ribbon-shaped, spirally coiled chloroplasts** with smooth
  or wavy margins, each carrying a row of **pyrenoids** (centres of starch
  formation).

The filament is **haploid (n)**.

```photo id="bio11/spirogyra" caption="Filaments of *Spirogyra* under the light microscope. Each cylindrical cell holds one or more green, spiral, ribbon-shaped chloroplasts, and the cross walls between the cells are plain to see." width="80"
```

### *Spirogyra* — reproduction

- **Vegetative**: **fragmentation** — the filament breaks and each piece grows
  by cell division. This is the commonest method.
- **Asexual** (rare): **akinetes** (thick-walled vegetative cells),
  **aplanospores**, and **azygospores** (parthenospores — formed from a gamete
  that fails to fuse).
- **Sexual**: by **conjugation**. The gametes are non-flagellate and amoeboid
  (**aplanogametes**). They look alike (morphologically **isogamous**), but one
  — called male, the + strain — moves into the other, which stays put: the
  process is **physiologically anisogamous**.

| | **Scalariform conjugation** | **Lateral conjugation** |
|---|---|---|
| Between | cells of **two filaments** lying side by side | **adjacent cells of the same filament** |
| Thallus | heterothallic | homothallic |
| How | papillae from opposite cells meet and form **conjugation tubes**, giving a **ladder-like** look; the contents of each male cell round off and pass through the tube | **indirect** (chain-forming): a tube forms beside the septum between neighbouring cells; **direct**: the gamete passes through a pore in the septum |
| After it | one filament **empty** (male), the other with a **zygospore** in each cell (female) | **alternate** cells empty and alternate cells with zygospores |

**The zygospore and meiosis.** The zygote secretes a thick three-layered
wall (exospore, mesospore, endospore) and becomes a resting **zygospore
(2n)** that survives drought and cold. On germination its diploid nucleus
divides by **meiosis** into **four haploid nuclei**; **three degenerate** and
**one survives**, so **each zygospore gives only one new filament**. Meiosis is
**zygotic** and the life cycle is **haplontic** — the zygospore is the only
diploid cell.

::: example Zygospore yield in scalariform vs lateral conjugation of Spirogyra
**Problem.** Two filaments of *Spirogyra*, each composed of 40 cells, undergo complete scalariform conjugation. How many zygospores are formed, and how many new filaments will germinate from them?

**Solution.** Each conjugation tube pairs one cell from the male-acting filament with one cell from the female-acting filament, producing **40 zygospores** (whereas lateral conjugation in a single 40-cell filament yields 20 zygospores). Each zygospore undergoes meiosis to form 4 haploid nuclei, of which 3 degenerate and 1 survives, so **40 haploid filaments** germinate.
:::

::: example Distinguishing algal classes by pigment and reserve food
**Problem.** A marine thalloid alga collected from the subtidal zone contains chlorophyll $a$, chlorophyll $d$, and $r$-phycoerythrin, stores floridean starch, and lacks flagellated stages throughout its life cycle. Assign its class.

**Solution.** Chlorophyll $a + d$, phycoerythrin, floridean starch, and complete absence of motile flagellated cells uniquely identify **Rhodophyceae (red algae)** such as *Polysiphonia* or *Gelidium*.
:::

::: example Ploidy accounting in Mucor and Saccharomyces
**Problem.** In *Mucor*, two compatible $(+)$ and $(-)$ hyphae fuse to form a zygospore ($2n = 16$). What is the chromosome number in a vegetative coenocytic hypha, a sporangiospore, and a germ-sporangium spore?

**Solution.** The vegetative mycelium of *Mucor* is haploid ($n = 8$). Asexual sporangiospores are mitospores ($n = 8$), and the zygospore undergoes meiosis before forming the germ sporangium, so germspores are also haploid ($n = \mathbf{8}$).
:::

## Chapter summary

- Fungi are eukaryotic, achlorophyllous absorptive heterotrophs with chitin walls and glycogen and oil as food; their sexual cycle is plasmogamy, karyogamy, meiosis.
- Phycomycetes have coenocytic hyphae and zygospores or oospores; Ascomycetes form 8 endogenous ascospores in an ascus; Basidiomycetes form 4 exogenous basidiospores on a basidium; Deuteromycetes have no known sexual stage.
- *Phytophthora* (late blight), *Albugo* (white rust), *Claviceps* (ergot), *Puccinia* (rust), *Ustilago* (smut), *Alternaria* (early blight) and *Colletotrichum* (red rot) are the classic disease fungi.
- Yeast is a unicellular ascomycete that buds, ferments sugar with zymase to ethanol and CO₂, and forms 4 ascospores; its cycle is haplo-diplontic.
- *Mucor* has a coenocytic mycelium without rhizoids, sporangia with a columella, heterothallic conjugation and a zygospore that is its only diploid stage.
- A lichen is a fungus (mycobiont) living with an alga or cyanobacterium (phycobiont); its forms are crustose, foliose and fruticose, and it spreads by soredia and isidia.
- Lichens are pioneers on bare rock and sensitive indicators of SO₂ pollution.
- Green algae store starch and have chl *a*, *b*; brown algae store laminarin and mannitol and have fucoxanthin and algin; red algae store floridean starch, have phycoerythrin and agar, and never have flagella.
- *Spirogyra* is a haploid unbranched filament with spiral chloroplasts; it reproduces by fragmentation and by scalariform or lateral conjugation; one zygospore gives one filament after zygotic meiosis.
