Developmental Botany carries **2 of the 40 Botany questions** and is the
story of how a flower makes a seed: the asexual shortcuts plants take,
the manufacture of the two gametophytes by **sporogenesis and
gametogenesis**, **pollination and double fertilization**, and what the
products become — **dicot and monocot embryos** and the **endosperm**.
The questions are unfailingly about ploidy (which cells are n, 2n, 3n),
about names (tapetum, scutellum, geitonogamy, chalazogamy) and about the
arithmetic of meiosis — how many mother cells a given number of seeds
requires.

::: key What the paper asks from this chapter
- **Asexual reproduction** — rhizome (ginger), tuber (potato eyes), bulb (onion), corm (*Colocasia*), runner, sucker, offset, leaf buds (*Bryophyllum*); cutting, layering, grafting; **apomixis** and nucellar polyembryony (citrus).
- **Male side** — anther wall (epidermis, endothecium, middle layers, **tapetum = nutritive**); PMC 2n → meiosis → tetrad of 4 microspores (n); exine of **sporopollenin** over intine; vegetative (tube) cell + generative cell → 2 male gametes; shed at 2- or 3-celled stage.
- **Female side** — ovule (funicle, integuments, micropyle, chalaza, nucellus); commonest **anatropous**; MMC 2n → meiosis → 4 megaspores, one functional → 3 mitoses → **Polygonum-type, 7-celled, 8-nucleate** embryo sac (egg + 2 synergids with filiform apparatus; central cell with 2 polar nuclei; 3 antipodals).
- **Pollination** — autogamy, **geitonogamy** (agent needed but genetically self), xenogamy; anemophily, hydrophily, entomophily, ornithophily, chiropterophily; contrivances: dicliny, dichogamy (protandry *Salvia*, protogyny *Plantago*), herkogamy, heterostyly, self-incompatibility; cleistogamy.
- **Double fertilization (Nawaschin, 1898)** — syngamy (n + n = 2n zygote) + triple fusion (n + n + n = 3n endosperm nucleus); entry by porogamy, chalazogamy (*Casuarina*), mesogamy (*Cucurbita*).
- **Embryos** — dicot (*Capsella*): basal cell → suspensor, apical → globular → heart → two cotyledons, plumule terminal; monocot: one cotyledon = **scutellum**, plumule in **coleoptile**, radicle in **coleorhiza**, epiblast.
- **Endosperm (3n)** — nuclear (coconut water), cellular (*Datura*), helobial; albuminous vs exalbuminous seeds; aleurone layer; **perisperm (2n)** from nucellus (black pepper).
:::

## 14.1 Asexual reproduction

In **vegetative propagation** an ordinary organ — root, stem, leaf or bud
— grows into a whole plant identical to the parent:

| Organ | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Underground stem | rhizome | ginger, turmeric |
| | tuber | potato (the "eyes" are buds) |
| | bulb | onion, garlic |
| | corm | *Colocasia*, *Crocus* |
| Sub-aerial stem | runner | *Cynodon* (dubo grass) |
| | sucker | mint, chrysanthemum |
| | offset | water hyacinth *Eichhornia* |
| Leaf | adventitious marginal buds | *Bryophyllum pinnatum* |
| Root | adventitious buds | sweet potato, *Dahlia* |

Growers copy the trick artificially: **cutting** (sugarcane, rose),
**layering** (jasmine, litchi), **grafting and budding** (mango, citrus,
apple in Mustang and Jumla) and **micropropagation** by tissue culture
(Unit 15). **Apomixis** is asexual reproduction wearing the disguise of
seed: an embryo forms **without fertilization**, so the seedling clones
its mother. In **nucellar polyembryony** (citrus, mango) extra embryos
sprout from the nucellus beside the sexual embryo. Breeders covet
apomixis because it could **fix hybrid vigour permanently** — the hybrid
would breed true from its own seed.

The trade-off is standard exam fare: asexual offspring are **quick and
exactly like the prized parent** (and the only route for seedless kinds
such as banana), but with **no variation** one new disease can take the
whole plantation; sexual reproduction is slower and needs pollinators,
but variation is the raw material of breeding and evolution.

::: example Ploidy arithmetic of the sac
**Problem.** A species' endosperm holds 24 chromosomes. State the chromosome
numbers of (a) the embryo, (b) an antipodal cell, (c) the megaspore mother
cell. Separately: how many meiotic divisions were needed to make 100 seeds'
worth of female and male gametophytes?
**Solution.** Endosperm is 3n = 24, so n = 8: (a) embryo 2n = **16**;
(b) antipodals are haploid, **8**; (c) the megaspore mother cell is 2n =
**16**. For 100 seeds: each embryo sac costs **one** megaspore meiosis (three
products wasted) → 100; each anther meiosis yields **four** pollen grains →
100 ÷ 4 = 25; total **125 meiotic divisions** — the classic asymmetry the
paper tests.
:::
## 14.2 Sporogenesis and gametogenesis in angiosperms

The plant in the field is the diploid **sporophyte**; the pollen grain
and the embryo sac are the reduced haploid **gametophytes**, kept inside
it. Angiosperms are **heterosporous**: the anther (microsporangium) makes
microspores, the ovule (megasporangium) makes megaspores.

### The male side — anther to pollen

An anther typically has two lobes, each with two pollen sacs. Its wall
has four layers: **epidermis, endothecium** (whose fibrous thickenings
spring the sac open at dehiscence), **middle layers**, and innermost the
**tapetum** — dense, often multinucleate cells that **nourish** the
developing spores and supply **sporopollenin** and wall proteins. If the
tapetum fails, the pollen is sterile; protection is the outer layers'
business, not the tapetum's.

Inside, each **pollen (microspore) mother cell (2n)** undergoes
**meiosis — microsporogenesis** — to give a **tetrad of four haploid
microspores**. Each matures into a pollen grain walled by the sculptured
outer **exine** of near-indestructible **sporopollenin** (pollen survives
in sediments for millennia, the basis of palynology) over a pectin–
cellulose **intine**. **Microgametogenesis**: the microspore nucleus
divides unequally into a large **vegetative (tube) cell** and a small
**generative cell**; the generative cell later divides into the **two
male gametes**. In about 60% of families pollen is shed at the 2-celled
stage. The mature male gametophyte is thus only three cells.

### The female side — ovule to embryo sac

The ovule hangs in the ovary on its stalk, the **funicle**; its body of
parenchyma, the **nucellus**, is wrapped in one or two **integuments**
leaving a pore, the **micropyle**, at the tip; the base opposite the
micropyle is the **chalaza**. In the commonest form, the **anatropous**
ovule, the body is inverted so the micropyle faces the funicle (others:
orthotropous in *Polygonum*, campylotropous in pea family).

An archesporial cell in the nucellus becomes the **megaspore mother cell
(2n)**, whose **meiosis — megasporogenesis** — gives a linear tetrad of
four megaspores. Typically three (the micropylar ones) degenerate and
**one functional (chalazal) megaspore** remains. Its nucleus divides by
**three successive mitoses — megagametogenesis** — to eight nuclei,
which wall off into the **Polygonum-type embryo sac: 7 cells, 8
nuclei**:

- At the micropylar end, the **egg apparatus**: the **egg cell** flanked
  by two **synergids** bearing the **filiform apparatus** that guides
  the pollen tube.
- In the middle, the large **central cell** with **two polar nuclei**
  (which may fuse into a diploid secondary nucleus before fertilization).
- At the chalazal end, three **antipodal cells**.

::: caution The ploidy map
Everything belonging to the sporophyte — nucellus, integuments, both
mother cells, later the seed coat and fruit wall — is **2n**. Everything
inside the pollen grain and embryo sac is **n** (the central cell carries
two n nuclei, becoming 2n only if they fuse as the secondary nucleus).
The zygote and embryo are **2n** again; the endosperm is **3n**. A
perisperm (next section) is **2n** — it is nucellus, not endosperm.
:::

## 14.3 Pollination and fertilization

**Pollination** is the transfer of pollen from anther to stigma — the
obligatory first step, since the non-motile male gametes can travel only
inside a pollen tube. Within one flower it is **autogamy
(self-pollination)**; between flowers of the same plant **geitonogamy**
(an agent is needed, but genetically it is still selfing); to another
plant of the species, **xenogamy (cross-pollination)**, the only kind
bringing new genes. Selfing is guaranteed by **homogamy** (sexes mature
together), **bud pollination** (pea, wheat) and **cleistogamy** — flowers
that never open (*Commelina*, *Viola*, *Oxalis*), which set seed even
without a single pollinator.

Cross-pollination needs an agent, and flowers advertise accordingly:

| Agent | Name | Syndrome | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wind | anemophily | dull, nectarless; torrents of light dry pollen; feathery stigma | maize, grasses, *Pinus* |
| Water | hydrophily | unwettable, often thread-like pollen | *Vallisneria*, *Hydrilla*, *Zostera* |
| Insects | entomophily | coloured, scented, nectar; sticky spiny pollen | mustard, *Salvia*, fig and its wasp |
| Birds | ornithophily | big red tubes, watery nectar, no scent | *Bombax* (simal), *Butea* |
| Bats | chiropterophily | large, dull, musky, opening at night | *Kigelia*, *Adansonia* |

Plants also evolve **contrivances against selfing**: **dicliny**
(unisexual flowers — monoecious maize, dioecious papaya), **dichogamy**
(**protandry**, anthers first: sunflower, *Salvia*; **protogyny**,
stigma first: *Plantago*, *Michelia*), **herkogamy** (physical barriers,
*Calotropis*), **heterostyly** (pin and thrum styles, *Primula*) and
**self-incompatibility**, a genetic block on own-pollen tubes (tobacco,
crucifers). Continuous selfing gives purity but **inbreeding
depression**; crossing gives variation and hybrid vigour at the price of
wasted pollen and dependence on agents.

**Fertilization.** On a receptive stigma the pollen hydrates and the
intine grows out as the **pollen tube**, tube nucleus at the tip, the two
male gametes behind, digesting through the style towards synergid
signals. It enters the ovule usually by the micropyle (**porogamy**),
sometimes through the chalaza (**chalazogamy**, *Casuarina*) or through
integument/funicle (**mesogamy**, *Cucurbita*), discharges into one
synergid, and the angiosperm signature follows (discovered by
**Nawaschin, 1898**, in *Lilium* and *Fritillaria*):

::: definition Double fertilization
**Syngamy** — one male gamete (n) + egg (n) → **zygote (2n)** → embryo.
**Triple fusion** — the second male gamete (n) + two polar nuclei (n + n)
→ **primary endosperm nucleus (3n)** → endosperm.
:::

The economy is the point: the nutritive endosperm is manufactured
**only when an embryo has actually been formed** — no fertilisation, no
wasted food (contrast the gymnosperms' endosperm-equivalent, the female
gametophyte, made in advance regardless).

::: example Worked example — the meiosis bill for a hundred seeds
**Problem.** A fruit contains 100 seeds. Assuming every pollen grain and
ovule was used, find (a) the minimum number of pollen grains needed,
(b) the number of meiotic divisions in the anthers, (c) the number of
meiotic divisions in the ovules, and (d) the total.

**Solution.** Each seed needs one embryo sac (one egg) and one pollen
tube carrying two male gametes.

(a) One pollen grain per ovule: **100 pollen grains**.

(b) Each pollen mother cell's meiosis gives **four** microspores, so
$100 / 4 =$ **25 meiotic divisions** in the anthers.

(c) Each megaspore mother cell's meiosis gives only **one** functional
megaspore (three degenerate), so 100 ovules cost **100 meiotic
divisions**.

(d) Total $25 + 100 =$ **125 meiotic divisions** — the female side is
four times as expensive per seed as the male.
:::

## 14.4 Monocot and dicot embryos; types and functions of endosperm

The zygote divides transversely into a **basal cell** (towards the
micropyle) and an **apical cell**. In the model dicot ***Capsella
bursa-pastoris*** the basal cell builds a filamentous **suspensor** that
thrusts the embryo into the nutritive endosperm (and often absorbs food
itself), while the apical cell builds the embryo proper: **globular
stage** → **heart-shaped stage** as the two cotyledons bulge out →
mature embryo with **two lateral cotyledons**, a **terminal plumule**
between them, hypocotyl and radicle.

The **monocot embryo** bears a **single terminal-looking cotyledon, the
shield-shaped scutellum**, pressed against the endosperm to absorb its
contents; the plumule sits laterally inside its hollow sheath, the
**coleoptile** (the spear that pierces the soil at germination), and the
radicle is capped by the **coleorhiza**; a small flap opposite the
scutellum, the **epiblast**, is often read as the vestige of the second
cotyledon.

| Feature | Dicot embryo | Monocot embryo |
|---|---|---|
| Cotyledons | two, lateral | one — the scutellum |
| Plumule | terminal, exposed | lateral, inside coleoptile |
| Radicle | exposed | inside coleorhiza |
| Epiblast | absent | present (grasses) |

The **primary endosperm nucleus (3n)** meanwhile divides to feed the
embryo, in one of three patterns:

- **Nuclear endosperm** — free nuclear divisions first (a multinucleate
  soup), walls formed later from the outside in, or never: the commonest
  type (cereals, *Capsella*). **Coconut "water" is free-nuclear
  endosperm**; the white kernel is its later cellular part.
- **Cellular endosperm** — a wall after every division from the start
  (*Datura*, *Petunia*).
- **Helobial endosperm** — intermediate: the first division walls off
  two unequal chambers, the larger then going free-nuclear (order
  Helobiales).

Seeds that keep the endosperm at maturity are **endospermic
(albuminous)** — cereals (whose outermost endosperm layer is the
protein-rich **aleurone** met in Unit 13), castor, coconut. In
**non-endospermic (exalbuminous)** seeds — pea, bean, gram — the
endosperm is consumed during development and the **cotyledons** become
the store. Where the nucellus itself persists and stores food, it is a
**perisperm** (black pepper, beet, cardamom) — diploid maternal tissue,
**not** endosperm at all. The endosperm's functions: nourish the embryo
in the seed and (in albuminous seeds) through germination, regulate
embryo growth hormonally, and stand as the triploid arena in which so
much of our food — wheat flour, rice, maize — actually resides.

::: caution
Four pairs the paper swaps: **geitonogamy looks like cross-pollination but
is genetically self** (same plant's genotype); **the coconut's liquid is
nuclear endosperm, not cell sap**; **coleoptile sheathes the plumule,
coleorhiza the radicle** — both monocot-only; and **endosperm is 3n while
the embryo beside it is 2n**, because triple fusion and syngamy used
different partners in the same sac.
:::

::: example Endosperm ploidy when parent ploidies differ
**Problem.** A tetraploid female plant ($4n = 48$) is pollinated by a diploid male plant ($2n = 24$). Find the chromosome number of the zygote and the primary endosperm nucleus (PEN).

**Solution.** The tetraploid female produces a diploid egg ($2n = 24$) and two diploid polar nuclei ($2n + 2n = 4n = 48$). The diploid male produces haploid male gametes ($n = 12$). Thus the zygote has $2n + n = 24 + 12 = \mathbf{36}$ chromosomes ($3n$), while the PEN has $4n + n = 48 + 12 = \mathbf{60}$ chromosomes ($5n$).
:::

## Formula and fact sheet

- **Asexual gallery**: rhizome ginger; tuber potato; bulb onion; corm *Colocasia*; runner *Cynodon*; sucker mint; offset *Eichhornia*; leaf buds *Bryophyllum*; apomixis = seed without fertilization (citrus nucellar embryos).
- **Male**: tapetum feeds, endothecium opens; 1 PMC (2n) → meiosis → 4 microspores; exine sporopollenin; tube cell + generative cell → 2 gametes.
- **Female**: 1 MMC (2n) → meiosis → 4 megaspores, 1 functional → 3 mitoses → 7 cells / 8 nuclei (Polygonum type); anatropous ovule commonest.
- **Pollination words**: autogamy/geitonogamy/xenogamy; agents anemo-, hydro-, entomo-, ornitho-, chiropterophily; protandry sunflower, protogyny *Plantago*; cleistogamy *Viola*.
- **Double fertilization** (Nawaschin 1898): syngamy n+n=2n embryo; triple fusion n+n+n=3n endosperm; entry porogamy (usual), chalazogamy *Casuarina*, mesogamy *Cucurbita*.
- **Embryos**: dicot — suspensor + 2 cotyledons + terminal plumule (*Capsella*); monocot — scutellum, coleoptile, coleorhiza, epiblast.
- **Endosperm 3n**: nuclear (coconut water), cellular (*Datura*), helobial; albuminous (cereals, castor) vs exalbuminous (pea, bean); perisperm 2n = nucellus (black pepper).
- **Arithmetic**: 1 seed = 1 ovule + 1 pollen grain; pollen meioses = seeds/4; ovule meioses = seeds; ploidy: sporophyte parts 2n, gametophyte parts n, embryo 2n, endosperm 3n.

## Chapter summary

- Plants clone themselves by rhizomes, tubers, bulbs, runners, leaf buds and even apomictic seed — fast and faithful, but variation-free; growers exploit the same tricks by cutting, layering and grafting.
- The anther's tapetum feeds microspores produced four-at-a-time by PMC meiosis; each becomes a sporopollenin-armoured pollen grain carrying a tube cell and a generative cell (source of the two gametes).
- The ovule's single functional megaspore builds, by three mitoses, the 7-celled, 8-nucleate Polygonum embryo sac — egg apparatus, central cell with two polar nuclei, three antipodals.
- Pollination spans autogamy to xenogamy on wind, water, insects, birds and bats, with cleistogamy guaranteeing selfing and dicliny, dichogamy, herkogamy, heterostyly and self-incompatibility enforcing crossing.
- Double fertilization — syngamy making the 2n zygote, triple fusion the 3n endosperm — is unique to angiosperms and economical: food tissue is made only when an embryo exists.
- The dicot embryo (suspensor, two cotyledons, terminal plumule) contrasts with the monocot's scutellum, coleoptile and coleorhiza.
- Endosperm develops by nuclear, cellular or helobial patterns and may persist (albuminous seeds, with aleurone in cereals) or be absorbed into the cotyledons (exalbuminous); perisperm is persistent 2n nucellus, not endosperm.

   (a) egg and polar nuclei (b) three antipodals (c) central cell and egg (d) one egg cell and two synergids

::: note Answers and explanations — Set C

**8.** (c) — Antipodals are gametophyte cells, haploid: $18 / 2 = 9$ chromosomes.
