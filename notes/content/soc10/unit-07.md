History is the story of how we came to be where we are. This unit covers two stories that are tied together. The first is the story of Nepal's struggle for democracy over the last seventy-five years, from the end of 104 years of Rana rule in 2007 BS (1951 AD) to the Constitution of Nepal 2072 and the federal republic of today. The second is the story of the two World Wars, the greatest conflicts in human history, in which thousands of Nepali soldiers fought and which reshaped the world in which Nepal now lives. The unit ends with a practical skill: how to identify and protect the historical places of your own locality.

::: key What the SEE paper expects from this unit
Questions ask for **causes and results** ("state any four causes of the First World War"), **who, when and where** ("name the leaders of the 2007 revolution"), **short notes** on events (Delhi Agreement, Jana Andolan 2046, Treaty of Versailles) and, in the 4-mark group, a structured explanation. Remember dates in both **BS and AD**, and learn the chronological order of events.
:::

### A short background

Modern Nepal was built by King **Prithvi Narayan Shah** of Gorkha, who captured Kathmandu in 1768 AD (1825 BS) and began the unification of Nepal. After the Anglo-Nepal War the **Treaty of Sugauli** (1816 AD) fixed Nepal's borders, and in 1846 AD (1903 BS) the **Kot Massacre** brought **Jung Bahadur Rana** to power. From then the Prime Minister's post became hereditary and the Shah kings became ceremonial. The Rana family ruled for **104 years**, from 1846 to 1951. That is where our story begins.

## 7.1 The revolution of 2007 BS and the Delhi Agreement

### Rana rule and why people rebelled

Under the Ranas, power belonged to a family. The "Shree Teen" Maharaja was both prime minister and the real ruler; the king was kept in the palace as a figurehead. By the 1940s the people had many grievances:

- No political rights: political parties, newspapers and public meetings were banned.
- Education was refused to ordinary people; only the Rana family's own schools were open (Durbar School), and there was no university.
- The Ranas lived in luxury while the tax burden fell on the poor; the state revenue was treated as the Rana family's property.
- Forced labour (*jhara*) and arbitrary punishments, and the **ban on foreign travel and trade** kept the country poor and isolated.
- Nepalis in India, who saw the Indian freedom struggle, brought home new ideas. After 1947 India was free and the British no longer protected the Ranas.

### Early movements

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1936 AD (1993 BS) | **Praja Parishad** founded (Tanka Prasad Acharya, Dharma Bhakta Mathema and others), the first political party; it worked secretly |
| 1941 AD (1997 BS) | Four martyrs executed by the Ranas: **Dharma Bhakta Mathema, Shukra Raj Shastri, Dasharath Chand, Ganga Lal Shrestha** |
| 1946 AD (2003 BS) | **Nepal National Congress** formed in India; Subarna Shamsher, **B. P. Koirala** and others |
| 1947 AD (2004 BS) | The Biratnagar jute-mill workers' strike led by B. P. Koirala and Girija Prasad Koirala |
| 1948 AD (2005 BS) | **Padma Shamsher** announced a limited constitution (Government of Nepal Act, 2004 BS), but it was never really put into practice; he resigned. **Mohan Shamsher** became prime minister |
| 1950 AD (2006 BS) | **Nepali Congress** formed by uniting the National Congress and the Nepal Democratic Congress; communists organised separately in the Nepal Communist Party (1949) |

### The revolution

The leaders of Nepali Congress decided to launch an armed struggle. King **Tribhuvan**, who was also unhappy with the Ranas, supported the plan. On **6 November 1950 (Kartik 21, 2007 BS)** King Tribhuvan, with his family, left the palace on a "hunting trip" and took shelter in the **Indian Embassy**, and on **11 November** the Congress's *Mukti Sena* (liberation army) attacked Rana posts at Birgunj, Biratnagar and elsewhere. Heavy fighting in the west (Palpa and Pokhara) and east gave the revolution the upper hand. The King flew to Delhi, which then became the centre of negotiations.

```photo id="soc10/tribhuvan" caption="King Tribhuvan Bir Bikram Shah (1906-1955). His escape to the Indian Embassy in November 1950 and his return to Kathmandu in February 1951 ended 104 years of Rana rule; he is honoured as the 'Father of the Nation' of the democratic era." width="56"
```

### The Delhi Agreement

Mediated by the government of India, a three-way agreement was reached in Delhi among the **Rana government, the Nepali Congress and King Tribhuvan**, known as the **Delhi Agreement (Delhi Compromise)**. It was signed in early **Falgun 2007 BS (February 1951)**, and the King returned to Kathmandu on **7 Falgun 2007 BS (18 February 1951)**. Its main provisions were:

1. The Rana autocracy would end; the King's authority would be restored.
2. A new **interim government**, headed by **Mohan Shamsher** and containing five representatives each of the Ranas and the Nepali Congress, would be formed (B. P. Koirala was Home Minister).
3. A **Constituent Assembly** would be elected to draft the constitution of the country.
4. Nepali Congress soldiers would lay down arms and be absorbed.

::: definition Democracy Day (Prajatantra Diwas)
Nepalis celebrate **7 Falgun** every year as Democracy Day, because on that day in 2007 BS the Rana regime ended and King Tribhuvan returned with a new political order.
:::

| Result of the revolution of 2007 BS | |
|---|---|
| Political | End of the 104-year Rana rule; multi-party politics begins; the King becomes the head of state |
| Social | Education, newspapers, civil rights; political parties are legal |
| Foreign relations | Nepal opens to the world; the Nepal–India Treaty of Peace and Friendship (July 1950) and membership of the UN later (1955) |

## 7.2 A decade of democracy (2007–2017 BS)

The first decade after the revolution was full of hope but also unstable. The cabinets changed frequently and the promised Constituent Assembly was repeatedly postponed.

*(Several dates vary by a few months in sources; learn the year and the order.)*

### The B. P. Koirala government (2016–2017 BS)

After the first general election of **February–March 1959 (Falgun 2015 to Chaitra 2015 BS)**, in which the Nepali Congress won a clear majority (74 of 109 seats), **Bishweshwar Prasad (B. P.) Koirala** became the first elected Prime Minister of Nepal. In about 18 months his government:

- abolished the **birta** system (land granted free by the state to nobles) and the *Jagirdari* system, moving towards land reform;
- began to reform the administration and the **budget** and promoted education and **Tribhuvan University**;
- tried to reduce corruption and introduced the idea of a "democratic socialism".

### The royal coup of 2017 BS

On **15 December 1960 (1 Poush 2017 BS)**, King **Mahendra** dismissed the elected government, dissolved Parliament, arrested Prime Minister B. P. Koirala and many ministers, and banned political parties. The king said that democracy was "unsuited" to Nepal and that parties were a threat to national unity and security. The coup ended the first democratic experiment after only about 18 months in power.

```photo id="soc10/mahendra" caption="King Mahendra Bir Bikram Shah (reigned 1955-1972). On 1 Poush 2017 BS (15 December 1960) he removed the elected government and later introduced the party-less Panchayat system." width="56"
```

::: caution Democracy Day and the coup
Do not confuse **7 Falgun 2007** (Democracy Day: the end of Rana rule) with **1 Poush 2017** (Mahendra's takeover that ended the first elected government). They are exactly ten years apart but have opposite meaning.
:::

## 7.3 Political events from 2017 to 2046 BS

### The Panchayat system

After the coup King Mahendra set up the **Panchayat system** in 2019 BS (1962 AD). It was a **party-less** system presented as guided democracy rooted in the village. The **Constitution of Nepal 2019 BS** (1962) concentrated power in the king:

- sovereignty and the executive authority were with the King;
- a four-tier council (village, town, district, zone) with a **Rastriya Panchayat** as the national legislature;
- class organisations (peasants, women, youth, elders) were created in place of parties;
- political parties remained banned, and rights such as speech and assembly were curtailed.

### Changes within the Panchayat period

| Year (BS) | Event |
|---|---|
| 2020 | **New Muluki Ain** (civil code) dropped the legal caste hierarchy; **Land Reform Act 2021** set land ceilings |
| 2028 (1971) | King Mahendra dies; **Birendra** becomes king (crowned 2031 BS); Jhapa movement begins in the east |
| 2031 (1975) | King Birendra proposes Nepal as a **Zone of Peace** |
| 2033 (1976) | B. P. Koirala returns from exile and is jailed; he is later released and the opposition grows |
| 2036 (1979) | Student movement begins in Kathmandu; King Birendra announces a **referendum** |
| 2037 Baisakh 20 (2 May 1980) | **Referendum**: Panchayat system (54.8%) narrowly beats multi-party system (45.2%) |
| 2037 | Third amendment: direct election to the Rastriya Panchayat; a "reformed" Panchayat |
| 2042 (1985) | Nepali Congress's civil disobedience (*Satyagraha*) campaign |

### Why people turned against the Panchayat

Corruption, lack of freedom, economic troubles (the **1989 Indian trade and transit embargo**), the influence of the royal palace on every body of government, the world-wide collapse of one-party systems (Berlin Wall, 1989) and a strong underground network of the Nepali Congress and the left parties led to a countrywide movement for democracy in 2046 BS.

### Achievements of the Panchayat era

Honest accounts mention both sides. In this period the road network grew (Mahendra Highway, Prithvi Highway, Tribhuvan Highway), schools and Tribhuvan University expanded, the new civil code of 2020 BS removed legal caste discrimination, and electrification and national planning started. But political freedom was lost.

## 7.4 Political events from 2046 to 2063 BS

### The People's Movement of 2046 BS

In **Falgun 2046 (February 1990)** the **Nepali Congress** and the **United Left Front** (seven communist groups) jointly started the **Jana Andolan I (People's Movement)** to restore democracy. The movement began on **7 Falgun 2046 (18 February 1990)**, which was Democracy Day. Mass demonstrations, strikes and *bandhs* spread to Kathmandu, Patan, Bhaktapur, Janakpur, Biratnagar, Pokhara, and many towns. The government used force and many people were killed, but the movement did not stop. On **26 Chaitra 2046 (8 April 1990)** King Birendra lifted the ban on political parties and dissolved the Panchayat system.

```photo id="soc10/janaandolan-1990" caption="A mass gathering at Khula Manch (Tundikhel), Kathmandu, in April 1990, during the People's Movement. The movement was led jointly by the Nepali Congress and the United Left Front; it restored multi-party democracy after 30 years of the Panchayat system." width="80"
```

- An **interim government** was formed under **Krishna Prasad Bhattarai** (Nepali Congress) with ministers from the left front, the king's nominees and human rights leaders.
- A Constitution Recommendation Commission, chaired by **Bishwanath Upadhyaya**, drafted a new constitution.

### The Constitution of the Kingdom of Nepal 2047

Promulgated on **23 Kartik 2047 (9 November 1990)**, it established:

| Feature | Provision |
|---|---|
| System | Constitutional monarchy and **multi-party parliamentary democracy** |
| Sovereignty | Vested in the **Nepali people** (not the king) |
| Parliament | Two houses: House of Representatives (205) and National Council (60) |
| Rights | Fundamental rights: freedom of speech, assembly, press, religion |
| Other | Independent judiciary, Election Commission, Public Service Commission |

### Democratic experience, 2048–2058 BS

| Year (BS / AD) | Event |
|---|---|
| 2048 / 1991 | First election: Nepali Congress wins a majority (110 of 205 seats); **Girija Prasad Koirala** is Prime Minister |
| 2051 / 1994 | Mid-term election; **CPN (UML)**, the largest party, forms a minority government under **Man Mohan Adhikari** |
| 2052 / 1996 | The **CPN (Maoist)** launches the "People's War" (13 February 1996, 1 Falgun) |
| 2056 / 1999 | Third general election |
| 2058 / 2001 | **Royal massacre** of 1 June 2001 (19 Jestha 2058); **Gyanendra** becomes king |

The Maoist conflict (**1996–2006 AD**) brought about **17,000** deaths, thousands of disappearances and displaced families, and badly damaged schools and local infrastructure. It also placed the questions of inclusion, a republic and a new constitution at the centre of politics. Political parties repeatedly failed to hold local elections in this time, and governments changed frequently.

### Royal takeover and the second People's Movement (2062/63 BS)

On **4 October 2002 (Asoj 2059)** King Gyanendra dismissed Prime Minister Sher Bahadur Deuba and then ruled through appointed governments. On **1 February 2005 (19 Magh 2061 BS)** he dismissed the government again, declared a state of emergency, took direct control and imprisoned party leaders.

The **Seven Party Alliance (SPA)** and the **CPN (Maoist)** signed a **12-point understanding** in Delhi on 22 November 2005 (7 Mangsir 2062) to fight the royal regime and move towards a republic.

The **Jana Andolan II** began in **April 2006 (Chaitra 2062)**. For 19 days huge crowds filled the streets of Kathmandu and every district headquarters, and on **24 April 2006 (11 Baisakh 2063)** King Gyanendra announced the restoration of the dissolved House of Representatives, after which sovereignty was returned to the people.

|---|---|

| Jana Andolan II starts | 6 April 2006 (Chaitra 24, 2062) |
| King restores the House | 24 April 2006 (11 Baisakh 2063) |
| Government–Maoist ceasefire, talks | May 2006 |

The **Comprehensive Peace Accord** ended the ten-year conflict. It was signed by Prime Minister Girija Prasad Koirala and Maoist chairman Pushpa Kamal Dahal "Prachanda"; the Maoists' fighters went to cantonments under United Nations supervision, and the Maoists joined the interim parliament.

### The Constitution of Nepal 2072 BS

It was promulgated by President Ram Baran Yadav on **3 Asoj 2072 (20 September 2015)**, after ten days of discussion with a vote of more than 90 per cent of the Constituent Assembly. Its main features:

- Nepal is an independent, indivisible, sovereign, secular, inclusive, democratic, socialism-oriented, federal democratic republican state.
- **Three tiers of government:** federal, provincial and local, with a division of powers.
- **7 provinces** (Koshi, Madhesh, Bagmati, Gandaki, Lumbini, Karnali, Sudurpashchim), **77 districts** and **753 local levels** (6 metropolitan cities, 11 sub-metropolitan cities, 276 municipalities and 460 rural municipalities).
- Federal parliament with the **House of Representatives** (275 members) and **National Assembly** (59 members); provincial assemblies; local assemblies.
- A list of **fundamental rights and duties**, including rights of women, Dalits, children, the elderly, rights to health, education, food and a clean environment.
- A mixed electoral system (first-past-the-post and proportional), with at least one-third of members of parliament to be women.

## 7.6 The First World War: causes and course

### What was the war?

The **First World War (1914–1918)** was a great war fought mainly in Europe, but also in Africa, the Middle East and the seas, between two groups of nations: the **Allied (Entente) Powers** and the **Central Powers**. More than 30 nations took part; about **70 million** soldiers were mobilised and about **9 to 10 million** soldiers died, along with millions of civilians.

### Causes

1. **Militarism.** Great powers piled up armies and navies; the German–British naval race made war look likely.
2. **The system of alliances.** Europe divided into two armed camps: the **Triple Alliance** (Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy) and the **Triple Entente** (Britain, France, Russia). A war between two nations would drag in the others.
3. **Imperialism.** Competition for colonies and markets in Africa and Asia (e.g. Morocco crises) caused jealousy, especially as Germany was a latecomer.
4. **Nationalism.** Slavic peoples in the Balkans wanted freedom from Austria-Hungary and the Ottoman Empire; France wanted back Alsace-Lorraine, lost to Germany in 1871.
5. **The immediate cause**, the **assassination** of Archduke **Franz Ferdinand**, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, and his wife by a Bosnian Serb, **Gavrilo Princip**, in **Sarajevo** on **28 June 1914**.

### Nepal in the First World War

Nepal was independent but friendly to Britain. Prime Minister **Chandra Shamsher** offered the British full support: Nepal sent about **200,000 Gurkha soldiers** (including those who were serving in the British Indian Army and Nepal's own army), who fought in France, Belgium, Mesopotamia, Gallipoli, Palestine and Egypt. More than **20,000 Nepalis were killed or wounded** (some sources give higher figures) and the Gurkhas won many gallantry medals. Nepal also gave money and materials and, after the war, the British recognised Nepal's independence in the **Treaty of Friendship of 1923**, and raised the annual payment.

## 7.7 Results of the First World War

### The peace settlement

After the armistice the victors met in Paris. The **Treaty of Versailles** was signed on **28 June 1919** in the Hall of Mirrors at the Palace of Versailles, France. The main terms were:

- Germany must accept sole responsibility for the war (the "war guilt clause");
- Germany must pay heavy **reparations** (fixed in 1921 at 132 billion gold marks);
- Germany lost territory (Alsace-Lorraine to France, the "Polish corridor", all its colonies);
- its army was limited to 100,000 men, with no air force or submarines.

```photo id="soc10/versailles" caption="The signing of the Treaty of Versailles in the Hall of Mirrors, 28 June 1919. Germany felt the terms were a humiliating 'diktat' (dictated peace), a feeling which later helped Hitler to power." width="86"
```

### Consequences

| Area | Result |
|---|---|
| **Human** | About 9–10 million soldiers killed and some 20 million wounded; millions of civilians died of famine and the 1918 influenza |
| **Political** | The **German, Austro-Hungarian, Russian and Ottoman empires collapsed**; new countries such as Poland, Czechoslovakia and Yugoslavia were born |
| **League of Nations** | Created in 1920, on the idea of US President **Woodrow Wilson's 14 points**, to keep peace; the USA never joined and it was weak |
| **Russia** | The Bolshevik (Communist) revolution of 1917 led to the **Soviet Union** (1922) |
| **Economic** | Debt, inflation, destroyed farms and cities; the USA emerged as the world's creditor |
| **Social** | Women took up jobs and won the right to vote in several countries; the rise of nationalism in the colonies (including India) |
| **Seeds of the next war** | The resentment of Germany and the weakness of the League |

## 7.8 The Second World War and its causes

The **Second World War (1939–1945)** was the most destructive war in history. It was fought between the **Allies** (Britain, France, the Soviet Union, the USA, China and others) and the **Axis** (Germany, Italy and Japan).

### Causes

1. **The unfair Treaty of Versailles.** Germans felt humiliated; revenge became a cause for the rise of Nazism.
2. **The Great Depression (1929).** Mass unemployment in Europe and the USA made extremist leaders popular.
3. **The rise of dictators:** **Adolf Hitler** (Germany, from 1933), **Benito Mussolini** (Italy, from 1922) and the military rulers of **Japan**, who all believed in expansion by force.
4. **The failure of the League of Nations** to stop aggression: Japan's invasion of Manchuria (1931) and Italy's conquest of Abyssinia (Ethiopia, 1935).
5. **Appeasement.** Britain and France tolerated Hitler's moves (the Rhineland 1936, Austria 1938, the **Munich Agreement** over Czechoslovakia, 1938) hoping to avoid war.
6. **The arms race and rival alliances:** the Rome–Berlin–Tokyo Axis (Anti-Comintern Pact) and the German–Soviet non-aggression pact of August 1939.
7. **The immediate cause:** Germany's invasion of **Poland on 1 September 1939**; Britain and France declared war on 3 September.

### Course of the war

- **1939–41:** Germany's *Blitzkrieg* (lightning war) conquers Poland, Denmark, Norway, the Netherlands, Belgium and **France (June 1940)**; Britain stands alone in the **Battle of Britain**.
- **1941:** Germany invades the **Soviet Union** (22 June); Japan attacks the US fleet at **Pearl Harbor** (7 December), and the USA enters the war.
- **1942–43:** turning points: **Stalingrad** (Soviet victory), El Alamein in North Africa and the American naval victory at **Midway**.
- **1944–45:** **D-Day** (6 June 1944) landings in Normandy; Allied forces enter Germany; Hitler dies by suicide (30 April); Germany surrenders (**8 May 1945**).

## 7.9 Results of the Second World War and Nepal's role

### Ending the war in the Pacific

Japan continued to fight. On **6 August 1945** the USA dropped an atomic bomb on **Hiroshima** and on **9 August** on **Nagasaki**; the Soviet Union entered the war against Japan, and Japan announced its surrender on **15 August** and signed on **2 September 1945**.

```photo id="soc10/hiroshima" caption="The mushroom cloud over Hiroshima, 6 August 1945, photographed from the bomber Enola Gay. The first atomic bomb killed about 70,000 people at once and many more later; it showed humanity that war could now end civilisation itself." width="62"
```

### Results

| Area | Result |
|---|---|
| **Human loss** | An estimated **70–85 million** people died, most of them civilians; the **Holocaust**, the Nazi murder of about **6 million Jews** and millions of others |
| **Political** | Germany was divided and occupied; Japan was occupied; the **USA and the Soviet Union** became superpowers and the **Cold War** began |
| **United Nations** | Founded on **24 October 1945** to replace the League of Nations |
| **Colonies** | Weakened Britain and France could not hold their empires: **India and Pakistan (1947)**, then most of Asia and Africa became independent |
| **Justice** | **Nuremberg** and **Tokyo trials** punished war criminals; the **Universal Declaration of Human Rights** (1948) followed |
| **Economic** | Europe rebuilt with the American **Marshall Plan**; the IMF and World Bank were created |
| **Science** | Nuclear weapons; also radar, jet engines, antibiotics and computers |

### Nepal's role

- Nepal, ruled by Prime Minister **Juddha Shamsher**, declared war on Germany at the beginning of the war and placed the Nepal Army and its resources at Britain's side.
- More than **250,000 Gurkha soldiers** served in the British Indian Army and in Nepal's own contingent in Burma, Italy, North Africa, Malaya and elsewhere, and thousands of them were killed or wounded.
- Nepal also supplied money, food, **timber**, ambulances and aeroplanes, and offered refuge to some victims.
- After the war the **Tripartite Agreement (1947)** between Nepal, India and Britain decided that six Gurkha regiments would go to the Indian army and four to the British army.
- The war made Nepal's soldiers world famous, but also showed the cost: families lost sons, and Nepal gained little in return. After the war, ideas of democracy reached Nepal, and the **2007 BS revolution** followed only five years later.

::: example Model answer: short note on the Treaty of Versailles
The Treaty of Versailles was signed on 28 June 1919 between Germany and the Allies at the end of the First World War. Germany accepted war guilt, lost land and colonies, had to pay reparations and its army was limited. It created resentment in Germany and is regarded as one of the causes of the Second World War.
:::

## 7.10 Identifying and protecting historical places of the local area

### What is a historical place?

A **historical place (site)** is a location which has an important link to the past, such as a palace, temple, stupa, fort, old rest house (*pati*), inscription, tomb, battlefield or village built long ago. Together with ancient objects and traditions, these are part of our **cultural heritage**.

### Famous sites of Nepal

| Site | District | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Lumbini** | Rupandehi | Birthplace of Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha); Ashoka Pillar; **World Heritage Site (1997)** |
| **Kathmandu Valley** (7 monument zones) | Kathmandu, Lalitpur, Bhaktapur | Durbar squares of Kathmandu, Patan and Bhaktapur, Swayambhunath, Boudhanath, Pashupatinath, Changu Narayan; **World Heritage Site (1979)** |
| **Sagarmatha and Chitwan National Parks** | Solukhumbu, Chitwan | Natural World Heritage Sites (1979, 1984) |
| **Janakpur** | Dhanusha | Janaki Mandir; associated with Sita and the Ramayana |
| **Gorkha Durbar** | Gorkha | Palace of Prithvi Narayan Shah, unification of Nepal |
| **Nuwakot, Palpa, Lo Manthang** | Hill and mountain | Ancient palaces and forts, medieval towns, the walled city of Mustang |
| **Ramgram, Kapilvastu** | Nawalparasi, Kapilvastu | Buddhist stupa and ruins of the Shakya capital |

```photo id="soc10/pokhara-annapurna" caption="The Annapurna range seen across Phewa Lake, Pokhara. Mountains, lakes and temples such as Tal Barahi on the lake are all part of the heritage that a locality should record and protect." width="86"
```

### How to identify the historical places of your locality

1. **Ask** the elders, teachers and local historians; collect stories and legends.
2. **Visit** the site and look for old buildings, inscriptions, sculptures, coins, tools, monuments and old trees or ponds.
3. **Read** local documents, the municipality's profile, old maps, newspapers and books.
4. **Record** the name, location, age, builder, materials, current condition and importance; take photographs; sketch a map.
5. **Report** the results to the ward or municipality and to the **Department of Archaeology**.

### Why protect them? How?

Heritage links the generations, builds identity, teaches history and earns income from tourism, but old buildings are fragile and non-renewable. Threats include earthquakes, weathering, encroachment, theft of statues, pollution, uncontrolled construction and neglect.

| Who | Protection |
|---|---|
| **State** | Laws such as the **Ancient Monument Preservation Act, 2013 BS**; Department of Archaeology; listing of monuments; budget |
| **Local government** | Municipal heritage plans, building controls near monuments, community museums |
| **Community** | User and guthi committees (Newar *guthi* tradition), clubs that clean, guard and use the site; festivals that keep the tradition alive |
| **Schools** | Heritage clubs, field visits, student surveys, exhibitions and awareness |
| **International** | UNESCO and development partners help with restoration after the 2015 earthquake |

::: key Conservation principle
Restoring a monument must use the **original design and traditional materials** (brick, timber, lime) and skilled craftsmen; a "new look" would destroy its historical value.
:::

## Chapter summary

- The **revolution of 2007 BS**, led by the Nepali Congress with King Tribhuvan, ended **104 years of Rana rule**; the **Delhi Agreement** (Falgun 2007) set up a Rana–Congress interim cabinet and promised a Constituent Assembly. Democracy Day: 7 Falgun.
- The first decade (2007–2017) saw frequent changes of government, the first general election (1959; NC won 74 of 109 seats; **B. P. Koirala** PM), then **King Mahendra's coup of 1 Poush 2017**.
- The **Panchayat system** (2019–2046) banned parties; a 1980 referendum narrowly kept it (54.8%).
- **Jana Andolan I** (7 Falgun – 26 Chaitra 2046) restored multi-party democracy; the **Constitution of 2047** made Nepal a constitutional monarchy; Maoist conflict **1996–2006**; royal takeover (2005); **Jana Andolan II** (April 2006); **Peace Accord** (21 Nov 2006); **Interim Constitution** (Jan 2007).
- The CA-I declared the **republic** (28 May 2008); CA-II produced the **Constitution of Nepal 2072** (20 September 2015): 7 provinces, 77 districts, **753 local levels**.
- **First World War (1914–18):** militarism, alliances, imperialism, nationalism; spark: Sarajevo, 28 June 1914. Results: Treaty of Versailles (1919), League of Nations, empires collapsed.
- **Second World War (1939–45):** Versailles, Depression, dictators, appeasement, invasion of Poland. Results: 70–85 million dead, UN (1945), superpowers, decolonisation.
- Nepal's Gurkhas served in both wars (about 200,000 and 250,000); Nepal recognised by Britain in 1923.
- Historical places are identified by asking, visiting, reading and recording, and protected by law, community and schools.
