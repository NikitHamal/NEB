Groups B and C of the Grade 11 Chemistry paper carry sixty-four of the seventy-five marks: eight questions of five marks and three of eight. This chapter gives worked practice for both, arranged by the four areas of the CDC syllabus — General and Physical, Inorganic, Organic, and Applied Chemistry. Every model answer is written the way you should write it in the hall: balanced equations with conditions over the arrow, numericals with the formula first, and structures named in full.

::: key How to use this chapter
- **Attempt first.** Cover the answer, write your own against the clock (12 minutes per 5-marker, 19 per 8-marker), then mark yourself.
- **Count your points.** A 5-mark theory answer wants about five markable statements; an 8-mark answer wants a structure: definition, equations or diagram, body, example or numerical.
- **Balance and label.** An unbalanced equation or a missing condition (catalyst, temperature, solvent) usually costs the mark.
- **Atomic masses used:** H $= 1$, C $= 12$, N $= 14$, O $= 16$, Na $= 23$, Cl $= 35.5$, Ca $= 40$, Cu $= 63.5$.
:::

## 6.1 General and Physical Chemistry (units 1–8, 48 hours)

**Group B — Short answer (5 marks each)**

1. A compound contains $40.0\ \%$ carbon, $6.7\ \%$ hydrogen and $53.3\ \%$ oxygen, and its molar mass is $180\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$. Find its empirical and molecular formulae. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
2. $6\ \text{g}$ of hydrogen and $56\ \text{g}$ of nitrogen are allowed to react to form ammonia. Identify the limiting reactant and find the mass of ammonia formed and of the reactant left over. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
3. On heating $25\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CaCO}_3$, $10\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CaO}$ is obtained. Find the theoretical and percentage yield. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
4. State Bohr's postulates. Calculate the wavelength of the photon emitted when the electron of a hydrogen atom falls from $n = 3$ to $n = 2$ ($E_n = -13.6/n^{2}\ \text{eV}$; $hc = 1240\ \text{eV nm}$). <span class="marks">[5]</span>
5. Write the four quantum numbers of the last electron of chlorine ($Z = 17$) and state why $n = 2$, $l = 2$ is not allowed. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
6. Explain why the first ionization energy of nitrogen is higher than that of oxygen, and why that of magnesium is higher than that of aluminium. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
7. Using VSEPR theory, predict and explain the shapes of $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, and account for their bond angles. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
8. Balance, by the oxidation number method, $\text{MnO}_4^{-} + \text{Fe}^{2+} + \text{H}^{+} \to \text{Mn}^{2+} + \text{Fe}^{3+} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
9. State Faraday's laws of electrolysis. Find the mass of copper deposited by a current of $2\ \text{A}$ in $30$ minutes from $\text{CuSO}_4$ solution ($1\ \text{F} = 96\,500\ \text{C}$). <span class="marks">[5]</span>
10. A gas occupies $500\ \text{cm}^{3}$ at $27^{\circ}\text{C}$ and $1\ \text{atm}$. Find its volume at STP, and state the law used. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
11. In the reaction $\text{N}_2\text{O}_4 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NO}_2$ at equilibrium, $[\text{N}_2\text{O}_4] = 0.04\ \text{mol L}^{-1}$ and $[\text{NO}_2] = 0.12\ \text{mol L}^{-1}$. Find $K_c$ and $K_p$ at $300\ \text{K}$ ($R = 0.0821\ \text{L atm mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$). <span class="marks">[5]</span>

::: note Answers — Group B, General and Physical
**1.** Moles in $100\ \text{g}$: C $= 40/12 = 3.33$; H $= 6.7/1 = 6.7$; O $= 53.3/16 = 3.33$. Divide by $3.33$: $1 : 2 : 1$. Empirical formula $\text{CH}_2\text{O}$ (mass $30$). $n = 180/30 = 6$. Molecular formula $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$.
**2.** $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \to 2\text{NH}_3$. Moles: $\text{N}_2 = 56/28 = 2$; $\text{H}_2 = 6/2 = 3$. Two moles of $\text{N}_2$ would need $6$ mol $\text{H}_2$, so **hydrogen is limiting**. $3$ mol $\text{H}_2$ gives $2$ mol $\text{NH}_3 = 34\ \text{g}$. $\text{N}_2$ used $= 1$ mol, left $= 1$ mol $= 28\ \text{g}$.
**3.** $\text{CaCO}_3 \to \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2$; $25\ \text{g} = 0.25$ mol, so theoretical $\text{CaO} = 0.25\times56 = 14\ \text{g}$. Percentage yield $= \dfrac{10}{14}\times100 = 71.4\ \%$.
**4.** Postulates: (i) electrons revolve in fixed circular orbits of definite energy without radiating; (ii) the angular momentum is quantized, $mvr = \dfrac{nh}{2\pi}$; (iii) energy is absorbed or emitted only when an electron jumps between orbits, $\Delta E = h\nu$. $\Delta E = 13.6\left(\dfrac{1}{4} - \dfrac{1}{9}\right) = 13.6\times\dfrac{5}{36} = 1.89\ \text{eV}$. $\lambda = \dfrac{1240}{1.89} = 656\ \text{nm}$ (the red $\text{H}_\alpha$ line of the Balmer series).
**5.** Cl: $1s^{2}2s^{2}2p^{6}3s^{2}3p^{5}$; the last electron is in $3p$: $n = 3$, $l = 1$. Filling the three $p$ orbitals ($m_l = -1, 0, +1$) with $\uparrow$ first and then pairing, the fifth electron has $m_l = 0$ and $m_s = -\tfrac{1}{2}$. For $n = 2$, $l$ may be only $0$ or $1$ ($l = 0$ to $n - 1$), so $l = 2$ is not allowed.
**6.** N: $2p^{3}$ is half-filled and extra stable; O: $2p^{4}$ has a paired electron, and pair repulsion makes it easier to remove, so $\text{IE}_{N} > \text{IE}_{O}$. Mg: $3s^{2}$ is a full subshell; Al: the outer electron is in the higher-energy, better shielded $3p$, so $\text{IE}_{Mg} > \text{IE}_{Al}$.
**7.** Electron pairs around the central atom repel and arrange as far apart as possible. $\text{NH}_3$: four pairs (three bond, one lone), tetrahedral arrangement, trigonal pyramidal shape, angle $107^{\circ}$. $\text{H}_2\text{O}$: four pairs (two bond, two lone), tetrahedral arrangement, bent shape, angle $104.5^{\circ}$. Repulsion order: lone–lone $>$ lone–bond $>$ bond–bond, so lone pairs squeeze the angle below $109.5^{\circ}$ (more lone pairs, smaller angle).
**8.** Oxidation numbers: Mn $+7 \to +2$ (gain of $5e^{-}$); Fe $+2 \to +3$ (loss of $1e^{-}$). Multiply Fe by $5$: $\text{MnO}_4^{-} + 5\text{Fe}^{2+} \to \text{Mn}^{2+} + 5\text{Fe}^{3+}$. Balance O with $4\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and H with $8\text{H}^{+}$: $\text{MnO}_4^{-} + 5\text{Fe}^{2+} + 8\text{H}^{+} \to \text{Mn}^{2+} + 5\text{Fe}^{3+} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Check charge: $-1 + 10 + 8 = +17$ and $2 + 15 = +17$ ✓.
**9.** First law: the mass deposited is proportional to the charge passed, $m = ZIt$. Second law: for the same charge, masses deposited are proportional to equivalent masses. $Q = It = 2\times1800 = 3600\ \text{C}$. $\text{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^{-} \to \text{Cu}$: $63.5\ \text{g}$ needs $193\,000\ \text{C}$. $m = \dfrac{63.5\times3600}{193\,000} = 1.18\ \text{g}$.
**10.** Charles' law with Boyle's law (combined gas law): $\dfrac{P_1V_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{P_2V_2}{T_2}$. $V_2 = \dfrac{500\times1\times273}{300\times1} = 455\ \text{cm}^{3}$.
**11.** $K_c = \dfrac{[\text{NO}_2]^{2}}{[\text{N}_2\text{O}_4]} = \dfrac{(0.12)^{2}}{0.04} = 0.36\ \text{mol L}^{-1}$. $\Delta n = 2 - 1 = 1$, so $K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n} = 0.36\times0.0821\times300 = 8.87\ \text{atm}$.
:::

**Group C — Long answer (8 marks each)**

1. (a) State Avogadro's law and deduce the relation between molecular mass and vapour density. [3] (b) Propane burns as $\text{C}_3\text{H}_8 + 5\text{O}_2 \to 3\text{CO}_2 + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$. For $11.2\ \text{L}$ of propane at STP, find the volume of oxygen needed, the volume of $\text{CO}_2$ formed, and the mass of water formed. [5] <span class="marks">[8]</span>
2. Describe the shapes of $\text{BeF}_2$, $\text{BF}_3$, $\text{CH}_4$, $\text{PCl}_5$ and $\text{SF}_6$ using VSEPR theory and the hybridization of the central atom. <span class="marks">[8]</span>
3. (a) Derive the ideal gas equation from Boyle's, Charles' and Avogadro's laws. [4] (b) Find the pressure exerted by $4.4\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CO}_2$ in a $10\ \text{L}$ vessel at $27^{\circ}\text{C}$. [2] (c) Why do real gases deviate from ideal behaviour? [2] <span class="marks">[8]</span>
4. (a) State the law of mass action. [1] (b) Derive the relation $K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}$. [4] (c) State the effect of pressure, temperature and a catalyst on $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$ ($\Delta H < 0$). [3] <span class="marks">[8]</span>

::: note Answers — Group C, General and Physical
**1. (a)** Equal volumes of all gases under the same conditions of temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules. Vapour density is the ratio of the mass of a volume of gas to the mass of the same volume of hydrogen, so $\text{V.D.} = \dfrac{M_{gas}}{M_{\text{H}_2}}$ (equal volumes contain equal molecules). The hydrogen molecule has mass $2$ (two atoms), so $\text{V.D.} = \dfrac{M}{2}$ and $M = 2\times\text{V.D.}$
**(b)** $11.2\ \text{L} = 0.5$ mol propane. Moles of $\text{O}_2 = 5\times0.5 = 2.5$ mol, volume $= 2.5\times22.4 = 56\ \text{L}$. Moles of $\text{CO}_2 = 3\times0.5 = 1.5$ mol, volume $= 33.6\ \text{L}$. Moles of $\text{H}_2\text{O} = 4\times0.5 = 2.0$ mol, mass $= 2\times18 = 36\ \text{g}$.
**2.** The central atom's electron pairs spread out as far as possible; hybridization is the mixing of orbitals to describe the geometry.

| Molecule | Pairs on central atom | Hybridization | Shape | Angle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\text{BeF}_2$ | 2 bond | $sp$ | linear | $180^{\circ}$ |
| $\text{BF}_3$ | 3 bond | $sp^{2}$ | trigonal planar | $120^{\circ}$ |
| $\text{CH}_4$ | 4 bond | $sp^{3}$ | tetrahedral | $109.5^{\circ}$ |
| $\text{PCl}_5$ | 5 bond | $sp^{3}d$ | trigonal bipyramidal | $90^{\circ}$ and $120^{\circ}$ |
| $\text{SF}_6$ | 6 bond | $sp^{3}d^{2}$ | octahedral | $90^{\circ}$ |

In $\text{PCl}_5$ the two axial bonds are longer than the three equatorial bonds because each axial bond has three nearest neighbours at $90^{\circ}$ against two for an equatorial bond. All the molecules above have no lone pairs, so the shape follows the pair arrangement.
**3. (a)** Boyle: $V \propto \dfrac{1}{P}$ ($n, T$ constant). Charles: $V \propto T$ ($n, P$ constant). Avogadro: $V \propto n$ ($P, T$ constant). Combining, $V \propto \dfrac{nT}{P}$, so $V = R\dfrac{nT}{P}$, i.e. $PV = nRT$, where $R$ is the universal gas constant ($8.314\ \text{J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$). **(b)** $n = 4.4/44 = 0.1$ mol, $T = 300\ \text{K}$: $P = \dfrac{nRT}{V} = \dfrac{0.1\times0.0821\times300}{10} = 0.246\ \text{atm}$. **(c)** The ideal gas assumes that molecules have no volume and no attraction. At high pressure the molecular volume is not negligible; at low temperature intermolecular attractions lower the pressure. So real gases deviate at high pressure and low temperature.
**4. (a)** The rate of a chemical reaction is directly proportional to the product of the active masses (molar concentrations) of the reactants, each raised to the power of its coefficient. **(b)** For $aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD$: $K_p = \dfrac{p_C^{c}p_D^{d}}{p_A^{a}p_B^{b}}$ and $K_c = \dfrac{[C]^{c}[D]^{d}}{[A]^{a}[B]^{b}}$. For a gas $p = \dfrac{n}{V}RT = [\,\,]RT$, so $p_i = [i]RT$. Substituting: $K_p = \dfrac{[C]^{c}[D]^{d}}{[A]^{a}[B]^{b}}(RT)^{(c + d) - (a + b)} = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}$. **(c)** Pressure: raising it shifts the equilibrium to the right (4 mol of gas become 2). Temperature: raising it shifts it to the left because the forward reaction is exothermic; lowering it favours ammonia but slows the rate. Catalyst: no change in the position or $K$; it only reaches equilibrium faster.
:::

## 6.2 Inorganic Chemistry (units 9–11, 35 hours)

**Group B — Short answer (5 marks each)**

1. Describe the preparation of ozone from oxygen, its structure, and the part played by chlorofluorocarbons in ozone layer depletion. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
2. Describe the laboratory preparation of ammonia, with the equation, the drying agent and the test for the gas. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
3. Write the equations for the reaction of copper with (a) dilute and (b) concentrated nitric acid, and state the role of the acid. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
4. Describe the brown ring test for a nitrate, with equations. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
5. Describe the laboratory preparation of chlorine and show with equations that it is a stronger oxidizing agent than bromine and iodine. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
6. Describe the preparation of hydrogen sulphide in Kipp's apparatus and give three of its reactions. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
7. Distinguish between calcination and roasting with an example and equation of each, and state what slag is. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
8. Explain the trend in the thermal stability of the carbonates of Group 2 elements and the solubility of their sulphates. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
9. Describe the preparation and setting of plaster of Paris and give two uses. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
10. Name the metal ions concerned in nerve impulses, in chlorophyll and in haemoglobin, and name two toxic metals with one effect each. <span class="marks">[5]</span>

::: note Answers — Group B, Inorganic
**1.** *Preparation:* dry oxygen is passed through a silent electric discharge in an ozonizer: $3\text{O}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{O}_3$, $\Delta H = +285\ \text{kJ}$ (about $10$ % conversion). *Structure:* a bent resonance hybrid, $\text{O}-\text{O}$ bond length $128\ \text{pm}$, angle $117^{\circ}$. *Depletion:* UV breaks a CFC ($\text{CF}_2\text{Cl}_2 \to \text{CF}_2\text{Cl}\cdot + \text{Cl}\cdot$); then $\text{Cl}\cdot + \text{O}_3 \to \text{ClO}\cdot + \text{O}_2$ and $\text{ClO}\cdot + \text{O} \to \text{Cl}\cdot + \text{O}_2$. Chlorine is regenerated, so one atom destroys thousands of ozone molecules.
**2.** Heat a mixture of ammonium chloride and slaked lime: $2\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{Ca(OH)}_2 \to \text{CaCl}_2 + 2\text{NH}_3\uparrow + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$. The gas is dried with quicklime (CaO) — not conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ or $\text{CaCl}_2$, which react with it. It is collected by downward displacement of air (lighter than air). Tests: it turns moist red litmus blue; a glass rod dipped in conc. HCl gives dense white fumes of $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$.
**3.** (a) Dilute: $3\text{Cu} + 8\text{HNO}_3 \to 3\text{Cu(NO}_3)_2 + 2\text{NO}\uparrow + 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$. (b) Concentrated: $\text{Cu} + 4\text{HNO}_3 \to \text{Cu(NO}_3)_2 + 2\text{NO}_2\uparrow + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (brown fumes). Nitric acid acts as an **oxidizing agent**: the nitrogen is reduced from $+5$ to $+2$ (dilute) or $+4$ (concentrated), while copper is oxidized from $0$ to $+2$.
**4.** Add freshly prepared $\text{FeSO}_4$ solution to the nitrate solution, then pour conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ slowly down the side of the tube. Nitrate is reduced: $\text{NO}_3^{-} + 3\text{Fe}^{2+} + 4\text{H}^{+} \to \text{NO} + 3\text{Fe}^{3+} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$. The NO combines with excess $\text{Fe}^{2+}$: $[\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O)}_6]^{2+} + \text{NO} \to [\text{Fe(H}_2\text{O)}_5\text{NO}]^{2+} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$. A **brown ring** appears at the junction of the two liquids.
**5.** $\text{MnO}_2 + 4\text{HCl} \to \text{MnCl}_2 + \text{Cl}_2\uparrow + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (warm; the gas is washed with water and dried with conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$, collected by upward displacement). Displacement: $\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{KBr} \to 2\text{KCl} + \text{Br}_2$ (orange); $\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{KI} \to 2\text{KCl} + \text{I}_2$ (violet in $\text{CCl}_4$). Chlorine displaces both, and $\text{Br}_2$ displaces $\text{I}_2$ but not $\text{Cl}^{-}$, so the order of oxidizing power is $\text{Cl}_2 > \text{Br}_2 > \text{I}_2$.
**6.** In Kipp's apparatus iron(II) sulphide reacts with dilute acid: $\text{FeS} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{FeSO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{S}\uparrow$. The gas has a rotten-egg smell. Reactions: (a) with copper sulphate: $\text{CuSO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{S} \to \text{CuS}\downarrow + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ (black); (b) reducing action: $\text{H}_2\text{S} + \text{Cl}_2 \to 2\text{HCl} + \text{S}$; (c) burning: $2\text{H}_2\text{S} + 3\text{O}_2 \to 2\text{SO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
**7.** *Calcination:* heating the ore in limited or no air to remove moisture and volatile matter, e.g. $\text{ZnCO}_3 \to \text{ZnO} + \text{CO}_2$. *Roasting:* heating the ore in excess air, e.g. $2\text{ZnS} + 3\text{O}_2 \to 2\text{ZnO} + 2\text{SO}_2$. *Slag:* the fusible product of the flux and gangue, e.g. $\text{CaO} + \text{SiO}_2 \to \text{CaSiO}_3$, which floats on the molten metal and is removed.
**8.** Carbonates decompose on heating, $\text{MCO}_3 \to \text{MO} + \text{CO}_2$, and the stability **increases down the group** ($\text{BeCO}_3 < \text{MgCO}_3 < \text{CaCO}_3 < \text{SrCO}_3 < \text{BaCO}_3$) because the larger cation polarizes the carbonate ion less. Sulphate solubility **decreases down the group**: $\text{BeSO}_4$ and $\text{MgSO}_4$ dissolve freely, $\text{CaSO}_4$ is sparingly soluble, and $\text{BaSO}_4$ is insoluble, because the lattice energy falls less quickly than the hydration energy.
**9.** Gypsum is heated to about $120^{\circ}\text{C}$: $\text{CaSO}_4\cdot2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{CaSO}_4\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O} + 1\tfrac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O}$. On mixing with water it sets to a hard mass as it re-forms gypsum: $\text{CaSO}_4\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O} + 1\tfrac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{CaSO}_4\cdot2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ with slight expansion. Uses: plaster for fractures, moulds and casts, wall plaster.
**10.** Nerve impulses: $\text{Na}^{+}$ and $\text{K}^{+}$ (sodium–potassium pump). Chlorophyll: $\text{Mg}^{2+}$. Haemoglobin: $\text{Fe}^{2+}$. Toxic metals: mercury (Minamata disease, nerve damage); lead (damage to the brain and kidney, anaemia); arsenic (skin lesions and cancer); cadmium (Itai-itai disease, kidney damage).
:::

**Group C — Long answer (8 marks each)**

1. Describe the extraction of sodium by Down's process, with the reactions at each electrode. <span class="marks">[8]</span>
2. Describe the chemical properties of concentrated sulphuric acid as a dehydrating agent and an oxidizing agent, with equations. <span class="marks">[8]</span>
3. Compare the halogens chlorine, bromine and iodine in physical state, preparation from their salts, and displacement reactions, and describe the tests for chloride, bromide and iodide ions. <span class="marks">[8]</span>

::: note Answers — Group C, Inorganic
**1.** *Raw material:* fused sodium chloride (m.p. $801^{\circ}\text{C}$) mixed with calcium chloride, which lowers the melting point to about $600^{\circ}\text{C}$. *Cell:* a steel container lined with firebrick; a graphite anode at the centre; a circular iron cathode; a nickel gauze diaphragm between them to keep sodium and chlorine apart; an inverted trough above the cathode to collect sodium; a dome over the anode to collect chlorine. *Reactions:* anode: $2\text{Cl}^{-} \to \text{Cl}_2 + 2e^{-}$; cathode: $\text{Na}^{+} + e^{-} \to \text{Na}$. Overall: $2\text{NaCl} \to 2\text{Na} + \text{Cl}_2$. Molten sodium floats on the electrolyte and rises into the collector. *By-product:* chlorine. *Why not aqueous NaCl:* hydrogen would be discharged at the cathode instead of sodium.
**2.** *Dehydrating agent:* it removes the elements of water. Sugar: $\text{C}_{12}\text{H}_{22}\text{O}_{11} \rightarrow\ [\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4] 12\text{C} + 11\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (black carbon). Blue vitriol turns white: $\text{CuSO}_4\cdot5\text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{CuSO}_4 + 5\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Ethanol gives ethene at $170^{\circ}\text{C}$. *Oxidizing agent (hot, concentrated):* $\text{Cu} + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{CuSO}_4 + \text{SO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$; $\text{C} + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{CO}_2 + 2\text{SO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$; $\text{S} + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to 3\text{SO}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Sulphur is reduced from $+6$ to $+4$. *Non-volatile acid:* $\text{NaCl} + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{NaHSO}_4 + \text{HCl}$, used to make hydrogen chloride.
**3.** *State:* $\text{Cl}_2$ greenish-yellow gas; $\text{Br}_2$ red-brown liquid; $\text{I}_2$ violet-black solid (sublimes). *Preparation:* $\text{MnO}_2 + 4\text{HCl} \to \text{MnCl}_2 + \text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$; $2\text{NaBr} + 3\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 + \text{MnO}_2 \to 2\text{NaHSO}_4 + \text{MnSO}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{Br}_2$; similarly iodide with $\text{MnO}_2$ and conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ gives $\text{I}_2$. *Displacement:* $\text{Cl}_2$ displaces $\text{Br}^{-}$ and $\text{I}^{-}$; $\text{Br}_2$ displaces $\text{I}^{-}$ only. *Tests (with $\text{AgNO}_3$ then ammonia):* $\text{Cl}^{-}$ white $\text{AgCl}$, soluble in dilute $\text{NH}_3$; $\text{Br}^{-}$ pale-yellow $\text{AgBr}$, soluble in conc. $\text{NH}_3$; $\text{I}^{-}$ yellow $\text{AgI}$, insoluble in $\text{NH}_3$. Also: free $\text{I}_2$ turns starch blue-black.
:::

## 6.3 Organic Chemistry (units 12–15, 30 hours)

**Group B — Short answer (5 marks each)**

1. Give the IUPAC names of $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}(\text{CH}_3)\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH(OH)CH}_3$, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CHO}$, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}{=}\text{CHCH}_3$ and $\text{CH}_3\text{C}{\equiv}\text{CCH}_3$. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
2. Describe the Lassaigne test for nitrogen, sulphur and chlorine in an organic compound. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
3. Write all the structural isomers of $\text{C}_4\text{H}_{10}\text{O}$ and name the type of isomerism between the alcohols and the ethers. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
4. Explain the inductive effect with examples of $+I$ and $-I$ groups, and use it to compare the acid strengths of acetic acid and chloroacetic acid. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
5. Write the equations for four methods of preparing an alkane (ethane or methane). <span class="marks">[5]</span>
6. State Markovnikov's rule and the peroxide effect, and give the products of the addition of HBr to propene in each case. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
7. Explain the acidic nature of ethyne, with two reactions, and give a test to distinguish ethyne from ethene. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
8. Describe Kekulé's structure of benzene and give the evidence for the resonance hybrid. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
9. Explain the mechanism of the nitration of benzene. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
10. Write the equations for the Friedel–Crafts alkylation and acylation of benzene. <span class="marks">[5]</span>

::: note Answers — Group B, Organic
**1.** 2-methylbutane; butan-2-ol; propanal; but-2-ene; but-2-yne.
**2.** The organic compound is fused with a piece of sodium, the hot tube is plunged into distilled water, boiled and filtered; this is the **sodium extract**, containing $\text{NaCN}$, $\text{Na}_2\text{S}$ and $\text{NaCl}$. *Nitrogen:* boil with $\text{FeSO}_4$, acidify with dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$; a **Prussian blue** colour or precipitate shows N. *Sulphur:* add lead acetate solution; a **black precipitate** of PbS shows S. *Chlorine:* acidify with dilute $\text{HNO}_3$, add $\text{AgNO}_3$; a **curdy white precipitate** of AgCl, soluble in $\text{NH}_3$, shows Cl.
**3.** Alcohols: butan-1-ol, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{OH}$; butan-2-ol, $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH(OH)CH}_3$; 2-methylpropan-1-ol, $(\text{CH}_3)_2\text{CHCH}_2\text{OH}$; 2-methylpropan-2-ol, $(\text{CH}_3)_3\text{COH}$. Ethers: ethoxyethane, $\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OC}_2\text{H}_5$; 1-methoxypropane, $\text{CH}_3\text{OCH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_3$; 2-methoxypropane, $\text{CH}_3\text{OCH(CH}_3)_2$. That is seven isomers. Alcohols and ethers are **functional** isomers; butan-1-ol and butan-2-ol are **position** isomers; butan-1-ol and 2-methylpropan-1-ol are **chain** isomers.
**4.** The inductive effect is the permanent displacement of the $\sigma$ electrons along a chain due to the electronegativity difference between atoms. Alkyl groups ($-\text{CH}_3$, $-\text{C}_2\text{H}_5$) push electrons ($+I$); $-\text{Cl}$, $-\text{NO}_2$ and $-\text{COOH}$ pull electrons ($-I$). In $\text{ClCH}_2\text{COOH}$ the chlorine withdraws electrons and stabilizes the carboxylate ion, so the acid ionizes more easily; in $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ the methyl group pushes electrons and destabilizes the anion. Hence chloroacetic acid is the stronger acid.
**5.** (i) Reduction of a haloalkane: $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Br} + 2[\text{H}] \rightarrow\ [\text{Zn/HCl}] \text{C}_2\text{H}_6 + \text{HBr}$. (ii) Wurtz reaction: $2\text{CH}_3\text{Br} + 2\text{Na} \rightarrow\ [\text{dry ether}] \text{C}_2\text{H}_6 + 2\text{NaBr}$. (iii) Decarboxylation: $\text{CH}_3\text{COONa} + \text{NaOH} \rightarrow\ [\text{CaO},\ \Delta] \text{CH}_4 + \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$. (iv) Hydrogenation: $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{H}_2 \rightarrow\ [\text{Ni},\ 573\ \text{K}] \text{C}_2\text{H}_6$. (v) Kolbe: $2\text{CH}_3\text{COONa} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow\ [\text{electrolysis}] \text{C}_2\text{H}_6 + 2\text{CO}_2 + 2\text{NaOH} + \text{H}_2$.
**6.** *Markovnikov:* when an unsymmetrical reagent HX adds to an unsymmetrical alkene, the hydrogen attaches to the carbon with more hydrogens already. $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}{=}\text{CH}_2 + \text{HBr} \to \text{CH}_3\text{CHBrCH}_3$ (2-bromopropane, via the more stable secondary carbocation). *Peroxide effect:* in the presence of peroxide only HBr adds the opposite way (free-radical mechanism): $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}{=}\text{CH}_2 + \text{HBr} \rightarrow\ [\text{peroxide}] \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{CH}_2\text{Br}$ (1-bromopropane).
**7.** The $sp$ carbon is more electronegative than $sp^{2}$ or $sp^{3}$ carbon ($50$ % $s$ character), so the terminal $\text{C}-\text{H}$ bond is polarized and its hydrogen is weakly acidic. (a) $2\text{HC}{\equiv}\text{CH} + 2\text{Na} \to 2\text{HC}{\equiv}\text{CNa} + \text{H}_2$. (b) $\text{HC}{\equiv}\text{CH} + 2[\text{Ag(NH}_3)_2]^{+}\text{OH}^{-} \to \text{AgC}{\equiv}\text{CAg}\downarrow + 4\text{NH}_3 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (white precipitate). Ethene gives no precipitate with ammoniacal $\text{AgNO}_3$, so this distinguishes ethyne from ethene.
**8.** Kekulé proposed a planar hexagon of six carbon atoms, each bonded to one hydrogen, with alternating single and double bonds, and a rapid shift between two forms. Limitations of the single structure: it predicts two different 1,2-disubstituted isomers (one across a single bond, one across a double bond), but only one is known; and it predicts addition reactions of the double bonds, but benzene undergoes substitution. Evidence for resonance: all six $\text{C}-\text{C}$ bonds are equal ($139\ \text{pm}$, between single $154$ and double $134$); the heat of hydrogenation is about $150\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$ against $360$ expected for three isolated double bonds, so benzene is stabilized by about $150\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$ (resonance energy).
**9.** Step 1: generation of the electrophile: $\text{HNO}_3 + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \rightleftharpoons \text{NO}_2^{+} + \text{H}_3\text{O}^{+} + 2\text{HSO}_4^{-}$. Step 2: attack of the $\pi$ electrons on $\text{NO}_2^{+}$ to form a resonance-stabilized carbocation (arenium ion). Step 3: loss of $\text{H}^{+}$ from the arenium ion restores the aromatic ring: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{HNO}_3 \rightarrow\ [\text{conc. H}_2\text{SO}_4,\ 323\ \text{K}] \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{NO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$.
**10.** Alkylation: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3\text{Cl} \rightarrow\ [\text{anhyd. AlCl}_3] \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CH}_3 + \text{HCl}$ (toluene). Acylation: $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6 + \text{CH}_3\text{COCl} \rightarrow\ [\text{anhyd. AlCl}_3] \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COCH}_3 + \text{HCl}$ (acetophenone). The $\text{AlCl}_3$ generates the electrophile $\text{R}^{+}$ or $\text{RCO}^{+}$.

2. Distinguish between batch and continuous processes, with one example of each. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
3. Distinguish between fixed capital and working capital in a chemical plant. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
4. List five environmental effects of the chemical industry and one control measure for each. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
5. Apply Le Chatelier's principle to choose the conditions for the Haber process. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
6. Write the chemistry of Ostwald's process for nitric acid. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
7. Write the reactions at each electrode in the diaphragm cell for sodium hydroxide, and name the function of the diaphragm. <span class="marks">[5]</span>

::: note Answers — Group B, Applied
**1.** (i) *Research and development:* the reaction is tested in the laboratory (yield, catalyst, conditions). (ii) *Process design and economic evaluation:* flow sheet, raw material and energy balance, cost estimate. (iii) *Pilot plant:* a small-scale unit tests the process, corrosion and safety. (iv) *Full-scale plant:* design, construction, commissioning and operation. (v) *Marketing and monitoring* of product quality and environmental impact.
**2.** *Batch:* the reactor is charged with a fixed amount of reactants, the reaction is completed, and the product is withdrawn before the next charge; suited to small-volume or varied products (dyes, drugs, soap). *Continuous:* feed enters and product leaves without interruption, in steady state; suited to large-volume products (ammonia, sulphuric acid). Continuous plants have lower labour cost per tonne and steadier quality; batch plants are more flexible.
**3.** *Fixed capital:* the money invested in land, buildings, machinery and installation; it is not consumed in production. *Working capital:* the money needed to run the plant from day to day — raw materials, wages, fuel, stocks and credit; it is recovered through sales. Total investment $=$ fixed capital $+$ working capital.
**4.** Air: $\text{SO}_2$ and $\text{NO}_x$ cause acid rain (use scrubbers, desulphurization). Water: untreated effluent poisons rivers (effluent treatment plant). Soil: solid waste and heavy metals (secure landfill, recycling). Climate: $\text{CO}_2$ emission (energy efficiency, capture). Ozone: CFCs (replace with safer coolants). Also: eutrophication from fertilizer run-off (controlled use).
**5.** $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$, $\Delta H = -92\ \text{kJ}$. *Pressure:* four moles of gas become two, so high pressure ($\approx200\ \text{atm}$) favours ammonia. *Temperature:* the reaction is exothermic, so low temperature favours it, but too low a temperature makes it slow; a compromise of $450$–$500^{\circ}\text{C}$ is used. *Catalyst:* finely divided iron with Mo or $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ as a promoter speeds the approach to equilibrium. *Removal of ammonia* by liquefaction pushes the equilibrium forward, and unreacted gases are recycled.
**6.** (i) $4\text{NH}_3 + 5\text{O}_2 \rightarrow\ [\text{Pt–Rh gauze},\ 800^{\circ}\text{C}] 4\text{NO} + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\Delta H = -905\ \text{kJ}$. (ii) NO is cooled and oxidized: $2\text{NO} + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{NO}_2$. (iii) $\text{NO}_2$ is absorbed in water in the presence of excess air: $4\text{NO}_2 + \text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 4\text{HNO}_3$ ($50$–$60\ \%$ acid, which can be concentrated by distillation with conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$).
**7.** Brine is fed into the anode compartment. Anode (titanium or graphite): $2\text{Cl}^{-} \to \text{Cl}_2 + 2e^{-}$. Cathode (steel): $2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 2e^{-} \to \text{H}_2 + 2\text{OH}^{-}$. $\text{Na}^{+}$ passes through the diaphragm to give NaOH solution at the cathode. The porous asbestos diaphragm **separates the two compartments and prevents $\text{Cl}_2$ from reacting with NaOH** (which would give hypochlorite and chlorate) and prevents $\text{H}_2$ and $\text{Cl}_2$ from mixing explosively.

**Group C — Long answer (8 marks each)**

1. Describe the manufacture of ammonia by Haber's process, giving the principle, the conditions with reasons, and a labelled flow sheet. <span class="marks">[8]</span>
2. Describe the manufacture of sodium hydroxide in the diaphragm cell, with a labelled diagram, the electrode reactions and the uses of the products. <span class="marks">[8]</span>

::: note Answers — Group C, Applied
**1.** *Principle:* $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$, $\Delta H = -92\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1}$. *Raw materials:* nitrogen from liquefied air; hydrogen from steam reforming of natural gas ($\text{CH}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{CO} + 3\text{H}_2$) or electrolysis of water. *Conditions:* $200$ atm (high pressure favours the side with fewer moles); $450$–$500^{\circ}\text{C}$ (a compromise between yield and rate); finely divided iron catalyst with Mo or $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ promoter; the poisons (e.g. sulphur compounds, CO) are removed from the gases first. *Flow sheet:* $\text{N}_2 + \text{H}_2$ (purified, $3 : 1$) $\to$ compressor ($200$ atm) $\to$ heat exchanger $\to$ catalytic converter ($450^{\circ}\text{C}$) $\to$ cooler and condenser (ammonia liquefies) $\to$ liquid ammonia storage; unreacted $\text{N}_2$ and $\text{H}_2$ are recycled to the compressor. The yield per pass is about $15$ %, and the overall yield is high because of recycling. *Uses:* fertilizers (urea, ammonium nitrate and sulphate), nitric acid, explosives, refrigerant.
**2.** *Cell:* a rectangular steel tank divided by a porous asbestos diaphragm on a perforated steel cathode; the anode is graphite or titanium. *Process:* saturated purified brine enters the anode compartment; chlorine is liberated at the anode, $2\text{Cl}^{-} \to \text{Cl}_2 + 2e^{-}$; at the cathode, $2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 2e^{-} \to \text{H}_2 + 2\text{OH}^{-}$. The brine seeps through the diaphragm into the cathode compartment, where $\text{Na}^{+}$ and $\text{OH}^{-}$ form NaOH; the liquor contains about $10$–$12$ % NaOH and unchanged NaCl. It is evaporated; NaCl crystallizes out and is recycled, and a $50$ % NaOH solution results. Overall: $2\text{NaCl} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{NaOH} + \text{H}_2 + \text{Cl}_2$. The diaphragm prevents the product mixing: otherwise $\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{NaOH} \to \text{NaCl} + \text{NaOCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$. *Uses:* NaOH — soap, paper, textiles, aluminium purification; $\text{Cl}_2$ — water treatment, bleaching, PVC; $\text{H}_2$ — ammonia, hydrogenation.

**Group C — Long answer (8 marks each)**

1. Describe the preparation and chemical properties of ammonia. <span class="marks">[8]</span>

::: note Answers to Group C
**1.** *Preparation:* laboratory — $2\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{Ca(OH)}_2 \to \text{CaCl}_2 + 2\text{NH}_3 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$, dried with CaO; industrial — Haber process. *Properties:* colourless gas with a pungent smell, very soluble in water (fountain experiment), lighter than air; basic: $\text{NH}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{NH}_4^{+} + \text{OH}^{-}$; $\text{NH}_3 + \text{HCl} \to \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$; reducing: $2\text{NH}_3 + 3\text{CuO} \to 3\text{Cu} + \text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$; burns in oxygen with a yellowish flame, $4\text{NH}_3 + 3\text{O}_2 \to 2\text{N}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (with Pt catalyst, NO is formed); complex formation with $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ and $\text{Ag}^{+}$. Uses: fertilizers, nitric acid, refrigerant, cleaning agent.
**2.** *Rutherford:* from $\alpha$-particle scattering by gold foil — most pass straight through, a few deflect, very few bounce back — he proposed a small, dense, positive nucleus with electrons revolving around it. *Limitations:* (i) a revolving electron should radiate energy continuously and spiral into the nucleus, making the atom unstable; (ii) it should give a continuous spectrum, but atoms give line spectra. *Bohr's remedy:* electrons move only in certain stationary orbits of fixed energy without radiating; angular momentum $mvr = nh/2\pi$; radiation is emitted or absorbed only when an electron jumps between orbits, $\Delta E = h\nu$, which explains the line spectrum of hydrogen. Bohr's model fails for atoms with more than one electron and does not explain the splitting of lines (Zeeman and Stark effects).
:::
