The English paper is the most regular paper a science student sits: eight questions, the same skills in the same order every year. This chapter sets out the grid, gives **two complete model papers** with answers, and ends with a ranked list of **predicted questions**. The prediction rests on one fact only: the grid fixes the question types and the syllabus fixes the texts. Nothing here is a claim about what will appear, and no question is copied from a past paper.

::: key The grid (75 marks, 3 hours)
| Q | Marks | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 15 | unseen passage |
| 2 | 5 x 2 = 10 | short literary questions |
| 3 | 2 x 5 = 10 | long literary questions |
| 4 | 7 | guided writing |
| 5 | 8 | functional writing |
| 6 | 10 | free writing |
| 7 | 10 | grammar, 10 items |
| 8 | 5 | vocabulary, 5 items |
:::

## 19.1 Pattern analysis

| Skill | Marks | Share of 75 | Where marks are lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading (Q1) | 15 | 20% | answers that copy half a sentence, vocabulary-in-context |
| Literature (Q2 + Q3) | 20 | 27% | answers with no text detail; a story told but not explained |
| Writing (Q4 + Q5 + Q6) | 25 | 33% | missing format, no paragraphs, off the topic |

::: note Q3: Long literary answers
**18. (a)** Jonathan Iwegbu is an optimistic, hard-working and resourceful man. After the Nigerian civil war he counts his blessings: five of six heads in his family are safe, his bicycle has survived and he turns it into a taxi. He restores his small house and sets his family to earn money. He is humble and religious, and says "Nothing puzzles God" about everything that happens. When thieves take the twenty pounds, he accepts the loss and starts the next day's work. His practical hope, not self-pity, helps him survive in a broken country.

**Or (b)** Aksionov is imprisoned for twenty-six years for a murder he did not commit. In prison he becomes humble and religious and loses the wish for revenge. When he sees that Makar Semyonitch is the true murderer, he first feels anger, but he does not give him away to the governor. When Makar confesses and begs pardon, Aksionov says that God will forgive them both. Forgiving him gives Aksionov peace of heart, and he no longer wishes to go home. The story shows that forgiveness frees the soul even when justice is late.

**19. (a)** In a satire, the writer mocks human faults to bring change. The speaker, the virus, admits that people hate it, but asks who is the greater killer. It lists human crimes: forests cut, rivers and air poisoned, animals killed and wars that killed millions. It says it makes no difference between rich and poor, or between religions and countries, so it teaches the equality that humans preach but do not practise. While people stayed indoors, the air cleared and animals returned. The accuser has become the accused. The poem ends by asking whether we have learned.

**Or (b)** Haldane, writing in 1940 in a heroic age of war, says that besides food, water, clothes and shelter he requires four things. The first is work that is hard but interesting, with a decent wage, since happiness is unimpeded activity. The second is freedom, especially of speech, which the libel law limits. The third is health, enough to work and enjoy life, not perfect health. The fourth is friendship. He does not ask for peace and security because he does not expect them in his age. The essay is simple, honest and generous.
:::

::: note Q4: Guided writing (model)
**Hard Work Wins**

Suman was a lazy student of Grade 10 who spent his days playing games. In the pre-board examination he failed in three subjects. Ashamed, he went to his old teacher, Mr Sharma, who gave him a kind but firm talk. "Success comes to those who plan and work," said the teacher. "Make a timetable and follow it."

Suman made a timetable of study, rest and exercise and followed it every day. He asked his teacher about problems he could not solve. Slowly his marks rose.

When the final examination results came out, Suman had passed with a good second division, and his parents were proud. He ran to thank his teacher, who smiled and said, "Your hard work did it."

**Moral:** Hard work and planning bring success.

::: note Q6: Essay (model)
**The Impact of Social Media on Students**

Social media, such as Facebook, TikTok and YouTube, has become a part of every student's daily life. Like any tool, it can help or harm.

On the positive side, social media connects students with friends, teachers and experts. Students can watch lessons on YouTube, join study groups and share notes. Young people can also show their talents and learn about the world. During the pandemic, many classes continued through online platforms.

However, there are serious problems. Many students spend several hours scrolling, which leaves less time for study and sleep. Constant notifications break their attention, and results suffer. Some students compare themselves with others and feel anxious or unhappy. There is also the danger of false news, cyberbullying and strangers who misuse personal information.

Students should therefore use social media wisely. They should fix a time limit, switch off notifications while studying, check the source of information before sharing and never share private details. Parents and teachers should guide rather than simply forbid.

In conclusion, social media is a good servant but a bad master. If students use it for learning and keep control of their time, it can be a blessing.
:::

::: note Q3: Long literary answers
**18. (a)** The giant is selfish at first: he builds a wall and a notice to keep the children out of the garden, and winter stays in it while spring, summer and autumn refuse to come. Only when the children creep in through a hole in the wall does spring return. The giant sees how selfish he has been, helps the little boy into the tree and pulls down the wall. Later the same boy takes him to paradise. Wilde teaches that selfishness brings coldness and loneliness, while love and sharing bring happiness.

**Or (b)** Dahl builds suspense by turning a simple game into a matter of life and death. The boy sets rules: black is a snake, red is burning coal and yellow is safe, and the prize is a puppy. The safe patches become fewer and he moves carefully with arms out. A wide black band stands in his way and he must take a huge stride. The tension grows with each step. The last sentence moves to the garden, where his mother is searching for him, and says nothing of what happened, leaving the reader to imagine the worst.

**19. (a)** The poem is spoken by a young widow at the grave of her soldier husband. She offers him roses, a ring and her youth. He, in return, has given her the smell of blood on his uniform, his returned body, medals and loneliness. Both lists are called gifts, which is bitterly ironic, because the war has given only injury. The calm voice and plain language make the horror of war more powerful than shouting. The poem ends with no consolation. It shows the private grief behind public heroism.

**Or (b)** In *Refund* a former pupil asks the school to return his fees because he learned nothing. He plans to fail an examination, and the teachers, afraid of scandal and a queue of other pupils, plan to pass him. His rudeness is marked as manners and his nonsense answers, such as the Thirty Years' War lasting "exactly seven metres", are praised with long theories. Finally the Mathematics Master traps him, saying that his correct sum is the difficult question, and he passes with distinction. The play shows that certificates can be given for nothing and that schools defend their reputation, not learning.

::: note Q6: Memoir (model)
**A Day I Will Never Forget**

I was twelve years old when I first stood on the stage of our school hall. It was the annual day, and I had agreed, without thinking, to recite a poem in front of five hundred people.

That morning I could not eat. My hands were cold and my legs felt like water. Behind the curtain, I heard my name called and my heart beat so loudly that I thought the audience could hear it. My English teacher squeezed my shoulder and whispered, "Look at the last row, not at the faces."

I walked to the microphone and for several seconds the poem simply left my head. The hall was silent. Then I looked at the last row, took a breath, and the first line came. By the second stanza my voice was steady, and by the end I even smiled.

When I finished, the hall rang with applause. My mother, sitting in the front row, was wiping her eyes.

I did not win a prize that day, but I won something more important. I learned that fear does not disappear, but it can be controlled. Since then, whenever I must speak in public, I remember the last row of the hall.
:::

## 19.4 Predicted questions

::: caution How this list is ranked
Ranks follow two reasons: how often a skill is asked (every paper asks Q1 to Q8) and how much of the syllabus a text or task can carry. A text ranked low is not unimportant. A text that has just appeared in a past paper is not "used up" either; examiners do not follow a rotation that anyone outside the board knows.
:::

### Tier A: practise before anything else

| Rank | Question | Reason |

## Chapter summary

- The paper is Q1 (15) + Q2 (10) + Q3 (10) + Q4 (7) + Q5 (8) + Q6 (10) + Q7 (10) + Q8 (5) = 75 marks in 3 hours.
- Literature answers need text detail: a name, an event or a quotation, then the point it proves.
- Writing is marked on format and organisation first: a letter has an address, a date, a subject, a salutation, a body and a closing; an essay has an introduction, body paragraphs and a conclusion.
- Do Q7 and Q8 carefully; they are 15 marks for a few minutes of work.
- The rankings above come from the grid and the syllabus; they are guidance for effort, not a promise about the paper.

1. What does the title "Trifles" mean in the play? <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Why is Jean unhappy in "Two Little Soldiers"? <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. How does the poet describe the soldier's returned "gifts" in "The Gift in Wartime"? <span class="marks">[2]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** "Trifles" means small, unimportant things. The men dismiss the women's interest in kitchen details as trifles, but those details solve the crime.
**2.** Jean is jealous and lonely because his friend Luc has started to meet a dairymaid, and he feels left out of the friendship.
**3.** The "gifts" are the smell of blood, the soldier's body, medals and loneliness. Calling them gifts is ironic, because each one is a loss.
:::

1. Write a notice of about 50 words for the school notice board announcing an inter-house quiz competition. <span class="marks">[8]</span>

::: note Model answer
```
              Shree Janata Higher Secondary School, Butwal
                                NOTICE
                                                    15 Falgun 2082

                      Inter-House Quiz Competition

An inter-house quiz competition will be held in the school hall on
Friday, 25 Falgun 2082, at 11 a.m. Each house should send a team of
three students. Interested students should give their names to the house
captain by Wednesday.

                                                    Sunita Rai
                                                    Secretary, Literary Club
```

A notice is marked for the school name, the word NOTICE, the date, a heading, the what, when, where and who, and the name and post of the writer. Keep it short.
:::
