This chapter does three things. It lays out the **pattern of the Grade 11 Mathematics paper** and shows how the teaching hours of the CDC syllabus translate into a sensible spread of marks. It then gives **two complete model papers** ($11\times1+8\times5+3\times8=75$ marks, three hours), each followed by a full solution. Finally it gives a **ranked list of predicted questions**, each with the reason for its rank.

::: caution What "predicted" means here
Nobody outside the examination board knows what will be set, and this chapter does not pretend to. A prediction is a judgement from two public facts: how many teaching hours the CDC syllabus gives a chapter, and how standard the question form is in a board paper (a truth table, a maxima problem, an area between curves). Treat the list as a guide to what to **practise first**, never as a promise. Every question below is an original practice item; none is claimed to be from a past paper.
:::

::: key How to sit a three-hour paper
Seventy-five marks in 180 minutes is 2.4 minutes a mark. Plan: **Group A, 15 minutes**; **Group B, 85 minutes** (about 10 per question); **Group C, 65 minutes** (about 22 per question); 15 minutes to check. In every written answer, write the formula, substitute, simplify, then box the result. Part-marks are given for method.
:::

## 13.1 Pattern and weighting of the paper

### 13.1.1 The grid

| Group | Questions | Marks | What the examiner does |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 11 multiple-choice questions | $11\times1=11$ | one mark each |
| B | 8 short-answer questions | $8\times5=40$ | five marks, often split $2+3$ or $1+4$ |
| C | 3 long-answer questions | $3\times8=24$ | usually three parts, for example $3+3+2$ |

The paper is out of 75; internal assessment adds 25. The last chapter is a choice: **Computational Methods or Mechanics** (Unit 20 or Unit 21); the question set on it carries an "Or".

### 13.1.2 Hours as a guide to marks

The CDC syllabus allots 160 working hours. The table converts each area's hours into a proportional share of 75 marks. It is **our guide, not the official specification grid**.

| Area (chapters) | Hours | Share of 75 marks |
|---|---|---|
| Algebra (1–8) | 44 | about 20.6 |
| Trigonometry (9–10) | 12 | about 5.6 |
| Analytic geometry (11–13) | 20 | about 9.4 |
| Vectors (14) | 12 | about 5.6 |
| Statistics and probability (15–16) | 12 | about 5.6 |
| Calculus (17–19) | 48 | about 22.5 |
| Computational methods or mechanics (20 or 21) | 12 | about 5.6 |

Calculus and algebra together are nearly **three-fifths** of the paper. A student who is secure in derivatives and integration and in quadratic equations, sequences, matrices and complex numbers has already met the bulk of the marks. Derivatives alone is 22 hours, the largest chapter of the book.

### 13.1.3 What the two model papers do

Both papers give Group A three algebra, three trigonometry/geometry/vectors, one statistics-or-probability and four calculus-or-methods questions, Group B one question from each major strand, and Group C two calculus questions plus one mixed question. The two papers do not repeat a question form, so working both covers more ground than working one twice.

::: note Group B — full solutions
**12.** Truth table for all four cases:

| $p$ | $q$ | $p\wedge q$ | $\sim(p\wedge q)$ | $\sim p$ | $\sim q$ | $\sim p\vee\sim q$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T | T | T | F | F | F | F |
| T | F | F | T | F | T | T |
| F | T | F | T | T | F | T |
| F | F | F | T | T | T | T |

The fourth and seventh columns are identical, so $\sim(p\wedge q)\equiv\sim p\vee\sim q$.

**13.** $f(f(x))=\frac{\frac{x+1}{x-1}+1}{\frac{x+1}{x-1}-1}=\frac{\frac{(x+1)+(x-1)}{x-1}}{\frac{(x+1)-(x-1)}{x-1}}=\frac{2x}{2}=x$. Since $f\circ f$ is the identity, $f$ is its own inverse: $f^{-1}(x)=\frac{x+1}{x-1}$.

**14.** $a+2d=11$ and $a+6d=23$. Subtract: $4d=12$, $d=3$, $a=5$. The A.P. is $5,8,11,14,\dots$ $S_{20}=\frac{20}{2}[2(5)+19(3)]=10(67)=670$.

**15.** In matrix form $AX=B$ with $A$ having rows $(3,5),(1,2)$ and $B=(13,5)$. $|A|=6-5=1$, so $A^{-1}$ has rows $(2,-5)$ and $(-1,3)$. $x=2(13)-5(5)=1$ and $y=-1(13)+3(5)=2$. Check: $3(1)+5(2)=13$ ✓, $1+2(2)=5$ ✓. So $x=1$, $y=2$.

**16.** $\alpha+\beta=\frac{5}{2}$, $\alpha\beta=\frac{1}{2}$. $\alpha^2+\beta^2=\left(\frac{5}{2}\right)^2-2\cdot\frac{1}{2}=\frac{25}{4}-1=\frac{21}{4}$. $\frac{\alpha}{\beta}+\frac{\beta}{\alpha}=\frac{\alpha^2+\beta^2}{\alpha\beta}=\frac{21/4}{1/2}=\frac{21}{2}$.

**17.** $\tan^{-1}\frac{1}{2}+\tan^{-1}\frac{1}{5}=\tan^{-1}\frac{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{5}}{1-\frac{1}{10}}=\tan^{-1}\frac{7/10}{9/10}=\tan^{-1}\frac{7}{9}$. Adding $\tan^{-1}\frac{1}{8}$: $\tan^{-1}\frac{\frac{7}{9}+\frac{1}{8}}{1-\frac{7}{72}}=\tan^{-1}\frac{65/72}{65/72}=\tan^{-1}1=\frac{\pi}{4}$.

**18.** The equation factorises as $(x-y)(x-2y)=0$, giving the lines $x-y=0$ and $x-2y=0$ (homogeneous, so both pass through the origin). Here $a=1$, $h=-\frac{3}{2}$, $b=2$: $\tan\theta=\frac{2\sqrt{h^2-ab}}{|a+b|}=\frac{2\sqrt{\frac{9}{4}-2}}{3}=\frac{2\times\frac{1}{2}}{3}=\frac{1}{3}$. So $\theta=\tan^{-1}\frac{1}{3}\approx18.4^\circ$.

**19.** Mode $=3\times42-2\times45=126-90=36$. $S_k=\frac{45-36}{9}=1$ (positively skewed). C.V. $=\frac{9}{45}\times100=20\%$.
:::

::: note Group C — full solutions
**20.** $f'(x)=3x^2-6x-9=3(x-3)(x+1)$, zero at $x=-1,3$.
(i) $f'>0$ for $x<-1$ and $x>3$; $f'<0$ for $-1<x<3$. So $f$ is **increasing** on $(-\infty,-1)$ and $(3,\infty)$ and **decreasing** on $(-1,3)$.
(ii) $f''(x)=6x-6$. At $x=-1$: $f''=-12<0$, a **local maximum**, $f(-1)=-1-3+9+5=10$. At $x=3$: $f''=12>0$, a **local minimum**, $f(3)=27-27-27+5=-22$.
(iii) $f''(x)=0\Rightarrow x=1$, and $f''$ changes sign there. $f(1)=1-3-9+5=-6$. The point of inflection is $(1,-6)$.

**21.** (i) By parts, $u=x$, $dv=\cos x\,dx$: $\int x\cos x\,dx=x\sin x-\int\sin x\,dx=x\sin x+\cos x+C$.
(ii) The curves meet where $x^2=4$, i.e. $x=\pm2$. The line is above the parabola, so area $=\int_{-2}^{2}(4-x^2)dx=2\int_0^2(4-x^2)dx=2\left[4x-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^2=2\left(8-\frac{8}{3}\right)=\frac{32}{3}$ square units.
(iii) $\int_0^2(3x^2+2x)dx=[x^3+x^2]_0^2=8+4=12$.

**22.** (i) Rationalise: $\frac{\sqrt{1+x}-1}{x}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{1+x}+1}{\sqrt{1+x}+1}=\frac{x}{x(\sqrt{1+x}+1)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+x}+1}\to\frac{1}{2}$.
(ii) Let $7\mathbf{i}+5\mathbf{j}=p(2\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j})+q(\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j})$. Then $2p+q=7$ and $p+2q=5$. Solving: $p=3$, $q=1$. So $7\mathbf{i}+5\mathbf{j}=3(2\mathbf{i}+\mathbf{j})+(\mathbf{i}+2\mathbf{j})$.
(iii) $d=\sqrt{(1+1)^2+(2-0)^2+(2-3)^2}=\sqrt{4+4+1}=3$.
:::

::: note Group B — full solutions
**12.** $B\cup C=\{2,3,4,6,7\}$, so $A-(B\cup C)=\{1,5\}$. $A-B=\{1,3,5\}$ and $A-C=\{1,2,5\}$, whose intersection is $\{1,5\}$. Both sides equal $\{1,5\}$.

**13.** Let $y=\frac{3x-1}{2x+5}$. Then $2xy+5y=3x-1\Rightarrow x(2y-3)=-1-5y\Rightarrow x=\frac{5y+1}{3-2y}$. So $f^{-1}(x)=\frac{5x+1}{3-2x}$, defined for $x\ne\frac{3}{2}$.

**14.** $\frac{a}{1-r}=15$ and $\frac{a^2}{1-r^2}=45$. Dividing the second by the first: $\frac{a}{1+r}=3$, so $a=3(1+r)$. From the first, $a=15(1-r)$. Equate: $3+3r=15-15r\Rightarrow18r=12\Rightarrow r=\frac{2}{3}$, and $a=15\times\frac{1}{3}=5$. Check: $\frac{5}{1/3}=15$ ✓; $\frac{25}{1-\frac{4}{9}}=45$ ✓.

**15.** Expand along row 1: $2(3\cdot1-2\cdot1)-0+1(1\cdot1-3\cdot1)=2(1)+1(-2)=0$. Since the determinant is 0, the matrix is **singular** and has **no inverse**.

**16.** Product of roots $=\frac{k}{2}=1\Rightarrow k=2$. The equation is $2x^2-5x+2=0$, so $(2x-1)(x-2)=0$ and the roots are $2$ and $\frac{1}{2}$ (reciprocals ✓).

**17.** $2\sin x\cos x-\cos x=0\Rightarrow\cos x(2\sin x-1)=0$. $\cos x=0\Rightarrow x=\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{3\pi}{2}$. $\sin x=\frac{1}{2}\Rightarrow x=\frac{\pi}{6},\frac{5\pi}{6}$. Solutions: $\frac{\pi}{6},\ \frac{\pi}{2},\ \frac{5\pi}{6},\ \frac{3\pi}{2}$.

**18.** Distance of $(-2,1)$: $\frac{|5(-2)-12(1)+13|}{\sqrt{25+144}}=\frac{|-9|}{13}=\frac{9}{13}$. Distance between the parallel lines: $\frac{|13-(-26)|}{13}=\frac{39}{13}=3$.

**20.** (i) $\frac{dx}{d\theta}=-a\sin\theta$, $\frac{dy}{d\theta}=b\cos\theta$. $\frac{dy}{dx}=-\frac{b\cos\theta}{a\sin\theta}=-\frac{b}{a}\cot\theta$.
(ii) $\frac{dy}{dx}=2x-4$. A horizontal tangent has slope 0: $x=2$, $y=4-8+5=1$. The point is $(2,1)$.
(iii) Slope of the tangent at $x=3$ is $2x=6$. The normal is perpendicular, so its slope is $-\frac{1}{6}$.

**21.** (i) By parts with $u=\ln x$, $dv=x\,dx$: $\int x\ln x\,dx=\frac{x^2}{2}\ln x-\int\frac{x^2}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{x}\,dx=\frac{x^2}{2}\ln x-\frac{x^2}{4}+C$.

(iii) Area $=\int_0^2 3x^2\,dx=[x^3]_0^2=8$ square units.

**22.** (i) Let $P$ divide $AB$ internally in the ratio $m:n$, so $AP:PB=m:n$ and $\overrightarrow{AP}=\frac{m}{m+n}\overrightarrow{AB}$. With $O$ as origin, $\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{a}=\frac{m}{m+n}(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})$. Hence $\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{a}+\frac{m(\mathbf{b}-\mathbf{a})}{m+n}=\frac{(m+n)\mathbf{a}+m\mathbf{b}-m\mathbf{a}}{m+n}=\frac{n\mathbf{a}+m\mathbf{b}}{m+n}$.
(ii) Red then black: $\frac{4}{10}\cdot\frac{6}{9}=\frac{24}{90}$. Black then red: $\frac{6}{10}\cdot\frac{4}{9}=\frac{24}{90}$. $P=\frac{48}{90}=\frac{8}{15}$.

## 13.4 Predicted Questions

The list below ranks the question forms most worth practising. Rank comes from the **chapter's hours** (13.1.2) and from how **standard** the question form is. Tier A forms are in chapters that are both heavy and rich in routine five-mark and eight-mark questions. Tier C forms are plausible but less certain. None is a promise.

### 13.4.1 Tier A — practise first

| Rank | Topic and likely form | Marks | Why it is ranked here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Maxima and minima, increasing or decreasing intervals, point of inflection of a cubic | 8 | Derivatives is the largest chapter (22 hours) and this is its standard long question. |
| 2 | Integration: by parts, by substitution, area between curves | 8 | Anti-derivatives has 16 hours, and "evaluate, evaluate, find the area" fits the $3+3+2$ split. |

## Chapter summary

- The paper is $11\times1+8\times5+3\times8=75$; calculus and algebra together carry nearly three-fifths of the hours.
- Plan the three hours: Group A 15 minutes, Group B 85, Group C 65, check 15.
- Calculus long questions follow a fixed pattern: **find $f'$, set it to zero, test with $f''$**; evaluate integrals by parts or substitution; area $=\int(\text{upper}-\text{lower})\,dx$.
- Memorise the quick tools: $\alpha^2+\beta^2=(\alpha+\beta)^2-2\alpha\beta$; $\tan^{-1}a+\tan^{-1}b=\tan^{-1}\frac{a+b}{1-ab}$; $d=\frac{|ax_1+by_1+c|}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}$; mode $=3\,\text{median}-2\,\text{mean}$.
- Section formula: $\frac{n\mathbf{a}+m\mathbf{b}}{m+n}$; Newton–Raphson $x_{n+1}=x_n-\frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}$.
- A prediction is a guide to what to practise first, never a promise of what will be set.
