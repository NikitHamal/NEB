Group A of the NEB Grade 11 Chemistry paper is eleven one-mark multiple-choice questions, drawn from across the seventeen units of the CDC syllabus. This chapter drills every unit in order. Each section opens with a **high-yield facts table** (the formulae, reagents and trends the objective questions keep returning to), then gives six to eight MCQs, then an answer key in which every answer carries a **one-line reason** and, where a wrong option encodes a real misconception, names it.

::: key What Group A actually tests
Almost nothing in Group A is a long calculation. It tests four things: a **definition or name**, a **formula or reagent**, a **one-step calculation** (a mole, a ratio, an oxidation number), or a **misconception** offered as a distractor. If an MCQ is taking you more than ninety seconds, you have misread it.
:::

::: caution No question here carries a year tag — and that is deliberate
NEB does not publish its Grade 11 question papers, and this book could not verify any item against an official released paper. Rather than print a year it cannot stand behind, it prints none. What it can stand behind is the **syllabus weighting** (General and Physical 48 h, Inorganic 35 h, Organic 30 h, Applied 15 h of 128), and every question below is built from it. Treat this as a bank of exam-shaped questions, not a leaked paper.
:::

## 5.1 Foundation and Fundamentals

| High-yield fact | Value / form |
|---|---|
| 1 amu | $\tfrac{1}{12}$ of the mass of one carbon-12 atom ($1.66\times10^{-27}\ \text{kg}$) |
| Relative molecular mass | sum of the relative atomic masses of all atoms in the formula |
| Molecular formula | $n\times$ empirical formula; $n = \dfrac{\text{molar mass}}{\text{empirical formula mass}}$ |
| Mass percentage of an element | $\dfrac{\text{mass of the element in one mole}}{\text{molar mass}}\times100$ |
| Radical | a group of atoms behaving as one unit and carrying a charge, e.g. $\text{NH}_4^{+}$, $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ |

1. One atomic mass unit is equal to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) the mass of one hydrogen atom (b) $\tfrac{1}{12}$ the mass of a carbon-12 atom (c) the mass of one proton exactly (d) the mass of one electron
2. The empirical formula of glucose, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$, is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\text{CH}$ (b) $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4\text{O}_2$ (c) $\text{CH}_2\text{O}$ (d) $\text{C}_3\text{H}_6\text{O}_3$
3. The percentage of oxygen in water ($\text{H}=1$, $\text{O}=16$) is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 11.1 % (b) 50 % (c) 88.9 % (d) 94.4 %
4. Which of the following is a radical? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\text{NaCl}$ (b) $\text{NH}_4^{+}$ (c) $\text{CO}_2$ (d) $\text{H}_2\text{O}$
5. The relative molecular mass of $\text{CaCO}_3$ ($\text{Ca}=40$, $\text{C}=12$, $\text{O}=16$) is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 56 (b) 84 (c) 100 (d) 116
6. A compound has empirical formula $\text{CH}$ and relative molecular mass 78. Its molecular formula is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$ (b) $\text{C}_4\text{H}_4$ (c) $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$ (d) $\text{C}_8\text{H}_8$

::: note Answers — 5.1
**1.** (b) — the definition of the amu.
**2.** (c) — divide the subscripts $6:12:6$ by 6.
**3.** (c) — $16/18 = 0.889$; (a) is the hydrogen percentage, a classic distractor.
**4.** (b) — $\text{NH}_4^{+}$ is a charged group that moves as a unit.
**5.** (c) — $40 + 12 + 48 = 100$; (b) is $\text{MgCO}_3$.
**6.** (c) — $78/13 = 6$, so $\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$.
:::

## 5.2 Stoichiometry

| High-yield fact | Value / form |
|---|---|
| Avogadro constant | $6.022\times10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$ |
| Molar volume at STP (273 K, 1 atm) | $22.4\ \text{L mol}^{-1}$ |
| Vapour density | $\text{V.D.} = \dfrac{M}{2}$ |
| Laws | conservation of mass (Lavoisier); definite proportions (Proust); multiple proportions (Dalton); reciprocal proportions (Richter); Gay-Lussac's law of gaseous volumes |
| Percentage yield | $\dfrac{\text{experimental yield}}{\text{theoretical yield}}\times100$ |
| Limiting reactant | the reactant used up first; it fixes the amount of product |

1. The number of molecules in $0.5$ mol of a gas is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $6.022\times10^{23}$ (b) $3.011\times10^{23}$ (c) $1.2\times10^{24}$ (d) $6.022\times10^{22}$
2. The volume at STP of $8\ \text{g}$ of oxygen ($\text{O}_2$) is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $2.8\ \text{L}$ (b) $5.6\ \text{L}$ (c) $11.2\ \text{L}$ (d) $22.4\ \text{L}$
3. The vapour density of a gas of molar mass $44\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 22 (b) 44 (c) 88 (d) 11
4. $\text{H}_2 + \text{Cl}_2 \to 2\text{HCl}$. The mass of HCl formed from $2\ \text{g}$ of hydrogen with excess chlorine is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $36.5\ \text{g}$ (b) $71\ \text{g}$ (c) $73\ \text{g}$ (d) $146\ \text{g}$
5. In CO and $\text{CO}_2$, $12\ \text{g}$ of carbon combines with $16\ \text{g}$ and $32\ \text{g}$ of oxygen. This illustrates the law of <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) conservation of mass (b) definite proportions (c) multiple proportions (d) reciprocal proportions
6. If the theoretical yield is $20\ \text{g}$ and $15\ \text{g}$ is obtained, the percentage yield is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 25 % (b) 75 % (c) 80 % (d) 133 %
7. Which postulate of Dalton's atomic theory is no longer accepted? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) atoms combine in simple whole-number ratios (b) atoms of different elements differ in mass (c) atoms are indivisible (d) compounds form by combination of atoms

::: note Answers — 5.2
**1.** (b) — $0.5\times6.022\times10^{23}$.
**2.** (b) — $8/32 = 0.25$ mol; $0.25\times22.4 = 5.6\ \text{L}$.
**3.** (a) — $M/2 = 22$.
**4.** (c) — $2\ \text{g}$ is 1 mol of $\text{H}_2$, giving 2 mol HCl $= 73\ \text{g}$; (a) forgets the factor of two.
**5.** (c) — the oxygen masses $16 : 32 = 1 : 2$ for a fixed mass of carbon.
**6.** (b) — $15/20\times100$.
**7.** (c) — atoms are divisible into electrons, protons and neutrons.
:::

## 5.3 Atomic Structure

| High-yield fact | Value / form |
|---|---|
| Quantum numbers | $n$ (size, energy), $l$ (shape: $0$ to $n-1$), $m_l$ (orientation: $-l$ to $+l$), $m_s$ ($\pm\tfrac{1}{2}$) |
| Electrons in a shell | $2n^{2}$ |
| Orbitals in $s, p, d, f$ | $1, 3, 5, 7$ |
| de Broglie wavelength | $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{mv}$ |
| Heisenberg | $\Delta x\cdot\Delta p \ge \dfrac{h}{4\pi}$ |
| Exceptions | Cr: $[\text{Ar}]3d^{5}4s^{1}$; Cu: $[\text{Ar}]3d^{10}4s^{1}$ |
| Rules | Aufbau (lowest energy first); Pauli (no two electrons with the same four quantum numbers); Hund (single occupancy of degenerate orbitals first) |

1. The number of orbitals in a $d$ subshell is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1 (b) 3 (c) 5 (d) 7
2. The maximum number of electrons in the shell with $n = 3$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 8 (b) 10 (c) 18 (d) 32
3. The electronic configuration of chromium ($Z = 24$) is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $[\text{Ar}]3d^{4}4s^{2}$ (b) $[\text{Ar}]3d^{5}4s^{1}$ (c) $[\text{Ar}]3d^{6}$ (d) $[\text{Ar}]4s^{2}4p^{4}$
4. The quantum number that determines the shape of an orbital is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) principal (b) azimuthal (c) magnetic (d) spin
5. Electrons occupy degenerate orbitals singly before pairing, according to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Hund's rule (b) Pauli's exclusion principle (c) Aufbau principle (d) Heisenberg's principle
6. The number of unpaired electrons in $\text{Fe}^{3+}$ ($Z = 26$) is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 5
7. Heisenberg's uncertainty principle states that <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) electrons move in fixed orbits (b) position and momentum cannot both be known exactly (c) no two electrons have the same four quantum numbers (d) matter has no wave nature

::: note Answers — 5.3
**1.** (c) — $l = 2$ gives $2l + 1 = 5$ orbitals.
**2.** (c) — $2n^{2} = 18$.
**3.** (b) — a half-filled $d$ subshell is more stable; (a) is the unadjusted Aufbau answer.
**4.** (b) — $l$ gives $s$ (spherical), $p$ (dumb-bell).
**5.** (a) — Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity.
**6.** (d) — $\text{Fe}\ 3d^{6}4s^{2}$ loses the $4s$ pair and one $3d$ electron, leaving $3d^{5}$: five unpaired.
**7.** (b) — (a) is Bohr's model and (c) is Pauli's principle.
:::

## 5.4 Classification of Elements and the Periodic Table

| High-yield fact | Value / form |
|---|---|
| Modern periodic law | properties of elements are periodic functions of their **atomic numbers** |
| Effective nuclear charge | $Z_{eff} = Z - S$ ($S$ = shielding constant) |
| Across a period (left to right) | atomic radius falls; ionization energy and electronegativity rise; metallic character falls |
| Down a group | atomic radius rises; ionization energy and electronegativity fall |
| Electronegativity (Pauling) | F $= 4.0$ (highest); O $= 3.5$; N $= 3.0$; Cl $= 3.0$ |
| Electron affinity | Cl has the largest magnitude, not F (small size, repulsion) |

1. The most electronegative element is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) oxygen (b) chlorine (c) fluorine (d) nitrogen
2. Which has the largest atomic radius? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Li (b) Na (c) K (d) Rb
3. Across a period from left to right the first ionization energy generally <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) increases (b) decreases (c) stays constant (d) first falls then rises sharply
4. The halogen with the highest electron affinity (magnitude) is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) F (b) Cl (c) Br (d) I
5. The correct order of ionic radii of the isoelectronic ions $\text{Na}^{+}$, $\text{Mg}^{2+}$, $\text{Al}^{3+}$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\text{Na}^{+} > \text{Mg}^{2+} > \text{Al}^{3+}$ (b) $\text{Al}^{3+} > \text{Mg}^{2+} > \text{Na}^{+}$ (c) all equal (d) $\text{Mg}^{2+} > \text{Na}^{+} > \text{Al}^{3+}$
6. An element of atomic number 17 belongs to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $s$-block, group 1 (b) $d$-block, group 7 (c) $p$-block, group 17 (d) $p$-block, group 7

::: note Answers — 5.4
**1.** (c) — F has the highest Pauling value, 4.0.
**2.** (d) — radius grows down the group; Rb is the lowest in the list.
**3.** (a) — the nuclear charge rises with no extra shell (small dips at Group 13 and 16 are exceptions).
**4.** (b) — fluorine's small $2p$ subshell repels the incoming electron, so Cl exceeds F.
**5.** (a) — same electrons, more protons pull harder: $\text{Na}^{+}$ is the largest.
**6.** (c) — Cl, $[\text{Ne}]3s^{2}3p^{5}$, is group 17 of the $p$-block (IUPAC numbering); (d) uses the old numbering.
:::

   (a) tetrahedral (b) trigonal pyramidal (c) planar (d) linear
2. The hybridization of boron in $\text{BF}_3$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $sp$ (b) $sp^{2}$ (c) $sp^{3}$ (d) $sp^{3}d$
3. The bond angle in water is about <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $90^{\circ}$ (b) $104.5^{\circ}$ (c) $109.5^{\circ}$ (d) $120^{\circ}$
4. Which molecule has zero dipole moment? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (b) $\text{NH}_3$ (c) $\text{CO}_2$ (d) $\text{HCl}$
5. The strongest hydrogen bonding occurs in <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) HCl (b) $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ (c) HBr (d) HF
6. A coordinate covalent bond is present in <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\text{CH}_4$ (b) $\text{NH}_4^{+}$ (c) NaCl (d) $\text{H}_2$
7. The shape of $\text{SF}_6$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) tetrahedral (b) trigonal bipyramidal (c) octahedral (d) square planar

::: note Answers — 5.5
**1.** (b) — three bond pairs and one lone pair; (a) ignores the lone pair.
**2.** (b) — three bond pairs and no lone pair give $sp^{2}$, trigonal planar.
**3.** (b) — two lone pairs compress the angle below $109.5^{\circ}$.
**4.** (c) — the two C=O dipoles of the linear molecule cancel.
**5.** (d) — F is the most electronegative and smallest; HF forms the strongest H-bonds.
**6.** (b) — the lone pair of N is donated to $\text{H}^{+}$.
**7.** (c) — six bond pairs, no lone pair.
:::

## 5.6 Oxidation and Reduction

| High-yield fact | Value / form |
|---|---|
| Oxidation | loss of electrons; increase in oxidation number |
| Reduction | gain of electrons; decrease in oxidation number |
| Rules | free element $= 0$; H usually $+1$; O usually $-2$ ($-1$ in peroxides); sum $=$ charge on the ion |
| Common values | S in $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$: $+6$; Mn in $\text{KMnO}_4$: $+7$; Cr in $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$: $+6$; Cl in $\text{HClO}_4$: $+7$ |
| Faraday's laws | $m = ZIt$; $1\ \text{F} = 96\,500\ \text{C}$ deposits one equivalent |
| Electrolysis | oxidation at the anode, reduction at the cathode |

1. The oxidation number of chromium in $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $+3$ (b) $+4$ (c) $+6$ (d) $+7$
2. In $2\text{Mg} + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{MgO}$ the oxidizing agent is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Mg (b) $\text{O}_2$ (c) MgO (d) none
3. The average oxidation number of sulphur in $\text{Na}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_3$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0 (b) $+2$ (c) $+4$ (d) $+6$
4. The charge needed to deposit one mole of copper from $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $96\,500\ \text{C}$ (b) $144\,750\ \text{C}$ (c) $193\,000\ \text{C}$ (d) $289\,500\ \text{C}$
5. Reduction takes place at the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) cathode (b) anode (c) salt bridge (d) electrolyte
6. The oxidation number of manganese is highest in <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\text{MnO}_2$ (b) $\text{MnSO}_4$ (c) $\text{K}_2\text{MnO}_4$ (d) $\text{KMnO}_4$

::: note Answers — 5.6
**1.** (c) — $2x + 2(+1) + 7(-2) = 0$ gives $x = +6$.
**2.** (b) — $\text{O}_2$ gains electrons and so oxidizes Mg.
**3.** (b) — $2(+1) + 2x + 3(-2) = 0$ gives $x = +2$ on average.
**4.** (c) — $\text{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^{-} \to \text{Cu}$ needs $2\times96\,500$.
**5.** (a) — reduction is gain of electrons at the cathode (RED CAT).
**6.** (d) — $\text{MnO}_2$ $+4$, $\text{MnSO}_4$ $+2$, $\text{K}_2\text{MnO}_4$ $+6$, $\text{KMnO}_4$ $+7$.
:::

## 5.7 States of Matter

| High-yield fact | Value / form |
|---|---|
| Boyle's law | $P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$ at constant $T$ |
| Charles' law | $\dfrac{V_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{V_2}{T_2}$ at constant $P$ ($T$ in kelvin) |
| Ideal gas equation | $PV = nRT$; $R = 8.314\ \text{J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1} = 0.0821\ \text{L atm mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$ |
| Dalton's law | $P_{total} = P_1 + P_2 + \cdots$ |
| Graham's law | $\dfrac{r_1}{r_2} = \sqrt{\dfrac{M_2}{M_1}}$ |
| Real gases deviate | at high pressure and low temperature |
| Solids | efflorescent loses water of crystallization to air ($\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3\cdot10\text{H}_2\text{O}$); deliquescent absorbs water and dissolves ($\text{CaCl}_2$, NaOH) |

1. A gas occupies $2\ \text{L}$ at $1\ \text{atm}$. At constant temperature and $4\ \text{atm}$ it occupies <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $0.25\ \text{L}$ (b) $0.5\ \text{L}$ (c) $1\ \text{L}$ (d) $8\ \text{L}$
2. The ratio of the rates of diffusion of $\text{H}_2$ and $\text{O}_2$ under the same conditions is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $1:4$ (b) $1:16$ (c) $4:1$ (d) $16:1$
3. The value of $R$ in $\text{L atm mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 8.314 (b) 0.0821 (c) 1.987 (d) 62.4
4. A gas at $27^{\circ}\text{C}$ is heated at constant pressure until its volume doubles. The new temperature is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $54^{\circ}\text{C}$ (b) $300\ \text{K}$ (c) $327^{\circ}\text{C}$ (d) $600^{\circ}\text{C}$
5. An efflorescent substance is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3\cdot10\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (b) anhydrous $\text{CaCl}_2$ (c) NaCl (d) conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$
6. A real gas behaves most ideally at <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) high pressure, low temperature (b) low pressure, low temperature (c) low pressure, high temperature (d) high pressure, high temperature
7. The boiling point of a liquid is the temperature at which <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) vapour pressure is $1\ \text{atm}$ always (b) vapour pressure equals the external pressure (c) the liquid freezes (d) the surface tension is zero

::: note Answers — 5.7
**1.** (b) — $P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$ gives $V_2 = 2\times1/4 = 0.5\ \text{L}$.
**2.** (c) — $\sqrt{32/2} = 4$; the lighter gas diffuses faster.
**3.** (b) — (a) is in joules; (d) is in $\text{L mmHg}$-type units.
**4.** (c) — $T$ doubles in kelvin: $300 \to 600\ \text{K} = 327^{\circ}\text{C}$; (a) doubles the Celsius value, the common trap.
**5.** (a) — washing soda loses water on standing.
**6.** (c) — molecules are far apart and fast, so forces and volume matter least.
**7.** (b) — (a) is the *normal* boiling point only.
:::

## 5.8 Chemical Equilibrium

| High-yield fact | Value / form |
|---|---|
| Law of mass action | the rate of a reaction is proportional to the product of the active masses (concentrations) of the reactants |
| For $aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD$ | $K_c = \dfrac{[C]^{c}[D]^{d}}{[A]^{a}[B]^{b}}$ |
| $K_p$ and $K_c$ | $K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}$; $\Delta n = n_{gas,products} - n_{gas,reactants}$ |
| Le Chatelier | a system at equilibrium opposes any change imposed on it |
| Catalyst | speeds both rates equally; does not change $K$ or the position |

1. For which reaction is $K_p = K_c$? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\text{H}_2 + \text{I}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{HI}$ (b) $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$ (c) $\text{PCl}_5 \rightleftharpoons \text{PCl}_3 + \text{Cl}_2$ (d) $2\text{SO}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{SO}_3$
2. For $\text{N}_2\text{O}_4 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NO}_2$, $\Delta n$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $-1$ (b) $0$ (c) $+1$ (d) $+2$
3. Increasing the pressure on $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$ <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) shifts it to the left (b) shifts it to the right (c) has no effect (d) changes $K_c$
4. A catalyst in a reversible reaction <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) shifts the equilibrium to the right (b) raises $K_c$ (c) helps equilibrium to be reached faster without changing it (d) stops the reverse reaction
5. The $K_c$ expression for $\text{PCl}_5 \rightleftharpoons \text{PCl}_3 + \text{Cl}_2$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\dfrac{[\text{PCl}_5]}{[\text{PCl}_3][\text{Cl}_2]}$ (b) $\dfrac{[\text{PCl}_3][\text{Cl}_2]}{[\text{PCl}_5]}$ (c) $[\text{PCl}_3][\text{Cl}_2]$ (d) $[\text{PCl}_5]$
6. At chemical equilibrium <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) the forward rate equals the reverse rate (b) the reaction stops (c) concentrations of reactants and products are equal (d) the forward rate is zero

::: note Answers — 5.8
**1.** (a) — $\Delta n = 2 - 2 = 0$.
**2.** (c) — $2 - 1 = +1$.
**3.** (b) — the right side has fewer gas moles ($2$ against $4$).
**4.** (c) — a catalyst changes the rate, not the position.
**5.** (b) — products over reactants, each to the power of its coefficient.
**6.** (a) — equilibrium is dynamic; (c) confuses equal rates with equal concentrations.
:::

## Chapter summary

- Group A is eleven one-mark MCQs drawn from all seventeen units; no unit is safe to skip, and the question types are: a name or definition, a formula or reagent, a one-step calculation, or a misconception.
- Stoichiometry: $N_A = 6.022\times10^{23}$; molar volume $22.4\ \text{L}$ at STP; $\text{V.D.} = M/2$; percentage yield $=$ actual$/$theoretical $\times100$.
- Atomic structure: $2n^{2}$ electrons per shell; $2l+1$ orbitals per subshell; Cr and Cu are the $3d$ exceptions; Hund, Pauli and Aufbau.
- Periodic trends: radius falls and IE and EN rise across a period; the reverse down a group; F is the most electronegative, Cl has the highest electron affinity.
- Shapes: linear $sp$, planar $sp^{2}$, tetrahedral $sp^{3}$, pyramidal and bent (lone pairs), $sp^{3}d$ and $sp^{3}d^{2}$ for $\text{PCl}_5$ and $\text{SF}_6$.
- Redox: oxidation is loss of electrons; $\text{Cr} = +6$ in dichromate, $\text{Mn} = +7$ in permanganate; $1\ \text{F} = 96\,500\ \text{C}$.
- Gases: $P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$, $PV = nRT$, $r_1/r_2 = \sqrt{M_2/M_1}$; $K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}$.
- Inorganic: ring test, Kipp's apparatus, Down's process, plaster of Paris $\text{CaSO}_4\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O}$; Haber (Fe), Ostwald (Pt–Rh), contact ($\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$), Solvay.
- Organic: Markovnikov and the peroxide effect (HBr only); Wurtz and Kolbe give alkanes; Baeyer and bromine water test unsaturation; Hückel $(4n+2)$; $-\text{NO}_2$ directs meta.

**Group A — Multiple choice (1 mark each)**

1. The number of moles in $11\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CO}_2$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $0.125$ (b) $0.25$ (c) $0.5$ (d) $4$
2. The shape of the methane molecule is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) planar (b) tetrahedral (c) pyramidal (d) linear
3. The gas liberated at the cathode during electrolysis of brine in a diaphragm cell is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) chlorine (b) oxygen (c) hydrogen (d) nitrogen
4. The hybridization of carbon in ethyne is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $sp^{3}$ (b) $sp^{2}$ (c) $sp$ (d) $sp^{3}d$

::: note Answers to Group A
**1.** (b) — $11/44 = 0.25$ mol.
**2.** (b) — four bond pairs, $109.5^{\circ}$.
**3.** (c) — $2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 2e^{-} \to \text{H}_2 + 2\text{OH}^{-}$ at the cathode.
**4.** (c) — each carbon forms one $\sigma$ bond to H and one to C, with two $\pi$ bonds.
:::

**Group B — Short answer (5 marks each)**

1. Calculate the mass of $\text{CaO}$ obtained by heating $50\ \text{g}$ of $\text{CaCO}_3$. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
2. State Le Chatelier's principle and apply it to the Haber process for pressure and temperature. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
3. Write the Lewis structure idea and shape of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and explain why its bond angle is below $109.5^{\circ}$. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
4. Distinguish between ethane, ethene and ethyne by two chemical tests. <span class="marks">[5]</span>

::: note Answers to Group B
**1.** $\text{CaCO}_3 \to \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2$; $50\ \text{g} = 0.5$ mol; one mole of CaCO₃ gives one mole of CaO; mass of CaO $= 0.5\times56 = 28\ \text{g}$.
**2.** If a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts to oppose the disturbance. $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3$ ($\Delta H < 0$, fewer moles on the right): high pressure favours ammonia; low temperature favours ammonia but slows the rate, so about $450^{\circ}\text{C}$ with an Fe catalyst is the compromise.
**3.** Oxygen has six valence electrons, two used in bonds, so there are two bond pairs and two lone pairs ($sp^{3}$). Lone pair–lone pair and lone pair–bond pair repulsion exceeds bond pair–bond pair repulsion, squeezing the angle to $104.5^{\circ}$; the shape is bent.
**4.** Bromine water: ethene and ethyne decolourise it, ethane does not. Ammoniacal $\text{AgNO}_3$: only ethyne gives a white precipitate. Baeyer's reagent is decolourised by ethene and ethyne but not ethane.
:::

**Group C — Long answer (8 marks each)**

1. Describe the manufacture of sulphuric acid by the contact process with the reactions, conditions and a flow sheet. <span class="marks">[8]</span>
2. Describe the Solvay process for sodium carbonate, giving the reactions and the role of ammonia recovery. <span class="marks">[8]</span>

::: note Answers to Group C
**1.** Steps: (i) *Sulphur dioxide:* $\text{S} + \text{O}_2 \to \text{SO}_2$ (or roasting pyrites, $4\text{FeS}_2 + 11\text{O}_2 \to 2\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 + 8\text{SO}_2$); the gas is purified and dried. (ii) *Oxidation:* $2\text{SO}_2 + \text{O}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{SO}_3$, $\Delta H = -197\ \text{kJ}$, over $\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$ at $400$–$450^{\circ}\text{C}$ and 1–2 atm; excess oxygen and removal of heat favour the yield. (iii) *Absorption:* $\text{SO}_3$ is dissolved in conc. $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ to give oleum: $\text{SO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \to \text{H}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_7$. (iv) *Dilution:* $\text{H}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_7 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$. Water is not used for direct absorption because it forms a mist. Flow sheet: burner $\to$ purifier $\to$ dryer $\to$ converter ($\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$) $\to$ absorption tower $\to$ oleum tank $\to$ dilution.
**2.** Brine is saturated with $\text{NH}_3$ in an ammonia tower. In the carbonating (Solvay) tower $\text{CO}_2$ is passed in: $\text{NaCl} + \text{NH}_3 + \text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{NaHCO}_3 + \text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$; sparingly soluble $\text{NaHCO}_3$ separates and is filtered. Heating gives soda ash: $2\text{NaHCO}_3 \to \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + \text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ (the $\text{CO}_2$ is recycled). $\text{CO}_2$ comes from heating limestone, $\text{CaCO}_3 \to \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2$, and the lime slakes to $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$. Ammonia is recovered: $2\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{Ca(OH)}_2 \to 2\text{NH}_3 + \text{CaCl}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Overall: $2\text{NaCl} + \text{CaCO}_3 \to \text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + \text{CaCl}_2$; ammonia is recycled, so only $\text{CaCl}_2$ is lost.
:::
