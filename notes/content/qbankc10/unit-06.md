## 6.1 Basic Concepts of Economics and Allocation of Resources

**Facts.** Economy = the system that produces, distributes and consumes. Scarcity → choice → opportunity cost (the **next best** alternative forgone, not the money paid). Factors of production: land/rent, labour/wages, capital/interest, enterprise/profit. Three central problems: what, how, for whom. $Y = C + S$; equilibrium when $S = I$. Nepal is a **mixed** economy.

**Group A — Very short answer (1 mark each)**

1. What is an economy? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
2. Define scarcity. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
3. What is opportunity cost? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
4. Write the rewards of land and capital. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
5. Write any two features of a capitalist economy. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
6. What is a mixed economy? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
7. What is allocation of resources? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
8. Write any two central problems of an economy. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
9. Write any two macroeconomic variables. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
10. What is per capita income? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
11. What is meant by the circular flow of income? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
12. What is a leak in the circular flow? <span class="marks">[1]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** The organised system by which the people of a country produce goods and services, distribute them and consume them to satisfy their wants with limited resources.
**2.** Scarcity means that the resources available to satisfy human wants are limited in supply relative to the wants for them.
**3.** The value of the next best alternative forgone when a choice is made.
**4.** Land — rent; capital — interest.
**5.** Any two of: private ownership; freedom of enterprise; profit motive; consumer sovereignty; price mechanism; competition; limited government.
**6.** An economy in which the private sector and the public sector exist side by side, with the market taking many decisions and the state taking others and regulating the rest. Nepal is a mixed economy.
**7.** The process of deciding how much of each factor of production shall be used, and where, to produce the goods and services the society wants.
**8.** Any two of: what to produce and how much; how to produce; for whom to produce.
**9.** Any two of: national income, per capita income, consumption, saving, investment, inflation, unemployment, money supply, balance of payments, population.
**10.** National income divided by population — the average income of one person in a year.
**11.** The continuous movement of factor services and goods from households to firms and back, and of income and expenditure from firms to households and back.
**12.** Saving, which leaves the circular flow of spending; investment is the corresponding injection.
:::

**Group B — Short answer (5 marks each)**

1. Distinguish between a capitalist, a socialist and a mixed economy. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
2. What is scarcity? Explain the relation between scarcity, choice and opportunity cost with an example. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
3. Explain the three central problems of an economy. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
4. Explain the circular flow of income in a two-sector economy and show the condition of equilibrium. <span class="marks">[5]</span>

::: note Answer outlines
**1.** *Capitalist:* private ownership; decisions by the market and prices; distribution by ability to pay; efficient but unequal. *Socialist:* state ownership; decisions by the central plan; distribution by need or work; equal but often inefficient. *Mixed:* both sectors; decisions shared; distribution by wages tempered by social policy; the aim is to keep the market's efficiency while curing its inequality and instability. Nepal is a mixed economy with a socialist orientation.
**2.** Scarcity means resources are limited relative to wants. It forces a **choice** between alternative uses, and a choice means something is given up — the **opportunity cost**, which is the value of the next best alternative alone, not the money paid and not the sum of all the alternatives. Example: a student with Rs. 30,000 ranks a computer, a coaching class and a smartphone; if he buys the computer, the opportunity cost is the value of the coaching class.
**3.** (i) *What to produce and how much* — which goods and services and in what quantities; answered by prices in a capitalist economy. (ii) *How to produce* — the technique, labour-intensive or capital-intensive; the method that produces a given output at the lowest cost is technically efficient. (iii) *For whom to produce* — distribution by ability to pay, by need or by work. Modern writers add the full utilisation of resources and growth over time.
**4.** In a two-sector economy households supply factor services and receive factor income (rent, wages, interest, profit); they spend that income on the firms' output, and the firms receive sales revenue with which they pay the factors again. The real flow and the money flow run in opposite directions, and the value of output equals income equals expenditure. With saving and investment, $Y = C + S$ and $Y = C + I$, so equilibrium requires **S = I**; if investment exceeds saving, income and employment tend to rise.
:::

## 6.2 Microeconomics

**Facts.** $TR = P \times Q$; $AR = \frac{TR}{Q} = Price$; $MR = \frac{\Delta TR}{\Delta Q}$. Under perfect competition $AR = MR = P$; under monopoly MR falls twice as fast as AR. $TC = TFC + TVC$; $MC = \frac{\Delta TC}{\Delta Q}$; MC cuts AC at AC's minimum. Law of variable proportions: MP then AP then stages. Equilibrium of the firm: $MC = MR$, with MC rising. $VMP = MP \times Price$.

**Group A — Very short answer (1 mark each)**

1. Define total revenue. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
2. How is average revenue related to price? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
3. What is marginal revenue? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
4. Under which market structure is AR equal to MR? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
5. State the law of demand. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
6. What is price elasticity of demand? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
7. Define marginal cost. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
8. How is total cost found? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
9. At which point does the marginal cost curve cut the average cost curve? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
10. What is the law of variable proportions? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
11. What is a monopoly? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
12. Write any two features of perfect competition. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
13. State the condition for a firm's equilibrium. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
14. What is rent? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
15. State the marginal productivity theory of distribution. <span class="marks">[1]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** The whole amount a firm receives from the sale of a given quantity: $TR = Price \times Quantity$.
**2.** Average revenue equals the price: $AR = \frac{TR}{Q} = Price$.
**3.** The addition to total revenue from selling one more unit: $MR = \frac{\Delta TR}{\Delta Q}$.
**4.** Under perfect competition.
**5.** Other things remaining the same, when the price of a good rises the quantity demanded falls, and when the price falls the quantity demanded rises.
**6.** The ratio of the percentage change in quantity demanded to the percentage change in price.
**7.** The addition to total cost from producing one more unit: $MC = \frac{\Delta TC}{\Delta Q}$.
**8.** Total cost = total fixed cost + total variable cost.
**9.** At the minimum point of the average cost curve.
**10.** As more and more units of a variable factor are applied to a fixed factor, total product first increases at an increasing rate, then at a diminishing rate, and finally declines.
**11.** A market with a single seller of a good that has no close substitute and blocked entry, giving the seller considerable control over price.
**12.** Any two of: very many buyers and sellers; homogeneous product; free entry and exit; perfect knowledge; perfect mobility of factors; no transport cost; the firm is a price taker.
**13.** $MC = MR$, with MC rising (cutting MR from below).
**14.** The reward for the use of land and other natural resources whose supply is fixed; a surplus over the minimum needed to bring the factor into use.
**15.** Under perfect competition each factor is paid a reward equal to the value of its marginal product, $VMP = MP \times Price$.
:::

1. Complete the following table and comment on the behaviour of TR and MR. <span class="marks">[5]</span>

| Price (Rs.) | Quantity | TR | AR | MR |

| 20 | 1 | … | … | … |
| 18 | 2 | … | … | … |

2. Explain the relation between AR and MR under perfect competition and under monopoly. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
3. Explain the relation between marginal cost and average cost, and between ATC and AVC. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
4. Explain the law of variable proportions with a table showing the three stages. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
5. Compare perfect competition with monopoly on any five points. <span class="marks">[5]</span>

::: note Answer outlines
**1.**

| Price | Quantity | TR | AR | MR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 1 | 20 | 20 | 20 |
| 18 | 2 | 36 | 18 | 16 |
| 16 | 3 | 48 | 16 | 12 |
| 14 | 4 | 56 | 14 | 8 |
| 12 | 5 | 60 | 12 | 4 |

AR equals the price; MR falls faster than AR (by Rs. 2 for each extra unit) because the price cut applies to all units sold; TR rises throughout but by a diminishing amount.
**2.** Under perfect competition the firm is one of a very large number and can sell any quantity at the market price, so price is constant and $AR = MR = Price$, drawn as one horizontal line. Under monopoly the firm is the whole market and must lower the price on **every** unit to sell one more, so AR slopes downwards and MR lies below it, falling twice as fast; MR reaches zero where TR is maximum and turns negative beyond it.
**3.** $ATC = AFC + AVC$; AFC falls continuously as output rises. AVC, ATC and MC are U-shaped. **MC cuts AVC and ATC at their minimum points**, because while MC is below AC the average must fall, and while it is above the average must rise — so MC must cross AC where AC stops falling. The vertical distance between ATC and AVC is AFC, so the two come closer as output grows but never meet.
**4.** Statement of the law, then the table: labour 1 to 7 with TP 10, 24, 39, 52, 60, 64, 64 and MP 10, 14, 15, 13, 8, 4, 0 and AP 10, 12, 13, 13, 12, 10.67, 9.14. Stage I — increasing returns: MP rising, AP rising and below MP (1st to 3rd worker). Stage II — diminishing returns: MP falling but positive, AP maximum where MP = AP, TP still rising (4th to 6th). Stage III — negative returns: MP negative, TP falling. The causes: under-use of the fixed factor and specialisation in stage I; full use of the fixed factor in stage II; mutual obstruction in stage III. The producer should operate in stage II.
**5.** Number of sellers — very many against one; product — homogeneous against one with no close substitute; entry — free against blocked; price control — none against considerable; demand curve faced by the firm — perfectly elastic against downward sloping; price and output — lower price and larger output against higher price and smaller output; selling cost — none against some publicity.
:::

## 6.3 Macroeconomics

**Facts.** $Budget\ deficit = Expenditure - Revenue$. Public revenue = tax + non-tax + capital receipts. Direct tax cannot be shifted; indirect can. Absolute advantage (Smith) = fewer resources; comparative advantage (Ricardo) = lower opportunity cost. $Terms\ of\ trade = \frac{Export\ price\ index}{Import\ price\ index} \times 100$. Balance of trade = goods only; balance of payments = everything. Nepal's rupee is pegged to the Indian rupee.

::: note Answers
**1.** The branch of economics that studies the income, expenditure and borrowing of the government and their effect on the economy.
**2.** Any two of: fees and charges, fines, registration fees, royalties, dividends from public enterprises, interest and rent, foreign grants.
**3.** The government's annual statement of its estimated revenue and proposed expenditure for the coming financial year, presented to the legislature and authorising collection and spending once approved.
**4.** A budget in which the estimated expenditure exceeds the estimated revenue.
**5.** The government's policy on taxation, public expenditure and public borrowing, used deliberately to influence income, employment and prices.
**6.** A direct tax is borne by the person on whom it is legally imposed and cannot be shifted; an indirect tax is shifted to another, ultimately the final consumer.
**7.** The ability of a country to produce a good with fewer resources than another country.
**8.** The ability of a country to produce a good at a lower opportunity cost than another; it is the basis of gainful trade.
**9.** The ratio of the index of a country's export prices to the index of its import prices, multiplied by 100.
**10.** The difference between the value of a country's exports of goods and its imports of goods in a year.
**11.** The complete record of all a country's economic transactions with the rest of the world in a year — goods, services, income, transfers and capital movements.
**12.** A deliberate official reduction in the value of a country's currency in terms of foreign currencies under a fixed exchange rate.
**13.** Any two of: concentration on India; a chronic and large trade deficit; exports dominated by a few primary and semi-processed goods; imports of manufactures, fuel and machinery; landlocked and transit-dependent.
**14.** Any two of: tariff (customs duty), quota, embargo, subsidy to domestic producers, administrative barriers.
**15.** The total amount the government has borrowed and not yet repaid, whether internally or from abroad.

**Group B — Short answer (5 marks each)**

1. What is public finance? Explain the sources of public revenue and the kinds of public expenditure. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
2. What is a budget? Explain the balanced, surplus and deficit budgets and when each is appropriate. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
3. Explain the theory of comparative advantage with an example and state the gains from international trade. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
4. Distinguish between the balance of trade and the balance of payments, and explain them with the Nepali case. <span class="marks">[5]</span>

::: note Answer outlines
**1.** Public finance studies the government's income, expenditure and debt. *Revenue:* tax revenue (income tax, VAT, excise, customs, land and house tax); non-tax revenue (fees, fines, registration fees, royalties, dividends, interest, rent, foreign grants); capital receipts (internal and external borrowing). *Expenditure:* by nature, recurrent (salaries, goods and services, interest) and capital (roads, buildings, machinery); by purpose, developmental (education, health, agriculture, infrastructure) and non-developmental (defence, general administration, debt service).
**2.** Definition, then: *balanced* — revenue equals expenditure; prudent and non-inflationary, appropriate in normal times. *Surplus* — revenue exceeds expenditure; used to check inflation, retire debt and build reserves. *Deficit* — expenditure exceeds revenue; used to raise demand and employment in a slump and to finance development, but continued year after year it causes inflation, growing debt and loss of confidence. Give $Deficit = Expenditure - Revenue$.
**3.** Ricardo's theory: trade benefits a country even when it is absolutely less efficient in both goods, provided relative efficiencies differ; specialise where the opportunity cost is lower. Example (100 units of labour each): Nepal 40 cloth or 80 rice; India 60 cloth or 100 rice. Opportunity cost of one cloth = 2 rice in Nepal and 1.67 in India, so India specialises in cloth and Nepal in rice; both gain. Gains: larger total output through the international division of labour, lower prices, wider variety, transfer of technology, competition, larger markets.
**4.** *Balance of trade* — goods only: merchandise exports minus merchandise imports; the visible part of the current account. *Balance of payments* — every transaction: goods, services, income, transfers and capital movements; a complete account that always balances through a change in reserves or borrowing. Nepali case: a chronic and large trade deficit, a very large remittance inflow in transfers, and therefore a manageable overall balance of payments; heavy dependence on India; a landlocked, transit-dependent economy.
:::

## 6.6 Quantitative Methods in Economics

**Facts.** Mean: $\frac{\Sigma X}{N}$, $\frac{\Sigma fX}{\Sigma f}$, $\frac{\Sigma fm}{\Sigma f}$, or step-deviation $A + \frac{\Sigma fd'}{\Sigma f} \times i$. Median (continuous): $L + \frac{\frac{N}{2} - c.f.}{f} \times i$. Quartiles use $\frac{N}{4}$ and $\frac{3N}{4}$. Mode (continuous): $L + \frac{f_1 - f_0}{2f_1 - f_0 - f_2} \times i$. **Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean.** Pie sector angle $= \frac{component}{total} \times 360^\circ$. The mid-value of 20–30 is 25.

::: note Answer outlines
**1.** Definition. *Importance:* definite presentation of facts; simplification; comparison; testing of theories; guidance of policy; forecasting. *Limitations:* it studies groups and not individuals; deals with quantitative facts only; figures can be manipulated; true only on average; faulty collection ruins the analysis; needs trained people and uniform methods; shows relations and not causes.
**2.** *Primary* — collected first-hand for the investigator's own purpose: original and exactly suited, but costly, slow and needing trained staff. *Secondary* — already collected by another agency: cheap, quick and often the only source, but possibly unsuitable, out of date or inaccurate. Methods of collecting primary data: direct personal investigation; the interview with a schedule; the questionnaire by post or e-mail; the telephone interview; the panel method; observation.
**3.** *Histogram* — touching rectangles over the class intervals, height equal to frequency; from it the mode can be read graphically. *Frequency polygon* — the mid-point frequencies joined by straight lines, closed at both ends to the neighbouring empty classes. *Ogive* — a cumulative frequency curve; the "less than" ogive plots the upper limits against the cumulative frequencies and rises from left to right, the "more than" ogive plots the lower limits and falls; where the two cross the value on the horizontal axis is the median, and reading at N/4 and 3N/4 gives the quartiles.
