Group A of the SEE Accountancy paper is eleven very short answer questions of one mark each, all compulsory, drawn from every unit of the syllabus. Almost nothing in them is a long calculation: they test a **definition**, a **name**, a **code**, a **formula**, a **one-step figure** or a **one-line journal voucher**. They can be answered in about fifteen minutes, and they are the cheapest eleven marks in the paper. This chapter drills all fifteen units in order. Each section opens with a short **facts line** — what the one-mark questions from that unit keep returning to — and then gives ten questions with a key.

::: key How to use this chapter
Read the facts line, answer the ten questions in writing, then check the key. Mark every question you got wrong on a single sheet; that sheet, not the whole book, is what you revise in the last week. Every question is an **original practice question** written to the pattern; none is claimed to have appeared in any year's paper.
:::

## 2.1 Office Management and Procedure

**Facts.** Office management is planning, organising, directing, coordinating and controlling office work. Its areas are personnel, layout, machines, records, forms, procedures and financial work. Office procedure: receipt, sorting, recording, action, noting, drafting, approval, dispatch, follow-up. Filing = store; indexing = find. Layout is open, closed or mixed.

1. Define office management. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
2. Write any two areas of office management. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
3. Name any two office procedures related to decision making. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
4. What is office layout? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
5. What is a noting? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
6. What is drafting? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
7. Define filing. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
8. What is indexing? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
9. Write any two objectives of filing. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
10. What is meant by the retention period of a record? <span class="marks">[1]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** Office management is the task of planning, organising, directing, coordinating and controlling the work of an office so that its objectives are achieved efficiently and economically.
**2.** Any two of: personnel; layout and environment; machines and equipment; records management; forms and stationery; communication; financial office work.
**3.** Any two of: receipt and sorting, recording, action, noting and drafting, approval and signature, dispatch, follow-up.
**4.** Office layout is the arrangement of furniture, machines, departments and staff within the available floor space so that work flows smoothly.
**5.** A noting is the written record of the facts of a case, the applicable rules, the views of the sections concerned and a proposed decision, written on the file for the deciding officer.
**6.** Drafting is the preparation, in the form in which it will be issued, of a letter or order to be sent out of the office.
**7.** Filing is the systematic arrangement and storage of documents so that any document can be traced and produced when needed.
**8.** An index is a guide that tells where a particular record is kept — it converts a name or subject into the file's address.
**9.** Any two of: to keep papers safe; to make documents easy to trace; to keep related papers together; to preserve evidence; to serve as the office memory; to save space and cost.
**10.** The retention period is the fixed length of time for which a class of record must be kept before it may be transferred or destroyed.
:::

## 2.5 Bank Reconciliation Statement

**Facts.** Cash book = the trader's record; passbook = the bank's record. A debit balance in the cash book is a credit balance in the passbook. Cheques issued but not presented are **added** to the cash book balance; cheques deposited but not collected are **less**. Bank charges and interest on overdraft are less; interest allowed and direct deposits are added.

1. What is a bank reconciliation statement? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
2. Write any two causes of the difference between the cash book and the passbook. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
3. What does a debit balance in the cash book (bank column) mean? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
4. If the cash book shows an overdraft, what kind of balance does the passbook show? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
5. A cheque issued but not presented makes the passbook balance higher or lower than the cash book balance? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
6. Write any two items recorded by the bank only. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
7. What is the adjusted cash book? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
8. A cheque deposited is dishonoured. Which book shows it first? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
9. Why is a bank reconciliation statement prepared? Give any one reason. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
10. Name the statement prepared to bring the cash book and the passbook into agreement. <span class="marks">[1]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** A statement prepared on a particular date to explain and reconcile the difference between the balance of the bank column of the cash book and the balance of the passbook.
**2.** Any two of: cheques issued but not presented; cheques deposited but not collected; bank charges; interest allowed or charged; direct deposit by a customer; standing instructions; dishonoured cheque; errors.
**3.** Money is lying in the bank — a favourable balance.
**4.** A debit balance (an overdraft in the cash book is a debit balance in the passbook).
**5.** Higher — the trader has already reduced his cash book, the bank has not.
**6.** Any two of: bank charges and commission, interest allowed or charged, dividend collected, direct deposit by a customer, standing instructions, dishonour of a cheque.
**7.** The cash book after all items appearing only in the passbook have been entered in it, so that the remaining difference is only a matter of time.
**8.** The passbook.
**9.** To check the accuracy of both records and find errors; to find the true bank balance; to detect dishonoured cheques and unrecorded charges; to prepare the balance sheet; to prevent fraud.
**10.** The bank reconciliation statement.
:::

## 2.6 Accounting Errors

**Facts.** One-sided errors affect the trial balance and need a suspense account; two-sided errors do not. Errors of principle, complete omission, compensating errors and the wrong account of the same class never affect the trial balance. The suspense account is credited when the debit column of the trial balance is short, and debited when the credit column is short.

1. What is an accounting error? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
2. What is an error of principle? Give one example. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
3. What is a compensating error? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
4. Which errors are detected by the trial balance? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
5. Which errors are not detected by the trial balance? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
6. What is a suspense account? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
7. If the debit column of the trial balance is short, on which side is the suspense account opened? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
8. A purchase of machinery was debited to the purchases account. What kind of error is this? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
9. Where is a debit balance of the suspense account shown in the balance sheet? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
10. Pass the rectifying entry: rent paid Rs. 6,000 was debited to the wages account. <span class="marks">[1]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** An accounting error is a mistake in recording, posting, casting, carrying forward or summarising a transaction, or in applying an accounting principle.
**2.** A mistake in applying an accounting rule, such as treating a capital expenditure as a revenue expense. Example: wages paid for installing a machine debited to the wages account instead of being added to the machine's cost.
**3.** One of two or more errors, each of the same amount and on opposite sides, which cancel each other so that the trial balance still agrees.
**4.** One-sided errors: casting and balancing errors, posting to the wrong side, a wrong amount posted in one account only, partial omission.
**5.** Two-sided errors: errors of principle, complete omission, compensating errors, wrong account of the same class, a transaction entered twice in both accounts.
**6.** A suspense account is a temporary account opened to hold the difference in the trial balance until the errors causing it are found and corrected.
**7.** On the credit side.
**8.** An error of principle.
**9.** On the assets side.
**10.** *Rent A/c Dr. 6,000; To Wages A/c 6,000.*
:::

## 2.13 Internal Control and Auditing

**Facts.** Internal control = segregation of duties, authorisation, documentation, physical control, reconciliation, supervision, rotation, internal audit. Internal audit is by an employee, continuous, reporting to the management; final audit is by an independent outsider after the year end. The Office of the Auditor General is the apex audit body; its report goes to the President, then to Parliament's Public Accounts Committee.

1. What is internal control? <span class="marks">[1]</span>

4. What is an audit? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
5. What is an internal audit? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
6. What is a final audit? <span class="marks">[1]</span>

9. Name the parliamentary committee that examines the audit report. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
10. Who appoints the Auditor General of Nepal? <span class="marks">[1]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** Internal control is the whole system of policies, procedures, records and checks adopted by an organisation to keep its work accurate, efficient, lawful and honest and to safeguard its assets.
**2.** Any two of: to safeguard assets; to keep the records accurate and complete; to secure efficiency; to ensure compliance with the law; to prevent and detect fraud and error; to make an audit possible.
**3.** Any two of: segregation of duties; authorisation; documentation; physical control; reconciliation; supervision; rotation and compulsory leave; internal audit; budget comparison.
**4.** An audit is an independent examination of the books, records and supporting documents of an organisation so that the auditor may express an opinion on whether the financial statements show a true and fair view.
**5.** An audit carried out by the organisation's own employees, continuously, to test whether the internal control system is working and whether the records are accurate and the rules followed.
**6.** The audit carried out after the year end on the completed books and final accounts by an independent external auditor, ending in a report with an opinion.
**7.** Any two of: financial (regularity) audit, performance audit, management audit, propriety audit.
**8.** The Office of the Auditor General (महालेखापरीक्षकको कार्यालय).
**9.** The Public Accounts Committee (सार्वजनिक लेखा समिति).
**10.** The President, on the recommendation of the Constitutional Council.
