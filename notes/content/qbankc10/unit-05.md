Set them as a **timed examination**: three hours, no book, no notes. Then mark them yourself against the key and write down every mark you lost and why. A model paper answered under exam conditions is worth more than three read at leisure.

11. Write the number of the bank cash book format. <span class="marks">[1]</span>

::: note Answers to Group A
**1.** Office management is the task of planning, organising, directing, coordinating and controlling the work of an office so that its objectives are achieved efficiently and economically.
**2.** 26 April 1956 A.D. (13 Baisakh 2013 B.S.).
**3.** Value Added Tax.
**4.** The complete, organised and numbered list of all the account heads used to record transactions, grouped into assets, liabilities, capital, income and expenses.
**5.** A statement prepared on a date to explain and reconcile the difference between the balance of the bank column of the cash book and the balance of the passbook.
**6.** A mistake in applying an accounting rule, such as treating a capital expenditure as a revenue expense — for example, wages paid for installing a machine debited to the wages account.
**7.** $Cost\ of\ goods\ sold = Opening\ stock + Purchases + Direct\ expenses - Closing\ stock$.
**8.** The government's main fund, into which all revenue must be deposited and from which nothing may be withdrawn except under an appropriation made by law.
**9.** 14224.
**10.** *Construction expenditure A/c Dr. (full bill); To Contractors' retention A/c (amount withheld); To Bank / DTCO A/c (net paid).*
**11.** म.ले.प.फा.नं. २०९ (A.G.F. No. 209).

### Group B — Short answer questions (8 × 5 = 40)

1. Explain any five importance of office management in a business organisation. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
2. What is insurance? Explain the principle on which insurance works and name any three kinds of non-life insurance. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
3. Distinguish between direct tax and indirect tax with suitable examples. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
4. From the following, compute the gross profit and the net profit. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
   Opening stock Rs. 30,000; purchases Rs. 1,80,000; carriage inward Rs. 8,000; wages Rs. 22,000; closing stock Rs. 40,000; sales Rs. 2,60,000; office salary Rs. 20,000; rent Rs. 10,000; advertisement Rs. 6,000; discount received Rs. 5,000.
5. The cash book of a firm showed a bank balance of Rs. 48,000 on 30 Chaitra. Cheques issued but not presented Rs. 15,000; cheques deposited but not collected Rs. 9,000; bank charges Rs. 800; interest allowed by the bank Rs. 2,300; a customer's direct deposit Rs. 6,000. Prepare the bank reconciliation statement. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
6. Rectify the following errors found after the preparation of the trial balance and state the effect on the trial balance. (a) The purchases book was undercast by Rs. 4,000. (b) A sale of Rs. 6,000 to Ram was posted to Ram's account as Rs. 600. (c) Rent paid Rs. 5,000 was debited to the wages account. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
7. What is expenditure accounting? Explain the codes used in expenditure accounting. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
8. Distinguish between commercial accounting and government accounting on any five points. <span class="marks">[5]</span>

::: note Answers to Group B
**1.** (i) *Speed of work* — a laid-out office with a written procedure clears a file quickly. (ii) *Accuracy of records* — systematic recording answers any question about a past transaction. (iii) *Coordination* — every department works from the same information. (iv) *Cost control* — the right stationery, furniture and staffing keep the cost per transaction low. (v) *Goodwill* — prompt and accurate replies win the confidence of customers and citizens. (Also: decision making, legal protection.)
**2.** Insurance is a contract in which the insurer, in return for a premium, agrees to pay the insured if a stated event happens. *Principle:* pooling of risk — many persons exposed to a similar risk each pay a small premium into a common fund, from which the few who suffer a loss are paid, so the loss of one is spread over the group; the conditions are many similar exposures, a loss measurable in money, and an insurable interest. *Kinds:* fire, marine, motor, health, accident, crop and livestock. (Any three.)
**3.** *Direct tax:* levied on income or property; borne by the person legally liable; cannot be shifted; collected directly from the taxpayer; examples income tax, house and land tax, capital gains tax. *Indirect tax:* levied on goods, services and transactions; collected by the seller and paid over; the burden is shifted to the final consumer; raises the price of the goods; examples VAT, excise duty, customs duty. State that the test is who **bears** the burden.
**4.** Net purchases = Rs. 1,80,000. Cost of goods sold $= 30{,}000 + 1{,}80{,}000 + 8{,}000 + 22{,}000 - 40{,}000 = Rs.\ 2{,}00{,}000$. Gross profit $= 2{,}60{,}000 - 2{,}00{,}000 = Rs.\ 60{,}000$. Indirect expenses $= 20{,}000 + 10{,}000 + 6{,}000 = Rs.\ 36{,}000$. Net profit $= 60{,}000 + 5{,}000 - 36{,}000 = Rs.\ 29{,}000$.

**6.** (a) The purchases book undercast means the debit side is short: *Purchases A/c Dr. 4,000; To Suspense A/c 4,000* — one-sided, the trial balance did not agree. (b) *Ram A/c Dr. 5,400; To Suspense A/c 5,400* — one-sided. (c) *Rent A/c Dr. 5,000; To Wages A/c 5,000* — two-sided, the trial balance agreed. The suspense account carries Rs. 9,400 on the credit side until it is cleared.
**7.** Expenditure accounting records what a government office spends against its approved budget head, the advances it gives and recovers, the deductions it holds and its bank balance. *Codes:* the organisational classification says who spends; the economic classification says what — **2** recurrent (21111 remuneration, 21112 allowances, 21121 office materials, 22111 uniform, 22311 travelling expenses, 22612 fuel, 22711 miscellaneous) and **3** capital (31111 machinery and equipment, 31121 furniture and fixtures, 31131 vehicles).

(Any five.)

### Group B — Short answer questions (8 × 5 = 40)

1. What is filing? Explain any five essential features of a good filing system. <span class="marks">[5]</span>

::: note Answers to Group B
**1.** Filing is the systematic arrangement and storage of documents so that any document can be traced and produced when needed. Features: simplicity; prompt location; economy; flexibility; safety; accessibility; compactness; cross-reference. (Any five explained.)
**2.** A central bank is the apex financial institution, dealing with the government and the banks rather than the public; Nepal Rastra Bank, established 26 April 1956 A.D. (2013 B.S.). Five functions: currency issue; banker to the government; banker's bank and clearing house; lender of last resort; monetary policy. (Also foreign exchange management, supervision of banks, promotion of the financial sector.)
**3.** VAT is an indirect tax on the value added at each stage of production and distribution; in Nepal from 1 Mangsir 2054 B.S., under the VAT Act 2052 and Rules 2053, at a single rate of 13 per cent. Every registered dealer charges tax on his sales (output tax) and pays tax on his purchases (input tax), and deposits output tax minus input tax, so that tax falls only on the value he added and the total equals the VAT on the final price.
**4.** *Closing stock* — credited in the trading account, current asset. *Outstanding salary* — added to salary in the profit and loss account, current liability. *Prepaid rent* — deducted from rent, current asset. *Depreciation* — debited to the profit and loss account, deducted from the asset. *Interest on capital* — debited to the profit and loss account, added to capital. *Goods taken by the proprietor* — credited to purchases, deducted from capital as drawings. (Any five, each with both effects.)

Retailer: output $13\% \times 2{,}60{,}000 = 33{,}800$, input 31,200, deposit **Rs. 2,600**. Total $= 26{,}000 + 5{,}200 + 2{,}600 = Rs.\ 33{,}800 = 13\% \times 2{,}60{,}000$. ✔

**7.** Retention $= 5\% \times 5{,}00{,}000 = Rs.\ 25{,}000$; net paid $= Rs.\ 4{,}75{,}000$.

**3.** *Store accounting (1).* The branch of government accounting that records the receipt, storage, issue and balance of goods held by a government office, in quantity and in value; goods are consumable (stationery, fuel, medicines) or durable (furniture, computers, vehicles).
*Objectives (2).* To record every receipt and issue with evidence; to show the stock balance at any time; to prevent loss, misuse and theft; to provide audit evidence; to help budgeting.
*Importance (2).* Public goods remain public property; every article is tied to a document and a person; the honest officer is protected; it supports the annual verification and the stock statement.
*Formats (3).* जिन्सी दाखिला फाराम (goods received), जिन्सी निकासा फाराम (issue), the store ledger with a continuous balance, the bin card, the stock statement (जिन्सी मौज्दात फाँटबारी), the verification form (जिन्सी जाँच फाराम) and the accounting voucher.
