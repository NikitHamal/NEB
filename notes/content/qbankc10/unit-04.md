Group C of the Accountancy paper is three questions of eight marks each, and in almost every paper at least one of them is a **full numerical** — a final-accounts problem, a bank cash book or a statement of expenditure — with a theoretical question offered as its alternative. Group B, too, regularly carries a bank reconciliation statement or a set of rectifying entries. This chapter is therefore a **drill book**: ten numericals of the kinds that actually appear, each worked out step by step with the table the examiner expects, followed by four Group C theoretical questions with answer outlines.

::: key How to use this chapter
Work each numerical **against the clock** — a Group C numerical should take about twenty minutes — and only then read the solution. Mark every line you got wrong on a single sheet. In the exam, the marks for a numerical are awarded for the **table**, the **starting figure**, the **treatment of each item** and the **arithmetic**, in that order: a correct method with one arithmetic slip still earns most of the marks. Each numerical is an original practice question.
:::

## 4.1 Bank reconciliation statement from the cash book balance

**Problem.** The cash book of Sagarmatha Traders showed a bank balance of Rs. 84,000 on 30 Chaitra. On comparing it with the passbook the following differences were found.
i. Cheques of Rs. 22,000 were issued but not presented for payment.
ii. Cheques of Rs. 14,000 were deposited but not yet collected by the bank.
iii. Bank charges of Rs. 1,200 had been debited by the bank only.
iv. Interest on investment of Rs. 8,000 had been collected by the bank and appeared only in the passbook.
v. A customer had deposited Rs. 12,000 directly into the bank, not recorded in the cash book.
vi. A cheque of Rs. 6,000 deposited earlier had been dishonoured.

**Required.** Prepare the bank reconciliation statement.

::: note Solution
Step 1 — Start with the cash book balance: Rs. 84,000.
Step 2 — Add the items that make the bank's record higher than the trader's: cheques issued but not presented (Rs. 22,000), interest collected by the bank (Rs. 8,000), direct deposit by a customer (Rs. 12,000).
Step 3 — Less the items that make the bank's record lower: cheques deposited but not collected (Rs. 14,000), bank charges (Rs. 1,200), cheque dishonoured (Rs. 6,000).

| Particulars | Rs. | Rs. |
|---|---|---|
| Balance as per cash book | | 84,000 |
| Add: cheques issued but not presented for payment | 22,000 | |
| Add: interest on investment collected by the bank | 8,000 | |
| Add: amount deposited directly by a customer | 12,000 | 42,000 |
| | | 1,26,000 |
| Less: cheques deposited but not yet collected | 14,000 | |
| Less: bank charges debited by the bank | 1,200 | |
| Less: cheque deposited but dishonoured | 6,000 | 21,200 |
| **Balance as per passbook** | | **1,04,800** |

**Answer.** Balance as per passbook Rs. 1,04,800.
:::

## 4.2 Bank reconciliation with an adjusted cash book

**Problem.** On 31 Ashad the cash book of Himal Traders showed a bank balance of Rs. 42,000. On comparison with the passbook the following were found.
i. The cash book showed a payment of Rs. 9,000 for insurance, but the bank had paid Rs. 10,000 on a standing instruction.
ii. Bank charges of Rs. 600 and interest on overdraft of Rs. 900 had been debited by the bank only.
iii. A customer had deposited Rs. 7,500 directly into the bank.
iv. A cheque of Rs. 4,000 deposited earlier had been dishonoured.
v. Cheques of Rs. 18,000 issued had not been presented; cheques of Rs. 9,000 deposited had not been collected.

**Required.** Prepare the adjusted cash book and the bank reconciliation statement.

::: note Solution
Step 1 — Items appearing **only in the passbook** adjust the cash book: the extra Rs. 1,000 of insurance paid, the bank charges, the interest on overdraft, the customer's direct deposit and the dishonoured cheque.
Step 2 — Items that are **timing differences** stay in the reconciliation: cheques issued but not presented and cheques deposited but not collected.

*Adjusted cash book (bank column)*

| Particulars | Rs. | Particulars | Rs. |
|---|---|---|---|
| To Balance b/d | 42,000 | By Insurance (additional paid by bank) | 1,000 |
| To Customer's direct deposit | 7,500 | By Bank charges | 600 |
| | | By Interest on overdraft | 900 |
| | | By Cheque dishonoured | 4,000 |
| | | By Balance c/d (adjusted) | 43,000 |
| **Total** | **49,500** | **Total** | **49,500** |

Step 3 — Adjusted balance $= 49{,}500 - 6{,}500 = Rs.\ 43{,}000$.
Step 4 — Reconcile to the passbook.

*Bank reconciliation statement as on 31 Ashad*

| Particulars | Rs. | Rs. |
|---|---|---|
| Balance as per adjusted cash book | | 43,000 |
| Add: cheques issued but not presented | | 18,000 |
| | | 61,000 |
| Less: cheques deposited but not collected | | 9,000 |
| **Balance as per passbook** | | **52,000** |

**Answer.** Adjusted cash book Rs. 43,000; balance as per passbook Rs. 52,000.
:::

## 4.3 Rectification of errors and the suspense account

**Problem.** The following errors were discovered **after** the trial balance had been prepared.
i. Salary paid Rs. 20,000 was wrongly debited to the house rent account.
ii. The sales book was overcast by Rs. 5,000.
iii. A purchase of machinery of Rs. 40,000 was debited to the purchases account.
iv. A sale of Rs. 7,000 to Bina was posted to Bina's account as Rs. 700.
v. Wages paid Rs. 3,000 for the erection of a machine were debited to the wages account.

**Required.** Pass the rectifying entries, state the effect on the trial balance and prepare the suspense account.

::: note Solution
Step 1 — (i) Two expense accounts, both amounts equal: *Salary A/c Dr. 20,000; To House Rent A/c 20,000.* Two-sided — the trial balance agreed.
Step 2 — (ii) The sales book overcast means the credit side is too high: *Sales A/c Dr. 5,000; To Suspense A/c 5,000.* One-sided — the suspense account was credited with the difference.
Step 3 — (iii) An error of principle: *Machinery A/c Dr. 40,000; To Purchases A/c 40,000.* Two-sided — the trial balance agreed, but profit is understated by Rs. 40,000 and machinery is understated.
Step 4 — (iv) Bina's account was debited Rs. 700 instead of Rs. 7,000, short by Rs. 6,300: *Bina A/c Dr. 6,300; To Suspense A/c 6,300.* One-sided.
Step 5 — (v) An error of principle: *Machinery A/c Dr. 3,000; To Wages A/c 3,000.* Two-sided.

*Suspense account*

| Particulars | Rs. | Particulars | Rs. |
|---|---|---|---|
| To Balance b/d (if any) | — | By Sales A/c (overcast) | 5,000 |
| | | By Bina A/c (short posting) | 6,300 |
| **Total** | **11,300** | **Total** | **11,300** |

**Answer.** The debit column of the trial balance was short by Rs. 11,300, carried in the credit of the suspense account and now cleared by the two one-sided corrections. The three two-sided errors (i), (iii) and (v) did not affect the trial balance at all.
:::

## 4.6 Bank cash book (A.G.F. No. 209)

**Problem.** The following transactions took place in the bank account of a District Coordination Unit during the month of Shrawan 2082. There was no opening balance.
i. 04/02 — budget release received from the Treasury and Comptroller Office, Rs. 6,00,000.
ii. 04/08 — payment order issued against 22311 office materials, Rs. 45,000.
iii. 04/12 — advance of Rs. 70,000 given to a section officer against 31111 for the purchase of a computer.
iv. 04/20 — the advance was cleared on a bill of Rs. 82,000, the excess being paid by payment order.
v. 04/25 — salary payment order for the month, total salary Rs. 2,40,000, after deducting provident fund Rs. 30,000 and income tax Rs. 5,000.
vi. 04/28 — the provident fund and income tax deducted were deposited.

**Required.** Prepare the bank cash book and state the closing balance.

::: note Solution
Step 1 — The release is money received: debit Rs. 6,00,000.
Step 2 — Office materials Rs. 45,000 and the advance Rs. 70,000 are payments: credits.
Step 3 — On clearance, only the excess of Rs. 82,000 − Rs. 70,000 = Rs. 12,000 is a fresh payment; the rest clears the advance.
Step 4 — Salary: only the net Rs. 2,40,000 − 30,000 − 5,000 = Rs. 2,05,000 leaves the bank.
Step 5 — The deductions deposited, Rs. 35,000, also leave the bank.

| Date | Voucher no. | Particulars | Debit (Rs.) | Credit (Rs.) | Balance (Rs.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 04/02 | 1 | Budget release received from the DTCO | 6,00,000 | | 6,00,000 |
| 04/08 | 2 | Payment order — 22311 office materials | | 45,000 | 5,55,000 |
| 04/12 | 3 | Advance to section officer — 31111 computer | | 70,000 | 4,85,000 |
| 04/20 | 4 | Excess of the computer bill over the advance (82,000 − 70,000) | | 12,000 | 4,73,000 |
| 04/25 | 5 | Salary of Shrawan, net of provident fund Rs. 30,000 and income tax Rs. 5,000 | | 2,05,000 | 2,68,000 |
| 04/28 | 6 | Provident fund and income tax deposited | | 35,000 | 2,33,000 |
| 04/30 | | Balance c/d | | 2,33,000 | — |
| | | **Total** | **6,00,000** | **6,00,000** | |

**Answer.** Total receipts Rs. 6,00,000; total payments Rs. 3,67,000; closing balance Rs. 2,33,000 (debit), subject to reconciliation with the passbook. Note that the computer head 31111 finally carries Rs. 82,000 of expenditure, while only Rs. 12,000 of it was paid after the advance.
:::

## 4.8 Government journal vouchers

**Problem.** Pass the journal vouchers for the following transactions of a District Administration Office.

ii. An advance of Rs. 90,000 was given to a section officer against 31121 for the purchase of furniture.

::: note Solution

## 4.9 Retention accounting

**Problem.** A contractor's running bill for a road contract is Rs. 6,00,000. A retention of 5 per cent is withheld. Later, a defect costing Rs. 18,000 is not remedied by the contractor. Show the journal vouchers (a) when the bill is passed, (b) when the retention is released in full if no defect appears, and (c) when the retention is adjusted and the balance forfeited.

::: note Solution
Step 1 — Retention $= 5\% \times 6{,}00{,}000 = Rs.\ 30{,}000$; net paid $= Rs.\ 5{,}70{,}000$.
Step 2 — (a) *Road construction expenditure A/c Dr. 6,00,000; To Contractors' retention A/c 30,000; To Bank / DTCO A/c 5,70,000.* (Being the running bill passed and retention of 5 per cent withheld.)
Step 3 — (b) *Contractors' retention A/c Dr. 30,000; To Bank A/c 30,000.* (Being the retention released on the expiry of the defect liability period.)
Step 4 — (c) *Contractors' retention A/c Dr. 30,000; To Repair and maintenance expenditure A/c 18,000; To Miscellaneous revenue A/c 12,000.* (Being the retention adjusted against the cost of remedying the defect and the balance forfeited to government revenue.)
**Answer.** Retention Rs. 30,000 in each case; on forfeiture Rs. 18,000 is set against the repair expenditure and Rs. 12,000 becomes revenue. Until it is released or forfeited, the retention is a **liability**, not revenue.
:::

## 4.10 Revenue statement

| Head | Description | Collected (Rs.) | Undeposited (Rs.) |

## 4.11 VAT chain

**Problem.** A producer sells goods to a wholesaler for Rs. 3,00,000, the wholesaler sells them to a retailer for Rs. 3,60,000 and the retailer sells them to the consumer for Rs. 4,00,000, all prices being exclusive of VAT. The VAT rate is 13 per cent. Compute the VAT deposited by each dealer and the total received by the government, and verify the total.

::: note Solution

| Stage | Sale price (Rs.) | Value added (Rs.) | Output tax (Rs.) | Input tax (Rs.) | Deposited (Rs.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Producer | 3,00,000 | 3,00,000 | 39,000 | — | 39,000 |
| Wholesaler | 3,60,000 | 60,000 | 46,800 | 39,000 | 7,800 |
| Retailer | 4,00,000 | 40,000 | 52,000 | 46,800 | 5,200 |
| **Total** | | **4,00,000** | | | **52,000** |

Step 1 — Producer: $13\% \times 3{,}00{,}000 = 39{,}000$.
Step 2 — Wholesaler: output $13\% \times 3{,}60{,}000 = 46{,}800$; less input 39,000 → 7,800.
Step 3 — Retailer: output $13\% \times 4{,}00{,}000 = 52{,}000$; less input 46,800 → 5,200.
Step 4 — Verification: $39{,}000 + 7{,}800 + 5{,}200 = 52{,}000 = 13\% \times 4{,}00{,}000$. ✔
**Answer.** Producer Rs. 39,000; wholesaler Rs. 7,800; retailer Rs. 5,200; total Rs. 52,000, borne entirely by the final consumer.
:::

## 4.13 Group C theoretical questions

1. What is a bank reconciliation statement? Explain its need and the causes that make the cash book and the passbook differ, and show the usual format starting from the cash book balance. <span class="marks">[8]</span>
2. What is an accounting error? Explain the types of errors and state which of them are detected by the trial balance and which are not. <span class="marks">[8]</span>
3. What are final accounts? Explain the steps in their preparation and the treatment of any five adjustments, stating in each case the two effects. <span class="marks">[8]</span>
4. What is a bank cash book? Explain its columns and the rules for its preparation, and state which items are entered on the debit side and which on the credit side. <span class="marks">[8]</span>

::: note Answer outlines
**1.** *Definition (1).* A statement prepared on a date to explain and reconcile the difference between the bank column of the cash book and the passbook. *Need (3).* To check the accuracy of both records and trace errors; to find the true bank balance; to detect dishonoured cheques, unrecorded charges and direct deposits; to prepare the balance sheet; to prevent fraud; to satisfy the auditor and the bank. *Causes (2).* Timing — cheques issued but not presented, cheques deposited but not collected. Bank-only entries — bank charges, interest, dividend collected, direct deposits, standing instructions, dishonoured cheques. Errors in either book. *Format (2).* Start with the cash book balance; add cheques issued but not presented, interest and dividends collected, direct deposits and wrong credits by the bank; less cheques deposited but not collected, bank charges and interest on overdraft, dishonoured cheques, standing instructions and wrong debits; arrive at the passbook balance. **2.** *Definition (1).* A mistake in recording, posting, casting, carrying forward or summarising a transaction, or in applying an accounting principle. *Types (4).* Errors of principle (capital expenditure treated as revenue and the reverse); errors of omission (complete and partial); errors of commission (wrong amount, wrong account, wrong side, wrong casting, wrong balance carried forward); compensating errors. Explain each with an example. *Detection (3).* The trial balance detects only **one-sided** errors — casting and balancing errors, posting to the wrong side, a wrong amount posted in one account, partial omission. It cannot detect **two-sided** errors — errors of principle, complete omission, compensating errors, the wrong account of the same class and a transaction entered twice in both accounts. Conclude that the trial balance proves arithmetical accuracy only.
**3.** *Definition (1).* The statements prepared at the end of the period from the trial balance after adjustments, to show the profit or loss and the financial position. *Steps (3).* Complete and agree the trial balance; bring in the closing entries; prepare the trading account and find the gross profit; prepare the profit and loss account and find the net profit; add the net profit to capital and deduct drawings; prepare the balance sheet; check that the two sides agree. *Adjustments (4).* Explain any five out of: closing stock, outstanding expense, prepaid expense, accrued income, income received in advance, depreciation, interest on capital, interest on drawings, goods taken by the proprietor, provision for doubtful debts, manager's commission — stating in each case the effect in the trading or profit and loss account **and** the effect in the balance sheet. Emphasise that every adjustment has two effects, and that a missing second effect is the commonest reason for a balance sheet not tallying.
