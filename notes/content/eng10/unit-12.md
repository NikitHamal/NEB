Human beings change nature in order to live better: they cut roads through hills, build cities and plant farms. Poets, on the other hand, often stop to admire what nature and cities look like when they are calm. This unit puts both views side by side. The first text is a factual article on the **Kathmandu–Terai/Madhesh Fast Track**, one of Nepal's biggest development projects. The second is a famous poem by William Wordsworth, "Composed upon Westminster Bridge", in which a great city looks as peaceful as a field. The grammar of the unit is **talking about the future**: *will, be going to, the future continuous and the future perfect*. The writing tasks are a **report** on a development work and a **notice**.

::: key What the examiner wants from this unit
From the article: facts (distance, benefits, challenges) and your opinion in a full-sentence discussion. From the poem: the speaker's feeling, the figures of speech and the central theme. From grammar: the right future form for plans, predictions, schedules and actions that will be in progress or complete. From writing: a report with a title, introduction, findings and conclusion, and a notice with all the necessary details.
:::

## 12.1 Kathmandu–Terai/Madhesh Fast Track

### About the text

This is an **informative article** about a **mega project**: a new expressway meant to link Kathmandu to the Terai (Madhesh) plains in much less time. The writer describes what the project is, how long it is, who will gain from it and what could go wrong. It is a good example of the kind of formal, factual prose that appears in the unseen reading section of the SEE paper.

### Summary

**What it is.** The Kathmandu–Terai/Madhesh Fast Track (KTFT) is a high-speed expressway being built from **Khokana** in Lalitpur to **Nijgadh** in Bara district, a length of about **72.5 kilometres**. It is being built under the supervision of the Nepal Army and is regarded as a project of national pride. Until now the journey from the capital to the Terai has depended on old hill highways with sharp curves, landslides and traffic jams. The fast track will replace many of these hours with a short, direct drive through the hills and forest of the Mahabharat range.

**Benefits.** The article lists several advantages.

- **Shorter travel time and less fuel.** Because travel time falls, Nepal will use less petrol and diesel, which are imported, and save foreign money.
- **Cheaper transport and trade.** Goods can be carried between India, the Terai and Kathmandu faster and at lower cost, reducing the congestion on the older roads.
- **Tourism.** Travel becomes cheaper, so Indian tourists, among others, can reach Kathmandu more easily than now.
- **Skilled people.** The project gives engineers, technicians and workers chances to practise and sharpen their skills.
- **Regional development.** People of the eastern Terai gain better access to health, education, jobs and markets in Kathmandu.

**Challenges.** The writer says the main challenge after completion will be to build **link roads** connecting the fast track to towns and highways, so that its benefits reach ordinary people. The writer also says that the success of the project depends chiefly on **technology**: the track needs bridges, tunnels, cuttings and careful engineering in a young, landslide-prone mountain range. The text also points to environmental risk, as the clearing of forests and hills may cause erosion, landslides and loss of biodiversity.

## 12.2 Composed Upon Westminster Bridge, September 3, 1802

### The poet

**William Wordsworth** (1770–1850) was an English poet born in Cockermouth in the Lake District. With his friend Samuel Taylor Coleridge he published *Lyrical Ballads* (1798), which started the **Romantic movement** in English poetry. Romantic poets valued feeling, imagination and nature over formal, artificial language. Wordsworth's best-known long poem is *The Prelude*, and among his short poems are "Daffodils" ("I wandered lonely as a cloud") and "Tintern Abbey". He became Poet Laureate in 1843. He believed that poetry should use "the real language of men" and that nature is a teacher of goodness.

### The poem

"Composed upon Westminster Bridge" is a **sonnet**: a poem of fourteen lines, with a fixed rhyme scheme. Wordsworth wrote it in 1802 while crossing the Thames in the early morning on his way to France; the title gives 3 September, though his sister Dorothy's journal suggests that the view was actually seen in July, so the date in the title may be inaccurate. The poem was published in 1807.

> Earth has not anything to show more fair: …
> Ne'er saw I, never felt, a calm so deep!
> The river glideth at his own sweet will: …
> And all that mighty heart is lying still!

### Summary and explanation

**Lines 1–3: a challenge.** The speaker says that the earth has nothing more beautiful to show than this view. Anyone who could pass such a touching sight without feeling it would be "dull of soul".

**Lines 4–8: the city dressed in morning.** The city wears the beauty of the morning like a garment. At that early hour it is silent and bare: ships, towers, domes, theatres and temples lie open to the fields and the sky. Because it is dawn, there is no coal smoke, and everything shines in the clear air.

**Lines 9–11: the sun and the calm.** The sun has never so beautifully spread his first light over valley, rock or hill. The speaker has never seen or felt a calm so deep.

**Lines 12–14: the sleeping heart.** The river glides at its own sweet will. The very houses seem asleep. And "that mighty heart", the whole great city, lies still.

### Figures of speech

| Device | Example | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Simile | "like a garment" | The city *wears* the morning, the way a person wears clothes. |
| Personification | "the river glideth at his own sweet will"; "houses seem asleep"; "mighty heart" | The river, the houses and the city seem alive. |
| Metaphor | "mighty heart" for London | The city is the heart of the nation, now at rest. |
| Apostrophe | "Dear God!" | A sudden cry that shows deep emotion. |
| Inversion | "Ne'er saw I" | Unusual word order that gives a dignified tone. |
| Imagery | "bright and glittering in the smokeless air" | Visual images make the morning bright. |

### Theme and tone

The central idea is that **beauty is not found only in the countryside**: a city, too, can be beautiful and peaceful when seen at sunrise, before the noise begins. The tone is **calm, reverent and full of wonder**. Wordsworth, who normally celebrates fields and mountains, is surprised and moved to find that "man-made" London can please him as much as nature does. The poem also suggests that nature and human works can live in harmony when pollution and bustle are absent.

::: caution Do not call it a poem about the countryside
The poem is set in a **city**. Its point is that the city shares in the beauty of the fields and the sky. Students who write "the poet praises the countryside" lose the marks.
:::

## 12.3 Grammar and writing tasks

### Grammar I and II — Talking about the future

English has no single future tense. It has several ways to talk about the future, and the right one depends on the meaning.

| Form | Structure | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Present simple** | V1 / V1+s | timetables, schedules | The exam **starts** at 11 o'clock. |
| **Present continuous** | am/is/are + V-ing | firm personal arrangements | We **are leaving** in ten minutes. |
| **be going to** | am/is/are going to + V1 | plans made before; predictions from present evidence | I **am going to** visit my uncle. Look at those clouds! It **is going to** rain. |
| **will + V1** | will / won't + V1 | instant decisions, offers, promises, predictions from opinion | The phone is ringing. I **will** answer it. I think he **will** be rich one day. |
| **Future continuous** | will be + V-ing | an action in progress at a given future time | At this time next week we **will be flying** to Dubai. |
| **Future perfect** | will have + V3 | an action completed *before* a future time | We **will have arrived** home by Sunday. |
| **Future perfect continuous** | will have been + V-ing | how long an activity will have lasted by a future time | By June I **will have been living** here for ten years. |

```figure caption="Which future? Where the action sits relative to a future moment."
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(5.0,2.7))
ax.plot([0,10],[0,0],color=INK,lw=1.4)
ax.plot([0],[0],'o',color=INK,ms=6); ax.text(0,0.18,'now',ha='center',fontsize=8,color=INK)
ax.plot([7],[0],'o',color=ACCENT,ms=7); ax.text(7,0.18,'future time\n(next Sunday, 5 p.m.)',ha='center',fontsize=7.4,color=ACCENT,linespacing=1.3)
ax.fill_between([6.1,7.9],[-0.12]*2,[0.12]*2,color=ACCENT,alpha=0.35)

ax.annotate('',xy=(6.85,-1.05),xytext=(2.0,-1.05),arrowprops=dict(arrowstyle='->',color=MUTED,lw=1))
ax.text(4.4,-1.2,'future perfect: will have finished (done BEFORE then)',ha='center',va='top',fontsize=7.4,color=INK)
ax.text(3.0,0.55,'will / going to: decide and predict',ha='center',fontsize=7.4,color=MUTED)
ax.set_xlim(-0.6,10.6); ax.set_ylim(-2.0,1.6); ax.axis('off')
```

**Rules to remember**

1. After *if, when, as soon as, before, after, until*, use the **present** form, not *will*: *If you touch the stove, you will burn yourself.*
2. *Will* is used for sudden decisions; *going to* for decisions made earlier.
3. State verbs (know, like, want) are rarely continuous: *will know*, not *will be knowing*.
4. The future perfect needs a deadline: *by tomorrow, by 2030, by the time you arrive*.
5. The continuous future gives a polite question: *Will you be using the car tonight?*

::: example Worked example 12.1 — choose the form
**Problem.** Fill in: "Tennis practice starts at 10 and we plan to play for two hours. By 12 o'clock we ______ (practise) for two hours."

**Solution.** The sentence looks at a future deadline (12 o'clock) and describes the length of an activity up to that point, so the answer is *will have practised* (or *will have been practising*).
:::

::: example Worked example 12.2 — will or going to?
**Problem.** (a) "Look at those clouds. It ______ rain." (b) "The phone is ringing. I ______ answer it."

**Solution.** (a) There is **present evidence** (clouds), so *is going to rain*. (b) A **sudden decision** made at the moment, so *will answer*.
:::

::: caution Will after "if"
*If it will rain, we will stay home* is wrong. Write *If it rains, we will stay home.*
:::

### Writing I — A report

A **report** gives an organised account of something you have observed or studied. It is formal, in the third person and usually in the present or past tense. A report on a development work in your community should follow this layout:

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| **Title** | e.g. *Report on the Construction of the Ward Road* |
| **Introduction** | what, where, when, who is doing it |
| **Findings / body** | details: progress, cost, workers, methods; benefits and problems |
| **Conclusion / recommendations** | your judgement and suggestions |
| **Writer, date** | name, position and date |

**Model report.**

*Report on the Construction of the Ward Road in Our Locality*

*By: Sita Gurung, Secretary, Green Child Club — 12 Poush 2081*

**Introduction.** The local government of Ward 5 has been constructing a 2-kilometre blacktopped road linking our village to the main highway. Work began in Kartik and is about 60 per cent complete. As a member of the Child Club, I visited the site on three days and spoke to the engineer and some villagers.

**Findings.** About 40 local workers are employed. Stones and gravel are brought from the nearby river, and a roller levels the surface. Villagers say that earlier the muddy track was impassable in the rainy season. Drainage ditches are being dug, and trees cut during the work are being replaced with new saplings. However, the dust is troubling the school children and there is no fence to protect them from the road.

**Conclusion and recommendations.** The road will save time, make it easier for patients to reach the hospital and help farmers to sell vegetables. The contractor should water the road to reduce dust, put up signs near the school, and complete the work before the monsoon.

### Writing II — A notice

A **notice** is a short public message. It must be brief and complete: **name of issuing body, the word NOTICE, date, heading, body (what, when, where, who), name and designation of the writer.** Use no salutation or long sentences.

**Task.** As Secretary of your school's Child Club, write a notice about a programme you will organise soon.

**Model.**

**Sunrise Secondary School Child Club**

**NOTICE**

Date: 15 Magh 2081

**Clean Community Awareness Programme**

All students and teachers are informed that the Child Club will organise a community cleanliness and hygiene awareness programme on **Saturday, 25 Magh 2081 at 8:00 a.m.** in the school ground. The programme will include a rally, group discussion and a demonstration on handwashing and waste disposal. Students of Grades 6–10 are requested to attend in school uniform and to bring a pair of gloves.

Interested students may give their names to the Club Secretary by Thursday.

Sunita Rai
Secretary, Child Club

::: tip Notice checklist
Issuer — heading — date — event — time — place — who should attend — contact — signature. If any of these is missing, you lose marks.
:::
