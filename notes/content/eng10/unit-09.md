In grammar you will practise **narrating the past** with the simple past, past continuous and past perfect; in writing you will write about **historical events**, give an **opinion** and tell a **story from an outline**.

## 9.1 Pratap Malla

### About the text

"Pratap Malla" is a short **biographical** text about one of the best-known kings of the Kathmandu Valley. It is arranged around the king's achievements: **politics and diplomacy**, **trade**, **religion and architecture**, and **literature**. A text like this is **objective and factual**; it uses the simple past and gives dates, names and places.

```photo id="eng10/hanuman-dhoka" caption="Hanuman Dhoka Durbar Square, Kathmandu. Pratap Malla enlarged the old royal palace here and set an image of Hanuman at its gate." width="84"
```

### Who was Pratap Malla?

**Pratap Malla** (1624–1674) was the Malla king of **Kantipur** (Kathmandu), one of the three kingdoms of the Valley (the others were Lalitpur/Patan and Bhaktapur). He ruled from about **1641 to 1674**, a reign of more than thirty years, and is remembered as a ruler who was at once a clever politician, a patron of art, a builder and a poet.

### What the text says

**1. Coming to power.** The textbook tells us that Pratap Malla kept his own father in jail because he wanted to become king soon. *(This is how the text presents it; historians have different views about how he succeeded his father, Laxmi Narasimha Malla.)*

**2. Politics and defence.** Kantipur was a small state surrounded by two rivals, Lalitpur and Bhaktapur. Pratap Malla kept it strong and safe by **diplomacy**: he played the kings of the two other cities against each other, siding with one or the other to keep a balance of power and to **deter** attacks. During his reign, Kantipur was never invaded.

**3. Trade.** Kantipur lay on the trade route between **Tibet** and **India**. Pratap Malla used this position, trading with both, and the city became rich. Wealth paid for his buildings.

**4. Religion and building.** He repaired and rebuilt many temples and monuments. Among his projects are additions to the old palace — which he named **Hanuman Dhoka** after the image of the monkey god Hanuman that he placed beside the gate to ward off evil — and the **Rani Pokhari**, a large pond with a small temple in its middle, said to have been built in memory of his son. He also built or restored shrines at Swayambhunath and set up water spouts and ponds for the people. Many temples of his time had gold-covered pinnacles.

**5. Literature and learning.** Pratap Malla was a learned man, a poet and a dramatist. He wrote in **many languages**, among them Sanskrit, Newari (Nepal Bhasa), Maithili, Hindi and Bengali. On a wall of the palace is a famous inscription in many languages and scripts. He gave himself the title **Kavindra (Kabindra)**, "king of poets", and invited scholars and musicians to his court, which shows his respect for knowledge.

### Themes and ideas

- **Statecraft.** A small state can survive by clever politics rather than force.
- **Trade brings wealth.** Position on trade routes built Kantipur's prosperity.
- **Culture as legacy.** Hanuman Dhoka, Rani Pokhari and the inscription still exist and still draw visitors.
- **A complex ruler.** He was ambitious, even ruthless, but also a poet and builder.

## 9.2 Flowers in Russian Culture

### About the text

This is an **informative text about customs**. It shows how people in Russia choose flowers for different occasions and why. Its central idea is that giving flowers is almost universal, but each culture adds its own rules — what the textbook calls **flower etiquette**. The writer groups the information flower by flower: carnation, poppy, lily, chamomile and others.

### What the text says

Everywhere in the world people give flowers to express goodwill and affection, and receiving them is heart-warming. In many countries the receiver does not mind which flowers they are given, so long as they are beautiful and smell sweet. In **Russia**, however, flowers have meanings, and a wrong choice can offend. Following the flower etiquette therefore saves you from embarrassment.

| Flower | Meaning in Russian culture | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Red carnation | A symbol of patriotism; connected with the Soviet era and the Bolshevik party (and the 1917 revolution) | Given to veterans and on Victory Day (9 May) |
| White carnation | Believed to bring bad luck; it is not given to performers | Some Russians put one under the pillow in order to wake up inspired |
| Red poppy | A symbol of fragile, short-lived love and weakness, so it is not given to a beloved person | Poppy seeds are eaten at weddings as a symbol of happiness; in some traditions they are put in the shoes of the couple as a symbol of fertility |
| Yellow flowers | Often a sign of separation or break-up | Do not give them to someone you love |
| White lily | Linked with death; a white-lily bouquet at a wedding foreshadows a split in the newly-wed couple | Avoid at weddings |
| Chamomile | A healing flower with medicinal use; also symbol of love and purity | Used in teas and treatments |

*General background (not necessarily in the textbook):* in Russia, as in many countries of Eastern Europe, an **odd number** of flowers is given to the living and an **even number** is for funerals.

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|

| palette | a range of colours | The garden had a bright palette of reds and yellows. |

**Compound words in the text:** gift-giving, heart-warming, cardboard, flower-gifting, flower etiquette, break-up, fortune-telling, newly-wed. A **compound word** is made of two words joined together (sometimes with a hyphen): *classroom*, *sunflower*, *well-known*.

### Themes

- **Symbols and meaning.** A flower is more than a plant; it can carry history, belief and emotion.
- **Cultural difference.** What delights people in one country may offend in another, so travellers should learn local customs.
- **Respect.** Following etiquette shows respect for the receiver.
- **Nature and tradition.** Flowers connect every stage of life: birth, love, marriage, death and celebration.

## 9.3 Grammar and writing tasks of the unit

### Grammar I: Simple past and past continuous

| Tense | Form | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple past | verb-ed / irregular | a **completed** action at a definite past time; a series of actions | The alarm went off. We woke and saw the sun. |
| Past continuous | was/were + verb-ing | an action that was **in progress** at a past time | I was doing my homework. |

#### Using them together

- A **long action** in the past continuous is **interrupted** by a **short action** in the simple past, usually with **when** or **while**:
  - *The alarm **went** while Trishna **was sleeping**.*
  - *I **was doing** my homework when the telephone **rang**.*
- **While** is followed by the **continuous** (the long action); **when** often introduces the **short** action.
- Two actions that **happened one after another** are both in the simple past: *When the doors **opened**, the audience **rushed** into the stadium.* **As soon as** the teacher **asked** the question, Brinda **replied**.
- Two long actions at the same time are both continuous: *While I was cooking, my brother was watching TV.*


::: caution Common mistakes
Do not write *While I walked, I was seeing him* (mixed). Verbs of state (*know, like, see, hear, want, be*) are normally **not** used in the continuous: say *I saw*, not *I was seeing*. Do not use the past continuous for a completed action: *He was writing three letters yesterday* (wrong when the letters are all finished).
:::

::: example Worked example
**Problem.** Fill in: "The alarm (go) while Trishna (sleep)."

**Solution.** The alarm is the short action; sleeping is the long one. *The alarm went while Trishna was sleeping.*
:::

::: example Worked example
**Problem.** Fill in: "When we (wake), we (see) the sun shining brightly."

**Solution.** Two actions one after another → simple past. *When we woke, we saw the sun shining brightly.*
:::

### Grammar II: Past simple and past perfect in narrative

The **past perfect** (*had + past participle*) shows an action that happened **before another past action**. The later event is in the simple past.

| Sentence | Order of events |
|---|---|
| Ram **had bought** the ticket before he **went** to the cinema. | bought first, then went |
| When I **reached** the station, the train **had left**. | train left first, then I reached |
| The children **arrived** five minutes ago. | one past event: simple past |

**Signal words:** *before, after, by the time, already, just, never … before, when* (+ earlier event), *as soon as*. After *after*, either tense may be used: *After she had finished / finished the work, she went home.*

**Narrative tenses at a glance**

| Tense | Role in a story |
|---|---|
| Simple past | the main events in order |
| Past continuous | background and actions in progress |
| Past perfect | events that happened earlier than the main story |

::: example Worked example
**Problem.** Complete: "Ram ____ (buy) the ticket before he went to the cinema."

**Solution.** The buying came first → *had bought*.
:::

::: example Worked example
**Problem.** Complete the story: "A young woman entered a shop. She (put) food in a bag and (wait) until no one was looking. When the shopkeeper (check) the goods, she (give) him a thousand-rupee note."

**Solution.** *She put food in a bag and waited until no one was looking. When the shopkeeper checked the goods, she gave him a thousand-rupee note.* (a sequence of completed actions → simple past)
:::

### Writing I: Writing about historical events and giving an opinion

**(a) A paragraph from a list of events.** Turn a list of dates into connected sentences. Use the **simple past**, **time connectives** (*in, on, later, after that, meanwhile, finally*) and linking words (*as a result, however*). Keep the order of time. Name people and places exactly.

**Task.** Write a couple of paragraphs about Nepal's journey from the Panchayat system to multiparty democracy, using the events 2017 BS to 2046 BS.

::: example Model paragraphs
Nepal's journey from the Panchayat system to multiparty democracy was long and full of struggle. On 1 Poush 2017 BS, King Mahendra dismissed the elected government of Prime Minister B. P. Koirala, dissolved parliament, arrested the leaders and banned political parties. In 2019 BS a new constitution established the party-less Panchayat system, in which the king held the real power.

Opposition to the system continued. In 2036 BS student protests spread across the country, and King Birendra announced a national referendum. On 20 Baisakh 2037 BS the people were asked to choose between the reformed Panchayat system and a multiparty system, and a narrow majority voted to keep the reformed Panchayat. Dissatisfaction remained, however. In Falgun 2046 BS the Nepali Congress and the left parties started the People's Movement (Jana Andolan), and thousands of people went to the streets. After weeks of protest and many deaths, King Birendra lifted the ban on political parties in Chaitra 2046 BS. In this way, the party-less system ended and multiparty democracy returned to Nepal.
:::

*(The dates above are the widely accepted ones; if your textbook list gives different details, follow the list in your book.)*

**(b) An opinion essay (about 200 words).** State your position in the first paragraph; give two or three reasons with examples; consider the opposite view briefly; and end with a strong conclusion.

**Task.** Some people think that studying history is a waste of time; others think it is essential. What do you think?

::: example Model essay
**Why We Must Study History**

Some people say that history is only a list of dead kings and old dates. I disagree. I believe that studying history is essential.

First, history teaches us lessons. By learning how people succeeded or failed in the past, we can avoid repeating their mistakes. The story of the Rana regime and the movements that ended it, for example, shows why freedom and equal rights matter.

Second, history gives us an identity. When we know who built Hanuman Dhoka or how Nepal became one country, we understand and respect our culture and we take pride in it. A person who does not know the past is like a tree without roots.

Third, history improves our thinking. It teaches us to compare evidence, to ask why things happened and to judge fairly.

It is true that some students find history boring when it is taught only by memorising dates. However, this is a problem of method, not of the subject. Visits to museums, old temples and stories make history live.

In conclusion, history is not a waste of time. It guides the present and helps to build a wiser future.
:::

### Writing II: A story from outlines

To write a story from outlines: (1) read all the cues and decide the **order**; (2) write in the **past tense**; (3) make a **beginning** (setting and characters), a **middle** (the problem and how it grows) and an **end** (the result); (4) use **connectives** (*once upon a time, one day, then, soon, at last, in the end*); (5) finish with a **moral** if the outline asks for it; (6) give the story a **title**.

**Task.** Write a story with the title "The Bat, the Birds and the Beasts" using the outline: *a great conflict between the birds and the beasts … two armies collected … the bat refuses to join … says he is a beast … beasts request … says he is a bird … peace made, no battle … rejoicing … bat wishes to join … both turn against him … threaten to tear him to pieces … bat flies away … moral.*

::: example Model story
**The Bat, the Birds and the Beasts**

Once upon a time there was a great quarrel between the birds and the beasts. Neither side would give way, so both gathered their armies and prepared for a terrible war.

When the armies were ready, the bat was asked to join the birds. He refused and said, "I am a beast. I have fur and teeth." Later the beasts asked him to join them. "I am a bird," he said, "because I have wings and fly." In this way the bat kept out of the fight and waited to see who would win.

Before the battle began, however, wise leaders on both sides spoke to each other, and peace was made. There was great rejoicing, and the birds and beasts began to celebrate together.

The bat, seeing that both sides were happy, wished to join the celebration. But the birds remembered that he had refused to fight with them, and the beasts remembered that he had refused to join them. Both turned against him and threatened to tear him to pieces. The frightened bat flew away and hid in a dark cave. Since that day he has come out only at night.

**Moral:** A person who is false to both sides will be trusted by neither.
:::

1. Write a story using these outlines: *a poor woodcutter … his axe falls into a river … a river-fairy appears … offers gold and silver axes … he refuses … fairy is pleased … gives him all three … a greedy neighbour copies … fairy punishes him … moral.* <span class="marks">[4]</span>
2. Write about 200 words on "The Importance of Preserving Our Cultural Heritage". <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** *Outline for answer:* title; beginning — a poor, honest woodcutter beside a river; middle — axe lost, the fairy tests him, he says only the iron axe is his, the fairy rewards honesty; the greedy neighbour tries the trick, lies and loses his own axe; moral — honesty is the best policy. Past tense, connectives.
**2.** *Outline:* introduction (heritage = temples, art, festivals, languages); why it matters (identity, tourism, education); threats (earthquake damage, neglect, pollution, theft); what to do (repair using traditional methods, teach children, laws, volunteering); conclusion.
:::
