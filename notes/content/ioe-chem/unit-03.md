Organic chemistry carries about **7 of 22 questions** (our estimate). The official model question "in Williamson's synthesis the haloalkane is treated with ... sodium alkoxide" shows the style: the reagent, the product or the test for a named reaction. Naming, isomer counts and acidity / basicity orders fill the rest. The six sub-areas follow one logic: what is the functional group, what attacks it, and what is formed.

::: key What the paper asks from this chapter
- IUPAC names, structural isomer counts, electronic effects (inductive, resonance, hyperconjugation).
- Named reactions and tests: Wurtz, Markovnikov, ozonolysis, Baeyer, Lucas, Williamson, Tollens, Fehling, iodoform, Cannizzaro, aldol, Hofmann, Sandmeyer, carbylamine.
- Orders: SN1 (3° > 2° > 1°), SN2 (reverse), acidity (RCOOH > phenol > alcohol), basicity of amines, carbocation stability.
- Aromaticity (Hückel), electrophilic substitution and directing groups.
- Mole calculations: combustion, empirical and molecular formula, degree of unsaturation.
:::

## 3.1 Introduction: principles, purification, nomenclature, isomerism, mechanism

**Hybridisation and bonding:** C in alkane sp³ (109.5°), alkene sp² (120°), alkyne sp (180°); a C–C triple bond is shortest; the % s-character (25, 33, 50) makes alkynes the most acidic hydrocarbons. **Classification:** acyclic / cyclic, aliphatic / aromatic, homologous series (differ by CH₂). **Functional groups:** –OH alcohol, –O– ether, –CHO aldehyde, >C=O ketone, –COOH acid, –COOR ester, –CONH₂ amide, –NH₂ amine, –NO₂ nitro, –X halo.

**IUPAC rules:** longest chain containing the principal functional group; lowest locants to the principal group, then to multiple bonds, then to substituents; alphabetical order. Priority: acid > ester > amide > aldehyde > ketone > alcohol > amine > alkene > alkyne > halide / nitro. CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ is **2-methylbutane**; CH₃COCH₃ propanone; CH₃CH₂CHO propanal; (CH₃)₂CHOH propan-2-ol; CH₃CH=CHCH₃ but-2-ene.

**Isomerism.** Structural: chain, position, functional, metamerism (diethyl ether and methyl propyl ether), tautomerism. Number of structural isomers: C₄H₁₀ 2, C₅H₁₂ 3, C₆H₁₄ 5, C₇H₁₆ 9; C₂H₆O 2 (ethanol, dimethyl ether); C₄H₈ (alkenes) 3 (+2 cycloalkanes). Stereo: geometrical (cis/trans, E/Z) and optical (a chiral C has four different groups; enantiomers rotate light equally and oppositely; a racemic mixture is inactive).
**Degree of unsaturation** $=\frac{2C+2+N-H-X}{2}$ (benzene C₆H₆: 4).

**Electronic effects:** inductive (–I: NO₂, CN, halogens, COOH; +I: alkyl); **resonance** (+R: OH, NH₂, OR; –R: NO₂, CHO, CN); hyperconjugation (explains the stability order of alkenes and carbocations: 3° > 2° > 1° > CH₃⁺). Reagents: **electrophiles** (H⁺, NO₂⁺, carbocations, AlCl₃) and **nucleophiles** (OH⁻, CN⁻, NH₃, H₂O); free radicals; carbanions. Reaction types: substitution (SN1, SN2), addition, elimination (E1, E2; Saytzeff: the more substituted alkene), rearrangement. **SN1:** two steps, carbocation, racemisation, 3° > 2° > 1°; **SN2:** one step, inversion (Walden), 1° > 2° > 3°.

**Purification:** crystallisation, sublimation (naphthalene), distillation (simple, fractional, steam, vacuum), chromatography ($R_f$ = distance of solute / of solvent). **Detection (Lassaigne):** sodium extract: N as NaCN gives Prussian blue, S as Na₂S gives violet with nitroprusside and black PbS, halogen as AgX (white AgCl, pale-yellow AgBr, yellow AgI). **Quantitative:** Dumas (N), Kjeldahl (N), Carius (halogens and S).

## 3.2 Hydrocarbons

**Alkanes** $C_nH_{2n+2}$ (sp³, σ only): preparation by hydrogenation, **Wurtz** (2RX + 2Na → R–R + 2NaX; 2CH₃Br → ethane), decarboxylation (soda lime), **Kolbe** electrolysis. Reactions: combustion; **free-radical halogenation** (initiation, propagation, termination; reactivity 3° > 2° > 1° H; Cl₂ less selective than Br₂); cracking and reforming. Boiling point rises with chain length and falls with branching.

**Alkenes** $C_nH_{2n}$: dehydration of alcohols, dehydrohalogenation (alc. KOH). Electrophilic addition: **Markovnikov** (H adds to the carbon with more H: propene + HBr → 2-bromopropane); **anti-Markovnikov** (Kharasch, with peroxides, HBr only); Br₂/CCl₄ decolourised (test of unsaturation); **Baeyer's test** (cold dil. alkaline KMnO₄ decolourised, diol formed); hydration; **ozonolysis** (O₃ then Zn/H₂O: C=C → two C=O; 2-methylpropene gives acetone and formaldehyde); hydrogenation; polymerisation (polythene).

**Alkynes** $C_nH_{2n-2}$: from CaC₂ + 2H₂O → HC≡CH + Ca(OH)₂. Acidic terminal H: with ammoniacal AgNO₃ (white) / Cu₂Cl₂ (red) precipitates; hydration (Hg²⁺/H₂SO₄): ethyne → ethanal, **propyne → propanone**; Lindlar's catalyst gives *cis*-alkene, Na/liquid NH₃ gives *trans*; trimerisation of ethyne gives benzene.

**Aromatic hydrocarbons.** Benzene is planar, six π electrons, **Hückel $(4n+2)\pi$ electrons** (benzene, naphthalene, the cyclopentadienyl anion; not cyclooctatetraene). It prefers **electrophilic substitution**: nitration (HNO₃/H₂SO₄, electrophile **NO₂⁺**), halogenation (Cl₂/FeCl₃), sulphonation, Friedel–Crafts alkylation and acylation (AlCl₃). Directing groups: –OH, –NH₂, –R, –X are *ortho/para* directors (halogens deactivate); –NO₂, –CN, –CHO, –COOH, –SO₃H are *meta* directors and deactivate; Friedel–Crafts fails on strongly deactivated rings. Toluene oxidises to benzoic acid.

## 3.3 Haloalkanes and haloarenes

Preparation: from alcohols (SOCl₂ best; HX/ZnCl₂), addition of HX, halogenation, Finkelstein (NaI/acetone). Reactions: **nucleophilic substitution** with aq. KOH (alcohol), alc. KOH (elimination to alkene), KCN (nitrile), NH₃ (amine), AgNO₂ (nitroalkane) vs KNO₂ (alkyl nitrite), NaOR (**Williamson** ether synthesis), Na (Wurtz), Mg in dry ether (**Grignard reagent** RMgX; with HCHO gives primary, other aldehydes secondary, ketones tertiary alcohols; with CO₂ gives acids; reacts with water to give an alkane). Reactivity R–I > R–Br > R–Cl; SN1: (CH₃)₃CBr > (CH₃)₂CHBr > CH₃CH₂Br > CH₃Br. **Haloarenes** are inert to SN (resonance, sp² C–X bond shorter) unless activated by ortho / para nitro groups; Sandmeyer and Fittig reactions; DDT, chloroform (CHCl₃, anaesthetic; stored in dark bottles because it forms phosgene), CCl₄, freons.

## 3.4 Alcohols, phenols and ethers

**Alcohols.** Preparation: hydration of alkenes, hydrolysis of RX, reduction of carbonyls (LiAlH₄, NaBH₄), Grignard addition. **Lucas test** (conc. HCl + anhydrous ZnCl₂): 3° turbid immediately, 2° in about 5 minutes, 1° only on heating. Reactions: with Na (H₂), esterification, dehydration (conc. H₂SO₄, 170 °C gives alkene; 140 °C gives ether), oxidation (1° → aldehyde (PCC) → acid; 2° → ketone; 3° resists), halide formation. Boiling points are high due to hydrogen bonding. Methanol is poisonous; ethanol from fermentation of sugar (zymase); denatured alcohol; **iodoform test** (I₂/NaOH, yellow CHI₃) is given by CH₃CH(OH)– and CH₃CO– groups: ethanol, propan-2-ol, ethanal, acetone — **not** methanol.

**Phenols.** More acidic than alcohols (phenoxide is resonance-stabilised) but weaker than carboxylic acids and H₂CO₃; electron-withdrawing groups raise acidity (**p-nitrophenol > phenol > p-cresol > ethanol**). Reactions: FeCl₃ gives a violet colour; bromine water gives white 2,4,6-tribromophenol; **Reimer–Tiemann** (CHCl₃/NaOH gives salicylaldehyde); **Kolbe** (CO₂ on sodium phenoxide gives salicylic acid, then aspirin); nitration to picric acid; coupling with diazonium salt. Phenol is made industrially from cumene.

**Ethers.** R–O–R′; **Williamson synthesis** R–X + R′ONa → R–O–R′ + NaX (best with a primary halide; tertiary halides give alkenes); low reactivity, peroxides on standing; cleaved by HI; diethyl ether anaesthetic and solvent.

## 3.5 Aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids and derivatives

**Preparation:** oxidation of 1° / 2° alcohols; Rosenmund (RCOCl, H₂, Pd/BaSO₄) for aldehydes; ozonolysis; Friedel–Crafts acylation for aromatic ketones; Stephen reduction. **Reactions:** nucleophilic addition to C=O (HCN gives cyanohydrin; NaHSO₃; Grignard; reactivity HCHO > RCHO > RCOR′ > ArCHO); condensation with NH₂OH (oxime), NH₂NH₂ (hydrazone), 2,4-DNP (orange precipitate for all carbonyls). **Distinguishing tests:** **Tollens' (silver mirror)** and **Fehling's (red Cu₂O)** only for aliphatic aldehydes (benzaldehyde does not respond to Fehling); iodoform for CH₃CO–; **ketones** are not oxidised easily. Reduction: Clemmensen (Zn–Hg/HCl) and Wolff–Kishner give CH₂; **aldol condensation** (needs α-H: CH₃CHO gives 3-hydroxybutanal; HCHO and benzaldehyde have none); **Cannizzaro** (no α-H, conc. NaOH: 2HCHO → CH₃OH + HCOONa; C₆H₅CHO → alcohol + benzoate). Formalin is 40 % HCHO.

**Carboxylic acids.** Acidity: HCOOH > CH₃COOH (alkyl +I weakens); –I substituents strengthen (ClCH₂COOH > CH₃COOH; CCl₃COOH very strong); benzoic acid is stronger than acetic acid. Preparation: oxidation of alcohols, hydrolysis of nitriles, Grignard + CO₂. Reactions: with NaHCO₃ (CO₂ effervescence — distinguishes acids from phenols), esterification (acid + alcohol, H⁺: CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ **ethyl acetate** + H₂O), acid chloride (SOCl₂), amide, reduction with LiAlH₄, **Hell–Volhard–Zelinsky** (α-bromination), decarboxylation (soda lime). **Derivatives** reactivity: RCOCl > anhydride > ester > amide; ester hydrolysis in base is **saponification** (soap); esters smell fruity. Acid anhydrides, amides (Hofmann bromamide: RCONH₂ + Br₂ + 4NaOH → RNH₂ + Na₂CO₃ + 2NaBr + 2H₂O, one carbon less).

## 3.6 Nitro compounds and amines

**Nitroalkanes:** from RX + AgNO₂; reduction gives amines; tautomerism (aci-form). **Nitrobenzene:** benzene + HNO₃/H₂SO₄; Sn/HCl gives aniline; in neutral medium gives phenylhydroxylamine; used for dyes.

**Amines.** Preparation: ammonolysis, reduction of nitro compounds / nitriles / amides, **Hofmann bromamide**, Gabriel (pure 1° amines). **Basicity:** in water the order is (CH₃)₂NH > CH₃NH₂ > (CH₃)₃N > NH₃ > C₆H₅NH₂ (aniline's lone pair is delocalised into the ring, so it is weakest). **Tests:** **carbylamine** (CHCl₃ + KOH, foul isocyanide) only for primary amines; **Hinsberg** (benzenesulphonyl chloride) separates 1°, 2°, 3°; **nitrous acid:** 1° aliphatic gives alcohol + N₂, 2° gives nitrosamine (yellow oil), 3° forms a salt; **aniline + NaNO₂/HCl at 0–5 °C gives benzenediazonium chloride**. **Diazonium salts:** Sandmeyer (CuCl, CuBr, CuCN replace N₂⁺ by Cl, Br, CN), Gattermann, replacement by OH (warm water), I (KI), H (H₃PO₂); **coupling** with phenol (alkaline) or aniline gives coloured azo dyes (–N=N–); aniline with Br₂ water gives 2,4,6-tribromoaniline; acetylation controls reactivity; aniline is *o/p*-directing, but nitration in strong acid gives *meta* (anilinium ion).

::: caution Organic traps
Markovnikov applies to HBr *without* peroxide only. Cannizzaro needs *no α-hydrogen*; aldol needs *an α-hydrogen*. Tollens' and Fehling's tests do not distinguish aromatic aldehydes in the same way. In aqueous basicity the tertiary amine is *not* the strongest. SN2 reactivity runs opposite to SN1. Acidity: look for the stability of the conjugate base.
:::

## Formula and fact sheet

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Wurtz | 2RX + 2Na → R–R |
| Markovnikov | H goes to C with more H |
| Lucas | 3° immediate, 2° slow, 1° heat |
| Williamson | RX + R′ONa → R–O–R′ |
| iodoform | CH₃CH(OH)– / CH₃CO– |
| Cannizzaro | no α-H; conc. NaOH |
| Hofmann | amide → amine, one C fewer |
| basicity (aq) | R₂NH > RNH₂ > R₃N > NH₃ > ArNH₂ |
| acidity | RCOOH > ArOH > ROH > HC≡CH |
| DBE | $\frac{2C+2+N-H-X}{2}$ |

## Chapter summary

- Name by the principal group; count isomers carefully (C₅H₁₂ has 3).
- Markovnikov for HX; peroxide reverses HBr; ozonolysis cuts at C=C.
- SN1 follows carbocation stability; SN2 follows steric access.
- Only carbonyls with α-H do aldol; only those without do Cannizzaro.
- Lucas distinguishes 1°, 2°, 3° alcohols; iodoform needs CH₃CH(OH)– or CH₃CO–.
- Diazotise aniline at 0–5 °C; then Sandmeyer or couple.

## Practice questions

**Set A — Recall (14 questions)**

1. The IUPAC name of CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ is
   (a) 2-methylbutane (b) 3-methylbutane (c) 2-ethylpropane (d) n-pentane
2. The number of structural isomers of C₄H₁₀ is
   (a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) 4
3. Propene reacts with HBr (no peroxide) to give mainly
   (a) 1-bromopropane (b) 3-bromopropane (c) 2-bromopropane (d) 1,2-dibromopropane
4. A test for unsaturation using cold dilute alkaline KMnO₄ is
   (a) Tollens' (b) Fehling's (c) Lucas (d) Baeyer's
5. The Wurtz reaction of methyl bromide with sodium gives
   (a) ethane (b) methane (c) propane (d) ethene
6. Lucas reagent is
   (a) conc. HCl + anhydrous AlCl₃ (b) conc. HCl + anhydrous ZnCl₂ (c) conc. H₂SO₄ + ZnCl₂ (d) HNO₃ + HCl
7. In Williamson's synthesis a haloalkane is treated with
   (a) ammonia (b) alcohol (c) sodium alkoxide (d) silver salt of a fatty acid
8. Phenol gives which colour with neutral FeCl₃?
   (a) red (b) green (c) yellow (d) violet
9. Tollens' reagent gives a silver mirror with
   (a) aldehydes (b) ketones (c) ethers (d) alcohols
10. The iodoform test is given by
    (a) methanol (b) ethanol (c) propan-1-ol (d) butan-1-ol
11. Acetic acid reacts with ethanol (H⁺) to give
    (a) methyl acetate (b) ethyl formate (c) ethyl acetate (d) diethyl ether
12. Aniline with NaNO₂ and HCl at 0–5 °C gives
    (a) nitrobenzene (b) phenol (c) azobenzene (d) benzenediazonium chloride
13. In aqueous solution the strongest base among these is
    (a) (CH₃)₂NH (b) CH₃NH₂ (c) (CH₃)₃N (d) NH₃
14. Which of these is aromatic?
    (a) cyclohexane (b) benzene (c) cyclooctatetraene (d) cyclohexene

::: note Answers and explanations — Set A
**1.** (a) — longest chain 4 C (butane) with a methyl on C-2.
**2.** (b) — butane and 2-methylpropane.
**3.** (c) — Markovnikov: H adds to the carbon with more H.
**4.** (d) — Baeyer's reagent is decolourised by C=C.
**5.** (a) — 2CH₃Br + 2Na → CH₃–CH₃ + 2NaBr.
**6.** (b) — conc. HCl with anhydrous ZnCl₂.
**7.** (c) — R–X + R′ONa → R–O–R′ + NaX.
**8.** (d) — violet complex.
**9.** (a) — aldehydes reduce [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺.
**10.** (b) — has CH₃CH(OH)–; methanol and primary alcohols other than ethanol do not.
**11.** (c) — CH₃COOC₂H₅.
**12.** (d) — diazotisation, stable only below 5 °C.
**13.** (a) — in water, 2° > 1° > 3° > NH₃ (solvation plus +I).
**14.** (b) — 6 π electrons ($4n+2$, $n=1$), planar and cyclic.
:::

**Set B — Understanding (8 questions)**

1. Ozonolysis of 2-methylpropene followed by reductive work-up gives
   (a) two molecules of formaldehyde (b) acetone and formaldehyde (c) two molecules of acetone (d) acetaldehyde and acetone
2. Hydration of propyne (HgSO₄/H₂SO₄) gives
   (a) propanal (b) propan-1-ol (c) propanone (d) propanoic acid
3. The compound most reactive in an SN1 reaction is
   (a) CH₃Br (b) CH₃CH₂Br (c) (CH₃)₂CHBr (d) (CH₃)₃CBr
4. The electrophile in the nitration of benzene is
   (a) NO₂⁺ (b) NO₂⁻ (c) NO₂ radical (d) HNO₃ molecule
5. Conversion of an amide into an amine with one carbon atom less uses
   (a) LiAlH₄ (b) Br₂ and NaOH (c) NaBH₄ (d) HNO₂
6. Formaldehyde with concentrated NaOH gives
   (a) methanol only (b) formic acid only (c) methanol and sodium formate (d) CO₂ and H₂
7. Which compound undergoes the aldol condensation?
   (a) HCHO (b) benzaldehyde (c) CCl₃CHO (d) CH₃CHO
8. Which is the most acidic?
   (a) p-nitrophenol (b) phenol (c) p-cresol (d) ethanol

::: note Answers and explanations — Set B
**1.** (b) — (CH₃)₂C=CH₂ gives (CH₃)₂C=O + HCHO.
**2.** (c) — enol CH₃C(OH)=CH₂ tautomerises to acetone.
**3.** (d) — tertiary carbocation is most stable.
**4.** (a) — HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻.
**5.** (b) — Hofmann bromamide degradation.
**6.** (c) — Cannizzaro: no α-H.
**7.** (d) — only acetaldehyde has α-hydrogens.
**8.** (a) — the –NO₂ group stabilises the phenoxide by –R and –I.
:::

**Set C — Application (6 questions)**

1. The number of moles of water formed on complete combustion of one mole of propane is
   (a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 5 (d) 8
2. The percentage of carbon in methane is
   (a) 80 (b) 25 (c) 75 (d) 85
3. The mass of acetic acid obtained by completely oxidising 9.2 g of ethanol is
   (a) 6 g (b) 9.2 g (c) 18 g (d) 12 g
4. A hydrocarbon contains 85.7 % C and 14.3 % H and has vapour density 28. Its molecular formula is
   (a) C₄H₈ (b) C₂H₄ (c) C₃H₆ (d) C₅H₁₀
5. The number of structural isomers of C₅H₁₂ is
   (a) 2 (b) 4 (c) 3 (d) 5
6. The degree of unsaturation of benzene (C₆H₆) is
   (a) 3 (b) 5 (c) 2 (d) 4

::: note Answers and explanations — Set C
**1.** (b) — C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O.
**2.** (c) — $\frac{12}{16}\times100$.
**3.** (d) — $\frac{9.2}{46}=0.2$ mol $\times60=12$ g.
**4.** (a) — C : H = 1 : 2, CH₂ (14); $M=2\times28=56$, so C₄H₈.
**5.** (c) — pentane, 2-methylbutane, 2,2-dimethylpropane.
**6.** (d) — $\frac{2\times6+2-6}{2}=4$ (3 C=C and 1 ring).
:::
