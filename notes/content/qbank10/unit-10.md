The SEE English paper is one of the most regular papers a student sits: the same four reading passages, four writing tasks and ten grammar items appear in the same order every year. This chapter sets out the grid, gives **two complete model papers** with answers, and ends with a ranked list of **predicted questions**. The ranking rests on one fact only: the grid fixes the question types and the syllabus fixes the texts. Nothing here is a forecast of what will appear, and no question is copied from a past paper.

::: key The grid (75 marks, 3 hours)
| Part | Marks | Task |
|---|---|---|
| Reading 1 | 5 | seen text (about 100 words), one question type |
| Reading 2 | 10 | seen text (about 200 words), two question types |
| Reading 3 | 10 | unseen text (about 200 words), two types |
| Reading 4 | 15 | unseen text (about 300 words), three types incl. vocabulary |
| Guided writing I | 5 | paragraph, chart, instructions, notice, advertisement |
| Guided writing II | 5 | news story, skeleton story, message, biography |
| Free writing I | 6 | paragraph, application, dialogue |
| Free writing II | 9 | letter, email, essay |
| Grammar | 10 | 5 reproduction items and 5 multiple-choice items in a passage |
:::

The figures follow the CDC specification grid for the 2080 curriculum as I could verify it. The split of the ten grammar marks and the marks of the single writing tasks should be checked against the grid your school gives you.

## 10.1 Pattern analysis

| Skill | Marks | Share of 75 | Where marks are lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading (R1 to R4) | 40 | 53% | copying half a sentence; skipping the vocabulary item |

| Grammar | 10 | 13% | tag questions, backshift in reported speech, passive forms of perfect tenses |

::: note Free writing II: model
**The Importance of Reading Books**

Books are the oldest and best friends of mankind. In this age of phones and videos, reading is still one of the most useful habits a student can have.

First, reading improves language. A person who reads widely learns new words and how sentences are built, and writes better. Second, books give knowledge: a single book can carry the experience of a lifetime. Third, reading relaxes the mind; a good story takes us away from worry. It also trains concentration, which students need for study.

Yet many young people read nothing but their textbooks. Libraries are empty while screens are busy. Schools should therefore hold reading hours and give prizes to readers. Parents should buy books as gifts.

In conclusion, a person who reads is never alone and never ignorant. We should read at least a little every day.
:::

::: note Grammar
**A.** 1. has been teaching (or has taught) — *for ten years* shows a period up to now. 2. did they — *nobody* makes the clause negative, so the tag is positive. 3. Nisha said that she was going to Pokhara. 4. The classroom is being cleaned by the students. 5. No one enjoyed the film.
**B.** 6. (b) to. 7. (b) in. 8. (b) where. 9. (a) Although. 10. (c) had — second conditional: *if + past, would + base verb*.
:::

::: note Guided writing I: model
**How to make a paper boat**
*You need:* one rectangular sheet of paper.
1. Fold the sheet in half along its length, then open it to leave a centre line.
2. Fold the top corners down to the centre line to make a triangle at the top.
3. Fold the lower strips up on both sides over the triangle.
4. Open the folded paper and flatten it into a diamond.
5. Fold the lower corners up and open again to form a boat.
6. Pull the two tips gently apart and set the boat on water.
:::

**Customer:** Here it is. I have also brought the box and the charger.

Kathmandu
20 Poush 2082

Dear Sapana,

I hope you are well. I am writing to tell you about the trip I took with my class last week. We went to Chitwan for three days. We left at six in the morning and reached by noon after a long, noisy bus ride. In the afternoon we visited the elephant breeding centre.

## Chapter summary

- The paper is Reading 40 (5 + 10 + 10 + 15), Writing 25 (5 + 5 + 6 + 9) and Grammar 10 = 75 marks in 3 hours.
- Seen-text items are answered from the book; unseen items from the passage in front of you.
- Writing is marked on format and organisation first: a letter needs an address, date, subject line and closing; a news story needs a headline and a dateline.
- Grammar: *nobody* gets a positive tag, *aren't I*, *the number of* is singular, *a number of* is plural, *if + present → will*.
- The ranking above comes from the grid and the syllabus, not from any knowledge of the paper.
