The subjective part of the Grade 10 English paper is everything that needs a sentence or a paragraph rather than a tick: the short-answer questions on the reading passages, and the four writing tasks that make up 25 of the 75 marks. This chapter gives, unit by unit, the short-answer questions that the 36 texts of the Grade 10 book can produce, with model answers, and then a model for each writing form named in the CDC specification grid. It ends with two unseen passages with answers.

::: key How to use this chapter
Write your own answer first. Then compare **points**, not words. A short answer (1 or 2 marks) needs one or two complete sentences taken from, or built on, the text. A writing task is marked on organisation, coherence and cohesion, subject matter, correctness, range of vocabulary and layout, so a correct layout alone earns marks. Every answer here is original practice material based on this series' book chapters; none is a copy of a board answer.
:::

## 9.1 The subjective questions in the grid

| Task | Marks | Forms the CDC grid names | About |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guided writing I | 5 | paragraph, description of tables or charts, instructions, recipe, advertisement, notice, rules | 100 words |
| Guided writing II | 5 | news story, skeleton story, condolence or congratulation message, invitation, thank-you letter, biography | 100 words |
| Free writing I | 6 | paragraph (view, opinion, experience), leave or job application, dialogue | 150 words |
| Free writing II | 9 | personal or official letter, letter to the editor, email, short essay | 200 words |

The marks and word counts above follow the CDC grid as published for the 2080 curriculum; the total of the four tasks is 25. Confirm the figures for a single task against the grid your school gives you, because boards sometimes publish them slightly differently.

## 9.3 Guided writing I (5 marks)

### A description of a chart

**Task.** The table shows the number of students of a school who joined clubs in 2080. Write a paragraph of about 100 words describing it.

| Club | Students |
|---|---|
| Sports | 120 |
| Music | 80 |
| Science | 60 |
| Art | 40 |

::: note Model answer
The table shows how many students of the school joined four clubs in 2080. The sports club was the most popular, with 120 students. Music came second with 80 students, while the science club attracted 60. The art club was the least popular, with only 40 members. Sports had three times as many members as art, and music had twice as many as art. In total, 300 students joined clubs. The table shows that students prefer activity and creative clubs to science, so the school should plan more science events.
:::

### An essay

**Task.** Write an essay of about 200 words on *The Advantages and Disadvantages of Social Media*.

::: note Model answer
**The Advantages and Disadvantages of Social Media**

Social media such as Facebook and TikTok has become part of daily life, and it has both good and bad effects.

Its first advantage is communication: we can talk to family abroad at no cost. It also spreads news quickly, helps students find study materials and lets small businesses reach customers. Some stories show it can even do good, for example when a post on Twitter helped to rescue a hawk in New York.

However, it has disadvantages too. Many students waste hours on it, lose sleep and read less. False news spreads fast and may cause panic. There are also risks of cyberbullying and of strangers misusing personal data.

In my view, social media is useful if we control it. We should limit screen time, check facts before sharing and keep our accounts private. Used wisely, it is a good servant; used carelessly, it is a bad master.
:::

### Reading 4: an article (15 marks)

> Nepal's mountain villages are fighting a new kind of problem: empty houses. Every year, thousands of young people leave for cities or foreign countries in search of work, and old people are left behind. In some villages of the hill districts, half the houses are locked.
>
> Yet a few villages are changing the story. In one hill village, a group of returnees started a homestay programme that brings tourists to stay with local families. The village now earns money from guests, and the young people who joined the programme say they no longer feel the need to leave. A local cooperative has also begun to make honey and herbal tea for sale. "We do not want to depend only on remittances," said a villager. "We want to build a life here."
>

**A. Choose the best answer (1 mark each)**
1. The main problem described is (a) too many tourists (b) empty houses in villages (c) floods (d) poor harvests
2. The returnees started (a) a school (b) a homestay programme (c) a factory (d) a bank

**B. Answer briefly (2 marks each)**
3. Why do young people leave the villages? 4. What does the cooperative make? 5. What do experts say is necessary for success?

**C. Vocabulary (1 mark each)**
6. Find a word in the first paragraph that means "people who are not young". 7. What does *depend on* mean in the second paragraph? (a) trust and need (b) refuse (c) watch (d) escape

**D. Give your opinion (3 marks)**
8. Do you think tourism can stop migration from villages? Give two reasons.

::: note Answers
**1.** (b). **2.** (b).
**3.** They go to cities or abroad in search of work.
**4.** It makes honey and herbal tea.
**5.** Roads, electricity and the internet must reach the villages.
**6.** old people (the passage says "old people are left behind"). **7.** (a).
**8.** *Sample:* Tourism can reduce migration only partly. It brings income, so young people can earn at home; homestays also give them pride in local culture. But it needs roads and the internet, and it is seasonal, so other jobs such as farming cooperatives are also needed.

## Chapter summary

- The subjective part of English is the short answers on four reading passages and 25 marks of writing in four tasks (guided I and II, free I and II).
- A short answer is one or two complete sentences; give the fact first, then the point.
- Writing is marked on organisation, coherence and cohesion, content, correctness, vocabulary and layout.
- A formal letter needs the sender's address, date, receiver, subject line, salutation, body and closing; a notice has an issuer, heading, date, details and writer's name and post.
- A news story has a headline, a dateline and the answers to who, what, when, where, why and how.
- An essay has an introduction, body paragraphs and a conclusion; keep to the word limit.
- For unseen passages find the answer line first and copy no more than you need.

## Practice questions

**Short answer (2 marks each)**

1. Why did Save the Children write to the UN Secretary-General? <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. How did the wax help Pelops? <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. What does the refrain "And so do I" show about the speaker of "Weathers"? <span class="marks">[2]</span>
4. Why does the country mouse prefer to go home? <span class="marks">[2]</span>

::: note Answers to Short answer
**1.** To ask that children under 18 be included in the UN Youth Advisory Group on climate change.
**2.** The wheel pins of the king's chariot were replaced by wax, so the wheels came off and Pelops won the race.
**3.** It shows that he likes the bright spring weather and that, like most people, he prefers the cheerful side of life.
**4.** He feels safe at home and prefers plain food in peace to a feast in fear.
:::

**Writing (5 to 9 marks)**

1. Write a notice for a science exhibition in your school. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
2. Write a letter to the editor about noise pollution near your school. <span class="marks">[9]</span>

::: note Answers to Writing
**1.** Use the notice model in section 9.3: issuer, heading, date, event, date and time, place, how to take part, name and post of the writer.
**2.** Use the letter-to-the-editor model in section 9.6: address, date, the editor, subject, the problem and its effects, solutions and a closing line.
:::
