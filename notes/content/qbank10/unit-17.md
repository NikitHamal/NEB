Optional Mathematics rewards exact recall of definitions and short methods, but an objective item still tests whether you understand why a rule works. This bank follows the 12 topic areas in the Grade 10 Optional Mathematics spine. Each section gives three original multiple-choice questions and a concise explanation. They are practice items, not copied or claimed past questions.

## 17.1 Sequence and Series

**Facts.** For an arithmetic sequence, $t_n=a+(n-1)d$ and $S_n=\frac{n}{2}[2a+(n-1)d]$. For a geometric sequence, $t_n=ar^{n-1}$ and $S_n=a\frac{r^n-1}{r-1}$ when $r\ne1$.

1. The 10th term of $4,7,10,\ldots$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 28 (b) 31 (c) 34 (d) 40
2. The sum of the first 5 terms of $2,6,18,\ldots$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 120 (b) 242 (c) 486 (d) 728
3. The arithmetic mean between 8 and 20 is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 12 (b) 14 (c) 16 (d) 28

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $a=4$, $d=3$, so $t_{10}=4+9(3)=31$.
**2.** (b) — the terms are 2, 6, 18, 54, 162; their sum is 242.
**3.** (b) — the arithmetic mean is $(8+20)/2=14$.
:::

## 17.2 Quadratic Equations and Graphs

**Facts.** For $ax^2+bx+c=0$, the discriminant is $D=b^2-4ac$. The graph of $y=ax^2+bx+c$ is a parabola; its axis is $x=-\frac{b}{2a}$.

1. The roots of $x^2-7x+12=0$ are <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 2 and 6 (b) 3 and 4 (c) -3 and -4 (d) 1 and 12
2. The discriminant of $2x^2+3x+5=0$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 31 (b) 49 (c) -31 (d) -49
3. The axis of symmetry of $y=x^2-6x+2$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $x=-3$ (b) $x=2$ (c) $x=3$ (d) $x=6$

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $(x-3)(x-4)=0$.
**2.** (c) — $D=3^2-4(2)(5)=9-40=-31$.
**3.** (c) — $x=-b/(2a)=6/2=3$.
:::

## 17.3 Matrices and Determinants

**Facts.** A matrix has an inverse only if its determinant is non-zero. For a $2\times2$ matrix with rows $(a,b)$ and $(c,d)$, its determinant is $ad-bc$.

1. A $2\times2$ matrix has first row $(2,1)$ and second row $(5,3)$. Its determinant is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1 (b) 6 (c) 11 (d) 13
2. A matrix of order $2\times3$ has <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 2 rows and 3 columns (b) 3 rows and 2 columns (c) 5 rows (d) 6 columns
3. If $|A|=0$, then $A^{-1}$ <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) is the zero matrix (b) does not exist (c) equals $A$ (d) always exists

::: note Answers
**1.** (a) — $2(3)-1(5)=1$.
**2.** (a) — matrix order is rows by columns.
**3.** (b) — a singular matrix has no inverse.
:::

## 17.4 Trigonometry: Multiple, Submultiple and Transformation Formulae

**Facts.** $\sin2A=2\sin A\cos A$, $\cos2A=\cos^2A-\sin^2A$, and $\sin(90^\circ-A)=\cos A$.

1. If $A=30^\circ$, then $\sin2A$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac12$ (b) $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ (c) 1 (d) 0
2. $\cos(90^\circ-\theta)$ is equal to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\cos\theta$ (b) $-\sin\theta$ (c) $\sin\theta$ (d) $\tan\theta$
3. If $\sin A=\frac35$ and $A$ is acute, $\cos A$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac25$ (b) $\frac45$ (c) $\frac35$ (d) $\frac54$

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $\sin60^\circ=\sqrt{3}/2$.
**2.** (c) — the co-function identity gives $\cos(90^\circ-\theta)=\sin\theta$.
**3.** (b) — $\cos A=\sqrt{1-\sin^2A}=\sqrt{16/25}=4/5$ for an acute angle.
:::

## 17.5 Trigonometric Equations and Height and Distance

**Facts.** In a right triangle, $\tan\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$. For $0^\circ≤\theta<360^\circ$, periodic solutions must include every angle in that interval.

1. If $\tan\theta=1$ and $0^\circ≤\theta<180^\circ$, then $\theta=$ <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $30^\circ$ (b) $45^\circ$ only (c) $90^\circ$ (d) $135^\circ$ only
2. A pole casts a 10 m shadow when the Sun's angle of elevation is $45^\circ$. The pole's height is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 5 m (b) $10\sqrt{2}$ m (c) 10 m (d) 20 m
3. The general solution of $\sin\theta=0$ in degrees is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\theta=90^\circ+360^\circ n$ (b) $\theta=180^\circ n$ (c) $\theta=45^\circ n$ (d) $\theta=360^\circ+ n$

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — tangent is positive in the first and third quadrants; only $45^\circ$ lies in the stated interval.
**2.** (c) — $h/10=\tan45^\circ=1$, so $h=10$ m.
**3.** (b) — sine is zero at every integer multiple of $180^\circ$.
:::

## 17.6 Angle between Two Lines and Pair of Straight Lines

**Facts.** A line $y=mx+c$ has slope $m$. Non-vertical lines are perpendicular when $m_1m_2=-1$ and parallel when $m_1=m_2$.

1. The slope of $3x+2y-8=0$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac32$ (b) $-\frac32$ (c) $\frac23$ (d) $-\frac23$
2. A line perpendicular to a line of slope $-\frac25$ has slope <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac25$ (b) $-\frac52$ (c) $\frac52$ (d) $-\frac25$
3. The equation $x^2-4xy+3y^2=0$ represents the pair <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $y=x$ and $y=3x$ (b) $y=-x$ and $y=-3x$ (c) $x=0$ and $y=0$ (d) one circle

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $2y=-3x+8$ gives slope $-3/2$.
**2.** (c) — the negative reciprocal of $-2/5$ is $5/2$.
**3.** (a) — $(x-y)(x-3y)=0$.
:::

## 17.7 Conic Sections and Circle

**Facts.** A circle with centre $(h,k)$ and radius $r$ has equation $(x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2$. A parabola has one focus and one directrix; an ellipse has eccentricity less than 1.

1. The centre and radius of $x^2+y^2-6x+4y-12=0$ are <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $(3,-2),5$ (b) $(-3,2),5$ (c) $(3,2),3$ (d) $(6,-4),12$
2. The eccentricity of an ellipse is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0 (b) less than 1 (c) equal to 1 (d) greater than 1
3. The equation of a circle centred at the origin with radius 4 is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $x+y=4$ (b) $x^2+y^2=4$ (c) $x^2+y^2=16$ (d) $x^2-y^2=16$

::: note Answers
**1.** (a) — completing squares gives $(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=25$.
**2.** (b) — ellipse eccentricity satisfies $0≤e<1$.
**3.** (c) — $x^2+y^2=r^2=16$.
:::

## 17.8 Vector

**Facts.** For $\vec a=(a_1,a_2)$, $|\vec a|=\sqrt{a_1^2+a_2^2}$. The dot product is $\vec a\cdot\vec b=a_1b_1+a_2b_2$.

1. The magnitude of $\vec a=(5,12)$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 7 (b) 13 (c) 17 (d) 60
2. $(2,3)\cdot(3,-2)$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0 (b) 5 (c) 12 (d) -12
3. If $\vec a=(1,2)$ and $\vec b=(4,8)$, the vectors are <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) perpendicular (b) parallel (c) equal in magnitude (d) opposite

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $\sqrt{5^2+12^2}=13$.
**2.** (a) — $2(3)+3(-2)=0$.
**3.** (b) — $\vec b=4\vec a$, so they are parallel.
:::

## 17.9 Linear Programming and Transformation

**Facts.** A linear-programming optimum over a bounded polygonal feasible region occurs at a corner point. A translation $(x,y)\mapsto(x+a,y+b)$ preserves lengths, angles and area.

1. The maximum of $Z=2x+y$ subject to $x+y≤4$, $x,y≥0$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 4 (b) 6 (c) 8 (d) no maximum
2. A translation by $(3,-2)$ maps $(1,4)$ to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $(4,2)$ (b) $(-2,6)$ (c) $(3,-2)$ (d) $(1,2)$
3. A reflection in the $y$-axis maps $(a,b)$ to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $(a,-b)$ (b) $(-a,b)$ (c) $(-a,-b)$ (d) $(b,a)$

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — at the vertices $(0,0),(4,0),(0,4)$, $Z$ is $0,8,4$.
**2.** (a) — add 3 to the $x$-coordinate and subtract 2 from the $y$-coordinate.
**3.** (b) — reflection in the $y$-axis reverses the sign of $x$ only.
:::

## 17.10 Continuity

**Facts.** $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)$ exists when the left- and right-hand limits agree. Continuity at $a$ requires that this common limit equal $f(a)$.

1. $\lim_{x\to2}\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) does not exist
2. The function $f(x)=x^2$ is continuous at <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $x=0$ only (b) $x=1$ only (c) all real $x$ (d) no real $x$
3. If the left-hand limit at $x=1$ is 3 and the right-hand limit is 5, the two-sided limit <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) is 4 (b) is 3 (c) is 5 (d) does not exist

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — cancel $x-2$ for $x\ne2$; the limit is $2+2=4$.
**2.** (c) — every polynomial function is continuous on the real line.
**3.** (d) — unequal one-sided limits mean the two-sided limit does not exist.
:::

## 17.11 Statistics: Measures of Dispersion

**Facts.** For ungrouped data, population variance is $\sigma^2=\frac{\sum(x-\bar x)^2}{N}$ and standard deviation is $\sigma=\sqrt{\sigma^2}$.

1. The range of $3,8,10,14$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 7 (b) 10 (c) 11 (d) 14
2. The mean of $2,4,6,8$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 20
3. The variance of $2,2,2,2$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 2 (d) 4

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — range $=14-3=11$.
**2.** (b) — mean $=(2+4+6+8)/4=5$.
**3.** (a) — every observation equals the mean, so all deviations are zero.
:::

## 17.12 Functions and Polynomials

**Facts.** A function assigns exactly one output to each input in its domain. By the remainder theorem, the remainder on division of $f(x)$ by $x-a$ is $f(a)$.

1. If $f(x)=2x^2-3$, then $f(2)$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1 (b) 5 (c) 8 (d) 11
2. The remainder when $x^3-2x+1$ is divided by $x-1$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) -2 (b) 0 (c) 1 (d) 2
3. The domain of $f(x)=\frac{1}{x-4}$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) all real numbers (b) all real numbers except 4 (c) $x>4$ (d) $x<4$

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — substitute $x=2$: $f(2)=2(2^2)-3=8-3=5$.
**2.** (b) — $f(1)=1-2+1=0$.
**3.** (b) — the denominator cannot be zero, so $x\ne4$.
:::

::: caution Check the arithmetic, not just the method
Substitute the complete input into the function before simplifying. For example, $f(2)=2(2^2)-3$ means square 2 first, then multiply by 2 and subtract 3.
:::
