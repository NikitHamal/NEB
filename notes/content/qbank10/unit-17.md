Optional Mathematics rewards recognition before calculation. A student who can name the correct formula, condition or graph feature in ten seconds has more time for the longer proof or numerical. This chapter is a compact retrieval bank for all twelve areas of the Grade 10 course. Cover the answers, say the reason aloud, then write down any formula that was not immediate.

::: key A reliable one-mark routine
Read the final word first: *root*, *domain*, *slope*, *condition*, *maximum* or *probability*. Then write the smallest fact that answers it. Do not begin a long calculation when substitution or a standard identity settles the question.
:::

:::

## 17.1 Sequence and series

**Recall card.** For an AP, $a_n=a+(n-1)d$ and $S_n=\frac n2[2a+(n-1)d]$. For a GP, $a_n=ar^{n-1}$ and $S_n=\frac{a(r^n-1)}{r-1}$ for $r\ne1$.

1. The common difference of $7,11,15,\ldots$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 7 (d) 11
2. The 10th term of the AP $3,6,9,\ldots$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 27 (b) 30 (c) 33 (d) 36
3. The common ratio of $2,6,18,\ldots$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 6 (d) 9

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — consecutive terms differ by $11-7=4$.  
**2.** (b) — $a_{10}=3+9(3)=30$.  
**3.** (b) — $6\div2=3$.
:::

## 17.2 Quadratic equations and graphs

**Recall card.** For $ax^2+bx+c=0$, discriminant $D=b^2-4ac$. If $D>0$, roots are real and distinct; if $D=0$, equal; if $D<0$, no real roots. The axis of $y=ax^2+bx+c$ is $x=-\frac b{2a}$.

1. The roots of $x^2-9=0$ are <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 3 only (b) $\pm3$ (c) $\pm9$ (d) 0 and 9
2. The discriminant of $x^2+4x+4=0$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $-8$ (b) 0 (c) 4 (d) 8
3. The graph of $y=-2x^2+1$ opens <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) upward (b) downward (c) to the right (d) to the left

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $x^2=9$, so $x=3$ or $x=-3$.  
**2.** (b) — $4^2-4(1)(4)=0$.  
**3.** (b) — a negative coefficient of $x^2$ makes a downward parabola.
:::

## 17.3 Matrices and determinants

**Recall card.** For a $2\times2$ matrix with entries $a,b,c,d$, determinant is $ad-bc$. The inverse exists only when $ad-bc\ne0$.

1. The determinant of entries $2,3,1,4$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 5 (b) 8 (c) 11 (d) $-5$
2. A square matrix is singular when its determinant is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1 (b) $-1$ (c) 0 (d) positive
3. If $A$ has an inverse, then $AA^{-1}$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $A$ (b) 0 (c) $I$ (d) $A^2$

::: note Answers
**1.** (a) — $2(4)-3(1)=5$.  
**2.** (c) — a zero determinant has no inverse.  
**3.** (c) — this is the defining inverse property.
:::

## 17.4 Trigonometric formulas

**Recall card.** $\sin(A+B)=\sin A\cos B+\cos A\sin B$; $\cos2A=1-2\sin^2A=2\cos^2A-1$; $\tan2A=\frac{2\tan A}{1-\tan^2A}$.

1. $\sin90^\circ$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0 (b) $\frac12$ (c) 1 (d) undefined
2. If $\tan A=1$, where $A$ is acute, $A$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $30^\circ$ (b) $45^\circ$ (c) $60^\circ$ (d) $90^\circ$
3. $\cos2A$ in terms of $\sin A$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $1+2\sin^2A$ (b) $1-2\sin^2A$ (c) $2\sin A$ (d) $\sin^2A-1$

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — the unit-circle value is 1.  
**2.** (b) — $\tan45^\circ=1$.  
**3.** (b) — one standard double-angle form.
:::

## 17.5 Identities, equations and height-distance

**Recall card.** A conditional identity holds only under a stated condition. Reject a negative length in height-and-distance work. Draw the right triangle before selecting a ratio.

1. The identity $\sec^2A-\tan^2A=$ <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) $\sec A$ (d) $\tan A$
2. In a right triangle, the ratio opposite/adjacent is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) sine (b) cosine (c) tangent (d) cosecant
3. A valid value of $\sin A$ is always in the interval <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $[-1,1]$ (b) $[0,\infty)$ (c) $(-\infty,\infty)$ (d) $[1,\infty)$

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — rearrange $\sec^2A=1+\tan^2A$.  
**2.** (c) — $\tan A=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$.  
**3.** (a) — sine cannot exceed 1 in magnitude.
:::

## 17.6 Straight lines and pair of lines

**Recall card.** For lines $m_1,m_2$, $\tan\theta=\left|\frac{m_1-m_2}{1+m_1m_2}\right|$. Parallel lines have equal slopes; perpendicular non-vertical lines have $m_1m_2=-1$.

1. The slope of $2x+y-5=0$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 2 (b) $-2$ (c) $\frac12$ (d) $-\frac12$
2. Lines with slopes 3 and $-\frac13$ are <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) parallel (b) perpendicular (c) coincident (d) vertical
3. A pair of straight lines through the origin has constant term <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1 (b) $-1$ (c) 0 (d) variable only

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $y=-2x+5$.  
**2.** (b) — product of slopes is $-1$.  
**3.** (c) — each line passes through $(0,0)$.
:::

## 17.7 Conic sections and circle

**Recall card.** A circle with centre $(h,k)$ and radius $r$ is $(x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2$. A parabola has one focus and one directrix.

1. The centre of $(x-2)^2+(y+3)^2=16$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $(2,3)$ (b) $(-2,3)$ (c) $(2,-3)$ (d) $(-2,-3)$
2. The radius of the circle in item 1 is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 2 (b) 4 (c) 8 (d) 16
3. The conic with eccentricity 1 is a <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) circle (b) ellipse (c) parabola (d) hyperbola

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — compare with $(x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2$.  
**2.** (b) — $r=\sqrt{16}=4$.  
**3.** (c) — a parabola has eccentricity 1.
:::

## 17.8 Vectors

**Recall card.** $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\cos\theta$. Perpendicular non-zero vectors have dot product 0. The magnitude of $(x,y)$ is $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$.

1. The magnitude of $3\mathbf i+4\mathbf j$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 5 (b) 7 (c) 12 (d) 25
2. If $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=0$ for non-zero vectors, the vectors are <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) equal (b) parallel (c) perpendicular (d) opposite
3. The dot product of $(2,1)$ and $(3,-2)$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $-8$ (b) 0 (c) 4 (d) 8

::: note Answers
**1.** (a) — $\sqrt{9+16}=5$.  
**2.** (c) — $cos90^\circ=0$.  
**3.** (c) — $2(3)+1(-2)=4$.
:::

## 17.9 Linear programming and transformation

**Recall card.** A feasible region satisfies every constraint. For a linear objective function, test the corner points. A transformation maps each point to an image point by a stated rule or matrix.

1. In linear programming, the common shaded region is called the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) axis (b) feasible region (c) objective line (d) gradient
2. A maximum or minimum of a linear objective occurs at a <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) random interior point (b) corner point of the feasible region (c) origin only (d) point outside the region
3. Under translation $(x,y)\mapsto(x+3,y-2)$, $(1,4)$ maps to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $(4,2)$ (b) $(-2,6)$ (c) $(3,-2)$ (d) $(4,6)$

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — it contains all permitted choices.  
**2.** (b) — evaluate the objective at each vertex.  
**3.** (a) — add 3 to $x$ and subtract 2 from $y$.
:::

## 17.10 Continuity

**Recall card.** A function is continuous at $x=a$ when left limit, right limit and $f(a)$ all exist and are equal.

1. A removable discontinuity is often shown by a <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) filled point (b) hole in a graph (c) horizontal line (d) maximum point
2. For continuity at $x=a$, the left and right limits must be <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) negative (b) positive (c) equal (d) zero
3. The function $f(x)=3x+2$ is continuous for <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) no real $x$ (b) positive $x$ only (c) integer $x$ only (d) every real $x$

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — the limiting value may exist even though the point is missing.  
**2.** (c) — otherwise the two-sided limit does not exist.  
**3.** (d) — every polynomial is continuous on the real line.
:::

## 17.11 Measures of dispersion

**Recall card.** $QD=\frac{Q_3-Q_1}{2}$, $MD=\frac{\sum|x-\bar x|}{n}$ and $\sigma=\sqrt{\frac{\sum(x-\bar x)^2}{n}}$. Lower coefficient of variation means greater consistency.

1. If $Q_1=12$ and $Q_3=20$, the quartile deviation is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 4 (b) 8 (c) 16 (d) 32
2. Standard deviation can never be <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0 (b) positive (c) negative (d) fractional
3. The more consistent series has the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) larger CV (b) smaller CV (c) larger mean only (d) smaller mean only

::: note Answers
**1.** (a) — $(20-12)\div2=4$.  
**2.** (c) — it is a square root of a non-negative quantity.  
**3.** (b) — variation relative to the mean is lower.
:::

## 17.12 Functions and polynomials

**Recall card.** A function gives exactly one output for each allowed input. The vertical-line test checks a graph. Remainder on division by $(x-a)$ is $f(a)$.

1. The range of $f(x)=x^2$ over all real $x$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) all real numbers (b) $x\ge0$ (c) $x\le0$ (d) $x\ne0$
2. The remainder when $f(x)=x^2-5x+6$ is divided by $x-2$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 6
3. A zero of $f(x)$ is a value of $x$ for which <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $f(x)=1$ (b) $f(x)=x$ (c) $f(x)=0$ (d) $x=0$ only

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — a square cannot be negative.  
**2.** (a) — $f(2)=4-10+6=0$.  
**3.** (c) — it is an $x$-intercept of the graph.
:::

## Chapter summary

- Use formulas as retrieval cues, not as a substitute for a diagram, sign check or condition.
- Before inverse matrices, check determinant $\ne0$; before division, check the denominator is not zero.
- For quadratic, conic, vector and line questions, sketch first when the sign or position matters.
- For linear programming, list every vertex and test the objective function at every vertex.
- For continuity, write the left limit, right limit and function value separately before saying “continuous”.
- For statistics, state the formula, make a working table and interpret a smaller CV as greater consistency.

## Practice questions

1. State the condition for the inverse of a $2\times2$ matrix to exist, and explain why that condition matters. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. A quadratic has discriminant $-9$. State the nature of its roots and what this tells you about its graph and the $x$-axis. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. Explain, in four ordered steps, how to solve a linear-programming maximum problem. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** Its determinant must be non-zero. A zero determinant is singular, so no matrix can undo its transformation and no inverse exists.

**2.** The roots are not real. The parabola does not meet the $x$-axis, because $y=0$ would have no real solution.

**3.** (i) Define the variables and objective function. (ii) Draw each constraint boundary and shade the allowed half-plane. (iii) Identify the common feasible region and list its corners. (iv) Substitute every corner in the objective function and report the greatest value with the corresponding point.
:::
