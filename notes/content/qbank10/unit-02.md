Group A of the SEE Science and Technology paper is ten multiple-choice questions, and Group B adds nine very short one-mark questions. Together they are 19 of the 75 marks, and they can be secured in about half an hour. This chapter drills all 19 textbook chapters in order. Each section opens with a short **facts line** (what the objective questions keep returning to), then gives seven MCQs, then a key in which every answer has a one-line reason.

::: key What the objective items test
Almost nothing in Groups A and B is a long calculation. They test a **definition**, a **unit or value**, a **one-step substitution** or a **misconception** offered as a distractor. The CDC model MCQs are often small scenarios, so read the stem slowly and eliminate two options first. If one MCQ takes more than 90 seconds, you have probably misread it.
:::

::: caution Practice items, not past questions
Every question below is an original **practice** question written to the pattern. None is claimed to have appeared in any year's paper. Where a numerical is used, the stem states the value of $g$ or other constants, and the key gives the working.
:::

## 2.1 Scientific Learning

**Facts.** The SI unit of length is the metre, of mass the kilogram, of time the second. A scientific method runs: observation, problem, hypothesis, experiment, analysis, conclusion. A hypothesis is a testable guess.

1. The correct order of the scientific method after observing a problem is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) conclusion, hypothesis, experiment (b) hypothesis, experiment, conclusion (c) experiment, hypothesis, conclusion (d) hypothesis, conclusion, experiment
2. A tentative, testable explanation of an observation is called a <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) theory (b) law (c) hypothesis (d) conclusion
3. The SI unit of mass is the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) gram (b) kilogram (c) pound (d) quintal
4. A length of 2.5 km, expressed in metres, is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 25 m (b) 250 m (c) 2500 m (d) 25000 m
5. The variable that the experimenter deliberately changes is the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) dependent variable (b) controlled variable (c) independent variable (d) constant
6. Which instrument measures the volume of an irregular solid by displacement? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) measuring cylinder (b) spring balance (c) stopwatch (d) thermometer
7. A project report should end with <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) the title (b) the conclusion and recommendations (c) the objectives (d) the acknowledgement

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — a hypothesis is stated, then tested by an experiment, then judged in the conclusion.
**2.** (c) — a hypothesis is a testable guess; a theory is already well tested.
**3.** (b) — the kilogram is the SI base unit of mass; the gram is a sub-unit.
**4.** (c) — $2.5\times1000=2500$ m.
**5.** (c) — the independent variable is the one the experimenter changes.
**6.** (a) — a measuring cylinder shows the water level before and after immersion.
**7.** (b) — a report closes with findings, conclusion and recommendations.
:::

## 2.2 Classification of Living Beings

**Facts.** Whittaker proposed five kingdoms in 1969: Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia. Linnaeus gave binomial nomenclature (genus then species). Fungal cell walls are made of chitin.

1. The five-kingdom classification was proposed by <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Linnaeus (b) Aristotle (c) Whittaker (d) Mendel
2. Which kingdom contains organisms without a true nucleus? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Protista (b) Fungi (c) Plantae (d) Monera
3. The cell wall of fungi is made of <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) cellulose (b) chitin (c) peptidoglycan only (d) lignin
4. In the name *Homo sapiens*, *sapiens* is the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) genus (b) family (c) species (d) order
5. *Amoeba* belongs to the kingdom <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Monera (b) Protista (c) Fungi (d) Animalia
6. Which pair is correctly matched? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) mushroom, Plantae (b) bacteria, Protista (c) yeast, Fungi (d) fern, Animalia
7. An organism that makes its own food and has a cell wall of cellulose is placed in <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Fungi (b) Plantae (c) Monera (d) Animalia

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — R. H. Whittaker, 1969.
**2.** (d) — Monera are prokaryotes: no nuclear membrane.
**3.** (b) — chitin is the fungal wall material; cellulose belongs to plants.
**4.** (c) — the first word is the genus (*Homo*), the second the species.
**5.** (b) — *Amoeba* is a single-celled eukaryote, so it is a protist.
**6.** (c) — yeast is a unicellular fungus; a mushroom is a fungus, bacteria are Monera and ferns are plants.
**7.** (b) — plants are autotrophs with cellulose cell walls.
:::

## 2.3 Honey Bee

**Facts.** Castes: queen, worker, drone. Development from egg to adult takes 16 days (queen), 21 (worker), 24 (drone). Only *Apis cerana* and *Apis mellifera* are kept in hives. Honey bees belong to class Insecta, order Hymenoptera.

1. The only egg-laying female of a bee colony is the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) worker (b) drone (c) queen (d) nurse bee
2. A drone develops from egg to adult in <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 16 days (b) 21 days (c) 24 days (d) 30 days
3. Which species is domesticated and native to Nepal? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) *Apis dorsata* (b) *Apis cerana* (c) *Apis florea* (d) *Apis laboriosa*
4. The main duty of the drone is to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) collect nectar (b) build the comb (c) mate with the queen (d) guard the hive
5. The sac in which the queen stores sperm is the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) crop (b) spermatheca (c) sting gland (d) pollen basket
6. Honey bees belong to the order <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Lepidoptera (b) Diptera (c) Hymenoptera (d) Coleoptera
7. The correct order of the bee life cycle is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) egg, pupa, larva, adult (b) egg, larva, pupa, adult (c) larva, egg, pupa, adult (d) egg, larva, adult, pupa

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — workers are sterile females; the queen lays all eggs.
**2.** (c) — queen 16, worker 21, drone 24 days.
**3.** (b) — *Apis cerana* is native and kept in hives; *A. dorsata* and *A. florea* are wild.
**4.** (c) — a drone mates with a virgin queen and dies afterwards.
**5.** (b) — the spermatheca keeps sperm alive for her whole life.
**6.** (c) — bees, wasps and ants are Hymenoptera.
**7.** (b) — complete metamorphosis: egg, larva, pupa, adult.
:::

## 2.4 Heredity

**Facts.** Mendel worked on the garden pea. A monohybrid F₂ ratio is 3 : 1 (phenotype) and 1 : 2 : 1 (genotype); a dihybrid ratio is 9 : 3 : 3 : 1. Humans have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs); females are XX, males XY. Down syndrome is trisomy 21 (47 chromosomes).

1. The father of genetics is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Darwin (b) Mendel (c) Lamarck (d) Watson
2. The phenotypic ratio of a monohybrid cross in F₂ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1 : 1 (b) 1 : 2 : 1 (c) 3 : 1 (d) 9 : 3 : 3 : 1
3. A cross of Tt × Tt gives tall (T is dominant) and dwarf offspring in the ratio <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1 : 3 (b) 3 : 1 (c) 1 : 1 (d) 2 : 1
4. The sex chromosomes of a human female are <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) XY (b) XX (c) YY (d) XO
5. The sex of a child is decided by the chromosome contributed by the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) mother (b) father (c) both equally (d) grandparents
6. Down syndrome results from <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) three copies of chromosome 21 (b) one X chromosome missing (c) a recessive gene on X (d) an extra Y chromosome
7. Colour blindness is more common in males because the gene is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) on the Y chromosome (b) dominant (c) recessive and on the X chromosome (d) on chromosome 21

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — Gregor Mendel, from his pea experiments.
**2.** (c) — the F₂ phenotypes are 3 dominant : 1 recessive.
**3.** (b) — TT, Tt, Tt are tall and tt is dwarf, so 3 : 1.
**4.** (b) — female XX, male XY.
**5.** (b) — the mother gives only X; the father gives X or Y.
**6.** (a) — trisomy 21, so 47 chromosomes.
**7.** (c) — a male has one X, so a single recessive gene shows; a female needs two.
:::

## 2.5 Physiological Structure and Life Process

**Facts.** The brain and spinal cord form the central nervous system. A reflex action is rapid and involuntary, and passes through the spinal cord. Insulin from the pancreas lowers blood sugar. The human heart has four chambers. The nephron is the functional unit of the kidney. Blood group O is the universal donor, AB the universal recipient.

1. The functional unit of the nervous system is the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) nephron (b) neuron (c) alveolus (d) villus
2. The hormone that lowers the blood sugar level is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) glucagon (b) adrenaline (c) insulin (d) thyroxine
3. The chamber of the heart that pumps oxygenated blood to the body is the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) right atrium (b) left atrium (c) right ventricle (d) left ventricle
4. A reflex action is controlled by the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) cerebrum (b) spinal cord (c) cerebellum (d) pituitary
5. The functional unit of the kidney is the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) neuron (b) nephron (c) ureter (d) bladder
6. The universal blood donor group is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) AB (b) A (c) B (d) O
7. Fertilisation in humans normally takes place in the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) uterus (b) ovary (c) fallopian tube (d) vagina

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — a neuron carries nerve impulses; the nephron belongs to the kidney.
**2.** (c) — insulin from the pancreas; glucagon raises sugar.
**3.** (d) — the left ventricle drives blood through the aorta to the body.
**4.** (b) — the reflex arc passes through the spinal cord, bypassing thinking.
**5.** (b) — each kidney has about a million nephrons.
**6.** (d) — group O red cells carry no A or B antigens.
**7.** (c) — the ovum meets the sperm in the fallopian tube, then travels to the uterus.
:::

## 2.6 Nature and Environment

**Facts.** Greenhouse gases: carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, water vapour, CFCs. Chlorofluorocarbons deplete the ozone layer. Acid rain has pH below 5.6 and is caused mainly by sulphur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen. The 3R principle is reduce, reuse, recycle.

1. The gas mainly responsible for global warming is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) oxygen (b) nitrogen (c) carbon dioxide (d) argon
2. The ozone layer is mainly damaged by <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) CFCs (b) methane only (c) oxygen (d) helium
3. Acid rain forms when rain water dissolves <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) CO and N₂ (b) SO₂ and NOₓ (c) O₂ and H₂ (d) Ar and Ne
4. Which is a biodegradable waste? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) plastic bag (b) glass bottle (c) vegetable peel (d) aluminium can
5. In a food chain, the producer is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) a deer (b) green grass (c) a tiger (d) a fungus
6. Which of these is NOT part of the 3R principle? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) reduce (b) reuse (c) recycle (d) refuse to recycle
7. Chitwan National Park is mainly protected to conserve <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) snow leopard in the high Himalaya (b) one-horned rhinoceros and tiger (c) yak (d) musk deer only

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — CO₂ is the most abundant added greenhouse gas.
**2.** (a) — chlorine released from CFCs breaks down ozone.
**3.** (b) — SO₂ and NOₓ form sulphuric and nitric acids in rain.
**4.** (c) — vegetable waste decays by microbes; the others do not.
**5.** (b) — producers make food by photosynthesis.
**6.** (d) — the principle is reduce, reuse, recycle.
**7.** (b) — Chitwan is known for the one-horned rhinoceros and the Bengal tiger.
:::

## 2.7 Motion and Force

**Facts.** $F=ma$; momentum $p=mv$; weight $W=mg$; $v=u+gt$ in free fall; universal law of gravitation $F=\dfrac{Gm_1m_2}{d^2}$; $G=6.67\times10^{-11}\ \text{N m}^2\text{kg}^{-2}$.

1. A force of 20 N acts on a 5 kg body. Its acceleration is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 100 m s⁻² (b) 15 m s⁻² (c) 4 m s⁻² (d) 0.25 m s⁻²
2. The momentum of a 3 kg body moving at 4 m s⁻¹ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 7 kg m s⁻¹ (b) 12 kg m s⁻¹ (c) 0.75 kg m s⁻¹ (d) 1.33 kg m s⁻¹
3. Which law explains why a passenger jerks forward when a bus stops suddenly? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Newton's first law (b) Newton's second law (c) Newton's third law (d) Boyle's law
4. With $g=10\ \text{m s}^{-2}$, the weight of a 6 kg object is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0.6 N (b) 6 N (c) 16 N (d) 60 N
5. A stone is dropped from rest. With $g=10\ \text{m s}^{-2}$, its speed after 3 s is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 3.3 m s⁻¹ (b) 13 m s⁻¹ (c) 30 m s⁻¹ (d) 90 m s⁻¹
6. If the distance between two masses is doubled, the gravitational force becomes <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) half (b) double (c) one-quarter (d) four times
7. The mass of a body on the Moon compared with its mass on Earth is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) one-sixth (b) six times (c) the same (d) zero

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — $a=F/m=20/5=4$ m s⁻².
**2.** (b) — $p=mv=3\times4=12$ kg m s⁻¹.
**3.** (a) — inertia: the body tends to keep moving.
**4.** (d) — $W=mg=6\times10=60$ N.
**5.** (c) — $v=gt=10\times3=30$ m s⁻¹.
**6.** (c) — $F\propto1/d^2$, so $1/2^2=1/4$.
**7.** (c) — mass does not change with place; only weight does.
:::

## 2.8 Pressure

**Facts.** $P=F/A$ (unit pascal, Pa = N m⁻²); liquid pressure $P=h\rho g$; Pascal's law: pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted equally in all directions; Archimedes' principle: upthrust equals the weight of fluid displaced; a body floats when its density is less than the fluid's. Atmospheric pressure at sea level is about $1.01\times10^{5}$ Pa.

1. A force of 500 N acts on an area of 0.25 m². The pressure is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 125 Pa (b) 2000 Pa (c) 500 Pa (d) 0.0005 Pa
2. The pressure at a depth of 10 m in water is (take $\rho=1000\ \text{kg m}^{-3}$, $g=10\ \text{m s}^{-2}$, ignore the atmosphere) <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $10^{3}$ Pa (b) $10^{4}$ Pa (c) $10^{5}$ Pa (d) $10^{6}$ Pa
3. The hydraulic press works on the principle of <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Archimedes (b) Pascal (c) Bernoulli (d) Boyle
4. A barometer is used to measure <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) temperature (b) atmospheric pressure (c) density (d) humidity
5. An iron nail sinks but a huge iron ship floats because the ship <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) has less mass (b) displaces water weighing as much as the ship (c) is hollow and empty (d) is painted
6. A sharp knife cuts better than a blunt one because the sharp edge has <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) less area, so more pressure (b) more area (c) more mass (d) less force
7. Upthrust on a fully submerged body does NOT depend on the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) volume of the body (b) density of the liquid (c) value of $g$ (d) material of the body

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $P=500/0.25=2000$ Pa.
**2.** (c) — $P=h\rho g=10\times1000\times10=10^{5}$ Pa.
**3.** (b) — Pascal's law transmits pressure through the confined oil.
**4.** (b) — a barometer reads atmospheric pressure.
**5.** (b) — the floating ship's weight equals the weight of water it displaces.
**6.** (a) — for the same force, smaller area gives larger pressure.
**7.** (d) — upthrust $=V\rho g$ depends on the volume, the liquid and $g$, not the body's material.
:::

## 2.9 Heat

**Facts.** $Q=mc\Delta T$ and $Q=mL$. Specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J kg⁻¹ °C⁻¹. Specific latent heat of fusion of ice is $3.36\times10^{5}$ J kg⁻¹ and of vaporisation of water is $2.26\times10^{6}$ J kg⁻¹. Water has maximum density at 4 °C. Conduction, convection and radiation are the three modes of heat transfer.

1. The heat needed to raise 2 kg of water by 10 °C is (take $c=4200$) <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 8400 J (b) 42 000 J (c) 84 000 J (d) 420 000 J
2. The heat needed to melt 0.5 kg of ice at 0 °C is (take $L=3.36\times10^{5}$ J kg⁻¹) <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $1.68\times10^{5}$ J (b) $3.36\times10^{5}$ J (c) $6.72\times10^{5}$ J (d) $1.68\times10^{4}$ J
3. Heat reaches us from the Sun mainly by <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) conduction (b) convection (c) radiation (d) all equally
4. Water has its maximum density at <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0 °C (b) 4 °C (c) 10 °C (d) 100 °C
5. Sea breeze blows from the sea to land during the day because <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) land heats up faster than water (b) water heats up faster than land (c) land is colder (d) the sea is hotter
6. Which material is the best conductor of heat? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) wood (b) glass (c) copper (d) plastic
7. The SI unit of specific heat capacity is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) J kg⁻¹ (b) J °C⁻¹ (c) J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ (d) W

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — $Q=2\times4200\times10=84\,000$ J.
**2.** (a) — $Q=0.5\times3.36\times10^{5}=1.68\times10^{5}$ J.
**3.** (c) — radiation travels through the vacuum of space.
**4.** (b) — anomalous expansion: water is densest at 4 °C.
**5.** (a) — warm air rises over land and cooler sea air flows in; land has a lower specific heat.
**6.** (c) — copper is a metal with free electrons.
**7.** (c) — $c=Q/(m\Delta T)$, unit J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.
:::

## 2.10 Wave

**Facts.** $v=f\lambda$; $f=1/T$. Sound needs a medium and travels about 340 m s⁻¹ in air; echo distance $d=vt/2$. Light obeys the laws of reflection; refractive index $n=\sin i/\sin r$. Lens power $P=1/f$ (f in metres, unit dioptre). Convex lens converges; concave lens diverges. Myopia is corrected by a concave lens.

1. A wave of frequency 50 Hz has wavelength 6.8 m. Its speed is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0.14 m s⁻¹ (b) 56.8 m s⁻¹ (c) 340 m s⁻¹ (d) 3400 m s⁻¹
2. Sound cannot travel through <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) steel (b) water (c) air (d) vacuum
3. An echo is heard 3 s after a shout. If the speed of sound is 340 m s⁻¹, the reflecting wall is at <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 170 m (b) 340 m (c) 510 m (d) 1020 m
4. The power of a convex lens of focal length 0.5 m is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0.5 D (b) 2 D (c) 5 D (d) 20 D
5. Short-sightedness (myopia) is corrected by a <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) convex lens (b) concave lens (c) plane mirror (d) prism
6. The bending of light as it passes from air to glass is called <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) reflection (b) dispersion (c) refraction (d) diffraction
7. White light splits into seven colours in a prism because <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) every colour has a different speed in glass (b) the prism makes colour (c) glass absorbs white (d) light changes frequency

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — $v=f\lambda=50\times6.8=340$ m s⁻¹.
**2.** (d) — sound is a mechanical wave and needs a medium.
**3.** (c) — $d=vt/2=340\times3/2=510$ m.
**4.** (b) — $P=1/0.5=2$ D.
**5.** (b) — a concave lens diverges rays so the image falls on the retina.
**6.** (c) — refraction is bending due to a change of speed.
**7.** (a) — dispersion: each colour has a different refractive index in glass.
:::

## 2.11 Electricity and Magnetism

**Facts.** $V=IR$; $P=VI=I^2R$; energy (kWh) = power (kW) × time (h). A transformer works on electromagnetic induction: $V_p/V_s=N_p/N_s$. A generator converts mechanical energy to electrical energy; a motor does the opposite. Fleming's left-hand rule gives the force on a current-carrying conductor.

1. A 12 V battery drives a current through a 4 Ω resistor. The current is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0.33 A (b) 3 A (c) 8 A (d) 48 A
2. The power of an appliance on 220 V drawing 5 A is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 44 W (b) 225 W (c) 1100 W (d) 1100 kW
3. A 2 kW heater runs for 3 hours. The energy used is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1.5 kWh (b) 5 kWh (c) 6 kWh (d) 12 kWh
4. A step-up transformer has <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) more turns in the primary (b) more turns in the secondary (c) equal turns (d) no secondary coil
5. A generator works on the principle of <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) electromagnetic induction (b) Pascal's law (c) refraction (d) electrolysis
6. The direction of force on a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field is found by <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Fleming's left-hand rule (b) Boyle's law (c) Snell's law (d) Archimedes' principle
7. A transformer has 100 primary turns and 1000 secondary turns. With 220 V in, the output is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 22 V (b) 220 V (c) 2200 V (d) 22 000 V

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $I=V/R=12/4=3$ A.
**2.** (c) — $P=VI=220\times5=1100$ W.
**3.** (c) — $E=2\times3=6$ kWh.
**4.** (b) — more secondary turns raise the voltage.
**5.** (a) — a coil turning in a magnetic field has an induced emf.
**6.** (a) — Fleming's left-hand rule gives the direction of the force.
**7.** (c) — $V_s=220\times1000/100=2200$ V.
:::

## 2.12 Universe

**Facts.** Light year $\approx9.46\times10^{12}$ km, a unit of distance. Sunlight takes about 500 s (8 min 20 s) to reach Earth. The Big Bang theory says the universe began from a hot, dense state about 13.8 billion years ago. The Milky Way is our galaxy. Mercury is the nearest planet to the Sun and Jupiter is the largest.

1. A light year is a unit of <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) time (b) speed (c) distance (d) brightness
2. Our galaxy is called the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Andromeda (b) Milky Way (c) Solar system (d) Orion
3. The planet nearest to the Sun is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Venus (b) Earth (c) Mercury (d) Mars
4. The theory that explains the origin of the universe from a hot, dense state is the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Big Bang theory (b) Heredity theory (c) Wave theory (d) Kinetic theory
5. Energy in the core of the Sun is released by <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) nuclear fission (b) nuclear fusion (c) burning of coal (d) chemical reaction
6. Sunlight takes 500 s to reach Earth. If light travels $3\times10^{5}$ km s⁻¹, the Earth–Sun distance is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $1.5\times10^{6}$ km (b) $1.5\times10^{8}$ km (c) $6\times10^{2}$ km (d) $1.5\times10^{10}$ km
7. A man-made body orbiting the Earth is called <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) a comet (b) an asteroid (c) an artificial satellite (d) a meteor

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — a light year is the distance light travels in a year.
**2.** (b) — the Milky Way contains our Sun.
**3.** (c) — Mercury orbits closest to the Sun.
**4.** (a) — the Big Bang theory.
**5.** (b) — hydrogen nuclei fuse to helium in the core.
**6.** (b) — $d=ct=3\times10^{5}\times500=1.5\times10^{8}$ km.
**7.** (c) — satellites are artificial; the Moon is natural.
:::

## 2.13 Information and Communication Technology

**Facts.** The CPU is the brain of the computer; RAM is volatile main memory; ROM is permanent. A network of networks is the internet; the web address is a URL. E-commerce is buying and selling over the internet. Cyber ethics and cyber crime are part of this chapter.

1. The brain of the computer is the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) monitor (b) CPU (c) keyboard (d) mouse
2. Which memory loses its data when power is switched off? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) ROM (b) hard disk (c) RAM (d) pen drive
3. The full form of URL is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Uniform Resource Locator (b) Universal Radio Link (c) United Resource List (d) Uniform Rule Logic
4. Buying goods through a website is called <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) e-commerce (b) e-mail (c) e-learning (d) e-voting
5. Sending false messages to steal a person's bank details is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) hacking only (b) phishing (c) browsing (d) downloading
6. Which is an output device? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) scanner (b) printer (c) keyboard (d) microphone
7. The worldwide network of connected computers is the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) intranet (b) LAN (c) internet (d) extranet

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — the CPU processes instructions.
**2.** (c) — RAM is volatile.
**3.** (a) — Uniform Resource Locator, the address of a web page.
**4.** (a) — e-commerce is trade over the internet.
**5.** (b) — phishing tricks users into giving private information.
**6.** (b) — a printer outputs information; the others are input devices.
**7.** (c) — the internet connects networks worldwide; a LAN is local.
:::

## 2.14 Classification of Elements

**Facts.** The modern periodic law arranges elements by increasing atomic number. The modern table has 18 groups and 7 periods. Group 1 is the alkali metals, group 17 the halogens, group 18 the noble gases. Across a period atomic size decreases; down a group it increases. Elements in a group have the same number of valence electrons.

1. The modern periodic law states that properties of elements are a periodic function of their <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) atomic mass (b) atomic number (c) density (d) valency only
2. An element with electronic configuration 2, 8, 7 is in <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) group 7, period 2 (b) group 17, period 3 (c) group 7, period 7 (d) group 3, period 17
3. Which group contains the noble gases? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) group 1 (b) group 2 (c) group 17 (d) group 18
4. Down a group the atomic size <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) decreases (b) increases (c) stays the same (d) first decreases then increases
5. Which element is an alkali metal? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) calcium (b) sodium (c) chlorine (d) neon
6. Atoms of elements in the same group have the same <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) number of shells (b) atomic number (c) number of valence electrons (d) mass number
7. The number of periods in the modern periodic table is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 3 (b) 7 (c) 8 (d) 18

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — Moseley's work led to atomic number as the basis.
**2.** (b) — three shells give period 3; seven valence electrons give group 17.
**3.** (d) — helium, neon, argon and the rest are in group 18.
**4.** (b) — each period down adds a shell.
**5.** (b) — sodium is in group 1; calcium is group 2.
**6.** (c) — the same valence electrons give similar properties.
**7.** (b) — seven horizontal rows.
:::

## 2.15 Chemical Reaction

**Facts.** Types: combination, decomposition, displacement, double displacement. An acid turns blue litmus red and has pH below 7; a base turns red litmus blue and has pH above 7. Neutralisation: acid + base → salt + water. Rate of reaction rises with temperature, concentration, surface area and a catalyst.

1. The reaction 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O is a <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) decomposition (b) displacement (c) combination (d) double displacement
2. The reaction HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O is called <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) neutralisation (b) combustion (c) decomposition (d) reduction
3. The pH of a neutral solution at 25 °C is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0 (b) 5 (c) 7 (d) 14
4. Which change speeds up a reaction? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) lowering temperature (b) increasing surface area (c) diluting the acid (d) removing the catalyst
5. In Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu, zinc <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) is displaced (b) displaces copper (c) is a catalyst (d) is a product only
6. Balanced form of Mg + O₂ → MgO has the coefficient of MgO as <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) 4
7. Lemon juice turns blue litmus <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) red (b) green (c) yellow (d) stays blue

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — two substances combine to form one.
**2.** (a) — acid and base give salt and water.
**3.** (c) — pH 7 is neutral.
**4.** (b) — more surface area gives more collisions.
**5.** (b) — zinc is more reactive than copper and displaces it.
**6.** (b) — $2\text{Mg}+\text{O}_2\to2\text{MgO}$.
**7.** (a) — citric acid changes blue litmus to red.
:::

## 2.16 Gases

**Facts.** Boyle's law: $P_1V_1=P_2V_2$ at constant temperature. Charles' law: $V_1/T_1=V_2/T_2$ at constant pressure, with $T$ in kelvin. Hydrogen is prepared from zinc and dilute sulphuric acid and burns with a pop; oxygen from heating potassium chlorate with manganese dioxide and relights a glowing splint; carbon dioxide from marble and dilute hydrochloric acid and turns lime water milky.

1. A gas at 2 atm occupies 6 L. At constant temperature, at 4 atm it occupies <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 3 L (b) 12 L (c) 6 L (d) 1.5 L
2. A gas of 6 L at 300 K is heated to 600 K at constant pressure. Its volume becomes <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 3 L (b) 6 L (c) 12 L (d) 18 L
3. Which gas gives a pop sound with a burning splint? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) oxygen (b) carbon dioxide (c) hydrogen (d) nitrogen
4. Lime water turns milky with <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) O₂ (b) H₂ (c) CO₂ (d) N₂
5. In the laboratory, hydrogen is prepared from <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) zinc and dilute H₂SO₄ (b) marble and HCl (c) KClO₃ and MnO₂ (d) copper and dilute H₂SO₄
6. The temperature 27 °C in kelvin is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 27 K (b) 246 K (c) 300 K (d) 373 K
7. MnO₂ in the preparation of oxygen from KClO₃ acts as a <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) reactant consumed (b) catalyst (c) reducing agent (d) product

::: note Answers
**1.** (a) — $P_1V_1=P_2V_2$: $2\times6=4\times V_2$, so $V_2=3$ L.
**2.** (c) — $V_2=V_1T_2/T_1=6\times600/300=12$ L.
**3.** (c) — hydrogen burns explosively with a pop.
**4.** (c) — CO₂ forms insoluble calcium carbonate.
**5.** (a) — zinc displaces hydrogen from dilute acid; copper does not.
**6.** (c) — $27+273=300$ K.
**7.** (b) — MnO₂ speeds the decomposition and is unchanged.
:::

## 2.17 Metal and Non-metals

**Facts.** Reactivity series (high to low): K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, H, Cu, Ag, Au. Rusting needs oxygen and water. Iron is extracted in the blast furnace; aluminium by electrolysis of alumina dissolved in molten cryolite. Alloys: brass (Cu + Zn), bronze (Cu + Sn). Sodium is stored under kerosene.

1. The most reactive metal in the list K, Na, Zn, Cu is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) K (b) Na (c) Zn (d) Cu
2. Rusting of iron requires <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) oxygen only (b) water only (c) oxygen and water (d) nitrogen and water
3. Brass is an alloy of <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) copper and zinc (b) copper and tin (c) iron and carbon (d) lead and tin
4. The ore from which aluminium is obtained is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) haematite (b) bauxite (c) galena (d) cinnabar
5. Which metal does NOT displace hydrogen from dilute acids? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) zinc (b) magnesium (c) copper (d) iron
6. Coating iron with zinc to prevent rusting is called <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) galvanisation (b) electrolysis (c) roasting (d) smelting
7. Which non-metal is a good conductor of electricity? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) sulphur (b) graphite (c) iodine (d) phosphorus

::: note Answers
**1.** (a) — potassium tops the reactivity series.
**2.** (c) — both are needed; iron(III) oxide forms.
**3.** (a) — brass is copper and zinc; bronze is copper and tin.
**4.** (b) — bauxite is hydrated aluminium oxide.
**5.** (c) — copper lies below hydrogen in the series.
**6.** (a) — galvanisation uses a zinc coat.
**7.** (b) — graphite has free electrons between its layers.
:::

## 2.18 Hydrocarbon and its Compounds

**Facts.** Alkanes $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$ (single bonds), alkenes $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$ (a double bond), alkynes $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n-2}$ (a triple bond). Successive members of a homologous series differ by CH₂. Ethanol is C₂H₅OH and acetic acid is CH₃COOH. Alcohol + carboxylic acid → ester + water.

1. The molecular formula of propane is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) C₃H₆ (b) C₃H₈ (c) C₃H₄ (d) C₂H₆
2. Ethene belongs to the series of <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) alkanes (b) alkenes (c) alkynes (d) alcohols
3. The functional group of a carboxylic acid is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) –OH (b) –CHO (c) –COOH (d) –CO–
4. The difference between two successive members of a homologous series is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) CH₂ (b) CH₃ (c) C₂H₄ (d) H₂
5. The compound present in vinegar is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) ethanol (b) methanol (c) acetic acid (d) formic acid
6. The formula of ethyne is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) C₂H₂ (b) C₂H₄ (c) C₂H₆ (d) CH₄
7. Ethanol reacts with acetic acid to form <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) ether (b) an ester (c) an alkane (d) soap

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $n=3$: $\text{C}_3\text{H}_{2(3)+2}=\text{C}_3\text{H}_8$.
**2.** (b) — it has the C=C double bond.
**3.** (c) — carboxyl group –COOH.
**4.** (a) — one carbon and two hydrogens.
**5.** (c) — vinegar is dilute acetic acid.
**6.** (a) — a triple bond: $\text{C}_2\text{H}_{2(2)-2}=\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$.
**7.** (b) — esterification gives ethyl ethanoate and water.
:::

## 2.19 Chemicals used in Daily Life

**Facts.** Soap is the sodium (or potassium) salt of a fatty acid, made by saponification. Cement is made from limestone and clay, with gypsum added to slow setting. Glass is made from silica (sand), soda ash and limestone. Urea contains about 46% nitrogen. NPK fertilisers supply nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium. Polythene is a polymer of ethene.

1. Soap is made by heating oil or fat with <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) dilute acid (b) sodium hydroxide (c) water only (d) ammonia
2. Gypsum is added to cement to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) make it set faster (b) slow down its setting (c) colour it (d) make it lighter
3. The raw materials of glass include <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) silica, soda ash and limestone (b) clay and gypsum (c) coal and iron (d) urea and potash
4. The most nitrogen-rich common fertiliser is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) urea (b) superphosphate (c) potash (d) lime
5. Polythene is a polymer of <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) methane (b) ethene (c) ethanol (d) benzene
6. Soap forms scum in hard water, whereas detergent <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) also forms scum (b) does not form scum (c) is acidic only (d) is not a cleaner
7. A plastic that softens on heating and can be remoulded is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) thermosetting (b) thermoplastic (c) bakelite (d) vulcanised rubber

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — fat plus NaOH gives soap and glycerol (saponification).
**2.** (b) — gypsum controls the setting time.
**3.** (a) — sand, soda ash and limestone are melted together.
**4.** (a) — urea is about 46% nitrogen.
**5.** (b) — polythene is made from many ethene molecules.
**6.** (b) — detergent calcium and magnesium salts remain soluble.
**7.** (b) — thermoplastics can be reshaped; bakelite is thermosetting.
:::
