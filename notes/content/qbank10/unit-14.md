Compulsory Social Studies in the SEE is a 75-mark, three-hour paper. Its very short questions, worth one mark each, are the objective part: a fact, a date, a name, a place. They are the quickest marks in the paper and the ones lost most often through careless recall. This chapter gives practice items for all ten units of the Grade 10 Social Studies book, with an answer key and a one-line reason for every item.

::: caution About the labels in this chapter
Every item here is an **original practice item** written to the pattern of the SEE and to the facts of the Social Studies book chapters in this series. None is claimed to be a question from a particular year's paper. A year printed beside a question is usually a guess, and a wrong label teaches you to trust the wrong thing.
:::

## 14.1 Where objective items sit in the paper

The Curriculum Development Centre (CDC) grid for Grade 10 Social Studies, as published for the 2080 curriculum, is a 75-mark paper of **24 questions**: 11 very short answer questions of 1 mark, 9 short answer questions of 4 marks and 4 long answer questions of 7 marks. Another source I checked prints a different split of the same 75 marks (11 x 1, 4 x 4 and 8 x 6). The 11 one-mark items are the same in both, which is why this chapter concentrates on them; check the exact short and long split in the grid your school gives you.

| Group | Questions | Marks each | Total | Style |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Very short | 11 | 1 | 11 | one fact, one word, a date or a name |
| B. Short | 9 | 4 | 36 | give reasons, define and explain |
| C. Long | 4 | 7 | 28 | explain, describe, analyse |

The unit hours below are those reported for the Grade 10 curriculum and are a good guide to how the 11 one-mark items are spread: almost every unit supplies one.

::: tip How to use the bank
Answer ten items of one unit in five minutes without notes, then check the key. For each miss, find the sentence in the book chapter and write the fact on a card. Numbers, dates and names are what these items test, so the cards should carry numbers, dates and names.
:::

## Chapter summary

- Group A of the paper is 11 one-mark items; the CDC grid then has 9 short (4-mark) and 4 long (7-mark) answers, so Group A is about 15% of the paper.
- Nepal's key numbers: population 29,164,578 (2021); 142 caste/ethnic groups; 124 languages; 7 provinces; 753 local levels; HoR 275 (165 + 110); NA 59.
- Key dates: 7 Falgun 2007 (Democracy Day), 1 Poush 2017 (royal coup), 7 Falgun – 26 Chaitra 2046 (Jana Andolan I), 21 Nov 2006 (Peace Accord), 28 May 2008 (republic), 20 Sept 2015 (Constitution), 3 Ashwin 2072.
- International: Nepal joined the UN in 1955, NAM in 1961, SAARC in 1985, BIMSTEC in 1997, WTO in 2004; the UN has 193 members and 6 organs.
- Population: growth rate 0.92%, peak 2.62% in 1981, density 198, sex ratio 95.59, literacy 76.2%.
- Economy: hydropower potential about 83,000 MW, installed about 3,500 MW; VAT 13%; fiscal year begins 1 Shrawan.
- Earth: temperature falls 6.5 °C per 1,000 m; Kilimanjaro 5,895 m, Aconcagua about 6,960 m, Denali 6,190 m.
