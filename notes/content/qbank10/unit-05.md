The SEE Compulsory Mathematics paper has no multiple-choice group. Its 16 questions are built from items worth 1 mark upward, and the first items of most questions test **knowledge**: a formula, a definition, a one-line value. This chapter drills those objective items chapter by chapter, in multiple-choice form so that you can check each one in seconds. Each section opens with a **facts line**, gives six items, and ends with a key that has a one-line reason for every answer.

::: key How to use the objective bank
These items are the 12 knowledge marks and some of the 18 understanding marks of the grid. They look easy, and the examiner means them to be; the risk is a slip in a formula or a unit. Work each item in under a minute, then read the reason. Take $\pi=\frac{22}{7}$ unless an item says otherwise.
:::

::: caution Practice items, not past questions
Every item is an original **practice** item written to the CDC pattern. None is claimed to have appeared in a particular year's paper. The numbers have been recomputed with a program as well as by hand.
:::

## 5.1 Sets

**Facts.** $n(A\cup B)=n(A)+n(B)-n(A\cap B)$; $(A\cup B)'=A'\cap B'$; $(A\cap B)'=A'\cup B'$; $n(U)=n(A\cup B)+n(A\cup B)'$.

1. If $n(A)=20$, $n(B)=15$ and $n(A\cap B)=5$, then $n(A\cup B)$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 40 (b) 35 (c) 30 (d) 25
2. If $n(U)=50$ and $n(A\cup B)=40$, then $n(A\cup B)'$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 10 (b) 90 (c) 40 (d) 20
3. $(A\cup B)'$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $A'\cup B'$ (b) $A'\cap B'$ (c) $A\cap B$ (d) $A\cup B'$
4. If $n(A)=25$ and $n(A\cap B)=8$, the number of elements only in $A$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 33 (b) 25 (c) 8 (d) 17
5. In a class of 40, every student likes football or cricket; 25 like football and 20 like cricket. The number liking both is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 5 (b) 10 (c) 15 (d) 45
6. If $A\subset B$, then $A\cap B$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $B$ (b) $\emptyset$ (c) $A$ (d) $A\cup B$

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — $20+15-5=30$.
**2.** (a) — $50-40=10$.
**3.** (b) — De Morgan's law.
**4.** (d) — $25-8=17$.
**5.** (a) — $25+20-40=5$.
**6.** (c) — every element of $A$ is already in $B$.
:::

## 5.2 Tax and Money Exchange

**Facts.** VAT at 13%: bill $=$ price $\times1.13$. With excise duty $e\%$ and VAT: price $\times(1+\frac{e}{100})\times1.13$. Currency: amount in one currency $=$ amount in the other $\times$ rate.

1. VAT at 13% on a price of Rs 2000 is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 13 (b) Rs 26 (c) Rs 130 (d) Rs 260
2. A bill is Rs 11,300 including 13% VAT. The price without VAT is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 9,830 (b) Rs 10,000 (c) Rs 11,287 (d) Rs 12,769
3. A shopkeeper sells goods worth Rs 8000 plus 13% VAT. The bill is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 8,013 (b) Rs 8,104 (c) Rs 9,040 (d) Rs 8,130
4. If USD 1 = Rs 130, USD 200 is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 650 (b) Rs 2,600 (c) Rs 13,000 (d) Rs 26,000
5. If Rs 160 = INR 100, then INR 5000 is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 3,125 (b) Rs 8,000 (c) Rs 80,000 (d) Rs 5,160
6. A factory price of Rs 1000 carries 10% excise duty and then 13% VAT. The final price is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 1,230 (b) Rs 1,243 (c) Rs 1,243.30 (d) Rs 1,100

::: note Answers
**1.** (d) — $0.13\times2000=260$.
**2.** (b) — $11300\div1.13=10000$.
**3.** (c) — $8000\times1.13=9040$.
**4.** (d) — $200\times130=26000$.
**5.** (b) — $5000\times\frac{160}{100}=8000$.
**6.** (b) — $1000\times1.10\times1.13=1243$; VAT applies to price plus excise.
:::

## 5.3 Compound Interest

**Facts.** $A=P\left(1+\frac{R}{100}\right)^{T}$; $CI=A-P$; half-yearly: $A=P\left(1+\frac{R}{200}\right)^{2T}$. For 2 years, $CI-SI=P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^{2}$.

1. The amount on Rs 10,000 for 2 years at 10% compound interest per annum is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 12,000 (b) Rs 12,100 (c) Rs 12,200 (d) Rs 2,100
2. The compound interest in item 1 is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 2,000 (b) Rs 2,100 (c) Rs 12,100 (d) Rs 1,000
3. Rs 4000 at 10% p.a. compounded half-yearly for 1 year amounts to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 4,400 (b) Rs 4,410 (c) Rs 4,420 (d) Rs 4,200
4. For Rs 8000 at 5% for 2 years, the difference between CI and SI is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 8 (b) Rs 20 (c) Rs 40 (d) Rs 800
5. A principal becomes Rs 1331 in 3 years at 10% compound interest. The principal is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 1,000 (b) Rs 1,100 (c) Rs 900 (d) Rs 1,210
6. In half-yearly compounding the rate used per period is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $R$ (b) $2R$ (c) $\frac{R}{2}$ (d) $\frac{R}{4}$

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $10000(1.1)^2=12100$.
**2.** (b) — $12100-10000=2100$.
**3.** (b) — rate per half-year 5%, two periods: $4000(1.05)^2=4410$.
**4.** (b) — $8000\times(0.05)^2=20$.
**5.** (a) — $1331\div(1.1)^3=1331\div1.331=1000$.
**6.** (c) — the annual rate is halved and the periods doubled.
:::

## 5.4 Population Growth and Depreciation

**Facts.** Growth: $P_T=P_0\left(1+\frac{R}{100}\right)^{T}$. Depreciation: $V_T=V_0\left(1-\frac{R}{100}\right)^{T}$. Net rate of growth $=$ birth rate $-$ death rate $\pm$ net migration rate.

1. A population of 20,000 grows at 5% a year. After 2 years it is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 22,000 (b) 22,050 (c) 22,500 (d) 21,000
2. A population of 5000 decreases by 10% a year. After 2 years it is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 4,000 (b) 4,050 (c) 4,500 (d) 3,500
3. A town has 24,200 people now; the growth rate is 10% a year. Two years ago it had <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 20,000 (b) 19,360 (c) 21,000 (d) 22,000
4. A machine of value Rs 80,000 depreciates 10% a year. After 2 years its value is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 64,000 (b) Rs 64,800 (c) Rs 72,000 (d) Rs 60,000
5. Birth rate 3%, death rate 1% and out-migration 0.5% give a net growth rate of <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1.5% (b) 2% (c) 2.5% (d) 4.5%
6. A quantity of 1000 grows 10% in year 1 and 20% in year 2. At the end it is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1,300 (b) 1,320 (c) 1,330 (d) 1,440

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $20000(1.05)^2=22050$.
**2.** (b) — $5000(0.9)^2=4050$.
**3.** (a) — $24200\div(1.1)^2=20000$.
**4.** (b) — $80000(0.9)^2=64800$.
**5.** (a) — $3-1-0.5=1.5$.
**6.** (b) — $1000\times1.1\times1.2=1320$.
:::

## 5.5 Plane Surfaces

**Facts.** Heron: $A=\sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}$ with $s=\frac{a+b+c}{2}$; circle $A=\pi r^2$, $C=2\pi r$; ring $A=\pi(R^2-r^2)$; sector $A=\frac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2$.

1. The area of a triangle with sides 13 cm, 14 cm and 15 cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 84 cm² (b) 90 cm² (c) 168 cm² (d) 42 cm²
2. A ring has outer radius 14 cm and inner radius 7 cm. Its area is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 154 cm² (b) 308 cm² (c) 462 cm² (d) 616 cm²
3. A path 1 m wide runs around the outside of a 20 m × 15 m garden. The area of the path is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 70 m² (b) 74 m² (c) 72 m² (d) 76 m²
4. The number of 20 cm × 20 cm tiles needed for a 6 m × 4 m floor is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 60 (b) 120 (c) 600 (d) 6000
5. The area of a quadrant (90° sector) of radius 14 cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 154 cm² (b) 308 cm² (c) 616 cm² (d) 77 cm²
6. The perimeter of a semicircle of diameter 14 cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 22 cm (b) 36 cm (c) 44 cm (d) 58 cm

::: note Answers
**1.** (a) — $s=21$; $\sqrt{21\times8\times7\times6}=\sqrt{7056}=84$.
**2.** (c) — $\frac{22}{7}(196-49)=\frac{22}{7}\times147=462$.
**3.** (b) — outer $22\times17=374$; inner $20\times15=300$; $374-300=74$.
**4.** (c) — area 24 m² over 0.04 m² per tile gives 600.
**5.** (a) — $\frac14\times\frac{22}{7}\times196=154$.
**6.** (b) — arc $\frac{22}{7}\times7=22$, plus diameter 14, gives 36.
:::

## 5.6 Cylinder and Sphere

**Facts.** Cylinder: curved surface area $2\pi rh$, total $2\pi r(r+h)$, volume $\pi r^2h$. Sphere: surface area $4\pi r^2$, volume $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$. Hemisphere: curved $2\pi r^2$, total $3\pi r^2$, volume $\frac{2}{3}\pi r^3$. 1 m³ = 1000 litres.

1. The curved surface area of a cylinder with $r=7$ cm and $h=10$ cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 220 cm² (b) 440 cm² (c) 1540 cm² (d) 308 cm²
2. The volume of the same cylinder is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 440 cm³ (b) 770 cm³ (c) 1540 cm³ (d) 3080 cm³
3. The surface area of a sphere of radius 7 cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 154 cm² (b) 308 cm² (c) 616 cm² (d) 1437 cm²
4. The total surface area of a solid hemisphere of radius 7 cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 308 cm² (b) 462 cm² (c) 616 cm² (d) 154 cm²
5. The volume of a sphere of radius 10.5 cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 2425 cm³ (b) 4851 cm³ (c) 4620 cm³ (d) 1617 cm³
6. A cylindrical tank has radius 0.7 m and height 2 m. Its capacity is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 308 litres (b) 1540 litres (c) 3080 litres (d) 30 800 litres

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $2\times\frac{22}{7}\times7\times10=440$.
**2.** (c) — $\frac{22}{7}\times49\times10=1540$.
**3.** (c) — $4\times\frac{22}{7}\times49=616$.
**4.** (b) — $3\pi r^2=3\times\frac{22}{7}\times49=462$.
**5.** (b) — $\frac43\times\frac{22}{7}\times(10.5)^3=4851$.
**6.** (c) — $\frac{22}{7}\times0.49\times2=3.08$ m³ $=3080$ litres.
:::

## 5.7 Prism and Pyramid

**Facts.** Prism: volume $=$ base area $\times$ height (length); lateral surface area $=$ perimeter of base $\times$ height; total $=$ lateral $+\ 2\times$ base area. Square pyramid: volume $=\frac{1}{3}\times$ base area $\times$ height; lateral area $=\frac{1}{2}\times$ perimeter $\times$ slant height; $l^2=h^2+\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2$. 1 m³ = 1000 litres.

1. A triangular prism has a base area of 6 cm² and length 10 cm. Its volume is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 16 cm³ (b) 30 cm³ (c) 60 cm³ (d) 120 cm³
2. A right triangular prism has a base with sides 3 cm, 4 cm, 5 cm and length 10 cm. Its lateral surface area is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 60 cm² (b) 120 cm² (c) 132 cm² (d) 12 cm²
3. The total surface area of the prism in item 2 is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 120 cm² (b) 126 cm² (c) 132 cm² (d) 144 cm²
4. A square pyramid has base side 6 cm and height 4 cm. Its slant height is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 4 cm (b) 5 cm (c) 6 cm (d) 7 cm
5. The volume of the pyramid in item 4 is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 24 cm³ (b) 48 cm³ (c) 72 cm³ (d) 144 cm³
6. The area of the four walls of a room 5 m × 4 m × 3 m is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 27 m² (b) 54 m² (c) 60 m² (d) 94 m²

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — $6\times10=60$.
**2.** (b) — perimeter $12\times10=120$.
**3.** (c) — base area $\frac12\times3\times4=6$, so $120+2(6)=132$.
**4.** (b) — $l=\sqrt{4^2+3^2}=5$.
**5.** (b) — $\frac13\times36\times4=48$.
**6.** (b) — $2(5+4)\times3=54$.
:::

## 5.8 Highest Common Factor and Lowest Common Multiple

**Facts.** Factorise completely first. HCF is the product of the common factors of least power; LCM is the product of all factors of greatest power. $\text{HCF}\times\text{LCM}=$ product of the two expressions.

1. The HCF of $x^2-y^2$ and $x+y$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $x-y$ (b) $x+y$ (c) $x^2-y^2$ (d) 1
2. The LCM of $3x^2y$ and $6xy^2$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $3xy$ (b) $6xy$ (c) $6x^2y^2$ (d) $18x^3y^3$
3. The HCF of $12a^2b$ and $18ab^2$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $36a^2b^2$ (b) $6ab$ (c) $6a^2b^2$ (d) $3ab$
4. The LCM of $x^2-4$ and $x-2$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $x-2$ (b) $x+2$ (c) $x^2-4$ (d) $(x-2)^2$
5. The HCF of $x^2+5x+6$ and $x^2+3x+2$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $x+1$ (b) $x+2$ (c) $x+3$ (d) $x-2$
6. If one expression is $(x+2)(x-3)$, the HCF is $x+2$ and the LCM is $(x+2)(x-3)(x+1)$, the other expression is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $x+1$ (b) $x-3$ (c) $(x+2)(x+1)$ (d) $(x-3)(x+1)$

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $x^2-y^2=(x-y)(x+y)$, so the common factor is $x+y$.
**2.** (c) — LCM of 3 and 6 is 6, and the highest powers are $x^2$ and $y^2$.
**3.** (b) — HCF of 12 and 18 is 6, lowest powers $a$ and $b$.
**4.** (c) — $x^2-4=(x-2)(x+2)$ already contains $x-2$.
**5.** (b) — $(x+2)(x+3)$ and $(x+1)(x+2)$ share $x+2$.
**6.** (c) — other $=\frac{\text{HCF}\times\text{LCM}}{\text{given}}=\frac{(x+2)\cdot(x+2)(x-3)(x+1)}{(x+2)(x-3)}=(x+2)(x+1)$.
:::

## 5.9 Radical and Surd

**Facts.** $\sqrt{ab}=\sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}$; the conjugate of $a+\sqrt{b}$ is $a-\sqrt{b}$ and $(a+\sqrt{b})(a-\sqrt{b})=a^2-b$. Rationalise by multiplying by the conjugate. If $x+\frac1x=k$ then $x^2+\frac{1}{x^2}=k^2-2$.

1. $\sqrt{12}$ in simplest form is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $3\sqrt{2}$ (b) $2\sqrt{3}$ (c) $4\sqrt{3}$ (d) $6$
2. $\sqrt{18}+\sqrt{8}$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\sqrt{26}$ (b) $5\sqrt{2}$ (c) $6\sqrt{2}$ (d) $13$
3. $(2+\sqrt{3})(2-\sqrt{3})$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1 (b) 7 (c) $4-\sqrt{3}$ (d) $2$
4. The rationalised form of $\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}-1}$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\sqrt{3}-1$ (b) $\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{2}$ (c) $\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{2}$ (d) $\sqrt{3}+1$
5. If $x=2+\sqrt{3}$, then $\frac1x$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $2+\sqrt{3}$ (b) $\sqrt{3}-2$ (c) $2-\sqrt{3}$ (d) $\frac{1}{2}$
6. If $x=2+\sqrt{3}$, then $x^2+\frac{1}{x^2}$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 4 (b) 14 (c) 16 (d) 18

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $\sqrt{4\times3}=2\sqrt{3}$.
**2.** (b) — $3\sqrt{2}+2\sqrt{2}=5\sqrt{2}$.
**3.** (a) — $4-3=1$.
**4.** (b) — $\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{(\sqrt{3})^2-1}=\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{2}$.
**5.** (c) — $(2+\sqrt{3})(2-\sqrt{3})=1$, so the reciprocal is $2-\sqrt{3}$.
**6.** (b) — $x+\frac1x=4$, so $x^2+\frac{1}{x^2}=16-2=14$.
:::

## 5.10 Indices

**Facts.** $a^m\times a^n=a^{m+n}$; $\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}$; $(a^m)^n=a^{mn}$; $a^0=1$; $a^{-n}=\frac{1}{a^n}$; $a^{\frac{m}{n}}=\sqrt[n]{a^m}$. If $a^x=a^y$ then $x=y$.

1. $2^3\times2^4$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $2^{12}$ (b) $4^7$ (c) $2^7$ (d) $2^{1}$
2. The value of $64^{\frac23}$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 4 (b) 8 (c) 16 (d) 32
3. The value of $2^{-3}$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $-8$ (b) $-6$ (c) $\frac{1}{6}$ (d) $\frac{1}{8}$
4. If $3^x=81$, then $x$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 27 (d) 5
5. $\frac{(x^2)^3}{x^4}$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $x^2$ (b) $x^3$ (c) $x^{10}$ (d) $x^{-2}$
6. The value of $(25)^0+5^{-1}\times5$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 5 (d) 6

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — add the powers: $2^{3+4}=2^7$.
**2.** (c) — $64^{2/3}=(4^3)^{2/3}=4^2=16$.
**3.** (d) — $2^{-3}=\frac{1}{2^3}$.
**4.** (b) — $81=3^4$.
**5.** (a) — $\frac{x^6}{x^4}=x^2$.
**6.** (b) — $1+\frac15\times5=1+1=2$.
:::

## 5.11 Algebraic Fraction

**Facts.** Factorise, then cancel common factors. Add or subtract over the LCM of the denominators. $\frac ab\div\frac cd=\frac{ad}{bc}$. The difference of squares: $a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)$.

1. $\frac{x^2-9}{x-3}$ simplifies to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $x-3$ (b) $x+3$ (c) $x^2-3$ (d) 3
2. $\frac1x+\frac1y$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac{2}{x+y}$ (b) $\frac{1}{xy}$ (c) $\frac{x+y}{xy}$ (d) $\frac{xy}{x+y}$
3. $\frac{a}{b}\div\frac{c}{d}$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac{ac}{bd}$ (b) $\frac{bc}{ad}$ (c) $\frac{ad}{bc}$ (d) $\frac{a}{bc}$
4. $\frac{1}{x-1}-\frac{1}{x+1}$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0 (b) $\frac{2}{x^2-1}$ (c) $\frac{2x}{x^2-1}$ (d) $\frac{1}{x^2-1}$
5. $\frac{x^2+3x+2}{x^2+x}$ simplifies to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac{x+2}{x}$ (b) $\frac{x+1}{x}$ (c) $\frac{x+2}{x+1}$ (d) 2
6. $\frac{2}{x}+\frac{3}{x}$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac{5}{2x}$ (b) $\frac{6}{x^2}$ (c) $\frac{5}{x}$ (d) $\frac{5}{x^2}$

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3}=x+3$.
**2.** (c) — common denominator $xy$.
**3.** (c) — invert the divisor and multiply.
**4.** (b) — $\frac{(x+1)-(x-1)}{x^2-1}=\frac{2}{x^2-1}$.
**5.** (a) — $\frac{(x+1)(x+2)}{x(x+1)}=\frac{x+2}{x}$.
**6.** (c) — same denominator, add numerators.
:::

## 5.12 Equations

**Facts.** Elimination or substitution for two linear equations. Quadratic by factorising or $x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$. Check roots in the original problem and reject any that make no sense (such as a negative length).

1. If $x+y=7$ and $x-y=1$, then $x$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 5 (d) 6
2. The roots of $x^2-5x+6=0$ are <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1 and 6 (b) $-2$ and $-3$ (c) 2 and 3 (d) $-1$ and $-6$
3. The solutions of $x^2=49$ are <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 7 only (b) $\pm7$ (c) $\pm 24.5$ (d) $-7$ only
4. The equation $x^2+2x+1=0$ has <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) two distinct roots (b) no real root (c) one repeated root $x=-1$ (d) root $x=1$
5. Two numbers have sum 10 and difference 2. The larger is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 8
6. The length of a rectangle is 3 m more than its breadth and its area is 40 m². The breadth is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 4 m (b) 5 m (c) 8 m (d) 10 m

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — adding gives $2x=8$.
**2.** (c) — $(x-2)(x-3)=0$.
**3.** (b) — both $7$ and $-7$ square to 49.
**4.** (c) — $(x+1)^2=0$.
**5.** (c) — $a+b=10$, $a-b=2$ gives $a=6$.
**6.** (b) — $b(b+3)=40\Rightarrow(b+8)(b-5)=0$; reject $b=-8$.
:::

## 5.13 Area of Triangles and Quadrilaterals

**Facts.** Triangle $=\frac12\times$ base $\times$ height. Parallelograms on the same base and between the same parallels are equal in area. A triangle and a parallelogram on the same base and between the same parallels: triangle $=\frac12$ parallelogram. A median divides a triangle into two triangles of equal area.

1. The area of a triangle of base 10 cm and height 6 cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 16 cm² (b) 30 cm² (c) 60 cm² (d) 36 cm²
2. A triangle and a parallelogram stand on the same base and between the same parallels. If the parallelogram has area 40 cm², the triangle has area <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 80 cm² (b) 40 cm² (c) 20 cm² (d) 10 cm²
3. A median divides a triangle of area 36 cm² into two triangles each of area <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 9 cm² (b) 12 cm² (c) 18 cm² (d) 36 cm²
4. The area of a rhombus with diagonals 10 cm and 24 cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 240 cm² (b) 120 cm² (c) 34 cm² (d) 60 cm²
5. The area of a trapezium with parallel sides 8 cm and 12 cm and height 5 cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 40 cm² (b) 50 cm² (c) 100 cm² (d) 60 cm²
6. Parallelograms on the same base and between the same parallels are <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) congruent (b) equal in area (c) similar only (d) equal in perimeter

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $\frac12\times10\times6=30$.
**2.** (c) — triangle is half the parallelogram.
**3.** (c) — a median bisects the area: $36\div2=18$.
**4.** (b) — $\frac12\times10\times24=120$.
**5.** (b) — $\frac12(8+12)\times5=50$.
**6.** (b) — equal in area, though not necessarily congruent.
:::

## 5.14 Construction

**Facts.** To construct a triangle equal in area to a quadrilateral, join a diagonal and draw a line through the opposite vertex parallel to that diagonal. Triangles on the same base and between the same parallels are equal in area. A rectangle equal to a triangle has the same base and half the height.

1. The theorem used to construct a triangle equal in area to a given quadrilateral is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) triangles on the same base and between the same parallels are equal in area (b) angle in a semicircle is 90° (c) Pythagoras' theorem (d) the sine rule
2. A rectangle equal in area to a triangle of base 8 cm and height 6 cm has the same base and a height of <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 6 cm (b) 12 cm (c) 3 cm (d) 4 cm
3. A square equal in area to a rectangle of 9 cm × 4 cm has side <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 5 cm (b) 6 cm (c) 6.5 cm (d) 13 cm
4. The area of a triangle with base 8 cm and height 6 cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 14 cm² (b) 48 cm² (c) 24 cm² (d) 12 cm²
5. To draw a line through a point parallel to a given line, you use <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) equal alternate angles (b) the median (c) the centroid (d) the perimeter
6. In the steps of construction, the compass arcs <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) should be rubbed out (b) should be left visible (c) are not required (d) are replaced by a ruler line

::: note Answers
**1.** (a) — shifting the vertex along a parallel keeps the area.
**2.** (c) — triangle area 24 cm²; with base 8 cm the rectangle height is $24\div8=3$ cm, half the triangle's height.
**3.** (b) — area 36, side 6 cm.
**4.** (c) — $\frac12\times8\times6=24$.
**5.** (a) — copy an angle so the alternate angles are equal.
**6.** (b) — examiners credit visible construction arcs.
:::

## 5.15 Circle

**Facts.** Angle at the centre $=2\times$ angle at the circumference on the same arc. Angles in the same segment are equal. Angle in a semicircle $=90^\circ$. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to $180^\circ$. A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact. A perpendicular from the centre bisects a chord.

1. An arc subtends 40° at the circumference. The angle at the centre is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 20° (b) 40° (c) 80° (d) 160°
2. The angle in a semicircle is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 45° (b) 90° (c) 120° (d) 180°
3. One angle of a cyclic quadrilateral is 110°. The opposite angle is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 70° (b) 110° (c) 90° (d) 250°
4. A point is 13 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 5 cm. The length of a tangent from the point is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 8 cm (b) 12 cm (c) 18 cm (d) 144 cm
5. Angles in the same segment standing on one chord are <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) complementary (b) supplementary (c) equal (d) double
6. A chord of length 16 cm lies in a circle of radius 10 cm. Its distance from the centre is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 4 cm (b) 6 cm (c) 8 cm (d) 12 cm

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — the centre angle is twice: $2\times40=80$.
**2.** (b) — a diameter subtends a right angle.
**3.** (a) — $180-110=70$.
**4.** (b) — $\sqrt{13^2-5^2}=\sqrt{144}=12$.
**5.** (c) — equal angles in the same segment.
**6.** (b) — half-chord 8, so $\sqrt{10^2-8^2}=6$.
:::

## 5.16 Trigonometry

**Facts.** $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$; $1+\tan^2\theta=\sec^2\theta$. $\sin30^\circ=\frac12$, $\cos30^\circ=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\tan45^\circ=1$, $\sin60^\circ=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. $\sin\theta=\cos(90^\circ-\theta)$.

1. The value of $\sin30^\circ$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ (b) $1$ (c) $\frac12$ (d) $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$
2. If $\sin\theta=\frac{5}{13}$ and $\theta$ is acute, then $\cos\theta$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac{12}{13}$ (b) $\frac{8}{13}$ (c) $\frac{5}{12}$ (d) $\frac{13}{12}$
3. $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0 (b) $\sin2\theta$ (c) $\tan^2\theta$ (d) 1
4. $\sin25^\circ$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\cos25^\circ$ (b) $\cos65^\circ$ (c) $\tan65^\circ$ (d) $\sin65^\circ$
5. The angle of elevation of a tower top from a point 20 m away is $45^\circ$. The height of the tower is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 10 m (b) 20 m (c) $20\sqrt{2}$ m (d) 40 m
6. The value of $\sin^230^\circ+\cos^230^\circ$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac12$ (b) $\frac34$ (c) $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ (d) 1

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — standard value.
**2.** (a) — hypotenuse 13, opposite 5, adjacent $\sqrt{169-25}=12$.
**3.** (d) — the basic identity.
**4.** (b) — complementary angles: $\sin\theta=\cos(90^\circ-\theta)$.
**5.** (b) — $\tan45^\circ=1=\frac{h}{20}$, so $h=20$ m.
**6.** (d) — $\frac14+\frac34=1$.
:::

## 5.17 Statistics

**Facts.** Range $=L-S$; coefficient of range $=\frac{L-S}{L+S}$. Quartile deviation $=\frac{Q_3-Q_1}{2}$; its coefficient is $\frac{Q_3-Q_1}{Q_3+Q_1}$. Standard deviation $\sigma=\sqrt{\frac{\sum(x-\bar x)^2}{n}}$. Coefficient of variation $=\frac{\sigma}{\bar x}\times100\%$; the smaller it is, the more consistent the data.

1. The largest value is 48 and the smallest is 12. The range is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 30 (b) 36 (c) 60 (d) 4
2. The coefficient of range in item 1 is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0.6 (b) 0.4 (c) 0.75 (d) 1.6
3. If $Q_1=20$ and $Q_3=40$, the quartile deviation is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 20 (b) 30 (c) 10 (d) 60
4. The coefficient of quartile deviation for $Q_1=20$ and $Q_3=40$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac12$ (b) $\frac13$ (c) $\frac23$ (d) 20
5. The standard deviation of 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 5, 7, 9 is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 5
6. A set has mean 30 and standard deviation 6. The coefficient of variation is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 5% (b) 18% (c) 20% (d) 180%

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $48-12=36$.
**2.** (a) — $\frac{36}{60}=0.6$.
**3.** (c) — $\frac{40-20}{2}=10$.
**4.** (b) — $\frac{20}{60}=\frac13$.
**5.** (b) — mean 5, squared deviations sum to 32, variance $\frac{32}{8}=4$, SD $=2$.
**6.** (c) — $\frac{6}{30}\times100=20\%$.
:::

## 5.18 Probability

**Facts.** $P(E)=\frac{\text{favourable outcomes}}{\text{total outcomes}}$; $P(E')=1-P(E)$; $P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)$. Without replacement, the second draw has one fewer ball.

1. When a fair coin is tossed, $P(\text{head})$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0 (b) $\frac12$ (c) 1 (d) $\frac14$
2. A die is thrown. The probability of a prime number is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac13$ (b) $\frac12$ (c) $\frac23$ (d) $\frac16$
3. If $P(A)=0.3$, then $P(A')$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0.3 (b) 0.07 (c) 0.7 (d) 1.3
4. A bag has 3 red and 2 blue balls. $P(\text{red})$ for one draw is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac23$ (b) $\frac25$ (c) $\frac35$ (d) $\frac15$
5. Two coins are tossed. $P(\text{at least one head})$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac14$ (b) $\frac12$ (c) $\frac34$ (d) 1
6. If $P(A)=0.5$, $P(B)=0.4$ and $P(A\cap B)=0.2$, then $P(A\cup B)$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0.9 (b) 0.7 (c) 0.1 (d) 0.4

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — one favourable outcome in two.
**2.** (b) — primes 2, 3, 5 give $\frac36=\frac12$.
**3.** (c) — $1-0.3$.
**4.** (c) — $\frac{3}{3+2}$.
**5.** (c) — $1-P(\text{TT})=1-\frac14$.
**6.** (b) — $0.5+0.4-0.2=0.7$.
:::
