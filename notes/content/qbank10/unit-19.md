## 19.1 Model Question Set 1

**Time: 3 hours. Full marks: 75. Attempt all questions. Give reasons and working.**

1. An AP has first term 5 and common difference 3. (a) Find its 15th term. (b) Find the sum of its first 15 terms. <span class="marks">[2+3]</span>
2. The roots of $x^2-px+12=0$ are 3 and 4. (a) Find $p$. (b) Draw a rough graph of $y=x^2-px+12$, marking its intercepts. <span class="marks">[2+3]</span>
3. Solve the simultaneous equations $2x+3y=13$ and $x-y=1$ by the matrix method. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
4. (a) Prove that $\frac{\sin A+\sin B}{\cos A+\cos B}=\tan\frac{A+B}{2}$, where defined. (b) Hence find $\frac{\sin20^\circ+\sin40^\circ}{\cos20^\circ+\cos40^\circ}$. <span class="marks">[4+1]</span>
5. From the top of a 15 m building, the angle of depression of a car is $30^\circ$. Find the horizontal distance of the car from the building. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
6. Find the equation of the circle through $(0,0)$, $(4,0)$ and $(0,6)$. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
7. Find the angle between vectors $\mathbf a=\mathbf i+\mathbf j$ and $\mathbf b=\mathbf i-\mathbf j$. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
8. Maximise $Z=5x+4y$ subject to $x+y\le5$, $2x+y\le8$, $x\ge0$, $y\ge0$. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
9. (a) Find the limit of $\frac{x^2-9}{x-3}$ as $x$ approaches 3. (b) State whether the function is continuous at $x=3$ if its value is defined as 6 there. <span class="marks">[3+2]</span>
10. For the values 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, find the standard deviation and coefficient of variation. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
11. Let $f(x)=x+2$ and $g(x)=3x$. Find $(f\circ g)(x)$ and $(g\circ f)(x)$. Are they equal? <span class="marks">[4]</span>
12. Factorise $x^3-4x^2-x+4$ completely and find all its zeros. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

## 19.2 Solutions to Model Set 1

::: note Questions 1 to 4
**1.** (a) $a_{15}=5+(15-1)3=5+42=$ **47**. (b) $S_{15}=\frac{15}{2}[2(5)+14(3)]=\frac{15}{2}(52)=$ **390**.

**2.** Sum of roots $=p=3+4=$ **7**. The equation is $y=x^2-7x+12=(x-3)(x-4)$. The graph is an upward-opening parabola crossing the $x$-axis at **$(3,0)$** and **$(4,0)$**, and the $y$-axis at **$(0,12)$**.

**3.** The coefficient determinant is $2(-1)-3(1)=-5$, so the inverse exists. The inverse-matrix calculation gives $x=\frac{16}{5}$ and $y=\frac{11}{5}$. Check directly: $2(\frac{16}{5})+3(\frac{11}{5})=13$ and $\frac{16}{5}-\frac{11}{5}=1$. Thus **$x=\frac{16}{5},y=\frac{11}{5}$**.

**4.** Use sum-to-product: $\sin A+\sin B=2\sin\frac{A+B}{2}\cos\frac{A-B}{2}$ and $\cos A+\cos B=2\cos\frac{A+B}{2}\cos\frac{A-B}{2}$. Cancelling common non-zero factors gives $\tan\frac{A+B}{2}$. With $A=20^\circ,B=40^\circ$, the value is **$\tan30^\circ=\frac1{\sqrt3}$**.
:::

::: note Questions 5 to 8
**5.** The angle of depression equals the angle of elevation at the car. Let distance be $d$. $\tan30^\circ=\frac{15}{d}$, so $d=15\sqrt3$. The car is **$15\sqrt3$ m**, about 25.98 m, from the building.

**6.** Let the equation be $x^2+y^2+Dx+Ey+F=0$. At $(0,0)$, $F=0$. At $(4,0)$, $16+4D=0$, so $D=-4$. At $(0,6)$, $36+6E=0$, so $E=-6$. Therefore **$x^2+y^2-4x-6y=0$**. Equivalently, $(x-2)^2+(y-3)^2=13$.

**7.** $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=1(1)+1(-1)=0$. Since neither vector is zero, $\cos\theta=0$, therefore **$\theta=90^\circ$**.

**8.** Boundary intersections give feasible vertices $(0,0),(4,0),(3,2),(0,5)$. Values of $Z$ are 0, 20, 23 and 20. Therefore **maximum $Z=23$ at $(3,2)$**.
:::

::: note Questions 9 to 12
**9.** (a) Factor: $\frac{x^2-9}{x-3}=\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3}=x+3$ for $x\ne3$. Hence the limit is **6**. (b) The left and right limits are both 6 and the stated value is 6, so it is **continuous at $x=3$**.

**10.** Mean $=8$. Deviations are $-4,-2,0,2,4$; squares sum to 40. $\sigma=\sqrt{\frac{40}{5}}=\sqrt8=2\sqrt2\approx2.83$. $CV=\frac{2.83}{8}\times100\%\approx$ **35.36%**.

**11.** $(f\circ g)(x)=f(3x)=3x+2$. $(g\circ f)(x)=g(x+2)=3x+6$. They are **not equal**, so function composition is not commutative here.

**12.** Group terms: $x^2(x-4)-1(x-4)=(x^2-1)(x-4)=(x-1)(x+1)(x-4)$. Zeros are **$1,-1,4$**.
:::

## 19.3 Model Question Set 2

**Time: 3 hours. Full marks: 75. Attempt all questions.**

1. The sum of the first $n$ terms of an AP is $S_n=3n^2+2n$. Find the first term and common difference. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
2. Solve $x^2-6x+5=0$ and find the equation whose roots are the reciprocals of these roots. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
3. Use Cramer's rule to solve $3x+2y=12$ and $x+y=5$. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
4. If $\tan A=\frac34$ and $A$ is acute, find $\sin2A$, $\cos2A$ and $\tan2A$. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
5. A kite is flown with 50 m of straight string at an angle of elevation $60^\circ$. If the hand is 1.5 m above the ground, find the height of the kite above the ground. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
6. Find the equation of the line through $(2,-1)$ parallel to $4x-3y+5=0$, then find its point of intersection with the $y$-axis. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
7. Find the centre and radius of $x^2+y^2-8x+2y-8=0$. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
8. A point $P(2,3)$ is reflected in the $x$-axis, then translated by vector $(4,-1)$. Find its final image. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
9. Minimise $C=4x+3y$ subject to $x+y\ge6$, $x\ge1$, $y\ge2$. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
10. Determine whether $f(x)=\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}$, with $f(2)=4$, is continuous at 2. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
11. Find the quartile deviation and coefficient of quartile deviation of 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
12. Find the remainder when $x^3-2x^2+5x-7$ is divided by $x-2$, and state whether $x-2$ is a factor. <span class="marks">[3]</span>

## 19.4 Solutions to Model Set 2

::: note Questions 1 to 4
**1.** $a_1=S_1=3+2=$ **5**. The second term is $S_2-S_1=(12+4)-5=11$, so common difference is **$d=6$**. Check: $a_n=5+(n-1)6=6n-1$; its sum is $\frac n2[2(5)+(n-1)6]=3n^2+2n$.

**2.** $(x-1)(x-5)=0$, so roots are **1 and 5**. Their reciprocals are 1 and $\frac15$. Sum $=\frac65$, product $=\frac15$. Required equation: $x^2-\frac65x+\frac15=0$, or **$5x^2-6x+1=0$**.

**3.** $\Delta=3(1)-2(1)=1$. $\Delta_x=12(1)-2(5)=2$ and $\Delta_y=3(5)-12(1)=3$. Thus **$x=2,y=3$**. Check: $6+6=12$ and $2+3=5$.

**4.** Take opposite 3, adjacent 4 and hypotenuse 5. Thus $\sin A=\frac35$, $\cos A=\frac45$. $\sin2A=2\sin A\cos A=$ **$\frac{24}{25}$**; $\cos2A=\cos^2A-\sin^2A=$ **$\frac7{25}$**; $\tan2A=\frac{2(3/4)}{1-(3/4)^2}=$ **$\frac{24}{7}$**.
:::

::: note Questions 5 to 8
**5.** Vertical height above the hand is $50\sin60^\circ=50\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{2}=25\sqrt3$ m. Add 1.5 m: **$25\sqrt3+1.5$ m**, about 44.80 m.

**6.** A parallel line has form $4x-3y+c=0$. Passing through $(2,-1)$ gives $8+3+c=0$, so $c=-11$. Equation: **$4x-3y-11=0$**. At the $y$-axis $x=0$, $-3y-11=0$, so intersection is **$(0,-\frac{11}{3})$**.

**7.** Complete squares: $(x-4)^2-16+(y+1)^2-1-8=0$. Hence $(x-4)^2+(y+1)^2=25$. Centre is **$(4,-1)$**, radius **5**.

**8.** Reflection in the $x$-axis: $(2,3)\mapsto(2,-3)$. Translation adds $(4,-1)$, giving **$(6,-4)$**.
:::

::: note Questions 9 to 12
**9.** The feasible boundary nearest the origin contains $(1,5)$ and $(4,2)$, both satisfying $x+y=6$. $C(1,5)=4+15=19$ and $C(4,2)=16+6=22$. The line $4x+3y$ rises faster with $x$, so the minimum is **$C=19$ at $(1,5)$**.

**10.** For $x\ne2$, $f(x)=x+2$. Therefore $\lim_{x\to2}f(x)=4$. The stated value is $f(2)=4$, so the left limit, right limit and value agree. It is **continuous at 2**.

**11.** With seven ordered values, $Q_1$ is the second value, 6, and $Q_3$ is the sixth value, 14. $QD=\frac{14-6}{2}=$ **4**. Coefficient $=\frac{14-6}{14+6}=\frac8{20}=$ **0.4**.

**12.** Remainder is $f(2)=8-8+10-7=$ **3**. Since the remainder is not zero, **$x-2$ is not a factor**.
:::

## 19.5 High-value revision priorities

### Tier A — make the method automatic

| Priority | Question form | Essential evidence in the answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Quadratic equations, roots and graph | factorisation or formula, roots, discriminant/graph feature |
| 2 | Trigonometric identities and height-distance | labelled triangle or identity line at every transformation |
| 3 | Matrices/Cramer's rule | determinant, non-zero condition, each substitution |
| 4 | Circle, line and vector numericals | standard form, substituted coordinates, interpreted result |
| 5 | Linear programming | graph, all feasible vertices, objective table, conclusion |

### Tier B — practise after Tier A

| Priority | Question form | Essential evidence in the answer |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | AP/GP term and sum | identify $a,d$ or $a,r$ before formula |
| 7 | Transformations and composition | ordered mapping; preserve transformation order |
| 8 | Continuity and limits | left limit, right limit and function value |
| 9 | Dispersion | working table; interpret the coefficient |
| 10 | Polynomial division and factor theorem | evaluate $f(a)$ and state the conclusion |

::: tip Final-week loop
Day 1: do all Tier A questions without notes. Day 2: correct them and copy only the missed first line of each method into a formula sheet. Day 3: sit one model set. Day 4: repair the weak areas. Repeat the loop with the second model set. This builds recall, working accuracy and timing together.
:::

## Chapter summary

- A full model is useful only when it is timed, checked and followed by targeted repair.
- In every coordinate question, state the form you are using before substituting values.
- A graph question needs labelled features, not an artistic curve.
- In a proof, give the identity used on each meaningful line; in a numerical, retain exact values until the final line when possible.
- The right answer in optimisation includes both the optimum value and the point where it occurs.
- “Predicted” means prioritised for practice, never guaranteed.
