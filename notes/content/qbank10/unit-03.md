## 3.1 Scientific Learning

1. Define a hypothesis and give one example. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Write any two precautions to take while measuring length with a metre rule. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. Describe the steps of the scientific method using the example of a plant that wilts. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** A hypothesis is a tentative, testable explanation of an observation. Example: "Plants kept in the dark will grow less than plants kept in light."

**2.** (i) Keep the eye vertically above the mark being read, to avoid parallax error. (ii) Start from a full-centimetre mark (not the worn end) and check the zero error.

**3.** (i) *Observation:* a plant in the corner wilts. (ii) *Problem:* why does it wilt? (iii) *Hypothesis:* it is not getting enough water. (iv) *Experiment:* water one pot and leave an identical pot unwatered. (v) *Analysis and conclusion:* if the watered plant recovers and the other wilts, the hypothesis is supported. Any four steps in order, with the example, earn full marks.
:::

## 3.2 Classification of Living Beings

1. Write two differences between Monera and Protista. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Why is the kingdom Fungi separate from Plantae? Give two reasons. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. Describe the five-kingdom system of classification with one example for each kingdom. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** Monera are prokaryotes with no nuclear membrane (bacteria); Protista are unicellular eukaryotes with a true nucleus (*Amoeba*). Monera lack membrane-bound organelles; Protista have them.

**2.** Fungi cannot make their own food (heterotrophic, saprophytic) whereas plants are autotrophs. The fungal cell wall is made of chitin whereas the plant cell wall is cellulose.

**3.** Whittaker (1969) divided life into five kingdoms. **Monera:** prokaryotic, unicellular (bacteria). **Protista:** unicellular eukaryotes (*Amoeba*). **Fungi:** heterotrophic, chitin wall (mushroom). **Plantae:** multicellular autotrophs (mango). **Animalia:** multicellular heterotrophs without cell wall (cow). Four creditable points: kingdoms with their basis and an example each.
:::

## 3.3 Honey Bee

1. Name the three castes of a honey bee colony and state one duty of each. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Write any two uses of honey bees to humans. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. Describe the life cycle of a honey bee, with the days each worker stage takes. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** Queen: lays eggs. Worker: collects nectar and pollen, builds comb, feeds larvae. Drone: mates with the queen.

**2.** Honey bees give honey, wax and royal jelly. They also pollinate crops, which raises fruit and seed yield.

**3.** A honey bee passes through four stages (complete metamorphosis): egg, larva, pupa, adult. For the worker: egg 3 days, larva about 6 days, pupa about 12 days, so adult emerges after **21 days** (queen 16, drone 24). The egg is laid in a cell; the larva is fed by nurse workers; the pupa is sealed in the cell with a wax cap; the adult chews its way out. A labelled four-stage diagram earns a mark.
:::

## 3.4 Heredity

1. State Mendel's law of segregation. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. A man with normal vision marries a woman who is a carrier of colour blindness. Give the possible genotypes of their sons. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. Show with a Punnett square the cross between two heterozygous tall pea plants (Tt × Tt). State the genotypic and phenotypic ratios. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. Explain how the sex of a baby is determined. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** The two alleles of a gene separate during gamete formation, so each gamete carries only one allele of each gene.

**2.** Father $X^{N}Y$; mother $X^{N}X^{c}$. Sons get the father's Y and one of the mother's X: $X^{N}Y$ (normal) or $X^{c}Y$ (colour-blind), each with probability 1/2.

**3.** Gametes: T, t from each parent.

| | T | t |
|---|---|---|
| **T** | TT | Tt |
| **t** | Tt | tt |

Genotypic ratio TT : Tt : tt = 1 : 2 : 1. Phenotypic ratio tall : dwarf = 3 : 1.

**4.** Human cells have 22 pairs of autosomes and one pair of sex chromosomes: XX in females, XY in males. Every egg carries X. Half the sperms carry X and half carry Y. If an X sperm fertilises the egg the baby is XX (girl); if a Y sperm does, the baby is XY (boy). So the father's sperm decides the sex, with equal chance of each.
:::

## 3.5 Physiological Structure and Life Process

1. Give reason: **The wall of the left ventricle is thicker than the wall of the right ventricle.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Differentiate between **systolic blood pressure and diastolic blood pressure** in two points. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. Describe the path of blood through the human heart (double circulation), naming the four chambers, four valves and main blood vessels. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. What is **high uric acid (gout)**? Mention its causes, symptoms and two dietary precautions. Also state the use of an **angiogram** in heart disease. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** The right ventricle pumps blood only a short distance to the nearby lungs, whereas the **left ventricle must pump oxygenated blood at high pressure through the aorta to every part of the body**.

**2.** **Systolic pressure ($120\ \text{mm Hg}$)** is the maximum arterial pressure during ventricular contraction; **diastolic pressure ($80\ \text{mm Hg}$)** is the minimum arterial pressure during ventricular relaxation.

**3.** Deoxygenated blood enters the **right auricle** from the vena cava, passes through the **tricuspid valve** to the **right ventricle**, and is pumped via the **pulmonary artery** to the lungs. Oxygenated blood returns via the **pulmonary veins** to the **left auricle**, passes through the **bicuspid (mitral) valve** to the **left ventricle**, and is pumped through the **aorta** to the whole body.

**4.** Excess uric acid formed from purine breakdown crystallises as sharp monosodium urate crystals in joints (especially the big toe), causing severe pain and swelling called **gout**. *Causes:* High intake of red meat, organ meat, alcohol/beer and low water intake. *Precautions:* Drink $3\text{–}4\ \text{L}$ of water daily and avoid red meat and alcohol. An **angiogram** is a special X-ray diagnostic test using a catheter and contrast dye to locate blockages inside the coronary arteries.
:::

## 3.6 Nature and Environment

1. Define greenhouse effect. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Write two measures to reduce solid waste at home. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. What is acid rain? Write its causes, two harmful effects and two preventive measures. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. Describe the causes and effects of ozone layer depletion. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** The greenhouse effect is the trapping of the Sun's heat in the atmosphere by gases such as carbon dioxide and methane, which keeps the Earth warm; an excess of these gases causes global warming.

**2.** Follow the 3R principle: reduce use of single-use plastics, reuse containers and bags, and compost kitchen waste.

**3.** Acid rain is rain with a pH below 5.6. Cause: sulphur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen from factories, vehicles and burning coal dissolve in rain to form sulphuric and nitric acids. Effects: it damages buildings and statues, harms crops and forests, and acidifies lakes so fish die. Prevention: use low-sulphur fuel, install scrubbers in chimneys, and reduce vehicle emissions.

**4.** Ozone in the stratosphere absorbs harmful ultraviolet rays. CFCs from old refrigerators, air-conditioners and aerosol sprays rise to the stratosphere; ultraviolet light breaks them to release chlorine, which destroys ozone. Effects: more UV reaches Earth, causing skin cancer and eye cataracts, reducing crop yield and harming plankton. Remedy: phase out CFCs (the Montreal Protocol).
:::

## 3.7 Motion and Force

1. State Newton's third law of motion with an example. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Find the weight of a 50 kg body on Earth ($g=10\ \text{m s}^{-2}$) and on the Moon ($g=1.67\ \text{m s}^{-2}$). <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. A car of mass 1500 kg accelerates uniformly from rest to $20\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ in 10 s. Find the acceleration and the force acting on it. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. A bullet of mass 20 g is fired from a gun of mass 4 kg with a velocity of $300\ \text{m s}^{-1}$. Calculate the recoil velocity of the gun and state the principle used. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** To every action there is an equal and opposite reaction, and they act on different bodies. Example: a rocket pushes gases down and the gases push the rocket up.

**2.** Earth: $W=mg=50\times10=500$ N. Moon: $W=50\times1.67=83.5$ N. (Mass stays 50 kg.)

**3.** $a=\dfrac{v-u}{t}=\dfrac{20-0}{10}=2\ \text{m s}^{-2}$. $F=ma=1500\times2=3000$ N.

**4.** Principle: **conservation of linear momentum**. Total momentum before firing is zero, so $m_1v_1+m_2v_2=0$.
$$ v_{\text{gun}}=-\frac{m_bv_b}{m_g}=-\frac{0.02\times300}{4}=-1.5\ \text{m s}^{-1} $$
The gun recoils at $1.5\ \text{m s}^{-1}$, opposite to the bullet.
:::

## 3.8 Pressure

1. State Pascal's law. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Why does a nail with a sharp point go into wood more easily than a blunt one? <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. In a hydraulic press, the small piston has an area of 5 cm² and the large piston 500 cm². A force of 100 N is applied to the small piston. Find the force on the large piston. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. A block of volume $0.002\ \text{m}^3$ is completely immersed in water ($\rho=1000\ \text{kg m}^{-3}$). Find the upthrust. If the block weighs 30 N in air, what is its apparent weight in water? <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** Pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted equally and undiminished in all directions.

**2.** The pointed tip has a very small area, so for the same force the pressure ($P=F/A$) is much greater and the nail penetrates.

**3.** By Pascal's law, $\dfrac{F_1}{A_1}=\dfrac{F_2}{A_2}$, so $F_2=F_1\dfrac{A_2}{A_1}=100\times\dfrac{500}{5}=10\,000$ N.

**4.** Upthrust $=V\rho g=0.002\times1000\times10=20$ N. Apparent weight $=30-20=10$ N.
:::

## 3.9 Heat

1. Define specific heat capacity and write its SI unit. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Why does water in a pond freeze from the top while the bottom stays at about 4 °C? <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. How much heat is required to raise the temperature of 0.5 kg of water from 20 °C to 100 °C? ($c=4200\ \text{J kg}^{-1}\ ^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$) <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. 200 g of water at 80 °C is mixed with 300 g of water at 20 °C. Find the final temperature of the mixture. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** Specific heat capacity is the heat needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 °C (or 1 K). Unit: J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.

**2.** Water is densest at 4 °C. On cooling, water above 4 °C sinks until the whole pond reaches 4 °C; below 4 °C the cooler water becomes lighter and stays on top, so ice forms at the surface while the bottom stays near 4 °C.

**3.** $Q=mc\Delta T=0.5\times4200\times(100-20)=0.5\times4200\times80=168\,000$ J.

**4.** Heat lost by hot water = heat gained by cold water. With final temperature $T$:
$$ 0.2\times4200\times(80-T)=0.3\times4200\times(T-20) $$
$$ 16 - 0.2T = 0.3T - 6 \implies 0.5T = 22 \implies T = 44\ ^\circ\text{C} $$
:::

## 3.10 Wave

1. Differentiate between transverse and longitudinal waves. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. State the laws of reflection of light. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. A ship sends an ultrasonic pulse towards the sea bed and receives the echo after 0.8 s. If the speed of sound in sea water is $1500\ \text{m s}^{-1}$, find the depth of the sea. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. An object is placed 30 cm in front of a convex lens of focal length 10 cm. Find the image distance and the magnification. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** In a transverse wave the particles vibrate perpendicular to the direction of the wave (light, waves on a string). In a longitudinal wave they vibrate parallel to it (sound).

**2.** (i) The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. (ii) The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal at the point of incidence lie in the same plane.

**3.** The pulse travels down and back, so the distance is $2d=vt$:
$$ d=\frac{vt}{2}=\frac{1500\times0.8}{2}=600\ \text{m} $$

**4.** Using $\dfrac{1}{f}=\dfrac{1}{u}+\dfrac{1}{v}$ with real distances positive: $\dfrac{1}{v}=\dfrac{1}{10}-\dfrac{1}{30}=\dfrac{3-1}{30}=\dfrac{1}{15}$, so $v=15$ cm (a real image beyond the lens). Magnification $m=\dfrac{v}{u}=\dfrac{15}{30}=0.5$, so the image is real, inverted and half the size.
:::

## 3.11 Electricity and Magnetism

1. State Ohm's law. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Write two differences between a motor and a generator. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. Resistors of 6 Ω and 3 Ω are connected in parallel across a 12 V battery. Find the total resistance, the total current and the current in each resistor. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. Four bulbs of 60 W each are used for 5 hours every day for 30 days. If one unit of electricity costs Rs 10, calculate the electricity bill. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
5. Explain the working of a step-down transformer. A transformer has 1000 primary turns on a 220 V mains. How many secondary turns are needed for 11 V? <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** At constant temperature, the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it: $V=IR$.

**2.** A motor converts electrical energy to mechanical energy (works on Fleming's left-hand rule); a generator converts mechanical energy to electrical energy (works on electromagnetic induction).

**3.** $\dfrac{1}{R}=\dfrac{1}{6}+\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{1+2}{6}=\dfrac{1}{2}$, so $R=2\ \Omega$. Total current $I=\dfrac{12}{2}=6$ A. Branch currents: $I_1=\dfrac{12}{6}=2$ A and $I_2=\dfrac{12}{3}=4$ A; check $2+4=6$ A.

**4.** Total power $=4\times60=240$ W $=0.24$ kW. Energy per day $=0.24\times5=1.2$ kWh. For 30 days: $1.2\times30=36$ kWh = 36 units. Bill $=36\times10=$ Rs 360.

**5.** A step-down transformer has fewer turns on the secondary than on the primary. An alternating current in the primary produces a changing magnetic flux in the iron core, which induces a smaller alternating emf in the secondary (electromagnetic induction). $\dfrac{V_p}{V_s}=\dfrac{N_p}{N_s}\Rightarrow\dfrac{220}{11}=\dfrac{1000}{N_s}\Rightarrow N_s=\dfrac{1000\times11}{220}=50$ turns.
:::

## 3.12 Universe

1. State **Hubble's law** and write its mathematical relation. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Write any two points showing the **importance of gravitational force in the universe**. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. Define **critical density** and explain the **open, flat and closed universe** models along with the **Big Crunch**. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. Describe the Big Bang theory and give two pieces of observational evidence for it. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** **Hubble's law:** The recession velocity ($v$) of a distant galaxy moving away from us is directly proportional to its distance ($d$): $v = Hd$, where $H$ is the Hubble constant.

**2.** Gravitational force pulls gas and dust clouds (nebulae) together to form stars and galaxies, and keeps planets, moons and galaxies bound in stable orbits and clusters.

**3.** **Critical density ($\rho_c \approx (1\text{–}3)\times 10^{-26}\ \text{kg m}^{-3}$)** is the average density of the universe at which gravity just balances cosmic expansion. (i) **Open universe ($\rho < \rho_c$):** expands forever; (ii) **Flat universe ($\rho = \rho_c$):** expansion slows and approaches zero velocity at infinity; (iii) **Closed universe ($\rho > \rho_c$):** strong gravity stops expansion and collapses all galaxies back into a single hot, dense singularity called the **Big Crunch**.

**4.** The universe began about $13.8$ billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense singularity that expanded rapidly and cooled to form atoms, stars and galaxies. Evidence: (i) Doppler **redshift** of light from distant galaxies (Hubble's law); (ii) **Cosmic Microwave Background Radiation (CMBR)** at $2.7\ \text{K}$.
:::

## 3.13 Information and Communication Technology

1. Differentiate between an **analog signal and a digital signal**, and between **baseband and broadband transmission**. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Define **digital citizenship** and **digital wellbeing**, with two precautions for digital wellbeing. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. Draw the block diagram of a **digital communication system** in the exact order and state the role of the input transducer, encoder, modulator and demodulator. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. Write in order the four steps to shorten a 6-minute video clip to a 4-minute video clip using video-editing software. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** An **analog signal** is a continuous wave, whereas a **digital signal** is a discrete binary pulse (`0` and `1`). **Baseband transmission** sends a single digital signal without modulation over short distances (Ethernet LAN), whereas **broadband transmission** modulates signals across multiple frequency channels to send many signals simultaneously over long distances (cable TV, high-speed internet).

**2.** **Digital citizenship** is the responsible, ethical and safe use of the internet by a netizen. **Digital wellbeing** is maintaining physical, mental and social health by balancing online screen time with offline activities. Precautions: (i) set daily screen-time limits; (ii) turn off unnecessary notifications during study and before bedtime.

**3.** Sequence: **Source → Input Transducer → Encoder → Modulator → Channel → Demodulator → Decoder → Output Transducer → Output Signal**. *Input transducer* (microphone) converts sound into electrical signals; *Encoder* compresses and digitises it into `0`s and `1`s; *Modulator* superimposes the digital data onto a carrier wave; *Demodulator* separates the data signal from the carrier wave at the receiver.

**4.** (i) Launch **Video Editor**, start a **New video project**, import the 6-minute video into the **Project library**, and drag it onto the **Storyboard**. (ii) Select the clip and click **Trim**. (iii) Move the **two blue start/end drag bars** to select the required 4-minute portion and click **Done**. (iv) Click **Finish video** → **Export** and save the 4-minute `.MP4` file.
:::

## 3.14 Classification of Elements

1. State the modern periodic law. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Why are noble gases chemically inert? <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. The atomic numbers of X and Y are 11 and 17. Write their electronic configurations, groups and periods, and the formula and type of bond in the compound they form. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. Describe how atomic size and metallic character change across a period and down a group. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** The physical and chemical properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers.

**2.** Their outermost shells are complete (2 electrons in helium, 8 in the rest), so they neither gain, lose nor share electrons.

**3.** X (11): 2, 8, 1 — group 1, period 3 (sodium). Y (17): 2, 8, 7 — group 17, period 3 (chlorine). X loses one electron to become X⁺ and Y gains it to become Y⁻, giving **XY** (NaCl) with an **ionic** bond.

**4.** Across a period (left to right): nuclear charge increases while the shell number is the same, so atomic size **decreases** and metallic character decreases. Down a group: extra shells are added, so atomic size **increases** and metallic character increases.
:::

## 3.15 Chemical Reaction

1. Balance: Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Define neutralisation with an example. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. Write four factors that affect the rate of a chemical reaction, with one example each. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. Balance the equation C₃H₈ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O and name the type of reaction. Then state what a pH of 3 tells you about a solution. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** $4\text{Al}+3\text{O}_2\rightarrow2\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$. (Aluminium 4 = 4; oxygen 6 = 6.)

**2.** Neutralisation is the reaction of an acid with a base to form a salt and water. Example: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O.

**3.** (i) *Temperature:* milk sours faster in a warm room. (ii) *Concentration:* concentrated acid reacts faster with marble than dilute acid. (iii) *Surface area:* powdered marble reacts faster than a lump. (iv) *Catalyst:* MnO₂ speeds the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide.

**4.** C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O. Carbon 3 = 3, hydrogen 8 = 8, oxygen 10 = 6 + 4. It is a **combustion** (oxidation) reaction. A pH of 3 is below 7, so the solution is **acidic** (fairly strongly), and it would turn blue litmus red.
:::

## 3.16 Gases

1. State the conditions (temperature, pressure, catalyst and promoter) required for manufacturing ammonia by **Haber's process** with the balanced equation. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. What happens when $\text{CO}_2$ is passed through lime water for a short time and for a long time? Write the balanced equations. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. Describe the **laboratory preparation of dry ammonia gas** with a balanced chemical equation, drying agent and collection method. Why is the hard-glass test tube inclined slightly downwards? <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. Describe the **ammonia fountain experiment** and state the two properties of ammonia proved by it. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
5. Describe the **laboratory preparation of carbon dioxide** with a chemical equation and apparatus description. Why is dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ not used in place of dilute $\text{HCl}$? <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** $\text{N}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons 2\text{NH}_3 + \text{Heat}$ at **$450\text{–}500\ ^\circ\text{C}$**, **$200\text{–}900\ \text{atm}$**, with **finely divided iron ($\text{Fe}$) catalyst** and **molybdenum ($\text{Mo}$) promoter**.

**2.** *Short time:* Lime water turns milky due to insoluble calcium carbonate: $\text{Ca(OH)}_2 + \text{CO}_2 \rightarrow \text{CaCO}_3 \downarrow + \text{H}_2\text{O}$. *Long time:* The milkiness disappears due to soluble calcium bicarbonate: $\text{CaCO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 \rightarrow \text{Ca(HCO}_3)_2$.

**3.** Heat a $2:1$ mixture of ammonium chloride ($\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$) and slaked lime ($\text{Ca(OH)}_2$): $2\text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{Ca(OH)}_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \text{CaCl}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 2\text{NH}_3 \uparrow$. Dry the gas by passing it through a **quicklime ($\text{CaO}$) tower** and collect it in an inverted jar by **downward displacement of air**. The test tube is inclined downwards so condensed water vapour does not trickle back onto the hot glass bottom and crack it.

**4.** When a few drops of water are squeezed into an inverted flask filled with $\text{NH}_3$, the gas dissolves immediately, creating a partial vacuum inside the flask; atmospheric pressure pushes water + phenolphthalein indicator up the jet tube as a pink fountain. It proves: (i) **extreme solubility of $\text{NH}_3$ in water**, and (ii) **basic (alkaline) nature of aqueous ammonia**.

**5.** Place marble chips ($\text{CaCO}_3$) in a Woulfe's bottle fitted with a thistle funnel and delivery tube, and pour dilute $\text{HCl}$ so the funnel end dips in acid: $\text{CaCO}_3 + 2\text{HCl} \rightarrow \text{CaCl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 \uparrow$. Collect in an upright jar by **upward displacement of air** ($\text{CO}_2$ is $1.5\times$ heavier than air). Dilute $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ is not used because insoluble $\text{CaSO}_4$ coats the marble and stops the reaction.
:::

## 3.17 Metal and Non-metals

1. Why is sodium stored under kerosene? <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Why do aluminium utensils not corrode easily? <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. What is rusting? State the conditions needed and two methods to prevent it. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. Describe the extraction of aluminium from its ore by electrolysis. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** Sodium is very reactive and reacts vigorously with oxygen and moisture of air, so it is kept under kerosene to keep air and water away.

**2.** Aluminium forms a thin, tough layer of aluminium oxide on its surface, which protects the metal below from further attack.

**3.** Rusting is the slow corrosion of iron in damp air to form reddish-brown hydrated iron(III) oxide. It needs both **oxygen and water**. Prevention: painting or oiling; galvanising (zinc coating); making alloys like stainless steel.

**4.** The ore is **bauxite** (Al₂O₃·2H₂O), purified to alumina (Al₂O₃). Alumina is dissolved in molten **cryolite** (which lowers the melting point) and electrolysed in a steel tank lined with carbon (cathode) using carbon anodes. At the cathode, Al³⁺ gains electrons to give molten aluminium that collects at the bottom; at the anode, oxygen is released and burns the carbon anodes to CO₂.
:::

## 3.18 Hydrocarbon and its Compounds

1. What is a homologous series? Give one example. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Write the molecular formulae of the first four alkanes. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. Write four differences between alkanes and alkenes. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. How is ethanol converted to ethanoic acid? Name the acid's functional group, and state how it reacts with ethanol. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** A homologous series is a family of organic compounds with the same functional group and similar properties, in which successive members differ by CH₂. Example: alkanes (CH₄, C₂H₆, C₃H₈).

**2.** Methane CH₄, ethane C₂H₆, propane C₃H₈, butane C₄H₁₀ (general formula $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$).

**3.**

| Alkanes | Alkenes |
|---|---|
| Single bonds only | Contain a C=C double bond |
| Saturated | Unsaturated |
| General formula $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$ | General formula $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n}$ |
| Undergo substitution; less reactive | Undergo addition; more reactive |

**4.** Ethanol (C₂H₅OH) is oxidised by an oxidising agent such as acidified potassium dichromate to ethanoic (acetic) acid, CH₃COOH. The functional group is carboxyl, –COOH. With ethanol, in the presence of concentrated H₂SO₄, it forms an ester and water: CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH → CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O.
:::

## 3.19 Chemicals used in Daily Life

1. Differentiate between **natural food preservatives and chemical food preservatives**, and state why **calcium carbide** is harmful for ripening fruits. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Write any two differences between **soap and detergent**, and explain why detergent is called a "soapless soap". <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. Describe the laboratory preparation of **soap (saponification)** and state the role of common salt ($\text{NaCl}$). <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. Differentiate between **chemical pesticides and biological pesticides** in two points, and write any four safety precautions to follow while storing and using pesticides. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** **Natural food preservatives** (salt, sugar, oil, lemon, vinegar) come from natural sources with no toxic side effects; **chemical food preservatives** (sodium benzoate, $\text{SO}_2$, BHA, BHT) are synthetic and harmful if overused. Calcium carbide ($\text{CaC}_2$) contains toxic arsenic and phosphorus impurities that cause skin rashes, eye irritation, ulcers and liver/kidney damage.

**2.** **Soap** is the sodium/potassium salt of long-chain fatty acids (forms scum in hard water, biodegradable); **detergent** is the synthetic sodium salt of alkyl benzene sulphonic acid or alkyl sulphate (does not form scum in hard water, often non-biodegradable). Because detergent cleans like soap but has a different chemical composition, it is called a "soapless soap".

**3.** Vegetable oil or animal fat is heated gently with concentrated sodium hydroxide ($\text{NaOH}$) solution while stirring in one direction: $\text{Fat/Oil} + 3\text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} 3\text{Soap} + \text{Glycerol}$. **Common salt ($\text{NaCl}$)** is added to decrease the solubility of soap and precipitate solid soap as an upper layer ("salting out"), which is filtered, washed and moulded.

**4.** **Chemical pesticides** (DDT, Malathion) are synthetic, broad-spectrum and non-biodegradable/polluting; **biological pesticides** (Neem, *Titepati*, Bt) come from living organisms, are target-specific and are eco-friendly. *Precautions:* (i) Use only pesticides permitted under the **Pesticides Management Act 2076** on JTA advice; (ii) wear PPE (mask, gloves, goggles, boots); (iii) store in labelled, sealed containers out of reach of children; (iv) observe the pre-harvest waiting period and wash hands with soap after use.
:::

### Model answer D1: human heart and double circulation (10 marks)

**Introduction.** The **heart** is a hollow, muscular, fist-sized organ that pumps blood to every part of the body. It lies in the chest between the two lungs, slightly to the left, and beats about 72 times a minute at rest. Blood carries oxygen, food, hormones and wastes, so the heart keeps every cell alive.

**Structure.** The heart has four chambers.

1. **Right atrium** receives deoxygenated blood from the body through the vena cava.
2. **Right ventricle** pumps this blood to the lungs through the pulmonary artery.
3. **Left atrium** receives oxygenated blood from the lungs through the pulmonary veins.
4. **Left ventricle** has the thickest wall and pumps oxygenated blood to the whole body through the aorta.

A muscular wall called the **septum** separates the right and left sides, so oxygenated and deoxygenated blood never mix. **Valves** between atrium and ventricle (tricuspid on the right, bicuspid on the left) and at the exits of the ventricles make blood flow in one direction only. The "lub-dub" sound is the closing of these valves.

**Double circulation.** In humans blood passes through the heart **twice** in one complete round.

1. **Pulmonary circulation:** right ventricle → lungs → left atrium. Blood loses carbon dioxide and takes oxygen in the lungs.
2. **Systemic circulation:** left ventricle → body → right atrium. Oxygenated blood gives oxygen and food to the tissues and returns with carbon dioxide.

Double circulation keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood apart and keeps the pressure high, so warm-blooded animals get enough oxygen to make body heat.

**Difference between arteries and veins**

| Basis | Artery | Vein |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Carries blood away from the heart | Carries blood towards the heart |
| Blood | Oxygenated (except pulmonary artery) | Deoxygenated (except pulmonary vein) |
| Wall | Thick and elastic | Thin |
| Valves | Absent | Present |
| Pressure | High, pulse can be felt | Low |
| Position | Deep in the body | Close to the skin |

**Conclusion.** The four-chambered heart and double circulation make the human blood system efficient. Blood pressure of about 120/80 mm of mercury is normal, and a healthy diet, exercise and no smoking protect the heart.

### Model answer D2: extraction of iron (10 marks)

**Introduction.** Iron is extracted from its ores by **metallurgy**. The chief ores are **haematite** ($Fe_2O_3$) and magnetite ($Fe_3O_4$). The extraction is done in a tall steel furnace lined with fire-bricks, called the **blast furnace**. Iron is the most used metal, in bridges, rods, vehicles and tools.

**Steps of metallurgy.**

1. **Crushing and concentration.** The ore is crushed and washed to remove soil and stones.
2. **Roasting.** It is heated in air to drive out moisture and change impurities into oxides.
3. **Charging.** A mixture of roasted ore, **coke** (carbon, the fuel and reducing agent) and **limestone** (the flux) is fed from the top.
4. **Hot blast.** Hot air is blown in from the bottom through pipes called tuyeres.

**Reactions inside the furnace.**

| Zone | Reaction |
|---|---|
| Bottom (burning of coke) | $C + O_2 \rightarrow CO_2$ |
| Middle (carbon monoxide forms) | $CO_2 + C \rightarrow 2CO$ |
| Upper (reduction of ore) | $Fe_2O_3 + 3CO \rightarrow 2Fe + 3CO_2$ |
| Limestone breaks down | $CaCO_3 \rightarrow CaO + CO_2$ |
| Slag formation | $CaO + SiO_2 \rightarrow CaSiO_3$ (slag) |

The molten iron sinks to the bottom, where it is tapped. The lighter slag floats on top of it and is removed separately, so it protects the iron from oxidation.

**Products.** The iron from the furnace is **pig iron** (cast iron) with about 4 percent carbon. It is hard but brittle. By removing carbon it is changed to **wrought iron** (almost pure) or to **steel** (0.1 to 1.5 percent carbon, with chromium and nickel for stainless steel).

**Rusting and its prevention.** Iron rusts in moist air: $4Fe + 3O_2 + 2xH_2O \rightarrow 2Fe_2O_3\cdot xH_2O$. It is prevented by painting, oiling, galvanising with zinc, or alloying.

**Conclusion.** Coke reduces the ore, limestone removes the sand as slag, and hot air keeps the furnace burning. Nepal imports most of its iron and steel, and the Himalayan rivers could power steel mills in future.

### Model answer D3: lens formula and numerical (10 marks)

**Introduction.** A **convex lens** is thicker in the middle and converges parallel rays to the **principal focus**. Its focal length $f$ is the distance from the optical centre to the focus. The lens is used in a camera, a magnifying glass, a microscope and spectacles for long sight.

**Lens formula and magnification.** Using the real-is-positive convention, $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{u} + \frac{1}{v}$, where $u$ is the object distance and $v$ is the image distance. Magnification $m = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{\text{height of image}}{\text{height of object}}$. Power of a lens $P = \frac{1}{f}$ with $f$ in metres, measured in dioptre (D).

**Numerical.** Given $u = 30$ cm, $f = 10$ cm.

1. Image distance: $\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{10} - \frac{1}{30} = \frac{3-1}{30} = \frac{2}{30}$, so $v =$ **15 cm**.
2. Magnification: $m = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{15}{30} =$ **0.5**.
3. Power: $f = 10\text{ cm} = 0.1\text{ m}$, so $P = \frac{1}{0.1} =$ **+10 D**.

**Nature of the image.** Because $v$ is positive, the image is **real**; it is formed on the opposite side of the lens, **inverted** and **diminished** to half the size of the object. The object is beyond $2f$ (20 cm), so the image lies between $f$ and $2f$.

**Images by a convex lens**

| Object position | Image position | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| Beyond 2f | Between f and 2f | Real, inverted, smaller |
| At 2f | At 2f | Real, inverted, same size |
| Between f and 2f | Beyond 2f | Real, inverted, larger |
| At f | Infinity | Real, inverted, highly magnified |
| Between O and f | Same side as object | Virtual, erect, larger |

**Conclusion.** The lens formula gives the exact position and size of an image. A camera works like this answer: the film or sensor is placed between f and 2f to get a small, real, inverted picture of a distant object.

## Chapter summary

- A 2-mark answer needs two separate points; a 4-mark answer needs four creditable steps. Numericals earn marks for formula, substitution, answer and unit.
- Worked results to know: $F=ma$ (car: 3000 N); recoil $1.5\ \text{m s}^{-1}$; hydraulic press $10\,000$ N; mixture temperature 44 °C; $V=IR$ circuit 6 A; Boyle 250 cm³; Charles 400 cm³.
- Diagrams earn marks: heart, bee life cycle, CO₂ apparatus, transformer.
- Say *why*, not only *what*: gypsum slows setting; cryolite lowers the melting point; the sharp point has a small area.
- Take the stated constant ($g$, $c$, $L$) from the question, and write the unit on every final answer.
