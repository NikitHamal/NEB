This chapter does three things. It sets out the **pattern of the SEE Science and Technology paper** and how the two model papers below spread their questions over the 19 chapters. It gives **two complete model papers**, each of 75 marks and three hours, followed by full solutions written the way you should write in the examination. And it ranks **practice priorities** — question forms worth rehearsing first — with the reason for each position.

::: caution What "predicted" means here
Nobody outside the examination board knows what will be set, and this chapter does not pretend to. A ranking here is a judgement from two public facts: the CDC specification grid (which fixes the number and size of questions) and how standard a question form is (a definition, a balanced equation, a one-step numerical). **These are not forecasts.** Treat the list as an order in which to practise, never as a promise that any question will appear. All questions in the model papers are original practice items, not past questions.
:::

::: key How to sit a three-hour paper
Seventy-five marks in 180 minutes is 2.4 minutes a mark. Plan it: **Groups A and B, 30 minutes** (19 one-mark questions); **Group C, 60 minutes** (14 questions of 2 marks, about 4 minutes each); **Group D, 70 minutes** (7 questions of 4 marks, about 10 minutes each); **20 minutes** to check. Bank the one-mark items first.
:::

::: note Group C — model answers
**20.** Fungi are heterotrophic (saprophytic) and cannot photosynthesise; their cell wall is made of chitin. (Also: they reproduce by spores.)

**21.** Bees pollinate flowers, raising fruit and seed yield; honey and wax give farmers extra income.

**22.** Genotype is the genetic make-up of an organism (for example Tt); phenotype is its observable character (tall).

**23.** The kidney removes urea and other wastes from the blood as urine, and it regulates the water and salt balance of the body.

**24.** Arteries carry blood away from the heart and have thick elastic walls with no valves; veins carry blood to the heart and have thinner walls with valves.

**25.** An ecosystem is a community of living organisms interacting with each other and with their non-living surroundings. Components: biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living).

**26.** A body at rest stays at rest and a body in motion keeps moving at constant velocity unless acted on by an external force. Example: passengers lurch forward when a bus brakes.

**27.** The pressure inside our body (blood and body fluids) is equal to the outside pressure, so the two balance and we feel no net force.

**28.** Water heats up and cools down slowly, so it is used as a coolant in engines; it also moderates the climate near seas and lakes (sea breeze, mild coastal climate).

**29.** Refraction is the bending of light when it passes from one medium to another because its speed changes. Snell's law: $\dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}=$ constant (the refractive index).

**30.** Electric power is the rate of doing electrical work (or energy used per second): $P=VI$. SI unit: watt (W).

**31.** A constellation is a group of stars that appears to form a pattern in the sky. Example: Orion (or Ursa Major, the Great Bear).

**32.** Respect others' privacy and do not share personal data; do not copy or share material without permission (also: do not spread false information or bully online).

**33.** Gold does not rust or corrode (unreactive), and it is malleable and has an attractive shining colour.
:::

::: note Group D — model answers
**34.** $h=\tfrac12gt^2\Rightarrow45=\tfrac12(10)t^2\Rightarrow t^2=9\Rightarrow t=3$ s. $v=gt=10\times3=30\ \text{m s}^{-1}$.

**35.** Step 1, melt ice: $Q_1=mL=0.2\times3.36\times10^{5}=67\,200$ J. Step 2, heat water from 0 to 30 °C: $Q_2=mc\Delta T=0.2\times4200\times30=25\,200$ J. Total $Q=67\,200+25\,200=92\,400$ J.

**36.** Series: $R=2+4=6\ \Omega$. $I=V/R=12/6=2$ A. Across 4 Ω: $V=IR=2\times4=8$ V.

**37.** A reflex action is a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus, controlled by the spinal cord without waiting for the brain. Reflex arc, in order: **receptor** (skin) → **sensory neuron** → **spinal cord** (relay/ interneuron) → **motor neuron** → **effector** (muscle). Example: pulling the hand from a hot object. **38.**

**40.** Esterification is the reaction of an alcohol with a carboxylic acid, in the presence of concentrated sulphuric acid, to form an ester and water. ## Chapter summary

- The paper is $10\times1+9\times1+14\times2+7\times4=75$; plan 30, 60, 70 and 20 minutes for A and B, C, D and checking.
- Marks come from steps: formula, substitution, answer, unit for numericals; labelled diagram plus points for descriptions.
- Quick results to hold: $F=ma$; $p=mv$; $P=h\rho g$; $Q=mc\Delta T$; $Q=mL$; $v=f\lambda$; $V=IR$; $P=VI$; $P_1V_1=P_2V_2$; $V_1/T_1=V_2/T_2$; $g=GM/R^2$.
- Biology anchors: F₂ ratios 3 : 1 and 9 : 3 : 3 : 1; bee development 16, 21, 24 days; heart path; reflex arc.
- Chemistry anchors: balanced equations; lab preparation with diagram and test; alkane $\text{C}_n\text{H}_{2n+2}$; cement: clinker plus gypsum.
- The ranked list is an order of practice, never a forecast.
