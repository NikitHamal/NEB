This chapter is for practice under exam conditions. Each set below is a complete
Mental Agility Test of **20 questions in the official proportions**: questions 1–5 verbal
reasoning, 6–10 numerical reasoning, 11–15 logical sequencing and 16–20 spatial relation
/ abstract reasoning. Sit each set in one go, against the clock, mark it with the
negative-marking rule, and then read *every* explanation — including those for the
questions you got right, since a lucky guess teaches nothing.

::: key How to use these sets
- **Time**: the CEE gives 180 minutes for 200 questions, about 54 seconds a question.
  Allow yourself **18 minutes** per set of 20.
- **Order**: do the questions you can answer at once first; mark the long ones (seating,
  nets) and come back to them.
- **Score**: +1 for a correct answer, −0.25 for a wrong one, 0 for a blank.
- **After the set**: list your wrong answers by section and go back to that unit
  (Unit 1 verbal, Unit 2 numerical, Unit 3 sequencing, Unit 4 spatial).
:::

### Guessing with negative marking

A wrong answer costs 0.25, a right one gains 1. With four options, the expected score of
a guess depends on how many options you can rule out first:

| Options ruled out | Chance of being right | Expected mark of a guess |
|---|---|---|
| none (blind guess) | 1/4 | 0.25 − 0.75 × 0.25 = +0.06 |
| one | 1/3 | 0.33 − 0.67 × 0.25 = +0.17 |
| two | 1/2 | 0.50 − 0.50 × 0.25 = +0.38 |

Even a blind guess has a small positive expected value under this rule, and every option
you eliminate makes guessing clearly worth it. What loses marks is not guessing but
*careless* errors on questions you could have solved — so check the units, the
direction, and what the question actually asks before you mark.

::: tip A pacing plan for the MAT block
Most students lose time on the figure questions. Spend about 12 minutes on questions
1–15 (under 50 seconds each), which leaves 6 minutes for the five figure questions.
In a net question, find the opposite faces first — it removes two or three options in
seconds.
:::

::: example Fast-elimination strategy on a 20-question MAT block
**Problem.** In a timed 20-question MAT section (target 16–18 minutes, 50 seconds per item), a candidate solves 15 items with certainty and eliminates two options on each of the remaining 5 items. What is the expected net MAT score if the candidate guesses on those 5 items?

**Solution.** Each 50/50 guess has $E = 0.5(+1) + 0.5(-0.25) = +0.375$ mark. Across 5 such items the expected gain is $5 \times 0.375 = +1.875$, giving an expected MAT score of **$16.88 / 20$** vs $15.00$ if left blank.
:::

::: example Decoding a two-step blood-relation and direction hybrid
**Problem.** A starts at $O$, walks $8\text{ m}$ North, turns right and walks $6\text{ m}$ to meet B, who is the only son of A's mother's father. How far is A from $O$, and how is B related to A?

**Solution.** By Pythagoras, distance $= \sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = \mathbf{10\text{ m}}$ North-East. "A's mother's father" is A's maternal grandfather; his only son is A's **maternal uncle**.
:::

::: example Quick parity and unit-digit check in number grids
**Problem.** In a $3 \times 3$ grid where each row satisfies $(\text{col 1})^2 + (\text{col 2})^2 = \text{col 3}$, row 3 has $17$ and $144$ in columns 1 and 2. Pick the missing entry from (a) 20920 (b) 21025 (c) 21024 (d) 21028 in under 5 seconds.

**Solution.** Check the **unit digit** only: $17^2$ ends in $9$ and $144^2$ ends in $6$; $9 + 6 = 15$, which ends in **$5$**. Only option **(b) 21025** ends in $5$ ($289 + 20736 = 21025$).
:::
