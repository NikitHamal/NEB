In Mathematics the one-mark items are one-step questions: a definition, a formula recalled correctly, a single substitution, a sign. They are also the items most often lost to carelessness rather than to ignorance. This chapter covers all twenty-one units of the Grade 8 textbook, grouped into ten sections. Each section opens with the **formulas and facts** the one-mark items keep returning to, then gives the items, then a key that shows the single step.

::: key The one-step habit
Before you write anything, ask: *which formula is this?* Write the formula, substitute, and only then calculate. Substituting into a half-remembered formula can turn an easy item into an avoidable error.
:::

## 5.1 Sets, Whole Numbers, Rational and Irrational Numbers

**Facts.** $n(A \cup B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A \cap B)$. A set with $n$ elements has $2^n$ subsets. Rational numbers can be written as $\frac{p}{q}$ with $q \neq 0$; $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{3}$ and $\pi$ are irrational. Every terminating or recurring decimal is rational. Zero is neither positive nor negative; it is a whole number but not a natural number. $0$ has no multiplicative inverse.

1. If $n(A) = 12$, $n(B) = 9$ and $n(A \cap B) = 4$, then $n(A \cup B)$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 17 (b) 21 (c) 25 (d) 13
2. The number of subsets of a set with 4 elements is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 4 (b) 8 (c) 16 (d) 32
3. Which of the following is an irrational number? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $0.25$ (b) $\frac{3}{7}$ (c) $\sqrt{9}$ (d) $\sqrt{7}$
4. The smallest whole number is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $-1$ (b) 0 (c) 1 (d) there is none
5. The additive inverse of $-\frac{5}{8}$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac{8}{5}$ (b) $-\frac{8}{5}$ (c) $\frac{5}{8}$ (d) $0$
6. If $A = \{1,2,3\}$ and $B = \{3,4\}$, then $A \cap B$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\{1,2,3,4\}$ (b) $\{3\}$ (c) $\{1,2\}$ (d) $\emptyset$
7. The decimal $0.\overline{6}$ expressed as a fraction is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac{6}{10}$ (b) $\frac{2}{3}$ (c) $\frac{3}{5}$ (d) $\frac{6}{11}$
8. Which statement is true? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) every integer is a whole number (b) every whole number is an integer (c) every rational number is an integer (d) $\sqrt{2}$ is rational

::: note Answers
**1.** (a) — $12+9-4 = 17$.
**2.** (c) — $2^4 = 16$.
**3.** (d) — $\sqrt{9}=3$ is rational; $\sqrt{7}$ is not.
**4.** (b) — whole numbers start at 0.
**5.** (c) — the additive inverse changes the sign.
**6.** (b) — only 3 is common.
**7.** (b) — $0.\overline{6} = \frac{6}{9} = \frac{2}{3}$.
**8.** (b) — $-3$ is an integer but not a whole number.
:::

## 5.2 Ratio, Proportion and the Unitary Method

**Facts.** In the proportion $a:b = c:d$, $ad = bc$. To divide $x$ in the ratio $m:n$, each share is $\frac{m}{m+n}x$ and $\frac{n}{m+n}x$. In **direct** proportion, one quantity rises as the other rises; in **inverse** proportion, the product stays constant. Mean proportional between $a$ and $b$ is $\sqrt{ab}$.

1. If $3:5 = x:20$, then $x$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 10 (b) 12 (c) 15 (d) 4
2. Rs 480 divided between two friends in the ratio $3:5$ gives the smaller share <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 160 (b) Rs 180 (c) Rs 240 (d) Rs 300
3. The mean proportional between 4 and 9 is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 6 (b) 6.5 (c) 13 (d) 36
4. If 6 workers build a wall in 10 days, 12 workers of the same capacity will take <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 20 days (b) 10 days (c) 5 days (d) 2 days
5. 5 pens cost Rs 125. The cost of 8 such pens is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 180 (b) Rs 200 (c) Rs 225 (d) Rs 250
6. The ratio 45 minutes : 2 hours in its simplest form is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $45:2$ (b) $3:8$ (c) $9:24$ (d) $1:3$
7. Which pair is in inverse proportion? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) number of books and their cost (b) speed and time for a fixed distance (c) distance and time at a fixed speed (d) side and perimeter of a square
8. If $a:b = 2:3$ and $b:c = 4:5$, then $a:c$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $2:5$ (b) $8:15$ (c) $5:8$ (d) $3:4$

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $x = \frac{3\times20}{5} = 12$.
**2.** (b) — $\frac{3}{8}\times480 = 180$.
**3.** (a) — $\sqrt{4\times9} = 6$.
**4.** (c) — inverse proportion: $\frac{6\times10}{12} = 5$ days.
**5.** (b) — one pen Rs 25, so $8\times25 = 200$.
**6.** (b) — $45:120 = 3:8$.
**7.** (b) — at fixed distance, speed × time is constant.
**8.** (b) — $a:b:c = 8:12:15$, so $a:c = 8:15$.
:::

## 5.3 Profit and Loss, Simple Interest

**Facts.** Profit $=$ S.P. $-$ C.P.; profit per cent $= \frac{\text{profit}}{\text{C.P.}}\times100$. Discount is always on the marked price; VAT is charged on the price after discount. Simple interest $I = \frac{PTR}{100}$; amount $A = P + I$.

1. An article bought for Rs 800 is sold for Rs 920. The profit per cent is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 12 % (b) 15 % (c) 20 % (d) 13 %
2. A shirt marked Rs 1,200 is sold at 10 % discount. Its selling price is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 1,080 (b) Rs 1,100 (c) Rs 1,020 (d) Rs 1,320
3. The simple interest on Rs 5,000 for 2 years at 8 % per annum is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 400 (b) Rs 800 (c) Rs 1,000 (d) Rs 80
4. If the cost price is Rs 500 and the loss is 12 %, the selling price is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 440 (b) Rs 480 (c) Rs 560 (d) Rs 400
5. Rs 2,000 becomes Rs 2,300 in 3 years at simple interest. The rate per annum is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 3 % (b) 5 % (c) 10 % (d) 15 %
6. VAT at 13 % on a bill of Rs 2,000 is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 130 (b) Rs 200 (c) Rs 260 (d) Rs 2,260
7. Profit per cent is always calculated on the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) selling price (b) marked price (c) cost price (d) discount
8. In how many years will Rs 4,000 earn Rs 960 as simple interest at 6 % per annum? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 5

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — profit Rs 120; $\frac{120}{800}\times100 = 15$ %.
**2.** (a) — $1200 - 120 = 1080$.
**3.** (b) — $\frac{5000\times2\times8}{100} = 800$.
**4.** (a) — $500 - 60 = 440$.
**5.** (b) — $I = 300$; $R = \frac{100\times300}{2000\times3} = 5$ %.
**6.** (c) — $13$ % of $2000 = 260$.
**7.** (c) — on the cost price.
**8.** (c) — $T = \frac{100\times960}{4000\times6} = 4$ years.
:::

## 5.4 Area and Volume

**Facts.** Rectangle $A = lb$; square $A = l^2$; triangle $A = \frac{1}{2}bh$; parallelogram $A = bh$; trapezium $A = \frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$; circle $A = \pi r^2$, circumference $= 2\pi r$. Cuboid: volume $= lbh$, total surface $= 2(lb+bh+lh)$. Cube: volume $= l^3$, surface $= 6l^2$. Cylinder: volume $= \pi r^2 h$, curved surface $= 2\pi rh$. Take $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$ unless told otherwise. $1$ m³ $= 1000$ litres.

1. The area of a triangle with base 12 cm and height 9 cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 108 cm² (b) 54 cm² (c) 21 cm² (d) 27 cm²
2. The volume of a cube of edge 5 cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 25 cm³ (b) 75 cm³ (c) 125 cm³ (d) 150 cm³
3. The circumference of a circle of radius 7 cm, with $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$, is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 22 cm (b) 44 cm (c) 154 cm (d) 49 cm
4. The total surface area of a cube of edge 4 cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 16 cm² (b) 64 cm² (c) 96 cm² (d) 24 cm²
5. The volume of a cylinder of radius 7 cm and height 10 cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1540 cm³ (b) 220 cm³ (c) 440 cm³ (d) 770 cm³
6. A tank 2 m × 1 m × 0.5 m holds, when full, <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 100 litres (b) 500 litres (c) 1000 litres (d) 1500 litres
7. The area of a trapezium with parallel sides 8 cm and 12 cm and height 5 cm is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 50 cm² (b) 100 cm² (c) 40 cm² (d) 60 cm²
8. If the side of a square is doubled, its area becomes <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) double (b) three times (c) four times (d) unchanged

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $\frac{1}{2}\times12\times9 = 54$ cm².
**2.** (c) — $5^3 = 125$ cm³.
**3.** (b) — $2\times\frac{22}{7}\times7 = 44$ cm.
**4.** (c) — $6\times4^2 = 96$ cm².
**5.** (a) — $\frac{22}{7}\times49\times10 = 1540$ cm³.
**6.** (c) — $1$ m³ $= 1000$ litres.
**7.** (a) — $\frac{1}{2}(8+12)\times5 = 50$ cm².
**8.** (c) — area varies as the square of the side.
:::

## 5.5 Indices and Algebraic Expressions

**Facts.** $a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$; $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$; $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$; $a^0 = 1$; $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$; $a^{1/2} = \sqrt{a}$. $(a+b)^2 = a^2+2ab+b^2$; $(a-b)^2 = a^2-2ab+b^2$; $a^2-b^2 = (a+b)(a-b)$.

1. $2^3 \times 2^4$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $2^7$ (b) $2^{12}$ (c) $4^7$ (d) $2^{1}$
2. The value of $5^0 + 3^0$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 2 (d) 8
3. $3^{-2}$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $-9$ (b) $\frac{1}{9}$ (c) $-\frac{1}{9}$ (d) 9
4. $(x+3)^2$ expands to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $x^2+9$ (b) $x^2+6x+9$ (c) $x^2+3x+9$ (d) $x^2+6x+6$
5. $x^2-16$ factorises as <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $(x-4)^2$ (b) $(x+4)(x-4)$ (c) $(x+16)(x-1)$ (d) $(x+4)^2$
6. $\frac{a^7}{a^3}$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $a^{10}$ (b) $a^{21}$ (c) $a^4$ (d) $a^{4/3}$
7. $\sqrt{64}$ written as a power of 2 is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $2^3$ (b) $2^4$ (c) $2^6$ (d) $2^8$
8. If $2^x = 32$, then $x$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 16

::: note Answers
**1.** (a) — add the indices.
**2.** (c) — $1+1 = 2$.
**3.** (b) — $3^{-2} = \frac{1}{3^2}$.
**4.** (b) — $(a+b)^2 = a^2+2ab+b^2$.
**5.** (b) — difference of two squares.
**6.** (c) — subtract the indices.
**7.** (a) — $\sqrt{64} = 8 = 2^3$.
**8.** (b) — $2^5 = 32$.
:::

## 5.6 Algebraic Fractions and Equations

**Facts.** Simplify a fraction by factorising and cancelling common factors — never cancel across a $+$ sign. To add fractions, take the LCM of the denominators. A linear equation keeps its balance: whatever is done to one side is done to the other. The graph of $y = mx + c$ is a straight line of slope $m$ cutting the $y$-axis at $c$.

1. $\frac{x^2-9}{x+3}$ simplifies to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $x-3$ (b) $x+3$ (c) $x-9$ (d) $x^2-3$
2. The solution of $3x - 7 = 11$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 4 (b) 6 (c) 18 (d) $\frac{4}{3}$
3. $\frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y}$ equals <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac{1}{x+y}$ (b) $\frac{x+y}{xy}$ (c) $\frac{2}{xy}$ (d) $\frac{xy}{x+y}$
4. The slope of the line $y = 4x - 5$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $-5$ (b) 4 (c) $\frac{4}{5}$ (d) 5
5. The line $y = 2x + 3$ cuts the $y$-axis at <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $(0,2)$ (b) $(0,3)$ (c) $(3,0)$ (d) $(2,0)$
6. If $\frac{x}{4} = \frac{9}{6}$, then $x$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 6 (b) 8 (c) 12 (d) 13.5
7. The value of $\frac{2a}{3} - \frac{a}{6}$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $\frac{a}{2}$ (b) $\frac{a}{3}$ (c) $\frac{3a}{6}$ (d) $\frac{a}{6}$
8. The solution of $\frac{x}{2} + 3 = 7$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 2 (b) 5 (c) 8 (d) 20

::: note Answers
**1.** (a) — $\frac{(x+3)(x-3)}{x+3} = x-3$.
**2.** (b) — $3x = 18$, $x = 6$.
**3.** (b) — common denominator $xy$.
**4.** (b) — the coefficient of $x$.
**5.** (b) — put $x = 0$.
**6.** (a) — $x = \frac{4\times9}{6} = 6$.
**7.** (a) — $\frac{4a-a}{6} = \frac{3a}{6} = \frac{a}{2}$.
**8.** (c) — $\frac{x}{2} = 4$, $x = 8$.
:::

## 5.7 Lines, Angles and Plane Figures

**Facts.** Angles on a straight line add to $180°$; angles at a point add to $360°$. Vertically opposite angles are equal. With parallel lines, corresponding and alternate angles are equal and co-interior angles are supplementary. Triangle angle sum $180°$; exterior angle = sum of the two opposite interior angles. Quadrilateral angle sum $360°$. Each interior angle of a regular $n$-gon is $\frac{(n-2)180°}{n}$; the exterior angles add to $360°$.

1. Two angles on a straight line are $x$ and $3x$. The value of $x$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $30°$ (b) $45°$ (c) $60°$ (d) $90°$
2. In a triangle two angles are $55°$ and $65°$. The third angle is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $50°$ (b) $60°$ (c) $70°$ (d) $120°$
3. Each interior angle of a regular hexagon is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $90°$ (b) $108°$ (c) $120°$ (d) $135°$
4. Vertically opposite angles are always <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) supplementary (b) complementary (c) equal (d) right angles
5. The exterior angle of a triangle is $110°$ and one opposite interior angle is $45°$. The other is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $55°$ (b) $65°$ (c) $70°$ (d) $35°$
6. The sum of the exterior angles of any convex polygon is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $180°$ (b) $360°$ (c) $540°$ (d) depends on the number of sides
7. A quadrilateral with both pairs of opposite sides parallel but no right angle is a <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) trapezium (b) parallelogram (c) rectangle (d) kite
8. Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal and one co-interior angle is $70°$. The other is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $70°$ (b) $20°$ (c) $110°$ (d) $140°$

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $4x = 180°$, $x = 45°$.
**2.** (b) — $180° - 120° = 60°$.
**3.** (c) — $\frac{(6-2)180°}{6} = 120°$.
**4.** (c) — equal.
**5.** (b) — $110° - 45° = 65°$.
**6.** (b) — always $360°$.
**7.** (b) — a parallelogram.
**8.** (c) — co-interior angles are supplementary.
:::

## 5.8 Congruency, Similarity and Solid Objects

**Facts.** Congruent figures have the same shape **and** size; tests: SSS, SAS, ASA, RHS. Similar figures have the same shape; corresponding sides are proportional and corresponding angles equal. For a solid: faces $F$, vertices $V$, edges $E$ satisfy Euler's relation $F + V - E = 2$. A cube has 6 faces, 8 vertices, 12 edges; a triangular prism 5 faces, 6 vertices, 9 edges; a square pyramid 5 faces, 5 vertices, 8 edges.

1. Which test does **not** prove two triangles congruent? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) SSS (b) SAS (c) AAA (d) RHS
2. A cube has edges numbering <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 6 (b) 8 (c) 12 (d) 16
3. In two similar triangles, corresponding sides are <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) equal (b) proportional (c) perpendicular (d) unrelated
4. A solid with 5 faces and 6 vertices has edges numbering <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 8 (b) 9 (c) 10 (d) 12
5. Two triangles have sides $3,4,5$ and $6,8,10$ cm. They are <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) congruent (b) similar but not congruent (c) neither (d) identical
6. The number of faces of a square pyramid is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 8
7. If two figures are congruent, they are <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) similar as well (b) never similar (c) similar only if squares (d) equal in area but not in shape
8. The RHS test applies only to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) equilateral triangles (b) right-angled triangles (c) isosceles triangles (d) all triangles

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — equal angles fix the shape, not the size.
**2.** (c) — 12 edges.
**3.** (b) — proportional.
**4.** (b) — $E = F+V-2 = 5+6-2 = 9$.
**5.** (b) — sides in the ratio $1:2$, so similar, not congruent.
**6.** (b) — four triangular faces and one square base.
**7.** (a) — congruent figures are similar with ratio 1.
**8.** (b) — right angle, hypotenuse and one side.
:::

## 5.9 Coordinates, Transformation, Tessellation, Bearing and Scale Drawing

**Facts.** In $(x,y)$, $x$ is measured along the horizontal axis. Quadrant signs: I $(+,+)$, II $(-,+)$, III $(-,-)$, IV $(+,-)$. Distance from the origin $= \sqrt{x^2+y^2}$. Reflection in the $x$-axis: $(x,y) \to (x,-y)$; in the $y$-axis: $(x,y) \to (-x,y)$. Translation by $\binom{a}{b}$: $(x,y) \to (x+a, y+b)$. Rotation through $180°$ about the origin: $(x,y) \to (-x,-y)$. A regular polygon tessellates if its interior angle divides $360°$ exactly — so triangle, square and hexagon do. Bearing is measured clockwise from north, written with three figures.

1. The point $(-3, 5)$ lies in quadrant <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) I (b) II (c) III (d) IV
2. The image of $(4, -2)$ after reflection in the $x$-axis is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $(-4,-2)$ (b) $(4,2)$ (c) $(-4,2)$ (d) $(2,4)$
3. The distance of $(6,8)$ from the origin is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 10 (b) 14 (c) 48 (d) $\sqrt{14}$
4. Which regular polygon does **not** tessellate by itself? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) equilateral triangle (b) square (c) regular pentagon (d) regular hexagon
5. The bearing of due west is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $090°$ (b) $180°$ (c) $270°$ (d) $360°$
6. The image of $(2,3)$ under translation $\binom{-1}{4}$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $(1,7)$ (b) $(3,-1)$ (c) $(-2,12)$ (d) $(1,-1)$
7. On a map of scale $1:50{,}000$, 3 cm represents <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 150 m (b) 1.5 km (c) 15 km (d) 150 km
8. Rotating $(5,-1)$ through $180°$ about the origin gives <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $(-5,1)$ (b) $(5,1)$ (c) $(-1,5)$ (d) $(1,-5)$

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — negative $x$, positive $y$.
**2.** (b) — the sign of $y$ changes.
**3.** (a) — $\sqrt{36+64} = 10$.
**4.** (c) — its interior angle $108°$ does not divide $360°$.
**5.** (c) — $270°$ clockwise from north.
**6.** (a) — $(2-1,\,3+4)$.
**7.** (b) — $3\times50{,}000 = 150{,}000$ cm $= 1.5$ km.
**8.** (a) — both signs change.
:::

## 5.10 Statistics

**Facts.** Mean $= \frac{\sum x}{n}$; for a frequency table, mean $= \frac{\sum fx}{\sum f}$. Median is the middle value of the ordered data — for even $n$, the mean of the two middle values. Mode is the most frequent value. Range $=$ largest $-$ smallest. In a pie chart, each item's angle $= \frac{\text{its frequency}}{\text{total}}\times360°$.

1. The mean of $4, 7, 9, 12$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 7 (b) 8 (c) 8.5 (d) 9
2. The median of $3, 7, 9, 11, 15$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 7 (b) 9 (c) 11 (d) 10
3. The mode of $2, 3, 3, 5, 7, 3, 8$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 5 (d) 8
4. The range of $12, 5, 20, 8$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 8 (b) 12 (c) 15 (d) 20
5. The median of $4, 8, 10, 14$ is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 8 (b) 9 (c) 10 (d) 12
6. In a pie chart of 60 students, the sector for 15 students has an angle of <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) $60°$ (b) $90°$ (c) $120°$ (d) $150°$
7. If the mean of 5 numbers is 12, their sum is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 12 (b) 17 (c) 60 (d) 2.4
8. Which average is most affected by a single very large value? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) mean (b) median (c) mode (d) range

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — $\frac{32}{4} = 8$.
**2.** (b) — the third of five ordered values.
**3.** (b) — 3 occurs three times.
**4.** (c) — $20-5 = 15$.
**5.** (b) — $\frac{8+10}{2} = 9$.
**6.** (b) — $\frac{15}{60}\times360° = 90°$.
**7.** (c) — $5\times12 = 60$.
**8.** (a) — the mean uses every value, so an outlier pulls it.
