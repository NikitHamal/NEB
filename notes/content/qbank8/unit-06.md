A Mathematics answer is marked in steps, and the steps are worth more than the final number. A two-mark question expects the formula and the substitution; a four-mark question expects the formula, the substitution, the working and the answer with its unit. Write every step, even the obvious one, because a marker can award a step you wrote and cannot award a step you did in your head.

::: key How a four-mark solution is marked
One mark for the correct formula or construction, one for correct substitution, one for correct simplification and one for the final answer with its unit. A wrong arithmetic slip with correct method still earns three of the four — but only if the steps are on the page.
:::

## 6.1 Sets

**1. If $A = \{1,2,3,4,5\}$ and $B = \{4,5,6,7\}$, find $A \cup B$, $A \cap B$ and $n(A \cup B)$.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> $A \cup B = \{1,2,3,4,5,6,7\}$; $A \cap B = \{4,5\}$; $n(A \cup B) = 7$.

**2. In a class of 40 students, 25 play football, 18 play volleyball and 8 play both. Find how many play at least one game and how many play neither.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> $n(F \cup V) = n(F) + n(V) - n(F \cap V) = 25 + 18 - 8 = 35$ play at least one game.
> Neither $= 40 - 35 = 5$ students.
> (A Venn diagram with 17, 8 and 10 in the three regions and 5 outside earns the method marks.)

## 6.2 Whole, Rational and Irrational Numbers

**1. Write any two differences between a rational and an irrational number with one example of each.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> A rational number can be written as $\frac{p}{q}$ with integers $p$ and $q \neq 0$, and its decimal form terminates or recurs — for example $\frac{3}{4} = 0.75$. An irrational number cannot be written as such a fraction and its decimal neither terminates nor recurs — for example $\sqrt{5} = 2.2360\ldots$

**2. Simplify: $\frac{3}{4} + \frac{5}{6} - \frac{2}{3}$.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> LCM of 4, 6 and 3 is 12: $\frac{9}{12} + \frac{10}{12} - \frac{8}{12} = \frac{11}{12}$.

**3. Insert any two rational numbers between $\frac{1}{3}$ and $\frac{1}{2}$.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> Write both with a common denominator large enough to leave room: $\frac{1}{3} = \frac{12}{36}$ and $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{18}{36}$. Two numbers between them are $\frac{14}{36} = \frac{7}{18}$ and $\frac{15}{36} = \frac{5}{12}$. (Checking: $0.333 < 0.388 < 0.416 < 0.5$.) Infinitely many such numbers exist, which is why the rationals are said to be dense.

## 6.3 Ratio, Proportion and the Unitary Method

**1. Divide Rs 1,750 among A, B and C in the ratio $2:3:5$.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> Total parts $= 10$; one part $= \frac{1750}{10} = 175$. A $= 350$, B $= 525$, C $= 875$ rupees.

**2. If 15 workers can dig a canal in 24 days, how many workers are needed to dig it in 18 days?** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> Work is inversely proportional to time: $15\times24 = x\times18$, so $x = \frac{360}{18} = 20$ workers.

**3. A pipe fills a tank in 6 hours and another in 4 hours. If both are opened together, how long will the tank take to fill?** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> In one hour the first fills $\frac{1}{6}$ of the tank and the second $\frac{1}{4}$.
> Together in one hour: $\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{2+3}{12} = \frac{5}{12}$.
> Time $= \frac{12}{5} = 2.4$ hours $= 2$ hours $24$ minutes.

## 6.4 Profit and Loss

**1. An article costing Rs 1,500 is sold at a profit of 18 %. Find the selling price.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> Profit $= \frac{18}{100}\times1500 = 270$; S.P. $= 1500 + 270 =$ **Rs 1,770**.

**2. A shopkeeper marks a jacket at Rs 4,000, allows 15 % discount and then charges 13 % VAT. Find the amount the customer pays.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> Discount $= 15$ % of $4000 = 600$; price after discount $= 3400$.
> VAT $= 13$ % of $3400 = 442$.
> Amount paid $= 3400 + 442 =$ **Rs 3,842**.
> (Note the order: discount is on the marked price, VAT on the discounted price.)

**3. By selling a bicycle for Rs 6,300 a trader loses 10 %. At what price should it be sold to gain 12 %?** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> Let the cost price be $x$. Then $0.90x = 6300$, so $x = 7000$.
> For a 12 % gain, S.P. $= 1.12\times7000 =$ **Rs 7,840**.

## 6.5 Simple Interest

**1. Find the simple interest on Rs 12,000 for 3 years at 9 % per annum.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> $I = \frac{PTR}{100} = \frac{12000\times3\times9}{100} =$ **Rs 3,240**.

**2. A sum of Rs 8,000 amounts to Rs 10,400 in 4 years at simple interest. Find the rate.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> $I = 10400 - 8000 = 2400$.
> $R = \frac{100I}{PT} = \frac{100\times2400}{8000\times4} = \frac{240000}{32000} =$ **7.5 % per annum**.

**3. In what time will Rs 15,000 earn Rs 4,500 as simple interest at 10 % per annum?** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> $T = \frac{100I}{PR} = \frac{100\times4500}{15000\times10} = 3$ years.

## 6.6 Area and Volume

**1. Find the area of a circle of radius 14 cm, taking $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> $A = \pi r^2 = \frac{22}{7}\times14\times14 = 616$ cm².

**2. A room is 6 m long, 4 m wide and 3 m high. Find the cost of painting its four walls and ceiling at Rs 90 per m².** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> Four walls $= 2h(l+b) = 2\times3\times(6+4) = 60$ m².
> Ceiling $= l\times b = 24$ m².
> Total area $= 84$ m²; cost $= 84\times90 =$ **Rs 7,560**.

**3. A cylindrical water tank of radius 1.4 m and height 2 m is full. Find the volume of water in litres, taking $\pi = \frac{22}{7}$.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> $V = \pi r^2 h = \frac{22}{7}\times1.4\times1.4\times2 = 12.32$ m³.
> $1$ m³ $= 1000$ litres, so the tank holds **12,320 litres**.

## 6.7 Indices and Algebra

**1. Simplify: $\frac{x^5 \times x^3}{x^4}$.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> $\frac{x^{5+3}}{x^4} = x^{8-4} = x^4$.

**2. Factorise: (i) $x^2 - 25$ (ii) $x^2 + 7x + 12$.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> (i) $(x+5)(x-5)$. (ii) $(x+3)(x+4)$, since $3+4 = 7$ and $3\times4 = 12$.

**3. Simplify: $\frac{x^2-9}{x^2+x-12}$.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> Numerator $= (x+3)(x-3)$. Denominator $= (x+4)(x-3)$.
> Cancelling the common factor $(x-3)$: $\frac{x+3}{x+4}$, valid for $x \neq 3$ and $x \neq -4$.

**4. Solve: $\frac{2x-1}{3} - \frac{x+2}{4} = 1$.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> Multiply through by 12: $4(2x-1) - 3(x+2) = 12$.
> $8x - 4 - 3x - 6 = 12 \Rightarrow 5x - 10 = 12 \Rightarrow 5x = 22$, so $x = \frac{22}{5} = 4.4$.

## 6.8 Equation and Graph

**1. Draw the graph of $y = 2x - 1$ for $x = -1, 0, 1, 2$ (give the table of values and describe the line).** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> Table: $x = -1 \to y = -3$; $x = 0 \to y = -1$; $x = 1 \to y = 1$; $x = 2 \to y = 3$.
> Plotting $(-1,-3), (0,-1), (1,1), (2,3)$ gives a straight line rising to the right, cutting the $y$-axis at $-1$ with slope 2 (for every 1 unit right, 2 units up).

**2. The sum of two numbers is 45 and their difference is 11. Find the numbers.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> Let the numbers be $x$ and $y$ with $x > y$.
> $x + y = 45$ and $x - y = 11$.
> Adding: $2x = 56$, so $x = 28$; then $y = 45 - 28 = 17$.
> Check: $28 + 17 = 45$ and $28 - 17 = 11$. The numbers are **28 and 17**.

## 6.9 Lines, Angles and Plane Figures

**1. Two angles of a triangle are $48°$ and $67°$. Find the third angle and the exterior angle at that vertex.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> Third angle $= 180° - (48° + 67°) = 65°$. Its exterior angle $= 180° - 65° = 115°$ (which equals $48° + 67°$, as the exterior-angle property requires).

**2. Find each interior angle of a regular polygon of 9 sides.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> Interior angle $= \frac{(n-2)180°}{n} = \frac{7\times180°}{9} = 140°$.

**3. In a parallelogram ABCD, $\angle A = 65°$. Find the other three angles, giving reasons.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal, so $\angle C = \angle A = 65°$.
> Co-interior angles between the parallel sides are supplementary, so $\angle B = 180° - 65° = 115°$.
> Then $\angle D = \angle B = 115°$.
> Check: $65 + 115 + 65 + 115 = 360°$.

## 6.10 Congruency and Similarity

**1. State the SAS test of congruency and illustrate it with a pair of triangles.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> If two sides and the **included** angle of one triangle equal the corresponding two sides and included angle of another, the triangles are congruent. In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle PQR$, if $AB = PQ$, $AC = PR$ and $\angle A = \angle P$, then $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR$.

**2. In two similar triangles the sides of the first are 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm, and the shortest side of the second is 9 cm. Find the other two sides of the second triangle.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> Scale factor $= \frac{9}{6} = 1.5$.
> Other sides $= 8\times1.5 = 12$ cm and $10\times1.5 = 15$ cm.
> (Check: $9:12:15 = 6:8:10$, so the triangles are similar.)

## 6.11 Solid Objects, Tessellation and Scale Drawing

**1. Verify Euler's relation for a triangular prism.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> A triangular prism has $F = 5$, $V = 6$, $E = 9$. Then $F + V - E = 5 + 6 - 9 = 2$, which verifies the relation.

**2. Explain why a regular pentagon does not tessellate while a regular hexagon does.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> For a tessellation, whole polygons must fill the $360°$ round a point with no gap and no overlap, so the interior angle must divide $360°$ exactly. A regular hexagon has interior angle $120°$, and $3\times120° = 360°$ — three hexagons meet exactly, as in a honeycomb. A regular pentagon has interior angle $108°$; three give $324°$ (a gap of $36°$) and four give $432°$ (an overlap), so no arrangement closes.

**3. A map is drawn to the scale $1:25{,}000$. Two villages are 7.2 cm apart on the map. Find the real distance in kilometres, and the map distance for a 5 km road.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> Real distance $= 7.2\times25{,}000 = 180{,}000$ cm $= 1.8$ km.
> For 5 km $= 500{,}000$ cm: map distance $= \frac{500000}{25000} = 20$ cm.

## 6.12 Coordinates and Transformation

**1. Plot $A(2,3)$, $B(2,-1)$ and $C(-3,3)$ and state which two of them lie on a line parallel to the $y$-axis.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>

> $A$ and $B$ both have $x = 2$, so $AB$ is parallel to the $y$-axis. ($A$ and $C$ both have $y = 3$, so $AC$ is parallel to the $x$-axis.)

**2. Find the image of the triangle with vertices $P(1,2)$, $Q(4,2)$, $R(1,5)$ under (i) reflection in the $y$-axis (ii) translation $\binom{2}{-3}$.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> (i) Reflection in the $y$-axis, $(x,y)\to(-x,y)$: $P'(-1,2)$, $Q'(-4,2)$, $R'(-1,5)$.
> (ii) Translation $(x,y)\to(x+2, y-3)$: $P''(3,-1)$, $Q''(6,-1)$, $R''(3,2)$.
> Both images are congruent to the original, since reflection and translation preserve lengths and angles.

## 6.13 Bearing

**1. A village B is 6 km due east of A, and C is 6 km due north of B. Find the bearing of B from A and of A from B.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> Bearings are measured clockwise from north. Due east is **$090°$**, so the bearing of B from A is $090°$.
> The bearing of A from B is the back bearing, $090° + 180° =$ **$270°$** (due west), which agrees with A lying west of B.

## 6.14 Statistics

**1. Find the mean, median and mode of: $5, 7, 7, 9, 12, 14, 14, 14, 18$.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> Sum $= 100$, $n = 9$, so mean $= \frac{100}{9} = 11.11$ (2 d.p.).
> Ordered already; the 5th value is the median $= 12$.
> 14 occurs three times, so the mode $= 14$.

**2. The marks of 40 students are given: $0$–$20$: 6, $20$–$40$: 10, $40$–$60$: 14, $60$–$80$: 7, $80$–$100$: 3. Find the mean using mid-values.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> Mid-values 10, 30, 50, 70, 90.
> $\sum fx = 6(10) + 10(30) + 14(50) + 7(70) + 3(90) = 60 + 300 + 700 + 490 + 270 = 1820$.
> Mean $= \frac{1820}{40} =$ **45.5 marks**.

**3. In a survey of 72 families, 24 use firewood, 30 use gas, 12 use electricity and 6 use biogas for cooking. Find the angle of each sector for a pie chart.** <span class="marks">[4]</span>

> Each angle $= \frac{\text{families}}{72}\times360° = \text{families}\times5°$.
> Firewood $= 120°$; gas $= 150°$; electricity $= 60°$; biogas $= 30°$.
> Check: $120 + 150 + 60 + 30 = 360°$.

::: tip The three habits that save marks
Write the formula before the numbers. Keep the unit with every answer. And check once: does the answer make sense? A tank of 12,320 litres is believable; a tank of 12 litres for a 2 m cylinder is not.
:::
