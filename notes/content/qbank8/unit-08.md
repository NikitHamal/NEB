The English paper does not reward memorised answers. Its one-mark items test **vocabulary in context**, **grammar in use** and **comprehension of a passage you have not seen before**. That sounds harder than recall, and it is also fairer: the skill transfers. This chapter trains the three in turn — words from the nineteen textbook units, the grammar points of the Grade 8 course, and short unseen passages with objective items.

::: key Where the one-mark items come from
Roughly: three or four from a reading passage, three or four from grammar (tense, voice, question tags, prepositions, articles, conditionals, reported speech), and two or three from vocabulary — synonyms, antonyms, words in context. None of them can be prepared by learning the textbook answers by heart.
:::

## 8.1 Vocabulary from the textbook units

**Facts.** The words below are the ones the units keep using: *itinerary, hospitality, altitude, endangered, habitat, migration, invention, anecdote, vegan, conservation, inaugurate, vacancy, applicant, deadline, memoir, forecast, humidity, custom, ritual, poacher*.

1. An *itinerary* is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) a ticket (b) a plan of a journey (c) a passport (d) a guide
2. An animal in danger of dying out completely is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) extinct (b) endangered (c) domestic (d) nocturnal
3. The place where a plant or animal naturally lives is its <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) habitat (b) habit (c) hamlet (d) harvest
4. A person who hunts animals illegally is a <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) ranger (b) poacher (c) porter (d) peasant
5. The word closest in meaning to *hospitality* is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) hospital treatment (b) friendly welcome of guests (c) hostility (d) hesitation
6. A short, interesting story about a real incident is an <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) epic (b) anecdote (c) essay (d) editorial
7. The opposite of *ancient* is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) old (b) antique (c) modern (d) historic
8. To *inaugurate* an airport means to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) close it (b) formally open it (c) repair it (d) expand it
9. The seasonal movement of birds from one region to another is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) migration (b) hibernation (c) emigration (d) rotation
10. A *vacancy* announcement invites <span class="marks">[1]</span>
    (a) complaints (b) applications for a post (c) tenders for goods (d) donations

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — an itinerary is the plan of a journey, not the ticket for it. **2.** (b) — *extinct* means already gone; *endangered* means still alive but at risk. **3.** (a) — a habitat is the place where a species naturally lives. **4.** (b) — a poacher hunts illegally; a ranger protects. **5.** (b) — *hospitality* is the friendly welcome of guests; *hostility* is its near-opposite. **6.** (b) — an anecdote is a short true story about a real incident. **7.** (c) — *ancient* and *modern* are the opposites; *old* and *antique* are near-synonyms. **8.** (b) — to inaugurate is to open formally. **9.** (a) — *hibernation* is winter sleep; *emigration* is of people leaving a country. **10.** (b) — a vacancy announcement invites applications for a post.
:::

## 8.2 Tense

**Facts.** Simple present for habits and facts (*she walks*); present continuous for now (*she is walking*); present perfect for a finished action with present relevance (*she has walked*), used with *just, already, yet, ever, never, since, for*. Simple past with a finished past time (*yesterday, last year, in 2019, ago*). Past continuous for an action in progress when another interrupted it. *Since* takes a point of time, *for* a length of time.

1. He ______ in Pokhara since 2019. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) lives (b) is living (c) has lived (d) lived
2. While I ______ my homework, the lights went out. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) do (b) was doing (c) have done (d) did
3. The sun ______ in the east. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) rise (b) rises (c) is rising (d) rose
4. She ______ her grandmother last Dashain. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) visits (b) has visited (c) visited (d) is visiting
5. I have known him ______ five years. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) since (b) for (c) from (d) by
6. Listen! Somebody ______ at the door. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) knocks (b) is knocking (c) knocked (d) has knocked
7. They ______ their project yet. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) haven't finished (b) didn't finish (c) don't finish (d) weren't finishing
8. By the time we reached the station, the train ______. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) left (b) has left (c) had left (d) was leaving

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — *since* + present perfect. **2.** (b) — an action in progress, interrupted. **3.** (b) — a general fact. **4.** (c) — finished past time. **5.** (b) — *for* + length of time. **6.** (b) — happening at this moment. **7.** (a) — *yet* with the present perfect. **8.** (c) — the earlier of two past actions.
:::

## 8.3 Voice

**Facts.** In the passive, the object becomes the subject and the verb becomes *be* + past participle: *They built the bridge* → *The bridge was built (by them)*. Keep the tense. Intransitive verbs (*sleep, arrive*) have no passive.

1. The passive of *They are repairing the road* is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) The road is repairing (b) The road is being repaired (c) The road has repaired (d) The road was repaired
2. The passive of *Someone has stolen my bag* is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) My bag has stolen (b) My bag was stolen (c) My bag has been stolen (d) My bag is stolen
3. The active of *The letter was written by Sita* is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Sita writes the letter (b) Sita wrote the letter (c) Sita has written the letter (d) Sita was writing the letter
4. The passive of *We must obey the rules* is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) The rules must obey (b) The rules must be obeyed (c) The rules must been obeyed (d) The rules are obeyed
5. Which sentence cannot be made passive? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) She sang a song. (b) He arrived late. (c) They cleaned the room. (d) I read the notice.
6. The passive of *People speak Nepali in Sikkim too* is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Nepali speaks in Sikkim too (b) Nepali is spoken in Sikkim too (c) Nepali has spoken in Sikkim too (d) Nepali was speaking in Sikkim too
7. The passive of *Did the teacher punish him?* is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Was he punished by the teacher? (b) Is he punished by the teacher? (c) Has he punished by the teacher? (d) Was he punish by the teacher?
8. In a passive sentence, the doer of the action is introduced by <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) *of* (b) *with* (c) *by* (d) *from*

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — present continuous passive: *is being repaired*. **2.** (c) — present perfect passive: *has been stolen*. **3.** (b) — the past simple active of *was written* is *wrote*. **4.** (b) — a modal passive keeps the modal: *must be obeyed*. **5.** (b) — *arrive* is intransitive, so it has no object to become a subject. **6.** (b) — present simple passive of a general fact. **7.** (a) — a past simple question becomes *Was … by …?* **8.** (c) — the doer takes *by*; *with* introduces the instrument.
:::

## 8.4 Question tags, articles and prepositions

**Facts.** A positive statement takes a negative tag and the reverse: *She is a nurse, isn't she? / He didn't come, did he?* *Let's* → *shall we*; imperative → *will you*; *I am* → *aren't I*. Use *a* before a consonant sound, *an* before a vowel sound (*an hour, a university*), *the* for something already known or unique. Fixed pairs: *afraid of, good at, interested in, depend on, listen to, arrive at (a place), congratulate on, married to*.

1. She has finished her work, ______? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) has she (b) hasn't she (c) isn't she (d) didn't she
2. Let's go for a walk, ______? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) will we (b) shall we (c) don't we (d) can we
3. Open the window, ______? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) do you (b) will you (c) shall we (d) aren't you
4. He is ______ honest officer. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
5. Mount Everest is ______ highest peak in the world. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
6. She is good ______ Mathematics. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) in (b) at (c) on (d) with
7. The success of the plan depends ______ your support. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) in (b) at (c) on (d) of
8. We arrived ______ the airport at six. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) in (b) at (c) to (d) on

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — a positive statement takes a negative tag, and the auxiliary is repeated: *hasn't she*. **2.** (b) — *Let's* always takes *shall we*. **3.** (b) — an imperative takes *will you*. **4.** (b) — *honest* begins with a vowel sound, so *an*. **5.** (c) — a unique superlative takes *the*. **6.** (b) — the fixed pair is *good at*. **7.** (c) — the fixed pair is *depend on*. **8.** (b) — *arrive at* a point, *arrive in* a country or city.
:::

## 8.5 Reported speech and conditionals

**Facts.** In reported speech, move the tense back one step, change pronouns and time words (*today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, here → there*), and use *said that / asked / told*. For questions use *asked if/whether* or the question word, in statement order. Conditionals: type 1 *If + present, will + verb*; type 2 *If + past, would + verb*; type 0 *If + present, present* for general truths.

1. He said, "I am tired." → He said that he ______ tired. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) is (b) was (c) has been (d) were
2. She asked, "Where do you live?" → She asked where I ______. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) live (b) lived (c) do live (d) am living
3. "Don't touch the wires," the teacher said. → The teacher ______ us not to touch the wires. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) said (b) told (c) asked that (d) says
4. If it rains tomorrow, we ______ the match. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) postpone (b) will postpone (c) would postpone (d) postponed
5. If I ______ a bird, I would fly over the Himalaya. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) am (b) was (c) were (d) will be
6. Water ______ if you heat it to 100 °C. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) boils (b) will boil (c) would boil (d) boiled
7. He said, "I will come tomorrow." → He said that he would come ______. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) tomorrow (b) the next day (c) yesterday (d) that day
8. "Have you finished?" she asked. → She asked ______ I had finished. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) that (b) what (c) if (d) which

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — the tense moves back one step: *am* becomes *was*. **2.** (b) — a reported question takes statement order and the tense moves back. **3.** (b) — *told* takes an object (*told us*); *said* does not. **4.** (b) — type 1: *if* + present, *will* + verb. **5.** (c) — type 2 uses *were* for every person. **6.** (a) — a general truth keeps the present simple in both clauses. **7.** (b) — *tomorrow* becomes *the next day* in reported speech. **8.** (c) — a yes/no question is reported with *if* or *whether*.
:::

## 8.6 Unseen passage 1

> Khaptad National Park, in the far west of Nepal, is a plateau of rolling meadows at about 3,000 metres, broken by patches of fir and oak. Twenty-two **patans**, or grassland clearings, open out among the trees, and in the monsoon they fill with wild flowers. The park was gazetted in 1984, partly to protect the forest and partly because the plateau had been the home of Khaptad Baba, a hermit whose ashram still stands beside the Triveni, where three streams meet. Pilgrims walk up for the full-moon festival in late summer; the rest of the year the park is almost empty, and the only sounds are the wind and the Himalayan monal calling from the ridge.

1. Khaptad National Park lies in <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) eastern Nepal (b) central Nepal (c) the far west (d) the Tarai
2. The word *patans* in the passage means <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) ponds (b) grassland clearings (c) forest trails (d) temples
3. The park was established in <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 1974 (b) 1984 (c) 1994 (d) 2004
4. *Triveni* is a place where <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) three streams meet (b) three paths cross (c) three temples stand (d) three districts meet
5. The passage suggests that the park is crowded <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) all year (b) in winter (c) around the late-summer full moon (d) never
6. The *Himalayan monal* in the passage is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) a flower (b) a bird (c) a stream (d) a ridge

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — *in the far west of Nepal*. **2.** (b) — the passage defines it. **3.** (b) — *gazetted in 1984*. **4.** (a). **5.** (c) — pilgrims walk up for the full-moon festival. **6.** (b) — it *calls from the ridge*.
:::

## 8.7 Unseen passage 2

> In 2010 the governments of thirteen countries where tigers still live agreed on a single target: to double the number of wild tigers by 2022. Nepal was the first country to reach it. The national count rose from 121 animals in 2009 to 355 in 2022, and most of the increase came from the parks of the Tarai — Chitwan, Bardiya, Parsa, Banke and Shuklaphanta. Three things made the difference: soldiers and rangers patrolling the parks, community forests that joined the parks into corridors so that young tigers could move and find territory, and village programmes that paid for losses to livestock so that farmers reported a tiger instead of poisoning it. Success has brought its own problem. More tigers in a crowded landscape means more encounters, and the number of people killed has risen with the number of tigers. The next ten years will be judged less by the count than by whether the two populations can share the same corridor.

1. The target agreed in 2010 was to double the tiger population by <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 2018 (b) 2020 (c) 2022 (d) 2030
2. Nepal's tiger count in 2022 was <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 121 (b) 198 (c) 235 (d) 355
3. According to the passage, community forests helped because they <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) supplied firewood (b) linked parks into corridors (c) provided tourist income (d) fenced the parks
4. Farmers reported tigers rather than poisoning them mainly because <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) they were afraid of the law (b) losses to livestock were compensated (c) tigers were rare (d) rangers asked them to
5. The word *encounters* in the passage means <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) counts (b) meetings (c) escapes (d) captures
6. The last sentence suggests that the writer is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) certain of failure (b) satisfied that the problem is solved (c) cautious about coexistence (d) opposed to conservation

::: note Answers
**1.** (c) — *to double the number of wild tigers by 2022*. **2.** (d) — 355, up from 121 in 2009. **3.** (b) — they joined the parks into corridors so young tigers could find territory. **4.** (b) — village programmes paid for livestock losses, so reporting was worth more than poisoning. **5.** (b) — an encounter here is a meeting between people and tigers. **6.** (c) — the writer says success has brought its own problem and the next decade will be judged by coexistence.
:::

## 8.8 Word formation and spelling

**Facts.** Noun from verb: *inform → information, decide → decision, arrive → arrival, conserve → conservation*. Adjective from noun: *danger → dangerous, beauty → beautiful, nature → natural*. Opposites with prefixes: *un-, in-, im-, dis-, ir-* (*unhappy, incorrect, impossible, disagree, irregular*). Commonly misspelled in examinations: *necessary, government, occurred, environment, separate, received, immediately, beginning*.

1. The noun form of *decide* is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) deciding (b) decision (c) decisive (d) decided
2. The adjective form of *nature* is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) naturally (b) naturalise (c) natural (d) nature's
3. The correct opposite of *regular* is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) unregular (b) inregular (c) irregular (d) disregular
4. Which word is spelt correctly? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) goverment (b) government (c) govermant (d) govenment
5. The noun form of *conserve* is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) conservative (b) conservation (c) conserving (d) conserved
6. Which word is spelt correctly? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) enviornment (b) environment (c) enviroment (d) envirenment
7. The opposite of *possible* is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) unpossible (b) dispossible (c) impossible (d) irpossible
8. The adjective form of *danger* is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) dangerly (b) dangerous (c) endanger (d) dangered

::: note Answers
**1.** (b) — *decide* → *decision*; *decisive* is the adjective. **2.** (c) — *nature* → *natural*; *naturally* is the adverb. **3.** (c) — *regular* takes the prefix *ir-*. **4.** (b) — *government* keeps the *n* of *govern*. **5.** (b) — *conserve* → *conservation*. **6.** (b) — *environment* keeps *iron* inside it, which is the easiest way to remember the spelling. **7.** (c) — *possible* takes *im-* before *p*. **8.** (b) — *danger* → *dangerous*.
:::
