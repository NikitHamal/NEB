This chapter does three things. It **reads the real papers** — the board papers of 2079, 2081, 2082 and 2083 and the model papers of 2078 and 2079, with two further official model sets — and tabulates what the examiner asks, unit by unit. It then sets **two complete model papers to the exact grid** of the Chemistry paper (3 hours, full marks 75, Group A 11 × 1, Group B 8 × 5, Group C 3 × 8), each **fully solved**. Finally it lists **forty-eight predicted questions, ranked**, every one with the reason it is predicted and a short model answer.

::: caution What "predicted" means here
No paper is leaked to anyone, and this chapter does not claim to know what will be set. A prediction is a judgement from three things that are shown in the tables below: how often the topic has come up in the eight papers studied, how large its share of the 128 teaching hours is, and how long it has been since it was last asked. Treat the list as a revision order, never as a guarantee. The 2080 paper could not be reached, so the analysis covers 2078 (model), 2079 (model and board), 2081, 2082, 2083 and model sets A and B.
:::

## 7.1 Pattern analysis of the Grade 12 Chemistry papers

### 7.1.1 The grid, and what has been fixed for years

| Part | Questions | Marks | What the examiner does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Group A | 11 multiple-choice questions | 11 × 1 = 11 | one mark each; in 2082 the booklet was given out later in the examination |
| Group B | Q12 to Q19 | 8 × 5 = 40 | two of the eight carry an internal **Or**; the five marks are split 1+4, 2+3, 1+1+3 or 1+1+1+2 |
| Group C | Q20 to Q22 | 3 × 8 = 24 | Q20 and Q21 carry an **Or**; Q22 is four two-mark parts from applied chemistry (six smaller parts in 2079) |

All questions are compulsory; the choice is only inside a question, between its two alternatives. The CDC publishes no per-unit grid for Grade 12, so every "weight" below is our reading of the papers. The one official figure is the cognitive-level split printed in the 2079 model paper: **knowledge 16 %, understanding 24 %, application 28 % and higher ability 32 %** of the 75 marks — so about three-fifths of the paper asks you to *use* chemistry, not to recite it.

### 7.1.2 Where the questions sit

The papers of 2081, 2082 and 2083 follow the same order, and the pattern is worth knowing before you open the paper.

| Slot | What was set in 2081 / 2082 / 2083 |
|---|---|
| **Group A**, Q1 to Q4 | four **general and physical** questions: equivalent weight or normality, Ka or pH, order of reaction or half-life, the ranking of E° values or Gibbs–Helmholtz |
| Group A, Q5 and Q6 | two **inorganic** questions: catalytic metals, coloured ions, the deep blue complex of copper, lithopone, purest form of iron |
| Group A, Q7 to Q11 | five **organic** questions: basicity order, Tollens' and iodoform tests, the product of a named reaction, the Grignard route |
| **Group B**, Q12 and Q13 | **physical**: a cell from two E° values, a heat of formation, a pH calculation, a titration, a rate calculation |
| Group B, Q14 to Q19 | six questions split **two inorganic to four organic**: a reaction chain, a list of named reactions, an amine, acid or alcohol question, and a pair on copper, mercury, zinc or rusting. The inorganic pair stood at Q14–Q15 in 2081 and at Q18–Q19 in 2082 and 2083 |
| **Group C**, Q20 | one **physical** question with an Or: volumetric, kinetics or ionic equilibrium |
| Group C, Q21 | one **organic** question with an Or: a long identification chain |
| Group C, Q22 | **applied**: cement, polymers, paper, nuclear chemistry, drugs — four two-mark parts |

Reading a typical paper, about **33 of the 75 marks are organic, 22 general and physical, 12 inorganic and 8 applied**. Each share is close to that area's share of the teaching hours (organic 55 of 128 hours, physical 40, inorganic 20, applied 13), which is the best argument for revising in proportion to hours.

Two things stand out. **Nothing in the syllabus is ignored:** twelve of the twenty-one units were set in at least seven of the eight papers. A gap is not a forecast, but a student with four weeks to spare should cover these after the very-high units, not before.

**Definitions asked again and again:** primary standard; equivalence point against end point; order against molecularity; rate law; Hess's law; heat of formation and of combustion; standard electrode potential; pH; Portland cement; natural against artificial radioactivity.

**Organic items that repeat across papers:** Victor Meyer's test (2078 model, 2079 model, 2081, 2083); chloroform with its five reactions (2079 model, 2081, 2082, 2083); Hoffmann's method (2079, 2079 model, 2082, 2083, model set B); an iodoform identification chain (2079 model, 2082, model set A); Reimer–Tiemann and Perkin in the named-reaction lists (2079 model, 2079, 2082, 2083); OPC against PPC (2078 model, 2079 model).

## 7.3 Solutions to Model Paper 1

::: note Group A — answers
**1.** (b) — Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂ uses two H⁺ per formula unit, so E = 106/2 = 53. Option (a) forgets the n-factor.
**2.** (a) — t½ = 0.693/k = 0.693/(6.93 × 10⁻³) = 100 min; option (d) multiplies instead of dividing.
**3.** (b) — [OH⁻] = 10⁻³ M, pOH = 3, pH = 14 − 3 = 11. Option (a) is the pOH.
**4.** (c) — ΔG = ΔH − TΔS is negative at every T only if ΔH < 0 and ΔS > 0, since then both terms help.
**5.** (c) — 2(+1) + x + 4(−2) = 0 gives x = +6 (manganate, green).
**6.** (b) — SO₂ reduces orange Cr₂O₇²⁻ to green Cr³⁺, which is why roasting of sulphide ores is detected this way.
**7.** (c) — Saytzeff's rule: the more substituted alkene, but-2-ene, is the major product; but-1-ene is minor.
**8.** (c) — acetophenone has CH₃CO– and gives iodoform; benzaldehyde does not. 2,4-DNP and NaHSO₃ are given by both, and Fehling's test by neither.
**9.** (c) — the electron-withdrawing chlorine stabilises the carboxylate; phenol (pKa 10) is far weaker than any carboxylic acid.
**10.** (b) — neutral medium stops reduction at the hydroxylamine; aniline needs acid, azobenzene alkali and p-aminophenol electrolysis in conc. H₂SO₄.
**11.** (b) — Hofmann bromamide degradation loses the carbonyl carbon: CH₃CH₂CONH₂ → CH₃CH₂NH₂.

::: note Group B — Q12 to Q14
**12.** (a) A **primary standard** is a substance so pure and stable that its standard solution can be prepared by direct weighing. **Yes**, oxalic acid crystals are a primary standard: pure, stable, not hygroscopic and of exact formula.
(b) Equivalent weight = 126/2 = 63.
$$ N = \frac{1.26/63}{0.200} = \frac{0.02}{0.200} = 0.10\ \text{N} $$
(c) N₁V₁ = N₂V₂ gives N(NaOH) = 0.10 × 20.0/25.0 = **0.080 N**. Strength = 0.080 × 40 = **3.2 g L⁻¹**.

**13.** (a) The **heat of combustion** is the enthalpy change when one mole of a substance burns completely in oxygen under standard conditions.
(b) The heats of combustion of C and H₂ are the heats of formation of CO₂ and H₂O. Combustion: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O, ΔH_c = −890.3.
$$ \Delta H_{c} = [\Delta H_{f}(\text{CO}_{2})+2\Delta H_{f}(\text{H}_{2}\text{O})]-\Delta H_{f}(\text{CH}_{4}) $$
$$ -890.3 = [-393.5+2(-285.8)]-\Delta H_{f} = -965.1-\Delta H_{f} $$
$$ \Delta H_{f}(\text{CH}_{4}) = -965.1+890.3 = -74.8\ \text{kJ mol}^{-1} $$

**14.** (a) The **standard electrode potential** is the potential of an electrode in contact with its 1 M ion solution (1 atm for a gas) at 25 °C, measured against the SHE (0.00 V).
(b) Zn is more negative, so Zn is the **anode** and Ag the **cathode**:
$$ \text{Zn}(s)\,|\,\text{Zn}^{2+}(1\,\text{M})\,\|\,\text{Ag}^{+}(1\,\text{M})\,|\,\text{Ag}(s) $$
(c) Zn + 2Ag⁺ → Zn²⁺ + 2Ag. (d) E°cell = 0.80 − (−0.76) = **+1.56 V**.
**Or:** Compare (1) and (2): [A] doubles, rate × 4, so order in A = **2**. Compare (1) and (3): [B] doubles, rate × 2, so order in B = **1**. Rate law: **rate = k[A]²[B]**.
$$ k = \frac{2\times10^{-3}}{(0.1)^{2}(0.1)} = 2.0\ \text{L}^{2}\,\text{mol}^{-2}\,\text{s}^{-1} $$

::: note Group B — Q15 to Q19
**15.** A = **1-bromopropane**, CH₃CH₂CH₂Br. B = **propene**: CH₃CH₂CH₂Br + KOH(alc.) → CH₃CH=CH₂ + KBr + H₂O. C = **2-bromopropane**: CH₃CH=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃CHBrCH₃ (Markovnikov). D = **propan-2-ol**: CH₃CHBrCH₃ + KOH(aq.) → CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ + KBr. E = **propanone**: CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ + [O] → (K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄) CH₃COCH₃ + H₂O.

**16.** Primary: ethanol; tertiary: 2-methylpropan-2-ol, (CH₃)₃COH. **Victor Meyer's method:** (i) the alcohol is warmed with red P + I₂ to give the iodide; (ii) the iodide is heated with AgNO₂ to give a nitro compound (a tertiary iodide gives an alkene or nitrite, not a nitro compound); (iii) HNO₂ (NaNO₂ + H₂SO₄) is added; (iv) NaOH is added. The **primary** alcohol gives R–CH₂NO₂ → nitrolic acid R–C(NO₂)=NOH, whose sodium salt is **blood red**. The **tertiary** alcohol gives no nitro compound and no reaction with HNO₂, so the solution is **colourless**.

**17.** (a) Heat the mixture with diethyl oxalate. Ethylamine gives the **solid** N,N′-diethyloxamide, (CONHC₂H₅)₂; diethylamine gives the **liquid** oxamic ester, C₂H₅OOC–CO–N(C₂H₅)₂. The solid is filtered off and the liquid distilled from it; each is hydrolysed with aqueous KOH, (CONHC₂H₅)₂ + 2KOH → 2C₂H₅NH₂ + (COOK)₂ and the ester to diethylamine, to regenerate the amines.
(b) With NaNO₂/HCl: ethylamine (primary) evolves **N₂** and gives ethanol, C₂H₅NH₂ + HNO₂ → C₂H₅OH + N₂ + H₂O; diethylamine (secondary) gives a **yellow oily N-nitrosoamine**, (C₂H₅)₂N–N=O, without gas.
**Or:** (a) CH₃COCH₃ + 4[H] → (Zn–Hg, HCl) CH₃CH₂CH₃ + H₂O. (b) 2HCHO + NaOH → CH₃OH + HCOONa. (c) C₆H₅CHO + (CH₃CO)₂O → (CH₃COONa, 453 K) C₆H₅CH=CHCOOH + CH₃COOH. (d) 2CH₃CHO → (dil. NaOH) CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CHO. (e) CH₃CHO + 2[Ag(NH₃)₂]OH → CH₃COONH₄ + 2Ag↓ + 3NH₃ + H₂O.

::: note Group C — Q20 to Q22
**20.** (a) **Order** is the sum of the powers of the concentration terms in the experimental rate law and may be zero, fractional or whole; **molecularity** is the number of molecules colliding in a single elementary step and is always a whole number (1, 2 or 3).
(b) Compare (1) and (2): [NO] doubles with [O₂] fixed, rate × 4 (2.5 × 10⁻⁶ → 1.0 × 10⁻⁵), so the order in NO = **2**. Compare (2) and (3): [O₂] doubles, rate × 2, so the order in O₂ = **1**. **Rate = k[NO]²[O₂].**
(c) From experiment (1):
$$ k = \frac{2.5\times10^{-6}}{(0.01)^{2}(0.01)} = \frac{2.5\times10^{-6}}{1.0\times10^{-6}} = 2.5\ \text{L}^{2}\,\text{mol}^{-2}\,\text{s}^{-1} $$
(d) $$ R = 2.5\times(0.03)^{2}\times(0.04) = 2.5\times9\times10^{-4}\times0.04 = 9.0\times10^{-5}\ \text{M s}^{-1} $$
(e) Overall order = 2 + 1 = **3** (third order).
**Or:** (a) A **weak electrolyte** is ionised only partly in solution, with ions in equilibrium with unionised molecules.
(b) For BOH ⇌ B⁺ + OH⁻ with 1 mol in V litres and degree of ionisation α, the equilibrium amounts are (1 − α), α and α, and C = 1/V:
$$ K_{b} = \frac{(\alpha/V)(\alpha/V)}{(1-\alpha)/V} = \frac{C\alpha^{2}}{1-\alpha} $$
For a weak base α is small, so 1 − α ≈ 1, Kb = Cα² and α = √(Kb/C).
(c) α = √(1.8 × 10⁻⁵/0.05) = √(3.6 × 10⁻⁴) = **0.019** (1.9 %).

3. **Titration of oxalic acid against KMnO₄: equivalent weight, normality from burette readings, why dilute H₂SO₄.** *Why:* 2079, 2082 and 2083, each with different data. *Answer:* E(KMnO₄) = 158/5 = 31.6; N₁V₁ = N₂V₂ with the mean of concordant readings; dilute H₂SO₄ supplies H⁺ and avoids MnO₂; KMnO₄ is the titrant and its own indicator.
4. **A table of initial rates: order in each reactant, rate law, k with its unit, a missing rate.** *Why:* every paper; 2079, 2081, 2083 and model set B in full. *Answer:* compare two experiments with one variable constant; rate = k[A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ; k = rate/([A]ᵐ[B]ⁿ) from one experiment, unit mol^(1−n) L^(n−1) s⁻¹; substitute for the missing value.
5. **Derive Ostwald's dilution law and find α for a weak acid.** *Why:* 2079 model, 2083 and model set A (three derivations), plus the numerical in 2082. *Answer:* Ka = Cα²/(1 − α) ≈ Cα², so α = √(Ka/C); for 0.01 M HCN (Ka = 4.8 × 10⁻¹⁰), α = 2.2 × 10⁻⁴, pH 5.66.
6. **pH, pOH and Kw calculations; dilution of a base.** *Why:* 2078 model, 2079, 2081, 2082, model set A. *Answer:* pH + pOH = 14; [H⁺] = √(KaC) for a weak acid; a base diluted until it is below 10⁻⁷ M OH⁻ tends to pH 7 and never below; Kw rises with temperature.
7. **Precipitation test from Ksp.** *Why:* 2078 model, 2083 and model set B. *Answer:* halve the concentrations after mixing equal volumes; if [A⁺][B⁻] > Ksp the salt precipitates. Example: if the mixture holds 0.01 M Ba²⁺ and 10⁻⁴ M SO₄²⁻, the product is 10⁻⁶ > 10⁻¹⁰ and BaSO₄ precipitates.
8. **Hess's law: heat of formation from heats of combustion or formation.** *Why:* seven of eight papers — every one except model set B. *Answer:* write the formation equation, then add the given equations (reversing and multiplying): ΔH_f = Σ ΔH_c(reactants) − Σ ΔH_c(products) or the formation form; for acetylene from ΔH_c of C, H₂, C₂H₂ it is +226 kJ mol⁻¹.
9. **Cell notation, anode and cathode, cell reaction and emf from two E° values.** *Why:* every paper, in three forms: two E° values, a pair with the SHE, and a feasibility question. *Answer:* the lower E° is the anode; E°cell = E°cathode − E°anode; write M|Mⁿ⁺ ‖ Xᵐ⁺|X with the 1 M and 1 atm conditions.
10. **Extraction of copper from copper pyrite, with the blast-furnace reactions for the matte.** *Why:* 2079 model, 2081, 2083 and model set B, with a furnace drawing asked in 2081 and 2083. *Answer:* flotation → roasting (2CuFeS₂ + O₂ → Cu₂S + 2FeS + SO₂) → smelting (FeO + SiO₂ → FeSiO₃; Cu₂O + FeS → Cu₂S + FeO) → Bessemer (2Cu₂O + Cu₂S → 6Cu + SO₂) → poling and electrolytic refining.
11. **Chloroform: preparation from ethanol and the five reactions.** *Why:* 2079 model, 2081, 2082, 2083 and model set B. *Answer:* ethanol + Cl₂ (from bleaching powder) → ethanal → chloral → + Ca(OH)₂ → CHCl₃; air → COCl₂; Ag → HC≡CH; acetone/KOH → chloretone; conc. HNO₃ → CCl₃NO₂; aniline/KOH → C₆H₅NC.
12. **The iodoform chain: X + CH₃MgBr → Y → Z, all giving iodoform.** *Why:* 2079 model, 2082 and model set A, with the 2081 MCQ. *Answer:* X = ethanal, Y = propan-2-ol, Z = propanone; the CH₃CO– or CH₃CH(OH)– group is oxidised and iodinated to CHI₃; Z with dilute NaOH gives diacetone alcohol.
13. **Hoffmann's separation of amines and the nitrous acid test.** *Why:* 2079 model, 2079, 2082, 2083, model set B. *Answer:* diethyl oxalate makes the primary amine a solid oxamide, the secondary a liquid oxamic ester and leaves the tertiary unchanged; KOH hydrolysis recovers them. HNO₂: primary gives N₂, secondary a yellow nitrosoamine.
14. **One equation for each of five named reactions (Tollens', Fehling's, aldol, Cannizzaro, Reimer–Tiemann, Perkin, Williamson, 2,4-DNP).** *Why:* seven of eight papers carry such a list. *Answer:* write one equation with the reagent over the arrow, for example 2HCHO + NaOH → CH₃OH + HCOONa for Cannizzaro, and C₆H₅CHO + (CH₃CO)₂O → C₆H₅CH=CHCOOH for Perkin.
15. **A reaction chain from benzene through nitrobenzene, aniline, benzenediazonium chloride and phenol or an azo dye.** *Why:* 2078 model, 2081, 2082, 2083 and both model sets. *Answer:* HNO₃/H₂SO₄ → Sn/HCl → NaNO₂/HCl at 273 K → H₂O warm (phenol) or phenol/NaOH (p-hydroxyazobenzene).
16. **Portland cement: raw materials, flow sheet, components, role of gypsum, OPC against PPC, quality control.** *Why:* every paper has one. *Answer:* limestone + clay → grinding → rotary kiln 1400–1500 °C → clinker → + 2–5 % gypsum (retarder) → ball mill; PPC has 15–35 % pozzolana, lower early strength, better durability.

### Tier 2 — seen in some papers, or every year in a small form

17. **Preparation, properties and uses of corrosive sublimate.** *Why:* set in both 2081 and 2083 and in model set B — three of eight papers, and in each of the last two board sittings that asked about mercury. *Answer:* HgSO₄ + 2NaCl → HgCl₂↑ + Na₂SO₄; with KI a scarlet precipitate dissolving in excess; with SnCl₂ white then grey; with NH₃ white Hg(NH₂)Cl; antiseptic and Nessler's reagent.
18. **Zinc: extraction from blende, the vertical retort, white vitriol, lithopone, why zinc is non-typical.** *Why:* 2079 and 2082, each of them a full five-mark question. *Answer:* flotation → 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂ → ZnO + C → Zn + CO at 1673 K; ZnSO₄ + BaS → ZnS + BaSO₄ (lithopone); Zn²⁺ is 3d¹⁰.
19. **Steel by the open hearth or Basic Oxygen Process; the role of spiegeleisen.** *Why:* the open hearth process was set in 2079; the Basic Oxygen Process has never been asked, so it is the likelier alternative. *Answer:* BOP: oxygen lance, basic dolomite lining, lime flux, no fuel; OHP: Fe₂O₃ oxidises C, Si, P, Mn; spiegeleisen restores carbon and removes oxygen.
20. **Rusting: the electrochemical theory and two methods of prevention.** *Why:* 2082; corrosion has not been repeated since, so it is due. *Answer:* anode Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻; cathode O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O; 4Fe²⁺ + O₂ + 4H₂O → 2Fe₂O₃ + 8H⁺, then Fe₂O₃·xH₂O; paint, galvanising, sacrificial anode.
21. **The chain A → (SOCl₂) B → (NH₃) C → (Br₂/KOH) D → (HNO₂) E.** *Why:* 2082 and 2081 (Or), and the 2079 model, each through an acid derivative. *Answer:* propanoic acid → propanoyl chloride → propanamide → ethylamine → ethanol; the Hofmann step loses one carbon.
22. **Differentiate order from molecularity.** *Why:* 2079 and 2083; it is also the opening part of the kinetics question. *Answer:* order is experimental and may be zero or fractional; molecularity is the number of molecules in one elementary step and is a whole number 1, 2 or 3.
23. **First-order integrated equation, t½ = 0.693/k and the time for 90 % completion.** *Why:* 2078 model, 2082 (MCQ on t½) and model sets; a derivation is asked in about half the papers. *Answer:* k = (2.303/t) log[a/(a − x)]; t½ = 0.693/k; t(90 %) = 2.303/k = 3.32 t½.
24. **Gibbs–Helmholtz: spontaneity, the temperature at which a reaction turns spontaneous, ΔG and K.** *Why:* model set B, the 2078 model entropy question and the 2082 MCQ; only Hess's law dominates the board papers so far, which leaves ΔG under-tested. *Answer:* ΔG = ΔH − TΔS; T = ΔH/ΔS at ΔG = 0; ΔG° = −2.303RT log K.
25. **Brønsted–Lowry against Lewis acids and bases, with conjugate pairs.** *Why:* 2082 (Or) and the opening part of ionic-equilibrium questions; the syllabus lists both concepts. *Answer:* B–L acid donates H⁺, Lewis acid accepts an electron pair; NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ has pairs NH₃/NH₄⁺ and H₂O/OH⁻.
26. **Reduction of nitrobenzene in acidic, neutral, alkaline and electrolytic media.** *Why:* 2079 model, 2081, 2083 (electrolytic) and the MCQs of 2078 model and 2079 model. *Answer:* Sn/HCl aniline; Zn/NH₄Cl phenylhydroxylamine; Na₃AsO₃ azoxybenzene; Zn/NaOH azobenzene then hydrazobenzene; electrolytic conc. H₂SO₄ p-aminophenol.
27. **Ethanoic acid from CH₃MgBr, and its action with NH₃, SOCl₂, P₂O₅, LiAlH₄.** *Why:* 2083 and model set A. *Answer:* CH₃MgBr + CO₂ → CH₃COOMgBr → CH₃COOH; then CH₃CONH₂, CH₃COCl, (CH₃CO)₂O and CH₃CH₂OH respectively.
28. **Order of acidic strength of substituted acids; the HVZ reaction.** *Why:* 2079 model (2 marks) and a recurring Group A stem. *Answer:* FCH₂COOH > ClCH₂COOH > C₆H₅COOH > CH₃COOH; the −I effect stabilises the anion; CH₃COOH + Cl₂ → (red P) ClCH₂COOH.
29. **Addition against condensation polymers; monomers of nylon 6,6, polystyrene and Bakelite.** *Why:* six of eight papers (2078 model, 2079 model, 2079, 2081, 2082, model set A). *Answer:* addition: no loss of atoms (polythene); condensation: loss of H₂O or HCl (nylon 6,6 from H₂N(CH₂)₆NH₂ and HOOC(CH₂)₄COOH; Bakelite from phenol and methanal; polystyrene from styrene).
30. **Paper: raw materials, fibrous and non-fibrous, paper against pulp, the Kraft process.** *Why:* six of eight papers, always as a two-mark part. *Answer:* fibrous (wood, bamboo, rags, lokta) supply cellulose; non-fibrous (china clay, rosin, starch, alum) are additives; Kraft: white liquor NaOH + Na₂S at 170 °C dissolves lignin.
31. **Natural against artificial radioactivity, with an example of each.** *Why:* 2079 model, 2083 and model set A (the 2081 paper asked for nuclear against chemical reactions). *Answer:* natural is spontaneous in U, Th, Ra; artificial is induced by bombardment, ²⁷Al + ⁴He → ³⁰P + n, ³⁰P → ³⁰Si + β⁺.
32. **Nuclear fusion as a thermonuclear reaction; fission with its chain reaction; nuclear power.** *Why:* 2082 (fusion) and the applied question every year. *Answer:* fusion needs 10⁷–10⁸ K to overcome repulsion; ²H + ³H → ⁴He + n; fission ²³⁵U + n → ¹⁴¹Ba + ⁹²Kr + 3n, controlled by rods in a reactor.

### Tier 3 — the gaps, and the rest of the syllabus

33. **Crystal field theory: its assumptions and the octahedral d-orbital splitting diagram.** *Why:* set in the 2078 model and model set A but in **no board paper of 2079–2083**; five teaching hours; a gap. *Answer:* ligands are point charges; the d-orbitals split into e_g (+0.6 Δo) and t₂g (−0.4 Δo); [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is blue because the d–d transition absorbs red-orange.
34. **What is a d–d transition? List the characteristics of transition metals.** *Why:* the 2079 model; the characteristic properties are also the reasons behind several Group A items (catalysts, green ions). *Answer:* promotion of an electron between split d-orbitals by visible light; variable oxidation states, coloured ions, complexes, catalysis, paramagnetism.
35. **Silver: extraction from argentite by the cyanide process; silver nitrate and silver chloride.** *Why:* **never set** in Group B or C in the eight papers, despite a full syllabus bullet; a clear gap. *Answer:* Ag₂S + 4NaCN ⇌ 2Na[Ag(CN)₂] + Na₂S (air removes Na₂S); 2Na[Ag(CN)₂] + Zn → Na₂[Zn(CN)₄] + 2Ag; Moebius refining.
36. **Iron: extraction in the blast furnace, the zones and the slag.** *Why:* **never set** in the eight papers (steel and rusting have been set, the extraction has not). *Answer:* Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂; CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂; CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ slag; pig iron is tapped from the hearth.
37. **Buffer solutions: Henderson–Hasselbalch equation and pH of an acidic buffer.** *Why:* no five-mark question on buffers in the eight papers although it is a named syllabus bullet. *Answer:* pH = pKa + log([salt]/[acid]); 0.2 M CH₃COOH + 0.1 M CH₃COONa (pKa 4.74) has pH 4.44.
38. **Chlorobenzene: low reactivity towards nucleophiles, DDT, Sandmeyer route.** *Why:* 2078 model, 2082, 2083 (the DDT part); a recurring 2-mark part. *Answer:* resonance and sp² carbon give the C–Cl bond partial double-bond character; 2C₆H₅Cl + CCl₃CHO → DDT; C₆H₅N₂Cl + CuCl → C₆H₅Cl.
39. **Ethers: Williamson's synthesis, the action of HI and the danger of peroxides.** *Why:* 2079, 2081, 2082 and 2083 (as list items). *Answer:* RONa + R′X → ROR′ + NaX (primary halide); C₂H₅OC₂H₅ + 2HI → 2C₂H₅I + H₂O; peroxides form in air, so keep over iron wire.
40. **Phenol: acidity against alcohol and water; Kolbe and Reimer–Tiemann reactions; tests.** *Why:* model set A, 2082 and 2083 (as list items); four teaching hours. *Answer:* phenoxide is resonance-stabilised, so phenol is more acidic than ethanol but weaker than carbonic acid; FeCl₃ gives violet; Br₂ water gives white 2,4,6-tribromophenol.
41. **Grignard reagent with aldehydes and ketones: preparation of 1°, 2° and 3° alcohols.** *Why:* the 2081 MCQ, 2079 model and 2082 chains depend on it. *Answer:* HCHO gives a primary, another aldehyde a secondary and a ketone a tertiary alcohol, each after hydrolysis.
42. **Fermentation of ethanol; absolute, denatured and power alcohol.** *Why:* 2079 (full question); it has not recurred. *Answer:* C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ → (invertase) 2C₆H₁₂O₆ → (zymase) 4C₂H₅OH + 4CO₂ overall (with one H₂O taken in); denatured alcohol contains methanol or pyridine.
43. **Basic strength of aniline against ammonia and ethylamine; coupling reaction.** *Why:* 2079 model, 2083 (MCQ) and chains in 2082, 2078 model. *Answer:* ethylamine > ammonia > aniline; in aniline the lone pair is delocalised; C₆H₅N₂Cl + phenol → p-hydroxyazobenzene.
44. **Structural formulae of aspirin and DDT; antipyretic against analgesic; habit-forming drugs.** *Why:* 2083 and model set B; the drug part of the service-of-mankind unit has come up twice in three sittings. *Answer:* aspirin is 2-acetoxybenzoic acid; paracetamol is an antipyretic, aspirin an analgesic; morphine is habit-forming.
45. **Radiocarbon dating: age of a sample from its ¹⁴C activity.** *Why:* the unit-21 syllabus names it, and numerical parts are rare in Q22, so it is unseen. *Answer:* N/N₀ = (½)^(t/5730); for 25 % activity, t = 11 460 years.
46. **The standard hydrogen electrode (diagram); the lead storage battery and the H₂–O₂ fuel cell.** *Why:* 2083 (reference electrode), model set B (diagram), and the 2078 model and 2083 MCQs on batteries and fuel cells. *Answer:* Pt foil in 1 M H⁺ with H₂ at 1 atm, E = 0.00 V; Pb + PbO₂ + 2H₂SO₄ → 2PbSO₄ + 2H₂O; 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O.
47. **Indicators and the choice of indicator in acid–base titrations.** *Why:* model sets A and B both open with it as an MCQ. *Answer:* strong acid–strong base: any; strong acid–weak base (Na₂CO₃): methyl orange; weak acid–strong base: phenolphthalein; weak–weak: no sharp end point.
48. **Activation energy, the effect of a catalyst and temperature (Arrhenius equation), energy-profile diagram.** *Why:* the 2079 model MCQ on the effect of temperature and the 2079 MCQ on catalysts; a full question on catalysis has not been set. *Answer:* a catalyst lowers Ea without changing ΔH; k = Ae^(−Ea/RT); the rate roughly doubles for each 10 °C rise.

## Chapter summary

- **The grid never changes:** 11 MCQs, 8 five-mark and 3 eight-mark questions, all compulsory, with internal alternatives in about two Group B and two Group C questions.
- **The paper has a shape.** In 2081, 2082 and 2083 Group A ran four physical, two inorganic, five organic questions; Group C ran one physical question (Q20), one organic chain (Q21) and an applied question of four parts (Q22).
- **Marks follow teaching hours.** Organic chemistry is about 44 % of the marks and 43 % of the hours, physical about 29 % and 31 %, inorganic 16 % and 16 %. Revise in proportion.
- **Twelve units have been set in seven or eight of the eight papers** — volumetric analysis, ionic equilibrium, kinetics, thermodynamics, electrochemistry, heavy metals, haloalkanes, alcohols, aldehydes and ketones, amines, service of mankind and cement — and these are the Tier 1 and Tier 2 questions above.
- **The gaps are real.** Crystal field theory, silver, blast-furnace iron, buffers and radiocarbon dating have not been set as five-mark questions in the board papers studied; they are Tier 3 because they are due, not because they are certain.
- **Two complete model papers** are solved in full; sit each in three hours, mark yourself by the steps in the solution, and then return to the unit-wise chapters for the topics where you lost marks.
