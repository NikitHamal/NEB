This chapter does three things. It **reads the real papers** — the NEB Grade 12 Mathematics papers of 2079, 2080, 2081 and 2082 and the CDC model question of 2080, all transcribed from the printed scans — and tabulates what the examiner asks, unit by unit. It then sets **two complete model papers** to the exact grid of the real paper ($11\times1+8\times5+3\times8=75$ marks, three hours), each with full solutions. Finally it lists **forty-four predicted questions**, ranked, with the reason for each rank and a short model answer.

::: caution What "predicted" means here
No paper is leaked to anyone, and this chapter does not claim to know what will be set. A prediction is a judgement from three things shown in the tables below: how often a topic appeared in the five papers read, how many marks its area carries in the CDC grid, and how long it has been since it last appeared. Five papers are a small sample. Use the list to decide **what to practise first**, never what to skip. Model papers and predicted questions are original, not reproductions, except where a year tag says that a question was read in that paper.
:::

::: key How to sit a three-hour paper
Seventy-five marks in $180$ minutes is $2.4$ minutes a mark. Plan it: **Group A, $15$ minutes** ($11$ marks, about $1.4$ min each); **Group B, $85$ minutes** ($40$ marks, about $10$ min per five-mark question); **Group C, $65$ minutes** ($24$ marks, about $22$ min per eight-mark question); $15$ minutes left to re-check. Since 2082 the eleven MCQs reach you as a separate booklet **$30$ minutes after the start** — begin with Group B, and answer Group A when the booklet arrives. Show the formula, every substitution and the units: a correct answer without working earns little in Group B or C.
:::

## 13.1 Pattern analysis of the Grade 12 Mathematics papers

### 13.1.1 The grid, and what has been fixed for years

| Group | Questions | Marks | What the examiner does |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 11 MCQs (Q1–Q11) | $11\times1=11$ | one mark each; **Q11 carries an "Or"** |
| B | Q12–Q19 | $8\times5=40$ | five marks split $1+4$, $2+3$ or five one-mark parts; **Q19 carries an "Or"** |
| C | Q20–Q22 | $3\times8=24$ | almost always three parts $3+3+2$, $2+3+3$ or $4+2+2$; **no "Or"** |

The same grid sits in all five papers. The CDC Specification Grid 2078 fixes the area-wise marks and the number of questions of each type:

| Area | Area-wise marks | MCQ | Group B | Group C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Algebra (Units 1–5) | 20 | 2 | 2 | 1 |
| Trigonometry + Analytic Geometry + Vector (6–9) | $6+9+6=21$ | 3 | 2 | 1 |
| Statistics and Probability (10–11) | 6 | 1 | 1 | — |
| Calculus (12–14) | 22 | 4 | 2 | 1 |
| Computational Methods **or** Mechanics (15 or 16) | 6 | 1 | 1 | — |
| **Total** | **75** | **11** | **8** | **3** |

The first column of figures printed beside each area in the syllabus ($44,12,20,12,12,48,12$; total $160$) is **working hours, not marks**. The CDC publishes only this 2078 grid for Grade 12 Mathematics; no revised grid exists for the 2080-revised curriculum, and the 2081 and 2082 papers still follow it exactly. The grid's own footnote says: "At least one multiple choice question should be asked from each area (Trigonometry, Analytic geometry and Vector)."

### 13.1.2 Where the questions sit

The papers of 2081, 2082 and the CDC model place every question in an order that follows the grid, and that order is worth knowing before you open the paper.

| Slot | What was set |
|---|---|
| **Group A**, Q1–Q2 | two **algebra** items (counting, complex numbers, Cramer's rule, sum of series) |
| Group A, Q3, Q4, Q5 | one item each from **trigonometry** (half-angle formula), **conics** (parabola, tangency, eccentricity) and **vectors** (cross product, smallest value) |
| Group A, Q6 | **probability**: $P(B/A)$ from three given numbers |
| Group A, Q7–Q10 | four **calculus** items: a derivative or L'Hospital limit, an integral, a differential-equation type or degree, a tangent slope |
| Group A, Q11 | **Computational Methods** ("eliminate $x$ or $y$") **Or Mechanics** (maximum height, momentum) |
| **Group B**, Q12–Q13 | **algebra**: Q12 is often five one-mark recall parts; Q13 is counting, a committee, a complex root or a system of equations |
| Group B, Q14–Q15 | **trigonometry + vectors + conics**: a half-angle proof and a dot product; a conic's eccentricity and a tangency condition |
| Group B, Q16 | **statistics**: a table of six or seven pairs, correlation and a regression estimate |
| Group B, Q17–Q18 | **calculus**: five one-mark recall parts, derivative, integral, tangent |
| Group B, Q19 | **simplex LPP Or Mechanics** |
| **Group C**, Q20 | **algebra**: binomial or complex number + induction + a matrix system |
| Group C, Q21 | **trigonometry + conics + vectors** in three parts |
| Group C, Q22 | **calculus**: rate of change + an integral + a differential equation |

The **2079 and 2080** papers differ in two ways. The simplex question was compulsory (Q18), and the Q11/Q19 "Or" paired Mechanics against an economics input–output problem. Those papers were written by the old-course cohorts (registration numbers 78 and 79); NEB's notice for the 2082 examinations records that the Mathematics curriculum was **revised in 2080 BS** and that the "New Course" paper goes to registration numbers beginning 80 and 81. That explains why **Group Theory, Rolle/Mean Value Theorem, inverse circular functions, trigonometric equations and three-dimensional planes** appear in 2079 and 2080 and **do not** appear in 2081, 2082 or the CDC model.

Three things stand out. **Nothing in Algebra or Calculus is ignored:** Units 2, 4, 5, 9, 10, 12, 13, 14 and 15 were each set as a Group B or C question in **all five** papers (Unit 3 in four, with a one-mark part in 2079). **Probability never had a subjective question** in the papers read; its marks are the single MCQ. And **Unit 7** was asked once, so Solution of Triangles is best learned as an extension of the half-angle and cosine-law work of Unit 6.

### 13.1.4 The numericals and derivations that recur

| Type | Forms seen | Papers |
|---|---|---|
| Simplex LPP | two variables, two constraints, maximise | 2079, 2080, 2081, 2082, CDC |
| Correlation/regression from a small table | $5$–$7$ pairs; estimate $y$ from $x$ or $x$ from $y$ | 2080, 2081, 2082, CDC (2079 from given lines) |
| Mathematical induction | identity or inequality | all five |
| Three-equation system | Cramer, row-equivalent, inverse | all five |
| Conditional probability MCQ | $P(B/A)=P(A\cap B)/P(A)$ | 2080, 2081, 2082, CDC |
| "Eliminate $x$ / $y$" MCQ | two simultaneous equations at Q10 or Q11 | all five |
| De Moivre | roots, polar form | 2080, 2081, 2082, CDC |
| Integrating factor | linear first-order ODE | 2079, 2081, 2082 |
| Inverse-hyperbolic derivative | $\cosh^{-1}$, $\text{sech}^{-1}$, $\coth^{-1}$ | 2080, 2081, 2082, CDC |

**Proofs set more than once:** the half-angle identity in a triangle (2081 twice, CDC); the sine law by vectors (2079) and the projection or cosine law; the like-parallel-forces theorem (2080, CDC); $n!$ versus $2^{n}$ by induction (2080, 2081).

**Printed items that could not be taken at face value** (they are discussed in Chapter 12 and should be recognised if a similar one appears): the 2081 induction item $n!<2^{n}$ (true only for $n\leq3$); the 2081 triangle with $a=3+\sqrt{3}$, $b=2$, $c=\sqrt{3}$ (violates the triangle inequality); the 2081 tangent "at $(2,5)$" to a curve passing through $(2,4)$; the 2080 "dependent" events that are in fact independent.

**How the marks are really won.** Group A is decided by fluency in one-line results. In Group B and C, the five-mark questions with **five one-mark parts** are the cheapest marks in the paper — they are pure recall (2079 Q12, 2080 Q12 and Q16, 2081 Q12 and Q17, 2082 Q12 and Q17). Learn the formula sheets of Chapters 11 and 12 and you start with about $10$ of those $40$ marks.

**13.** (a) All committees: $C(10,4)=210$. Committees with no woman, four men: $C(6,4)=15$.
$$ 210-15=195 $$
(b) $r=\sqrt{1+1}=\sqrt{2}$, $\tan\theta=-1$ with the point $(-1,1)$ in the **second quadrant**, so $\theta=\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$:
$$ z=\sqrt{2}\Big(\cos\frac{3\pi}{4}+i\sin\frac{3\pi}{4}\Big) $$
$$ z^{8}=(\sqrt{2})^{8}\Big(\cos6\pi+i\sin6\pi\Big)=16(1+0)=16 $$
**Check:** $(-1+i)^{2}=-2i$, so $(-1+i)^{8}=(-2i)^{4}=16$. ✓

**14.** (a) By the sine law, $a=k\sin A$, $b=k\sin B$, $c=k\sin C$. Then
$$ a^{2}-b^{2}=k^{2}(\sin^{2}A-\sin^{2}B)=k^{2}(\sin A-\sin B)(\sin A+\sin B) $$
Using $\sin A-\sin B=2\cos\frac{A+B}{2}\sin\frac{A-B}{2}$ and $\sin A+\sin B=2\sin\frac{A+B}{2}\cos\frac{A-B}{2}$, the product is $\sin(A+B)\sin(A-B)$. Since $A+B=180^{\circ}-C$, $\sin(A+B)=\sin C$:
$$ \frac{a^{2}-b^{2}}{c^{2}}=\frac{k^{2}\sin C\sin(A-B)}{k^{2}\sin^{2}C}=\frac{\sin(A-B)}{\sin C} $$ ∎
(b) $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}=2(1)+3(-2)+(-1)(2)=-6$ and $|\vec{b}|=\sqrt{1+4+4}=3$:
$$ \text{projection of }\vec{a}\text{ on }\vec{b}=\frac{\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}}{|\vec{b}|}=\frac{-6}{3}=-2 $$
The negative sign means the projection points opposite to $\vec{b}$.

**15.** (a) Focus $(a,0)=(3,0)$ gives $a=3$, and the directrix $x=-3$ agrees. The parabola opens to the right:

3. **(Unit 4, induction inequality)** Prove by induction that $2^{n}>n^{2}$ for all $n\geq5$. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
   *Why:* inequalities were set in 2080 ($n!\geq2^{n}$) and 2081 ($n!<2^{n}$); **test small $n$ first**.
   *Answer:* $n=5$: $32>25$. Assume $2^{k}>k^{2}$ with $k\geq5$. Then $2^{k+1}>2k^{2}=k^{2}+k^{2}\geq k^{2}+2k+1=(k+1)^{2}$, because $k^{2}\geq2k+1$ for $k\geq3$. ∎ (The base case is $5$, not $1$: $2^{3}<3^{2}$ and $2^{4}=4^{2}$.)
4. **(Unit 10, regression)** The table gives $x$: $10,12,14,16,18$; $y$: $15,17,22,24,27$. Find $r$, the regression line of $y$ on $x$, and estimate $y$ at $x=20$. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
   *Why:* correlation and regression from a table in **5 of 5** papers.
   *Answer:* $\bar{x}=14$, $\bar{y}=21$; $\sum dx\,dy=62$, $\sum dx^{2}=40$, $\sum dy^{2}=98$. $r=\dfrac{62}{\sqrt{40\times98}}=0.990$; $b_{yx}=\dfrac{62}{40}=1.55$; $y-21=1.55(x-14)$; at $x=20$, $y=21+1.55\times6=30.3$.
5. **(Unit 5, system of equations)** Solve $3x+y-z=4$, $x-2y+2z=6$, $2x+3y+z=10$ by Cramer's rule. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
   *Why:* a three-equation system in **5 of 5** papers.
   *Answer:* $D=-28$, $D_x=-56$, $D_y=-28$, $D_z=-84$; so $x=2$, $y=1$, $z=3$. **Check:** $6+1-3=4$ ✓; $2-2+6=6$ ✓; $4+3+3=10$ ✓.
6. **(Unit 3, De Moivre roots)** Find the fourth roots of $-16$. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
   *Why:* De Moivre roots in **4 of 5** papers (2080, 2081, 2082, CDC).
   *Answer:* $-16=16(\cos\pi+i\sin\pi)$, so the roots are $2\Big[\cos\dfrac{\pi+2k\pi}{4}+i\sin\dfrac{\pi+2k\pi}{4}\Big]$, $k=0,1,2,3$: $\sqrt{2}(1+i)$, $\sqrt{2}(-1+i)$, $\sqrt{2}(-1-i)$, $\sqrt{2}(1-i)$.
7. **(Unit 11, conditional probability)** If $P(A)=0.5$, $P(B)=0.4$ and $P(A\cup B)=0.7$, find $P(A/B)$. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   *Why:* an MCQ on $P(B/A)$ or $P(A/B)$ in **4 of 5** papers (2080, 2081, 2082, CDC).
   *Answer:* $P(A\cap B)=0.5+0.4-0.7=0.2$; $P(A/B)=\dfrac{0.2}{0.4}=0.5$.
8. **(Unit 13, partial fractions)** Integrate $\displaystyle\int\frac{x+1}{x(x-2)(x+3)}\,dx$. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
   *Why:* partial fractions were set in **3 of 4** verified years (2079, 2081, 2082).
   *Answer:* $\dfrac{x+1}{x(x-2)(x+3)}=-\dfrac{1}{6x}+\dfrac{3}{10(x-2)}-\dfrac{2}{15(x+3)}$; integral $=-\dfrac16\ln|x|+\dfrac{3}{10}\ln|x-2|-\dfrac{2}{15}\ln|x+3|+C$.
9. **(Unit 12, inverse-hyperbolic derivative)** Find $\dfrac{d}{dx}\tanh^{-1}(\sin x)$ and $\dfrac{d}{dx}\text{sech}^{-1}(x^{2})$. <span class="marks">[2+2]</span>
   *Why:* an inverse-hyperbolic derivative in **4 of 5** papers (2080, 2081, 2082, CDC).
   *Answer:* $\dfrac{\cos x}{1-\sin^{2}x}=\sec x$; and $-\dfrac{2x}{x^{2}\sqrt{1-x^{4}}}=-\dfrac{2}{x\sqrt{1-x^{4}}}$ for $0<x<1$.
10. **(Unit 12, L'Hospital)** Evaluate $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to0}\frac{x\cos x-\sin x}{x^{3}}$. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* a limit by L'Hospital in 2080 and 2081 (and an MCQ in 2081, CDC).
    *Answer:* form $\dfrac00$: $\dfrac{-x\sin x}{3x^{2}}=-\dfrac{\sin x}{3x}\to-\dfrac13$.
11. **(Unit 12, rate of change)** Air is pumped into a spherical balloon at $100\ \text{cm}^{3}\text{s}^{-1}$. How fast is the radius increasing when it is $5$ cm? <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* a rate question in 2081, 2082 and the CDC model.
    *Answer:* $\dfrac{dV}{dt}=4\pi r^{2}\dfrac{dr}{dt}$, so $\dfrac{dr}{dt}=\dfrac{100}{4\pi(25)}=\dfrac{1}{\pi}=0.318\ \text{cm s}^{-1}$.
12. **(Unit 14, linear ODE)** Solve $\dfrac{dy}{dx}+y\tan x=\sec x$. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* an integrating-factor question in 2079, 2081 and 2082.
    *Answer:* I.F. $=e^{\int\tan x\,dx}=\sec x$; $y\sec x=\int\sec^{2}x\,dx=\tan x+C$; $y=\sin x+C\cos x$.
13. **(Unit 8, ellipse)** For the ellipse $4x^{2}+9y^{2}=36$ find the eccentricity, the foci, the latus rectum and the directrices. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
    *Why:* an eccentricity/foci question in **4 of 5** papers.
    *Answer:* $\dfrac{x^{2}}{9}+\dfrac{y^{2}}{4}=1$; $a=3$, $b=2$, $c=\sqrt{5}$; $e=\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{3}$; foci $(\pm\sqrt{5},0)$; latus rectum $\dfrac{2b^{2}}{a}=\dfrac83$; directrices $x=\pm\dfrac{a}{e}=\pm\dfrac{9}{\sqrt{5}}$.
14. **(Unit 9, area by cross product)** Find the area of the triangle with vertices $A(1,2,3)$, $B(2,4,5)$, $C(0,1,2)$. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* an area by the vector product in 2081 and in the CDC model.
    *Answer:* $\vec{AB}=(1,2,2)$, $\vec{AC}=(-1,-1,-1)$; $\vec{AB}\times\vec{AC}=(0,-1,1)$; area $=\dfrac12\sqrt{0+1+1}=\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\approx0.707$.
15. **(Unit 9, dot product and angle)** Find the angle between $\vec{a}=\vec{i}+2\vec{j}+3\vec{k}$ and $\vec{b}=3\vec{i}+2\vec{j}+\vec{k}$. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
    *Why:* a dot-product item in **4 of 5** papers (2080, 2081, 2082 and MCQs).
    *Answer:* $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}=3+4+3=10$, $|\vec{a}|=|\vec{b}|=\sqrt{14}$; $\cos\theta=\dfrac{10}{14}=\dfrac57$; $\theta=44.4^{\circ}$.
16. **(Unit 6, half-angle proof)** If $a$, $b$, $c$ are in A.P., prove that $\cot\dfrac A2$, $\cot\dfrac B2$, $\cot\dfrac C2$ are in A.P. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* half-angle proofs in 2081 (twice), 2082 and the CDC model.
    *Answer:* $\cot\dfrac A2=\dfrac{s(s-a)}{\Delta}$ and similarly for $B$, $C$. If $a,b,c$ are in A.P., so are $s-a$, $s-b$, $s-c$; multiplying each by the constant $\dfrac{s}{\Delta}$ keeps them in A.P. ∎

### 13.4.2 Tier B — asked in two or three of the papers read

17. **(Unit 2, middle terms)** Find the middle term(s) of $(x+2y)^{7}$. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
    *Why:* a middle-term question in 2079 and 2082; $n$ **odd** gives two terms.
    *Answer:* $T_{4}=C(7,3)x^{4}(2y)^{3}=280x^{4}y^{3}$ and $T_{5}=C(7,4)x^{3}(2y)^{4}=560x^{3}y^{4}$.
18. **(Unit 1, committee)** A committee of $5$ is chosen from $7$ men and $4$ women. In how many ways can it contain exactly $2$ women? At least $2$ women? <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* committee items in 2082 and the CDC model; last counted in 2082.
    *Answer:* exactly $2$: $C(4,2)C(7,3)=6\times35=210$. At least $2$: $C(11,5)-C(7,5)-C(4,1)C(7,4)=462-21-140=301$.
19. **(Unit 2, approximation)** Expand $(1+x)^{-1/2}$ to three terms and estimate $(1.04)^{-1/2}$. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* "approximation using binomial expansion" is a named syllabus bullet that has not yet been set as a full question; an easy three marks.
    *Answer:* $1-\dfrac x2+\dfrac{3x^{2}}{8}$; with $x=0.04$: $1-0.02+0.0006=0.9806$ (exact $0.98058$).
20. **(Unit 9, vector proof)** Prove the cosine law $a^{2}=b^{2}+c^{2}-2bc\cos A$ by the vector method. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* vector proofs of a triangle law in 2079 (sine law) and 2082.
    *Answer:* with $\vec{BC}=\vec{AC}-\vec{AB}$, $|\vec{BC}|^{2}=(\vec{AC}-\vec{AB})\cdot(\vec{AC}-\vec{AB})=b^{2}+c^{2}-2\vec{AB}\cdot\vec{AC}=b^{2}+c^{2}-2bc\cos A$. ∎
21. **(Unit 16, Lami)** A lamp of weight $50$ N hangs from two strings inclined at $30^{\circ}$ and $45^{\circ}$ to the horizontal. Find the tensions. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
    *Why:* an equilibrium-of-strings question in 2081 and the CDC-style mechanics.
    *Answer:* the angles between the tensions and the weight are $120^{\circ}$ and $135^{\circ}$, and between the tensions $105^{\circ}$. Lami: $\dfrac{T_1}{\sin135^{\circ}}=\dfrac{T_2}{\sin120^{\circ}}=\dfrac{50}{\sin105^{\circ}}$, so $T_{1}=36.6$ N (string at $30^{\circ}$) and $T_{2}=44.8$ N (string at $45^{\circ}$).
22. **(Unit 16, like parallel forces)** The resultant $20$ N of two like parallel forces $P$ and $Q$ acts at $C$ on $AB$ with $AC=4$ cm and $CB=6$ cm. Find $P$ and $Q$. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* like parallel forces in 2080 and the CDC model.
    *Answer:* $P\cdot AC=Q\cdot CB$, so $4P=6Q$; $P+Q=20$; $P=12$ N at $A$, $Q=8$ N at $B$.
23. **(Unit 16, projectile)** A projectile is fired at $100\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ at $30^{\circ}$ ($g=10$). Find the time of flight, greatest height and range. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
    *Why:* projectile numericals in 2079 and as the Group A "Or" item in 2081 and 2082.
    *Answer:* $T=\dfrac{2u\sin\theta}{g}=10$ s; $H=\dfrac{u^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{2g}=125$ m; $R=\dfrac{u^{2}\sin2\theta}{g}=866$ m.
24. **(Unit 15, Gauss–Seidel)** Solve $5x+y+z=7$, $x+5y+z=7$, $x+y+5z=7$ by Gauss–Seidel from $(0,0,0)$, three iterations. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
    *Why:* in the CDC model (Q19a); the method is on the syllabus beside the simplex.
    *Answer:* the matrix is diagonally dominant. Iteration 1: $(1.4,\ 1.12,\ 0.896)$; 2: $(0.9968,\ 1.0214,\ 0.9964)$; 3: $(0.9964,\ 1.0014,\ 1.0004)$, approaching $(1,1,1)$.
25. **(Unit 13, $\int dx/(a+b\cos x)$)** Evaluate $\displaystyle\int\frac{dx}{5+4\cos x}$. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* this integral family was set in 2081 and 2082.
    *Answer:* $a=5>b=4$: $\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{25-16}}\tan^{-1}\Big(\sqrt{\dfrac{5-4}{5+4}}\tan\dfrac x2\Big)=\dfrac23\tan^{-1}\Big(\dfrac13\tan\dfrac x2\Big)+C$.
26. **(Unit 14, homogeneous ODE)** Solve $(x^{2}-y^{2})\,dx+2xy\,dy=0$. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* "is it homogeneous?" MCQs in 2081 and 2082 and a solve-it item in the CDC model.
    *Answer:* $y=vx$: $1-v^{2}+2v\big(v+x\frac{dv}{dx}\big)=0$, so $\dfrac{2v\,dv}{1+v^{2}}=-\dfrac{dx}{x}$; $\ln(1+v^{2})=-\ln x+c$; $x(1+v^{2})=C$, that is $x^{2}+y^{2}=Cx$.
27. **(Unit 14, exact ODE)** Solve $(2x+y)\,dx+(x+2y)\,dy=0$. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* an exact equation was the subject of 2081 Q22c.
    *Answer:* $\dfrac{\partial M}{\partial y}=1=\dfrac{\partial N}{\partial x}$, exact; $x^{2}+xy+y^{2}=C$.
28. **(Unit 7, solve a triangle)** Solve the triangle with $b=8$, $c=5$, $A=60^{\circ}$. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
    *Why:* solving a triangle in 2081 Q21a (and as a Group A theme in the CDC model).
    *Answer:* $a^{2}=64+25-2(8)(5)\cos60^{\circ}=49$, $a=7$; $\cos B=\dfrac{a^{2}+c^{2}-b^{2}}{2ac}=\dfrac17$, $B=81.79^{\circ}$; $C=38.21^{\circ}$.
29. **(Unit 8, tangency)** Find the condition for the line $lx+my+n=0$ to touch the circle $x^{2}+y^{2}=a^{2}$. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* tangency conditions in 2081 Q15b, 2082 Q4 and Q21c.
    *Answer:* the distance from the centre to the line equals the radius: $\dfrac{|n|}{\sqrt{l^{2}+m^{2}}}=a$, so $n^{2}=a^{2}(l^{2}+m^{2})$.
30. **(Unit 8, ellipse from data)** Find the equation of the ellipse with vertices $(\pm5,0)$ passing through $\big(3,\tfrac{12}{5}\big)$, and its eccentricity. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* an equation-from-data question in 2081 (hyperbola) and the CDC model (ellipse).
    *Answer:* $a=5$; $\dfrac{9}{25}+\dfrac{144/25}{b^{2}}=1$ gives $b^{2}=9$; $\dfrac{x^{2}}{25}+\dfrac{y^{2}}{9}=1$; $e=\dfrac{4}{5}$.
31. **(Unit 10, rank correlation)** Ranks of five students in two tests are $(1,3),(2,1),(3,2),(4,5),(5,4)$. Find Spearman's coefficient. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* rank correlation is on the syllabus and never yet set; a cheap three marks.
    *Answer:* $d=-2,1,1,-1,1$; $\sum d^{2}=8$; $r_{s}=1-\dfrac{6\times8}{5\times24}=0.6$.
32. **(Unit 11, draws)** Two cards are drawn without replacement from $52$. Find $P$(both hearts), and $P$(a leap year has $53$ Sundays). <span class="marks">[2+1]</span>
    *Why:* conditional-probability MCQs every year; the leap-year idea in the CDC model.
    *Answer:* $\dfrac{13}{52}\times\dfrac{12}{51}=\dfrac{1}{17}$; a leap year has $2$ extra days, and $2$ of the $7$ pairs contain Sunday, so $\dfrac27$.

### 13.4.3 Tier C — not yet asked as a full question, or asked only once

33. **(Unit 3, Euler)** State Euler's formula and deduce $e^{i\pi}+1=0$ and $\cos\theta=\dfrac{e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta}}{2}$. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* on the syllabus; no question on it in the five papers — a gap that cannot last for ever.
    *Answer:* $e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$; at $\theta=\pi$, $e^{i\pi}=-1$. Adding $e^{i\theta}$ and $e^{-i\theta}=\cos\theta-i\sin\theta$ gives $2\cos\theta$.
34. **(Unit 3, cube roots of unity)** If $\omega$ is a complex cube root of unity, show that $(1+\omega-\omega^{2})^{3}=-8$. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* cube roots of unity appear as an MCQ (2079) but not as a proof.
    *Answer:* $1+\omega=-\omega^{2}$, so $1+\omega-\omega^{2}=-2\omega^{2}$; $(-2\omega^{2})^{3}=-8\omega^{6}=-8$.
35. **(Unit 1, circular)** In how many ways can $5$ men and $5$ women sit round a table so that they sit alternately? <span class="marks">[2]</span>
    *Why:* circular permutations are on the syllabus and were not set in the five papers.
    *Answer:* fix the men round the table in $(5-1)!=24$ ways; the women go into the $5$ gaps in $5!=120$ ways: $24\times120=2880$.
36. **(Unit 8, tangent length)** Find the length of the tangent from $(5,4)$ to the circle $x^{2}+y^{2}=16$. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
    *Why:* a tangent-length MCQ in the CDC model (Q4).
    *Answer:* $\sqrt{x_1^{2}+y_1^{2}-a^{2}}=\sqrt{25+16-16}=5$.
37. **(Unit 8, parabola)** Find the equation of the parabola with vertex at the origin, axis along the $y$-axis, passing through $(4,-2)$. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
    *Why:* a parabola item in 2081 (the reflector); this is its companion.
    *Answer:* $x^{2}=4ay$ with $16=4a(-2)$, $a=-2$: $x^{2}=-8y$.
38. **(Unit 8, normal)** Find the equation of the normal to $y^{2}=4ax$ at the point $(at^{2},2at)$. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* a normal condition was the 2082 Group C part.
    *Answer:* slope of the tangent $=\dfrac{2a}{y}=\dfrac1t$; the normal has slope $-t$: $y-2at=-t(x-at^{2})$, that is $y+tx=2at+at^{3}$.
39. **(Unit 17, group)** Show that the set $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$ under addition modulo $5$ is an abelian group. <span class="marks">[5]</span>
    *Why:* group theory in 2079 and 2080 only (the old course); a reserve topic.
    *Answer:* closed (the remainder on division by $5$ is in the set); associative; identity $0$; inverses $0\to0$, $1\to4$, $2\to3$, $3\to2$, $4\to1$; commutative since $a+b=b+a$.
40. **(Unit 17, Rolle)** Verify Rolle's theorem for $f(x)=\sin x$ on $[0,\pi]$. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* Rolle/MVT in 2080 only; absent since.
    *Answer:* continuous and differentiable; $f(0)=f(\pi)=0$; $f'(x)=\cos x=0$ at $c=\dfrac{\pi}{2}\in(0,\pi)$.
41. **(Unit 17, Mean Value Theorem)** Find $c$ of the Mean Value Theorem for $f(x)=x^{3}$ on $[0,2]$. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* as above.
    *Answer:* $3c^{2}=\dfrac{8-0}{2}=4$, $c=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}=1.155\in(0,2)$.
42. **(Unit 17, Simpson)** Estimate $\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}x^{2}\,dx$ by Simpson's rule with $4$ strips. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* numerical integration is in the retained list, unasked.
    *Answer:* $h=0.5$; $S=\dfrac{0.5}{3}\big[0+4+4(0.25+2.25)+2(1)\big]=\dfrac{16}{6}=2.667$, which equals the exact $\dfrac83$ (Simpson is exact for quadratics).
43. **(Unit 12, percentage error)** The radius of a sphere is measured as $7$ cm with an error of $0.02$ cm. Find the percentage error in its volume by differentials. <span class="marks">[3]</span>
    *Why:* differentials appear only as a one-mark recall part (2081 Q17c); a full question is overdue.
    *Answer:* $\dfrac{dV}{V}=3\dfrac{dr}{r}=3\times\dfrac{0.02}{7}=0.00857$, that is $0.857\%$.
44. **(Unit 12, differentials)** Use differentials to find an approximate value of $\sqrt{26}$. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
    *Why:* as above.
    *Answer:* $y=\sqrt{x}$, $x=25$, $dx=1$: $dy=\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{25}}\times1=0.1$, so $\sqrt{26}\approx5.1$ (exact $5.0990$).
