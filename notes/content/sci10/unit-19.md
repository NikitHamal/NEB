## 19.1 Food preservatives and artificial fruit ripening agents

### What are food preservatives?

::: definition Food preservatives
**Food preservatives** are natural or synthetic substances added to food items to prevent or slow down spoilage, decay and loss of nutritional value caused by microorganisms (bacteria, fungi, moulds and yeasts) or oxidation, thereby extending their shelf life.
:::

**Why food preservatives are used (Advantages — SEE 2080 Gandaki/Sudurpaschim):**
1. They inhibit the growth of bacteria, moulds and yeast, keeping food safe for a long period.
2. They preserve the colour, flavour, texture and nutritional quality of food and reduce food wastage and cost.
3. They allow seasonal foods, fruits and vegetables to be stored and transported to distant markets.

### Classification of Food Preservatives

1. **Natural (Organic / First-grade) food preservatives:**
   Substances obtained directly from natural sources and used in homes without chemical synthesis:
   - **Common salt ($\text{NaCl}$):** Draws water out of microbial cells by **osmosis (plasmolysis)** and prevents bacteria/fungi from growing in pickles (*achar*), dried meat and fish.
   - **Sugar:** High sugar concentration dehydrates microbes in jams, jellies and murabba.
   - **Mustard oil and spices (turmeric, chilli, fenugreek, garlic, ginger):** Form an airtight layer over pickles that cuts off oxygen and inhibits bacterial growth.
   - **Vinegar (acetic acid), lemon/lime juice (citric acid) and honey:** Create an acidic medium in which decay bacteria cannot survive.
   - **Natural physical methods:** Sun-drying (*gundruk*, *sinkamari*, dry meat), boiling/pasteurisation, freezing/refrigeration, smoking and airtight canning.
2. **Chemical (Synthetic / Second-grade) food preservatives:**
   Synthetic compounds added in small, regulated quantities to packaged and processed foods:
   - **Sodium benzoate ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{COONa}$):** Preserves acidic foods such as fruit juices, squashes, jams and carbonated soft drinks.
   - **Potassium metabisulphite ($\text{K}_2\text{S}_2\text{O}_5$) and Sulphur dioxide ($\text{SO}_2$):** Preserve colourless fruit juices, squashes and dried fruits.
   - **Sorbic acid and Sorbates (potassium/calcium sorbate):** Prevent mould and yeast growth in cheese, yoghurt and bakery goods.
   - **Calcium propionate:** Prevents bread mould in baked loaves and cakes.
   - **Nitrates and Nitrites ($\text{NaNO}_3, \text{NaNO}_2$):** Preserve cured meat, sausages and bacon.
   - **BHA (Butylated Hydroxyanisole) and BHT (Butylated Hydroxytoluene):** Antioxidants that prevent rancidity (oxidation) of edible oils, butter and packaged snacks.

| Basis | Natural (Organic) Food Preservatives | Chemical (Synthetic) Food Preservatives |
|---|---|---|
| **Source** | Obtained directly from natural plant, animal or mineral sources (salt, sugar, oil, lemon, vinegar, turmeric) | Synthesised artificially in chemical laboratories (sodium benzoate, $\text{SO}_2$, BHA, BHT, sorbates) |
| **Health effects** | Safe for human health with virtually no toxic side effects | Excessive or unregulated intake causes allergies, asthma, hyperactivity, stomach ulcers, and liver/kidney damage |
| **Environmental impact** | Biodegradable and eco-friendly | Chemical manufacturing and residues can pollute water and soil |
| **Where used** | Homemade pickles, jams, dried vegetables (*gundruk*) and traditional foods | Commercial packaged juices, tinned foods, bakery items and soft drinks |

### Artificial Fruit Ripening Agents

To bring unripe fruits (such as mangoes, bananas and papayas) to market quickly, three chemical agents are commonly used:

1. **Calcium carbide ($\text{CaC}_2$ — Harmful / Banned):**
   When calcium carbide comes in contact with moisture in the air, it reacts to release **acetylene gas ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$)**, which artificially heats and turns the skin of fruits yellow:
   $$\text{CaC}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Ca(OH)}_2 + \text{C}_2\text{H}_2 \uparrow \text{ (acetylene gas)}$$
   - **Why calcium carbide is dangerous (SEE 2080):** Commercial calcium carbide contains traces of toxic **arsenic and phosphorus hydrides**. Eating fruits ripened with $\text{CaC}_2$ causes **skin itching and rashes, red and itchy eyes, mouth ulcers, vomiting, diarrhoea, dizziness, breathing difficulty**, and chronic damage to the **liver, kidneys and nervous system** (and cancer).
2. **Ethylene gas ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_4$ — Safe):**
   Ethylene is the **natural plant hormone** produced by ripening fruits themselves. Releasing controlled concentrations of pure ethylene gas in ripening chambers ripens fruits uniformly and safely without toxic residues.
3. **Ethephon ($2$-chloroethylphosphonic acid):**
   Used as a regulated aqueous spray or dip that slowly releases ethylene gas to ripen fruits evenly.

## 19.2 Cleaning chemicals: soap, detergent and natural cleansers

### Traditional / Natural Cleaning Substances

Before synthetic cleansers, Nepali households used eco-friendly natural substances that are biodegradable and non-polluting:
- **Ritha (Soapnut):** Contains natural *saponin* that produces lather in water; used to wash hair, woollens and delicate fabrics.
- **Wood ash (*Kharani*):** Contains potassium carbonate ($\text{K}_2\text{CO}_3$, mild alkali); used to scrub greasy cooking utensils.
- **Sajji matti (Fuller's earth) and Lemon/Lime juice (*Amilo*):** Citric acid in lemon reacts with copper/brass tarnish (basic copper carbonate) to restore shine on metal vessels.

### Soap and the Laboratory Saponification Process

::: definition Soap and Saponification
- **Soap** is the **sodium or potassium salt of a long-chain higher fatty acid** (such as stearic acid, palmitic acid or oleic acid) having cleansing properties in water. *Examples:* **Sodium stearate ($\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COONa}$)** and **sodium palmitate ($\text{C}_{15}\text{H}_{31}\text{COONa}$)**.
- **Saponification** is the chemical reaction in which vegetable oil or animal fat (a triglyceride ester of glycerol and fatty acids) is heated with an aqueous alkali ($\text{NaOH}$ or $\text{KOH}$) to produce **soap and glycerol**.
:::

$$\text{Fat or Oil} + 3\text{NaOH} \xrightarrow{\Delta} 3\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COONa (Soap)} + \text{C}_3\text{H}_5\text{(OH)}_3$$

```photo id="sci10/soap" caption="Bars of ordinary hard soap (sodium salt of fatty acids), manufactured by heating vegetable oil or animal fat with sodium hydroxide and salting out with common salt." width="54"
```

- **Laboratory preparation of soap (Textbook & SEE 4-mark procedure):**
  - *Materials required:* Vegetable oil (e.g. $40\ \text{mL}$ coconut, mustard or cottonseed oil) or animal fat, sodium hydroxide ($\text{NaOH}$, $15\ \text{g}$ dissolved in $50\ \text{mL}$ water), common salt ($\text{NaCl}$), beaker, glass rod, Bunsen burner, funnel, filter paper and mould.
  - *Procedure:*
    1. Take about $40\ \text{mL}$ of vegetable oil in a beaker and add $50\ \text{mL}$ of concentrated $\text{NaOH}$ solution.
    2. Heat the mixture gently while stirring continuously in one direction with a glass rod until it forms a thick, uniform paste.
    3. Add a spoonful of **common salt ($\text{NaCl}$)** ("salting out") and stir well. Because soap is insoluble in concentrated salt solution, **solid soap precipitates out and floats as an upper layer**, leaving glycerol and excess alkali dissolved below.
    4. Allow the mixture to cool, filter it through filter paper, wash the solid residue (soap) with a little cold water, and press it into a mould to harden for a day.
- **Hard soap vs Soft soap:** Using **$\text{NaOH}$** produces **hard soap** (laundry and bathing bars); using **$\text{KOH}$** produces **soft soap** (liquid handwash, shaving cream and shampoo).

### Detergents ("Soapless Soap")

::: definition Detergent
A **detergent** is a synthetic petrochemical cleansing agent — typically the **sodium salt of a long-chain alkyl benzene sulphonic acid** or **sodium alkyl sulphate** — that cleans like soap but has a different chemical composition and **does not form insoluble scum with hard water**. Therefore, detergent is called a **"soapless soap"**.
:::

- **Common examples of detergents:**
  1. **Sodium lauryl sulphate** ($\text{C}_{12}\text{H}_{25}\text{OSO}_3\text{Na}$)
  2. **Sodium alkyl benzene sulphonate** (e.g. sodium dodecylbenzene sulphonate)
  3. **Sodium pyrophosphate**
- **Why detergent is more efficient than soap in laundry (SEE 2080 Lumbini):**
  Hard water contains dissolved calcium ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$) and magnesium ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$) ions. Ordinary soap reacts with $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ to form sticky, insoluble curdy precipitate (**scum**, $(\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COO})_2\text{Ca} \downarrow$), wasting soap and staining clothes. In contrast, the calcium and magnesium salts of detergents are **soluble in water**, so detergents lather freely in both **hard water and soft water** (and even acidic water) and remove dirt without vigorous hand-rubbing.
- **Why detergent is considered a greater pollutant than soap (SEE 2080 Karnali):**
  Soap is made from natural vegetable oils or fats and is completely **biodegradable** (broken down by aquatic bacteria). Many synthetic detergents contain branched hydrocarbon chains and phosphates that are **non-biodegradable**; when discharged into ponds, rivers and soil, they persist as toxic foam, cause **eutrophication (excessive algal bloom that depletes dissolved oxygen)**, kill fish and aquatic life, and degrade soil fertility.
- **Other household cleaning chemicals and their effects:** **Phenyl** (contains phenol/chlorophenol, a disinfectant that can pollute water and soil), **bleaching powder ($\text{CaOCl}_2$)** (disinfects water and toilets, but chlorine can react with organic matter to form toxic compounds), and **hydrochloric acid toilet cleaners** (corrosive; kill beneficial septic/soil bacteria).

| Basis | Soap (SEE 2080 Bagmati) | Detergent |
|---|---|---|
| **Chemical nature** | Sodium or potassium salt of long-chain fatty acids (e.g. sodium stearate) | Sodium salt of long-chain alkyl benzene sulphonic acid or alkyl sulphate |
| **Raw materials** | Natural vegetable oils or animal fats + alkali ($\text{NaOH}/\text{KOH}$) | Synthetic petrochemicals (hydrocarbons from petroleum) + $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ + $\text{NaOH}$ |
| **Action in hard water** | Forms insoluble white scum with $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions; wastes soap | Does **not** form scum; gives abundant lather in both hard and soft water |
| **Biodegradability** | Easily biodegradable; less harmful to the environment | Often non-biodegradable; causes water pollution and algal blooms (eutrophication) |
| **Cleansing power** | Milder cleansing action; gentle on skin | Stronger cleansing action; prolonged contact can cause skin irritation |

## 19.3 Pesticides: chemical and biological pesticides and safety precautions

### What are pesticides? Chemical vs Biological Pesticides

::: definition Pesticides
**Pesticides** are chemical or biological substances used to kill, repel or control harmful organisms (**pests** such as insects, weeds, fungi, rodents and mites) that damage agricultural crops, stored grains, livestock and human health.
:::

| Basis | Chemical Pesticides | Biological Pesticides (Biopesticides) |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin** | Synthetic chemicals manufactured in factories | Derived from natural organisms (plants like **Neem**, *Titepati*, garlic, wood ash, bacteria like *Bacillus thuringiensis*, fungi or natural predators like ladybirds) |
| **Specificity** | Broad-spectrum; kills both harmful pests and beneficial insects (such as honey bees and earthworms) | Target-specific; harms only the target pest without killing pollinators or beneficial fauna |
| **Environmental persistence** | Often non-biodegradable (especially organochlorines like DDT); causes biomagnification in food chains | Biodegradable and eco-friendly; leaves no toxic residue in soil, water or crops |
| **Pest resistance** | Repeated use causes pests to develop genetic resistance | Pests rarely develop resistance |
| **Speed of action** | Acts very rapidly on contact or ingestion | Acts more gradually but provides sustainable long-term control |
| **Examples** | DDT, BHC, Malathion, Parathion, Aldrin, Dieldrin, $2,4\text{-D}$, Zinc phosphide | Neem oil (azadirachtin), *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt), *Titepati* extract, Trichoderma |

### Classification of Chemical Pesticides

1. **By target organism:**
   - **Insecticides (kill insects):**
     - *Organochlorines (persistent, non-biodegradable):* **DDT** (*Dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane*), **BHC** (*Benzene hexachloride* / Gammaxene / Lindane), **Aldrin**, **Dieldrin**, **Methoxychlor**.
     - *Organophosphates (degradable but highly toxic to nerves):* **Malathion**, **Parathion**, **Fenitrothion**, **Dimethoate (Rogor)**.
     - *Carbamates:* **Carbaryl (Sevin)**, **Carbofuran**, **Baygon**.
   - **Fungicides (kill fungi and moulds):** **Bordeaux mixture** ($\text{CuSO}_4 + \text{Ca(OH)}_2$), **Mancozeb**, **Carbendazim**, **Sulphur powder**.
   - **Herbicides / Weedicides (kill unwanted weeds):** **$2,4\text{-D}$** ($2,4$-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid), **Glyphosate**, **Butachlor**, **Atrazine**.
   - **Rodenticides (kill rats and mice):** **Zinc phosphide ($\text{Zn}_3\text{P}_2$)**, **Aluminium phosphide (Celphos)**, **Warfarin**.
   - **Miticides / Acaricides (kill mites and ticks):** **Dicofol**, **Propargite**.
2. **By mode of action:**
   - **Contact pesticides:** Kill pests when absorbed through their skin/cuticle upon direct spray (e.g. Malathion, DDT).
   - **Stomach poisons:** Kill chewing/biting insects when they eat treated leaves or bait (e.g. Lead arsenate, Zinc phosphide).
   - **Systemic pesticides:** Absorbed by plant roots or leaves and translocated through the sap, killing sap-sucking pests (e.g. Dimethoate, Imidacloprid).
   - **Fumigants:** Produce poisonous vapour/gas to kill pests in stored grain godowns (e.g. Aluminium phosphide / Celphos).

::: caution Why DDT is banned in many countries (Give reason — SEE pattern)
**DDT** (*Dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane*) is a **non-biodegradable** organochlorine insecticide that persists in soil and water for decades. It kills beneficial insects (like honey bees) alongside pests, enters food chains, and undergoes **biomagnification** (accumulating in fatty tissues of fish, birds and humans), causing thin eggshells in birds, ecological imbalance, reproductive disorders and cancer.
:::

### Precautions in Storing and Using Chemical Pesticides (SEE 2080 Koshi)

1. Import, production, sale and use of pesticides must strictly follow the **Pesticides Management Act 2076 (जीवनाशक विषादी व्यवस्थापन ऐन, २०७६)**; banned pesticides must never be used.
2. Buy and apply pesticides only in the exact dose recommended by an agricultural technician (**JTA / JT**).
3. Always wear **personal protective equipment (PPE)** — mask, gloves, goggles, boots and long sleeves — and spray **with the wind**, never against the wind.
4. Store pesticides in **clearly labelled, sealed, leak-proof containers** locked **out of reach of children and livestock**, far from food and drinking water.
5. Wash hands, face and clothes thoroughly with soap and water after spraying, and never wash spray tanks in rivers, ponds or drinking-water sources.
6. Strictly observe the **waiting period (pre-harvest interval)**: do not harvest, sell or consume vegetables and fruits immediately after spraying.
7. Adopt **Integrated Pest Management (IPM)** and biological pesticides (Neem, *Titepati*, pheromone traps) to minimise chemical use.

## 19.4 Chemical pollution and its management

### What is chemical pollution?

::: definition Chemical pollution
**Chemical pollution** is the contamination and degradation of the natural quality of air, water, soil and food by harmful synthetic or industrial chemicals (such as chemical pesticides, non-biodegradable detergents, plastics, heavy metals, cement dust, glass/ceramic waste, chemical fertilisers and toxic effluents), causing health hazards to humans and imbalance in ecosystems.
:::

```photo id="sci10/plastic-waste" caption="Non-biodegradable plastic waste and chemical refuse pollute soil and waterways for centuries unless segregated and recycled using the 3R principle." width="58"
```

### Major sources, causes and effects of chemical pollution in our locality

| Source / Pollutant | Biodegradability | Harmful Effects on Health and Environment | Proper Management Measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Plastics, glass, ceramics and synthetic fibres** | **Non-biodegradable** (persist for centuries) | Block drains and soil pores; burning plastics releases toxic dioxins and $\text{CO}$; broken glass/ceramics injure people and animals | Apply the **3R principle (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle)**; segregate non-biodegradable waste; ban single-use thin plastics |
| **2. Chemical pesticides and chemical fertilisers** | Persistent / leach into water | Kill soil microbes, earthworms and bees; cause **eutrophication** in lakes; cause cancer, neurological and kidney disorders | Use **organic compost/manure**, **biopesticides** and IPM; apply only on JTA advice |
| **3. Synthetic detergents, phenyl and bleaching powder** | Mostly non-biodegradable | Create persistent foam in rivers, reduce dissolved oxygen, kill aquatic animals and irritate human skin | Use **biodegradable detergents**, natural cleansers (*Ritha*, ash) and treat domestic wastewater before discharge |
| **4. Cement and brick-kiln dust / industrial effluents** | Inorganic particulate & chemical waste | Cement dust causes bronchitis, asthma and silicosis, and forms a crust on leaves that blocks photosynthesis | Keep cement bags tightly sealed and covered; install **dust collectors / scrubbers** and effluent treatment plants |
| **5. Harmful food colours and calcium carbide** | Synthetic additives | Lead/chromate food dyes and $\text{CaC}_2$ cause allergies, ulcers, liver/kidney damage and cancer | Use natural food colours (turmeric, saffron, beetroot) and ethylene gas for ripening |

::: example Identifying fruit-ripening hazard (SEE 2080 Grade Increment Question)
**Problem.** A person ate mangoes ripened artificially to reach the market early. Soon after, he developed skin itching and rashes, red and itchy eyes, and breathing difficulty. (a) Which chemical was likely used to ripen the mangoes? (b) Which gas does it release with moisture, and what is a chronic effect of its excessive use?

**Solution.** (a) The chemical used was **calcium carbide ($\text{CaC}_2$)**, which contains toxic arsenic and phosphorus impurities.

(b) It reacts with moisture to release **acetylene gas ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$)**: $\text{CaC}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{Ca(OH)}_2 + \text{C}_2\text{H}_2 \uparrow$. Chronic exposure damages the **liver, kidneys, lungs and nervous system** and increases the risk of cancer.
:::

::: example Soap vs Detergent in hard water
**Problem.** When sodium stearate soap ($\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COONa}$) is used in well water containing calcium chloride ($\text{CaCl}_2$), lather does not form easily, whereas sodium lauryl sulphate detergent lathers immediately. Explain with the chemical equation.

**Solution.** Sodium stearate reacts with $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ions in hard well water to form insoluble white **calcium stearate scum**:
$$2\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COONa} + \text{CaCl}_2 \longrightarrow (\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COO})_2\text{Ca} \downarrow + 2\text{NaCl}$$
Detergents form water-soluble calcium and magnesium salts, so no scum precipitates and lather forms immediately.
:::

::: example Classifying daily-life chemicals
**Problem.** Classify the following chemicals into their correct groups: (i) Sodium stearate, (ii) Sodium benzoate, (iii) Dieldrin, (iv) $2,4\text{-D}$, (v) Zinc phosphide, (vi) Sodium lauryl sulphate.

**Solution.** (i) **Sodium stearate:** Soap; (ii) **Sodium benzoate:** Chemical food preservative; (iii) **Dieldrin:** Insecticide (organochlorine pesticide); (iv) **$2,4\text{-D}$:** Herbicide (weedicide); (v) **Zinc phosphide:** Rodenticide; (vi) **Sodium lauryl sulphate:** Synthetic detergent.
:::

## Chapter summary

- **Food preservatives** prevent microbial spoilage of food. **Natural preservatives** (salt, sugar, mustard oil, vinegar, lemon juice, spices, sun-drying, freezing) are safe and eco-friendly; **chemical preservatives** (sodium benzoate, $\text{SO}_2$, potassium metabisulphite, sorbates, calcium propionate, BHA, BHT) must be used only within safe limits.
- **Artificial fruit ripening:** **Calcium carbide ($\text{CaC}_2$)** releases acetylene gas and contains toxic arsenic/phosphorus impurities (banned/harmful); **ethylene gas ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_4$)** and regulated **ethephon** are safer alternatives.
- **Soap** is the sodium or potassium salt of a long-chain fatty acid (e.g. sodium stearate, $\text{C}_{17}\text{H}_{35}\text{COONa}$), prepared by **saponification** (heating vegetable oil or animal fat with $\text{NaOH}/\text{KOH}$ and adding **$\text{NaCl}$** to precipitate solid soap, leaving **glycerol** as by-product).
- **Detergent ("soapless soap")** is a synthetic petrochemical salt of alkyl benzene sulphonic acid or alkyl sulphate (e.g. sodium lauryl sulphate). It works in both hard and soft water without forming scum, but non-biodegradable detergents cause water pollution and eutrophication.
- **Pesticides:** **Insecticides** (DDT, BHC, Malathion, Aldrin, Dieldrin), **Fungicides** (Bordeaux mixture, Mancozeb), **Herbicides** ($2,4\text{-D}$, Glyphosate), **Rodenticides** (Zinc phosphide), **Miticides** (Dicofol). **Biological pesticides** (Neem, *Titepati*, Bt) are target-specific and biodegradable.
- Store and use pesticides strictly under the **Pesticides Management Act 2076** with PPE, proper labelling and waiting periods. Manage **non-biodegradable pollutants** (plastics, glass, ceramics, synthetic fibres) through the **3R principle** and proper waste segregation.

**Group B — Short answer (2 marks each)**

1. Write the full forms of **DDT** and **BHC**. Why is salt ($\text{NaCl}$) added during the process of making soap? <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Differentiate between **soap and detergent** in any two points. (SEE 2080 Bagmati) <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. Give reason: **Detergent is considered more efficient in laundry than soap, yet it is a greater pollutant than soap.** (SEE 2080 Karnali / Lumbini) <span class="marks">[2]</span>
4. Differentiate between **natural (organic) food preservatives and chemical food preservatives** in two points. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
5. Differentiate between **chemical pesticides and biological pesticides** in two points. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
6. Give reason: **We should store and use chemical pesticides very carefully** (SEE 2080 Koshi), and **the use of DDT causes imbalance in the ecosystem**. <span class="marks">[2]</span>

::: note Answers to Group B
**1.** **DDT:** *Dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane*; **BHC:** *Benzene hexachloride*. Common salt ($\text{NaCl}$) is added during soap making to decrease the solubility of soap and **precipitate solid soap out of the glycerol solution** ("salting out").
**2.** (i) **Soap** is the sodium/potassium salt of long-chain fatty acids made from vegetable oil or animal fat, whereas **detergent** is the sodium salt of alkyl benzene sulphonic acid made from petrochemicals. (ii) Soap forms insoluble scum in hard water and is biodegradable, whereas detergent works in hard water without scum and is often non-biodegradable.
**3.** Detergent is more efficient in laundry because its calcium/magnesium salts are water-soluble, so it lathers freely even in hard water without hand-rubbing. It is a greater pollutant because synthetic detergents are non-biodegradable and contain phosphates that cause algal blooms (eutrophication) and water/soil pollution.
**4.** (i) **Natural food preservatives** (salt, sugar, oil, vinegar, lemon) come from natural sources and have no harmful side effects, whereas **chemical food preservatives** (sodium benzoate, $\text{SO}_2$, BHA, BHT) are synthetic and can cause allergies or organ harm if overused. (ii) Natural preservatives preserve homemade foods, whereas chemical preservatives are used in commercial packaged foods.
**5.** (i) **Chemical pesticides** (DDT, Malathion) are synthetic, kill beneficial insects alongside pests, and pollute the environment, whereas **biological pesticides** (Neem oil, Bt, *Titepati*) come from living organisms, are target-specific and are biodegradable. (ii) Pests develop resistance to chemical pesticides, which rarely happens with biopesticides.
**6.** Pesticides are poisonous chemicals; careful storage and use prevent accidental poisoning of children/livestock and protect beneficial insects and water sources. DDT is non-biodegradable, kills beneficial insects, and undergoes **biomagnification** up the food chain, harming fish, birds and biodiversity.
:::

**Group C — Long answer (4 marks each)**

1. Explain the laboratory process of making soap with a labelled diagram, listing the required materials and the role of sodium chloride. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
2. What are food preservatives? Write any two advantages of using food preservatives and two precautions to remember while using them. Also explain how fruits are ripened artificially using ethylene gas and why calcium carbide should be avoided. (SEE 2080) <span class="marks">[4]</span>
3. What precautions should be taken while buying, storing and spraying chemical pesticides? Mention any four points. What alternatives would you suggest to a village facing health and soil problems from excessive pesticide use? <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. What is chemical pollution? Mention the causes of pollution from industrial products (cement, glass, ceramics, plastics, chemical fertilisers and detergents) in your locality and write four practical ways to manage them. (SEE 2080) <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers to Group C
**1.** *Materials:* Vegetable oil ($40\ \text{mL}$), $\text{NaOH}$ ($15\ \text{g}$ in $50\ \text{mL}$ water), common salt ($\text{NaCl}$), beaker, burner, filter paper. *Process:* (i) Mix vegetable oil and $\text{NaOH}$ solution in a beaker and heat gently while stirring in one direction until a thick paste forms (saponification: $\text{Fat/Oil} + \text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{Soap} + \text{Glycerol}$). (ii) Add common salt ($\text{NaCl}$) and stir; soap precipitates as a solid upper layer because soap is insoluble in brine. (iii) Cool and filter the solid soap on filter paper, wash with cold water and press into a mould. (Draw Figure 1b.)
**2.** Substances added to food to prevent microbial decay are food preservatives. *Advantages:* (i) protect food from bacteria/fungi and extend shelf life; (ii) reduce food wastage and allow transport. *Precautions:* (i) use only permitted preservatives within the recommended dose; (ii) check manufacturing/expiry dates and store in cool, airtight containers. *Ripening:* Ethylene gas ($\text{C}_2\text{H}_4$, the natural ripening hormone) is released in temperature-controlled chambers to ripen fruits safely. Calcium carbide ($\text{CaC}_2$) must be avoided because it contains arsenic and phosphorus traces that cause skin rashes, eye irritation, ulcers and liver/kidney damage.
**3.** *Precautions:* (i) Use only pesticides permitted under the **Pesticides Management Act 2076** as prescribed by a JTA; (ii) wear PPE (mask, gloves, goggles, boots) while spraying; (iii) store in labelled, sealed containers out of reach of children; (iv) observe the pre-harvest waiting period before selling or eating crops and wash hands with soap after use. *Alternatives:* Switch to **biological pesticides** (Neem, *Titepati*, ash, cow urine), **organic compost/manure**, crop rotation and **Integrated Pest Management (IPM)**.
**4.** Contamination of air, water and soil by harmful synthetic/industrial chemicals is chemical pollution. *Causes:* haphazard dumping of non-biodegradable plastics, glass, ceramics and synthetic fibres; overuse of chemical fertilisers and pesticides; untreated discharge of detergent/phenyl wastewater; and unsealed cement/industrial dust. *Management:* (i) segregate biodegradable and non-biodegradable wastes and apply the **3R principle** to plastics, glass and metals; (ii) keep cement bags covered and install industrial dust/effluent filters; (iii) use organic manure and biopesticides instead of excess agrochemicals; (iv) use biodegradable cleansers and never discharge chemical wastewater directly into rivers or farmland.
:::
