Every cell in your body needs a continuous supply of oxygen and digested nutrients, and every cell produces waste products such as carbon dioxide, urea and uric acid that must be carried away. In the **CDC Grade 10 Science and Technology** curriculum, this unit focuses on the **human blood circulatory system** — **composition and functions of blood**, **external and internal structure of the human heart**, **arteries, veins and capillaries**, **systemic and pulmonary (double) circulation**, **ABO and Rh blood groups**, and four major cardiovascular and metabolic conditions examined in SEE: **blood pressure (hypertension/hypotension)**, **heart attack (with angiogram and open-heart surgery)**, **diabetes (hyperglycemia)**, and **high uric acid (gout)**.

## 5.1 Human blood: composition, blood cells and functions

### What is blood?

::: definition Blood
**Blood** is a red, opaque, slightly alkaline ($\text{pH } 7.35\text{–}7.45$) **fluid connective tissue** that circulates continuously through the heart and blood vessels to transport gases, nutrients, hormones and metabolic wastes throughout the body. A healthy adult has about **$5\text{ to }5.5\ \text{litres}$** of blood (about $7\text{–}8\%$ of body weight).
:::

```photo id="sci10/blood-smear" caption="A stained smear of human blood under the light microscope. The numerous pink biconcave discs are red blood cells (RBCs); the larger cells with dark purple lobed nuclei are white blood cells (WBCs); tiny specks between cells are platelets." width="68"
```

### Composition of Blood

Blood consists of two main components:
1. **Plasma ($55\%$ of blood volume):** A pale-yellow, faintly alkaline liquid matrix consisting of **$90\text{–}92\%$ water** and **$8\text{–}10\%$ dissolved solids** — plasma proteins (**albumin** for osmotic balance, **globulin** for antibodies/immunity, **fibrinogen** and **prothrombin** for blood clotting), digested nutrients (glucose, amino acids, fatty acids, vitamins), inorganic salts ($\text{NaCl}$, $\text{NaHCO}_3$), hormones, enzymes, and nitrogenous wastes (urea, uric acid). *Serum* is plasma from which fibrinogen (clotting protein) has been removed.
2. **Blood Corpuscles / Blood Cells ($45\%$ of blood volume):** Three types of formed elements suspended in plasma:

| Feature | Red Blood Corpuscles (RBC / Erythrocytes) | White Blood Corpuscles (WBC / Leucocytes) | Platelets (Thrombocytes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shape and nucleus** | Circular, **biconcave, disc-shaped**; **non-nucleated** at maturity in mammals | Irregular / **amoeboid** shape; **nucleated** (lobed or spherical nucleus) | Very small spindle or oval cytoplasmic fragments; **non-nucleated** |
| **Colour and pigment** | Red due to iron-rich respiratory pigment **haemoglobin** | **Colourless** (lack haemoglobin) | Colourless |
| **Normal count (per $\text{mm}^3$)** | **$4.5\text{ to }5.5\ \text{million/mm}^3$** (men $\sim 5\text{–}5.5\text{M}$, women $\sim 4.5\text{–}5\text{M}$) | **$4,000\text{ to }11,000\text{/mm}^3$** ($6,000\text{–}10,000\text{/mm}^3$) | **$1.5\text{ to }4\ \text{lakh/mm}^3$** ($150,000\text{–}400,000\text{/mm}^3$) |
| **Site of formation** | **Red bone marrow** of long bones, ribs and vertebrae | Bone marrow, **lymph nodes** and spleen | Red bone marrow (from megakaryocytes) |
| **Average lifespan** | **$120\ \text{days}$** | **$2\ \text{days to } 2\ \text{weeks}$** (some lymphocytes longer) | **$5\text{ to }10\ \text{days}$** ($7\text{–}8\ \text{days}$) |
| **Site of destruction** | **Liver and spleen** ("graveyard of RBCs") | Liver, spleen and at the site of infection (as **pus**) | Spleen and liver |
| **Main function** | **Transports oxygen** (as oxyhaemoglobin) from lungs to tissues and part of $\text{CO}_2$ back | **Defends the body against germs** (phagocytosis and antibody production); called "soldiers of the body" | **Coagulation (clotting) of blood** at wounds by releasing thromboplastin to prevent bleeding |
| **Related disorders** | Deficiency → **Anaemia**; abnormal excess → **Polycythemia** | Uncontrolled excess (blood cancer) → **Leukaemia**; deficiency → **Leukopenia / Neutropenia** | Deficiency (e.g. in dengue) → internal bleeding (**Thrombocytopenia**); genetic clotting failure → **Haemophilia** |

- **Types of WBCs:** (i) **Granulocytes** (neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils — engulf bacteria and manage allergies); (ii) **Agranulocytes** (monocytes and lymphocytes — produce antibodies for immunity).

### Main Functions of Blood

1. **Transportation:** Carries $\text{O}_2$ from lungs to tissues (via haemoglobin: $\text{Hb} + 4\text{O}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{Hb(O}_2)_4$) and $\text{CO}_2$ from tissues to lungs; transports digested food from the small intestine to cells, hormones from endocrine glands to target organs, and wastes (urea, uric acid) to the kidneys.
2. **Protection (Immunity and Clotting):** WBCs engulf invading bacteria and produce antibodies; platelets and fibrinogen seal wounds by forming a **fibrin blood clot**.
3. **Regulation (Homeostasis):** Distributes heat evenly to maintain body temperature at **$37\ ^\circ\text{C}$**, and regulates water balance and blood $\text{pH}$ ($7.4$).

## 5.2 Structure and working of the human heart

### External and Internal Structure of the Human Heart

The **heart** is a hollow, muscular, cone-shaped pumping organ about the size of a closed fist ($\sim 300\ \text{g}$ in adults), situated in the thoracic cavity between the two lungs with its pointed **apex tilted slightly to the left**.

```photo id="sci10/heart-real" caption="External view of a human heart showing the muscular ventricles and the major blood vessels emerging from its superior base." width="52"
```

- **Pericardium:** The heart is enclosed in a double-walled protective membrane called the **pericardium**. The narrow space between the two membranes is filled with **pericardial fluid**, which **protects the heart from mechanical shocks/injuries and reduces friction** while the heart beats. The heart wall itself is made of involuntary, fatigue-resistant **cardiac muscle (myocardium)** supplied with oxygen and nutrients by the **coronary arteries**.
- **Four chambers of the heart:** A muscular wall called the **septum** (inter-auricular and inter-ventricular septum) divides the heart longitudinally into a **right half (containing deoxygenated/impure blood)** and a **left half (containing oxygenated/pure blood)** so the two never mix:
  1. **Right Auricle (Right Atrium):** Thin-walled upper right chamber that receives **deoxygenated blood** from the whole body through the **superior vena cava** (precaval) and **inferior vena cava** (postcaval).
  2. **Right Ventricle:** Lower right chamber that receives deoxygenated blood from the right auricle and pumps it into the **pulmonary artery** towards the lungs for oxygenation.
  3. **Left Auricle (Left Atrium):** Thin-walled upper left chamber that receives **oxygenated blood** from the lungs through **four pulmonary veins**.
  4. **Left Ventricle:** Lower left chamber that receives oxygenated blood from the left auricle and pumps it at high pressure into the **aorta** to supply the entire body.

::: tip Why the wall of the left ventricle is thicker than the right ventricle (SEE favourite)
- **Ventricles have thicker walls than auricles** because auricles only pump blood into the adjacent ventricles just below them, whereas ventricles must pump blood out of the heart to distant organs.
- **The left ventricle has the thickest muscular wall of all four chambers** (about three times thicker than the right ventricle) because the right ventricle only pumps blood a short distance to the nearby lungs, whereas the **left ventricle must pump oxygenated blood at high pressure through the aorta to every part of the body from head to toe**.
:::

### The Four Valves of the Heart

Valves act as one-way doors that allow blood to flow in one direction only and **prevent backflow**:

| Valve | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Tricuspid valve** (3 flaps) | Guarding the right auriculo-ventricular aperture (between right auricle and right ventricle) | Allows deoxygenated blood to flow from right auricle to right ventricle and prevents backflow |
| **2. Bicuspid / Mitral valve** (2 flaps) | Guarding the left auriculo-ventricular aperture (between left auricle and left ventricle) | Allows oxygenated blood to flow from left auricle to left ventricle and prevents backflow |
| **3. Pulmonary semilunar valve** (3 half-moon pockets) | At the base of the **pulmonary artery** leaving the right ventricle | Allows blood into the pulmonary artery and prevents backflow into the right ventricle |
| **4. Aortic semilunar valve** (3 half-moon pockets) | At the base of the **aorta** leaving the left ventricle | Allows oxygenated blood into the aorta and prevents backflow into the left ventricle |

### Working of the Heart: Systole, Diastole, Heartbeat and Pulse

- **Systole and Diastole:** One complete cardiac cycle ($\sim 0.8\ \text{s}$) consists of rhythmic **contraction (systole)** and **relaxation (diastole)** of the heart chambers. During ventricular systole, the tricuspid and bicuspid valves snap shut, producing the first heart sound (**"LUB"**), and blood is pumped into the pulmonary artery and aorta. During ventricular diastole, the semilunar valves close to prevent backflow, producing the second heart sound (**"DUB"**), tracked by a **stethoscope**.
- **Heartbeat and Pulse Rate:** In a healthy resting adult, the heart beats **$72\text{ times per minute}$** ($60\text{–}100\ \text{bpm}$). Each contraction of the left ventricle forces a wave of blood through the elastic arteries; this rhythmic expansion and recoil felt over a superficial artery (such as the **radial artery at the wrist**) is called the **pulse**. Each ventricle pumps about **$70\ \text{mL}$** of blood per beat (**stroke volume**), giving a **cardiac output** of $72 \times 70\ \text{mL} \approx 5.04\ \text{L/min}$.

## 5.3 Blood vessels and double circulation

### Types of Blood Vessels: Arteries, Veins and Capillaries

| Basis | Arteries | Veins | Capillaries |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direction of blood flow** | Carry blood **away from the heart** to organs | Carry blood **towards the heart** from organs | Microscopic network **connecting arterioles to venules** inside tissues |
| **Wall thickness and elasticity** | **Thick, muscular and elastic** walls to withstand high pumping pressure | **Thin, less muscular and less elastic** walls | **Extremely thin — one cell thick** (endothelium only) for easy diffusion |
| **Lumen (internal bore)** | **Narrow** lumen | **Wide** lumen | Very narrow (about the diameter of one RBC) |
| **Valves** | **Absent** (except semilunar valves at the base of aorta and pulmonary artery) | **Present** at intervals to prevent backflow of low-pressure blood | **Absent** |
| **Blood pressure and flow** | **High pressure**; blood flows in rhythmic spurts (pulsatile) | **Low pressure**; blood flows smoothly without pulse | Pressure falls gradually; slow steady flow |
| **Location in body** | Usually **deep-seated** inside muscles | Usually **superficial** (visible as bluish lines under skin) | Penetrate every living tissue and organ |
| **Type of blood carried** | **Oxygenated blood** (*Exception:* **pulmonary artery** carries deoxygenated blood) | **Deoxygenated blood** (*Exception:* **pulmonary veins** carry oxygenated blood) | Exchange $\text{O}_2$, nutrients, hormones and $\text{CO}_2$/wastes with tissue cells |

### Blood Circulation in Humans: Pulmonary and Systemic (Double) Circulation

Discovered by **William Harvey (1628)**, human blood passes through the heart **twice** during one complete circuit around the body, known as **double circulation**:

1. **Pulmonary Circulation (Heart → Lungs → Heart):**
   Deoxygenated blood from the **right ventricle** is pumped through the **pulmonary artery** to the **lungs**, where it releases $\text{CO}_2$ and absorbs $\text{O}_2$ in the alveolar capillaries. The freshly **oxygenated blood** returns through **four pulmonary veins** into the **left auricle**:
   - **Pulmonary path:** Right Ventricle → Pulmonary Artery → Lungs (oxygenation) → Pulmonary Veins → Left Auricle.
2. **Systemic (General) Circulation (Heart → Body Organs → Heart):**
   Oxygenated blood from the **left ventricle** is pumped through the **aorta** and its branching arteries/capillaries to **all parts of the body** (except the lungs). After supplying $\text{O}_2$ and nutrients and picking up $\text{CO}_2$, the **deoxygenated blood** returns via the **superior and inferior vena cava** into the **right auricle**:
   - **Pulmonary path:** Right Ventricle → Pulmonary Artery → Lungs → Pulmonary Veins → Left Auricle.
   - **Systemic path:** Left Ventricle → Aorta → Body Tissues → Vena Cava → Right Auricle.

## 5.4 Blood groups, blood pressure, heart attack and angiography

### ABO and Rh Blood Group Systems

In 1901 **Karl Landsteiner** classified human blood into four **ABO groups** (**A, B, AB and O**) according to the presence or absence of two glycoprotein **antigens (A and B)** on the surface membrane of **RBCs** and two **antibodies (anti-a and anti-b)** in the **plasma**:

| Blood Group | Antigen on RBC surface | Antibody in Plasma | Can Donate Blood To | Can Receive Blood From |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Group A** | Antigen A | Antibody b (anti-B) | A, AB | A, O |
| **Group B** | Antigen B | Antibody a (anti-A) | B, AB | B, O |
| **Group AB** | Both Antigen A and B | **None** | AB only | **A, B, AB, O (Universal Recipient)** |
| **Group O** | **None** | Both anti-A and anti-B | **A, B, AB, O (Universal Donor)** | O only |

- **Rh Factor (Rhesus antigen):** Discovered in Rhesus monkeys by Landsteiner and Wiener (1940). A person whose RBCs carry the **Rh antigen (D-antigen)** is **Rh-positive ($\text{Rh}^+$)** ($\sim 85\text{–}95\%$ of people); a person lacking it is **Rh-negative ($\text{Rh}^-$)**. Before any blood transfusion, both ABO and Rh compatibility (**cross-matching**) must be verified to prevent fatal clumping (**agglutination**) of RBCs.

### Blood Pressure: Systolic and Diastolic Pressure

::: definition Blood pressure
**Blood pressure** is the lateral pressure exerted by circulating blood against the elastic walls of the arteries. It is measured over the **brachial artery** of the upper arm using an instrument called a **sphygmomanometer** (or digital blood-pressure monitor) and expressed in **millimetres of mercury ($\text{mm Hg}$)**.
:::

```photo id="sci10/bp-monitor" caption="A digital blood-pressure monitor displaying systolic pressure (SYS, 120 mm Hg), diastolic pressure (DIA, 80 mm Hg) and pulse rate (PUL)." width="50"
```

1. **Systolic Blood Pressure (upper reading):** The maximum arterial pressure when the **left ventricle contracts (ventricular systole)** and forces blood into the aorta. Normal adult range: **$90\text{ to }130\ \text{mm Hg}$** (ideal average: **$120\ \text{mm Hg}$**).
2. **Diastolic Blood Pressure (lower reading):** The minimum arterial pressure when the **ventricles relax (ventricular diastole)** and refill with blood. Normal adult range: **$60\text{ to }90\ \text{mm Hg}$** (ideal average: **$80\ \text{mm Hg}$**).
- **Normal adult blood pressure** is written as **$120/80\ \text{mm Hg}$**.
- **High Blood Pressure (Hypertension):** Persistent blood pressure above **$140/90\ \text{mm Hg}$**. *Causes:* High salt and saturated-fat diet, obesity, smoking, alcohol consumption, mental stress, lack of physical exercise and heredity. *Prevention:* Reduce salt/oily food, exercise daily, avoid tobacco and alcohol, manage stress and have regular check-ups.

### Heart Attack (Myocardial Infarction), Angiogram and Open-Heart Surgery

- **Heart Attack (Myocardial Infarction):** The heart muscle (myocardium) receives its own oxygen and nutrients through the **coronary arteries**. When cholesterol, fat, calcium and fibrous plaque deposit inside the inner wall of a coronary artery (**atherosclerosis**) or a blood clot (**coronary thrombosis**) blocks it, blood and oxygen supply to part of the heart muscle stops, causing that cardiac tissue to die. This medical emergency is a **heart attack**.
  - *Symptoms:* Severe crushing **pain and tightness in the centre/left of the chest** radiating to the left arm, shoulder, neck, jaw or back; profuse cold sweating; shortness of breath; dizziness, nausea and anxiety.
- **Angiogram (Coronary Angiography — Diagnosis):**
  A specialised diagnostic X-ray test used to **locate the exact site and percentage of blockage or narrowing inside the coronary arteries**. A thin, flexible tube (**catheter**) is inserted through an artery in the groin (femoral artery) or wrist (radial artery) up to the coronary arteries, a **radio-opaque contrast dye** is injected into the bloodstream, and high-speed X-ray images (**angiograms**) reveal the blocked segment.
- **Treatment of Coronary Blockage and Heart Disease:**
  1. **Angioplasty and Coronary Stent:** A tiny balloon at the tip of a catheter is inflated inside the narrowed coronary artery to widen it, and a expandable wire-mesh tube (**stent**) is left in place to keep the artery open.
  2. **Open-Heart Surgery and Coronary Artery Bypass Grafting (CABG):** Surgical procedure in which the chest is opened, the patient's circulation is temporarily supported by a **heart-lung machine (cardiopulmonary bypass)** while the heart is stopped, and the surgeon repairs/replaces damaged heart valves, closes septal defects ("holes in the heart"), or grafts a healthy blood vessel from the leg/chest (**bypass graft**) around a blocked coronary artery to restore blood flow. In Nepal, major cardiac procedures are performed at centres such as the **Shahid Gangalal National Heart Centre** and **Manmohan Cardiothoracic Vascular and Transplant Centre** in Kathmandu.

## 5.5 Diabetes (hyperglycemia) and uric acid (gout)

### Diabetes Mellitus (Hyperglycemia / मधुमेह)

::: definition Diabetes (Hyperglycemia)
**Diabetes mellitus (hyperglycemia)** is a metabolic disorder in which the concentration of **glucose (sugar) in the blood rises above the normal level** because the **pancreas** either does not secrete enough **insulin hormone** or the body's cells fail to respond properly to insulin, causing excess glucose to be excreted in urine.
:::

- **Normal blood glucose level:** Fasting: **$70\text{ to }110\ \text{mg/dL}$** (or $70\text{–}100\ \text{mg per } 100\ \text{mL}$ of blood); $2\text{ hours}$ after a meal (post-prandial): **below $140\ \text{mg/dL}$**.
- **Role of Insulin:** Secreted by the **beta ($\beta$) cells of the Islets of Langerhans in the pancreas**, insulin converts excess blood glucose into **glycogen** stored in the liver and muscles and helps body cells absorb glucose for energy.
- **Main symptoms (the "3 Ps" + fatigue):** (1) **Polyuria** (frequent urination); (2) **Polydipsia** (excessive thirst); (3) **Polyphagia** (excessive hunger); (4) unexplained weight loss, blurred vision, fatigue and **slow healing of wounds/cuts**.
- **Causes & Risk Factors:** Heredity, obesity, sedentary lifestyle (lack of exercise), overeating refined carbohydrates/sugary foods, mental stress and pancreatic damage.
- **Prevention and Management:** Regular physical exercise and morning walks, balanced low-sugar/high-fibre diet, maintaining healthy body weight, stress reduction, regular blood-sugar monitoring (**glucometer**), and medical treatment (oral antidiabetic tablets or **insulin injections**).

### High Uric Acid (Hyperuricemia) and Gout (युरिक एसिड / बाथ)

::: definition Uric Acid and Gout
**Uric acid ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_4\text{N}_4\text{O}_3$)** is a nitrogenous waste product formed in the liver from the breakdown of **purines** (nitrogen-containing compounds found in body cells and protein-rich foods like red meat, organ meat, seafood, pulses and beer). Normally, uric acid dissolves in blood, is filtered by the **kidneys**, and leaves the body in **urine**. When the kidneys fail to excrete enough uric acid or the body produces too much, blood uric acid rises (**hyperuricemia**; normal adult level: **$3.5\text{–}7.2\ \text{mg/dL}$ in men** and **$2.6\text{–}6.0\ \text{mg/dL}$ in women**). Excess uric acid crystallises as sharp, needle-like **monosodium urate crystals** inside joints (especially the **big toe, ankle, knee and fingers**), causing painful inflammation called **gout**.
:::

- **Causes of High Uric Acid:** (1) Excessive consumption of **purine-rich foods** (red meat — mutton/buff/pork, liver/kidney organ meats, small dried fish, crab, heavy pulses/lentils, mushrooms); (2) drinking **alcohol (especially beer)** and fructose-sweetened cold drinks; (3) **insufficient water intake (dehydration)** and reduced kidney filtration; (4) obesity, heredity and lack of exercise.
- **Symptoms:** Sudden, severe **throbbing pain, redness, swelling, warmth and stiffness in the joints** (most commonly starting at the base of the **big toe** at night/early morning), difficulty walking, and formation of **uric acid kidney stones**.
- **Prevention and Dietary Control (frequently asked in SEE):**
  1. **Drink plenty of water ($3\text{–}4\ \text{litres}$ daily)** so the kidneys flush uric acid out in urine.
  2. **Avoid or strictly limit purine-rich foods** (red meat, organ meats, alcohol/beer, heavy legumes, canned/junk foods).
  3. **Eat vitamin-C-rich and alkaline foods:** Cherries, lemon/citrus fruits, *amla*, green leafy vegetables, cucumber, low-fat dairy and whole grains.
  4. **Exercise regularly**, maintain normal body weight, and lightly **roast pulses before cooking** or discard excess foam while boiling dal.

::: example Cardiac output calculation
**Problem.** The heart of a resting adult beats $72\ \text{times per minute}$ and pumps $70\ \text{mL}$ of blood per beat from each ventricle. Calculate the volume of blood pumped by the left ventricle (a) in $1\ \text{minute}$ and (b) in $24\ \text{hours}$.

**Solution.** (a) Cardiac output per minute $= 72 \times 70\ \text{mL} = 5040\ \text{mL} = 5.04\ \text{litres/min}$.

(b) Volume pumped in $24\ \text{hours} = 5.04 \times 60 \times 24 = 7257.6\ \text{litres}$ (about $7260\ \text{L/day}$).
:::

::: example Identifying blood vessel and blood cell disorders (SEE pattern)
**Problem.** A patient's blood report shows: $\text{RBC count} = 2.9\ \text{million/mm}^3$, $\text{Fasting blood glucose} = 185\ \text{mg/dL}$, and $\text{Serum uric acid} = 9.2\ \text{mg/dL}$. (a) Which three health conditions is the patient suffering from? (b) Which hormone deficiency causes the second condition?

**Solution.** (a) Low RBC count ($< 4.5\ \text{million/mm}^3$) indicates **anaemia**; high fasting blood glucose ($> 110\ \text{mg/dL}$) indicates **diabetes mellitus (hyperglycemia)**; high serum uric acid ($> 7.0\ \text{mg/dL}$) indicates **hyperuricemia (gout)**.

(b) Deficiency of **insulin** hormone secreted by the pancreas causes diabetes mellitus.
:::

::: example Blood group compatibility
**Problem.** Why is a person with blood group **O⁻** called a universal donor, and a person with blood group **AB⁺** called a universal recipient?

**Solution.** Group **O⁻** RBCs have **neither Antigen A, Antigen B nor Rh antigen** on their surface, so they are not clumped by antibodies in any recipient's plasma. Group **AB⁺** plasma has **neither anti-A, anti-B nor anti-Rh antibodies**, so the patient can safely receive RBCs of any blood group without agglutination.
:::

## Chapter summary

- **Human blood ($5\text{–}5.5\ \text{L}$, $\text{pH } 7.4$):** **Plasma ($55\%$)** + **Blood cells ($45\%$)**: **RBCs / Erythrocytes** ($4.5\text{–}5.5\ \text{million/mm}^3$, biconcave, non-nucleated, haemoglobin carries $\text{O}_2$, lifespan $120\ \text{days}$, deficiency causes **anaemia**); **WBCs / Leucocytes** ($4,000\text{–}11,000\text{/mm}^3$, nucleated, fight germs, uncontrolled excess causes **leukaemia**, deficiency causes **leukopenia**); **Platelets / Thrombocytes** ($1.5\text{–}4\ \text{lakh/mm}^3$, non-nucleated, clot blood).
- **Human heart:** Protected by double-layered **pericardium** with **pericardial fluid**. Four chambers: **Right Auricle, Right Ventricle** (deoxygenated blood) and **Left Auricle, Left Ventricle** (oxygenated blood; **thickest wall** to pump blood to the whole body). Four valves: **tricuspid, bicuspid (mitral), pulmonary semilunar and aortic semilunar**.
- **Blood vessels:** **Arteries** carry blood away from the heart under high pressure (thick elastic wall, narrow lumen, no valves; *exception:* pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood). **Veins** carry blood towards the heart under low pressure (thin wall, wide lumen, **have valves**; *exception:* pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood). **Capillaries** are one-cell-thick exchange vessels.
- **Double circulation:** **Pulmonary** ($\text{RV} \rightarrow \text{Pulmonary artery} \rightarrow \text{Lungs} \rightarrow \text{Pulmonary veins} \rightarrow \text{LA}$) and **Systemic** ($\text{LV} \rightarrow \text{Aorta} \rightarrow \text{Body} \rightarrow \text{Vena cava} \rightarrow \text{RA}$).
- **Blood pressure:** Normal adult value **$120/80\ \text{mm Hg}$** (systolic/diastolic), measured by a **sphygmomanometer**.
- **Heart attack:** Blockage of **coronary arteries** by cholesterol plaque/clot. **Angiogram (coronary angiography)** locates the blockage using contrast dye and X-rays; treated by **angioplasty/stent** or **open-heart bypass surgery (CABG)**.
- **Diabetes (hyperglycemia):** Excess blood glucose due to **insulin** deficiency; causes frequent urination, thirst, hunger and slow wound healing. **Uric acid (gout):** Excess purine breakdown product forms urate crystals in joints (big toe), controlled by drinking plenty of water and avoiding red meat and alcohol.

**Group B — Short answer (2 marks each)**

1. Give reason: **The muscular wall of the left ventricle is much thicker than that of the right ventricle.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. Give reason: **Arteries have thick, elastic walls and no internal valves, whereas veins have thin walls and are provided with valves.** <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. Differentiate between **red blood cells (RBCs) and white blood cells (WBCs)** in two points. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
4. Differentiate between **pulmonary circulation and systemic circulation** in two points. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
5. Differentiate between **systolic blood pressure and diastolic blood pressure** in two points. <span class="marks">[2]</span>
6. What is an **angiogram**? Why does a person suffering from high uric acid experience severe pain in the big toe and joints, and what two dietary precautions should they follow? <span class="marks">[2]</span>

::: note Answers to Group B
**1.** The right ventricle pumps blood only a short distance to the nearby lungs, whereas the **left ventricle must pump oxygenated blood at high pressure through the aorta to every part of the body**. To generate this high pressure, its muscular wall is much thicker.
**2.** Blood in **arteries** is pumped directly from the ventricles at **high pressure** in rhythmic spurts, so arteries need thick elastic walls and have no risk of backflow. Blood in **veins** flows back towards the heart at **low pressure**, Often against gravity, so veins have thin walls and **internal valves** to prevent backflow.
**3.** (i) **RBCs** are biconcave, non-nucleated, red cells containing haemoglobin that transport oxygen ($4.5\text{–}5.5\ \text{million/mm}^3$), whereas **WBCs** are amoeboid, nucleated, colourless cells that fight germs ($4,000\text{–}11,000\text{/mm}^3$). (ii) Average lifespan of RBCs is $120\ \text{days}$, whereas WBCs live for a few days to two weeks.
**4.** (i) **Pulmonary circulation** carries deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lungs and returns oxygenated blood to the left auricle. (ii) **Systemic circulation** carries oxygenated blood from the left ventricle through the aorta to all body tissues and returns deoxygenated blood via the vena cava to the right auricle.
**5.** (i) **Systolic pressure ($120\ \text{mm Hg}$)** is the maximum arterial pressure during ventricular contraction (systole). (ii) **Diastolic pressure ($80\ \text{mm Hg}$)** is the minimum arterial pressure during ventricular relaxation (diastole).
**6.** An **angiogram** is a special X-ray diagnostic test using a catheter and radio-opaque dye to detect blockages in coronary arteries. In high uric acid, sharp **monosodium urate crystals** deposit inside joints, causing inflammation and pain. Precautions: (i) **drink plenty of water** ($3\text{–}4\ \text{L/day}$); (ii) **avoid purine-rich red meat, organ meats and alcohol/beer**.
:::

**Group C — Long answer (4 marks each)**

1. Draw a neat labelled diagram of the internal structure of the human heart and trace the complete pathway of blood through it (double circulation). <span class="marks">[4]</span>
2. Differentiate between **arteries and veins** in any four points. Why does blood not clot inside healthy blood vessels, and how do platelets help clot blood at an external wound? <span class="marks">[4]</span>
3. What is a **heart attack (myocardial infarction)**? Mention two main causes, two symptoms, and explain the roles of **angiography** and **open-heart / bypass surgery** in its diagnosis and treatment. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. What is **diabetes mellitus (hyperglycemia)**? State its main cause, three symptoms, and four preventive or control measures. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers to Group C
**1.** Draw the labelled heart diagram (Figure 1) showing the four chambers, septum, four valves and four great vessels. *Pathway:* Superior/inferior vena cava bring deoxygenated blood from the body into the **right auricle** → through the **tricuspid valve** into the **right ventricle** → pumped through the **pulmonary artery** to the **lungs** for oxygenation → returns via **pulmonary veins** into the **left auricle** → through the **bicuspid valve** into the **left ventricle** → pumped through the **aorta** to the whole body.
**2.** Give any four rows from the Artery vs Vein table (direction, wall thickness, lumen width, valves, pressure, blood type). Inside healthy blood vessels, blood does not clot because of the natural anticoagulant **heparin** produced by the liver (and smooth endothelium). At a wound, exposed platelets rupture and release **thromboplastin**, which in the presence of $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ions converts prothrombin into **thrombin**; thrombin converts soluble plasma **fibrinogen** into a mesh of insoluble **fibrin threads** that trap blood cells to form a clot.
**3.** Death of a part of the heart muscle due to sudden blockage of a **coronary artery** is a heart attack. *Causes:* Cholesterol/fatty plaque deposition (atherosclerosis) and blood clot (thrombosis) in a coronary artery, aggravated by hypertension, smoking and obesity. *Symptoms:* Severe chest pain radiating to the left arm/jaw, cold sweating and breathlessness. **Angiography** injects contrast dye via a catheter to locate the exact coronary blockage on X-ray; **open-heart bypass surgery (CABG)** uses a heart-lung machine and grafts a healthy blood vessel around the blocked coronary artery to restore blood supply to the heart muscle.
**4.** **Diabetes mellitus** is a condition of abnormally high blood glucose ($> 110\ \text{mg/dL}$ fasting) caused by insufficient secretion or action of **insulin** hormone from the pancreas. *Symptoms:* Frequent urination (polyuria), excessive thirst (polydipsia), excessive hunger (polyphagia), weight loss, fatigue and slow healing of wounds. *Control measures:* (i) Regular physical exercise and morning walks; (ii) balanced diet low in refined sugar and white rice, rich in green vegetables and fibre; (iii) maintaining healthy weight and reducing stress; (iv) regular blood-sugar monitoring and taking prescribed antidiabetic medicine or insulin.
