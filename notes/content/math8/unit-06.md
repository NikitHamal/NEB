The unitary method is the habit of finding the value of **one** first. If 7 kg
of rice costs Rs 560, then 1 kg costs Rs 80, and any other quantity can be
found in one multiplication. When the quantities move in opposite directions —
workers and days, taps and time, speed and time — the same habit works, but the
"one" you find is a single unit of *work*, not of price.

::: key What this chapter asks of you
You should be able to find the unit value and use it, decide whether a problem
is a direct or an inverse variation, solve problems about time and work, and
solve problems about time, speed and distance.
:::

## 6.1 The unitary method in direct variation

**Method.** Find the value of one unit by division, then multiply by the number
of units required.

::: example The price of a quantity
**Problem.** If 8 litres of milk cost Rs 720, what do 15 litres cost?

**Solution.** Cost of 1 litre $=720\div8=\text{Rs }90$.

Cost of 15 litres $=15\times90=\text{Rs }1350$.

**Answer.** Rs 1,350.

**Check.** $720:1350=8:15$ ✓ — the ratio of the quantities equals the ratio of
the costs, which is what direct variation means.
:::

::: example Working upwards from a smaller unit
**Problem.** A car covers 156 km in 3 hours at a steady speed. How far will it
go in 7 hours, and how long will it take to cover 260 km?

**Solution.** Distance in 1 hour $=156\div3=52$ km.

In 7 hours: $7\times52=364$ km.

For 260 km: $260\div52=5$ hours.

**Answer.** 364 km; 5 hours.

**Check.** $52\times5=260$ ✓, and the speed 52 km/h is the same throughout.
:::

::: caution Do not multiply first
In a sum such as "5 men dig 20 m in a day; how much will 8 men dig?", the quick
temptation is to write $20\times\frac85$ without thinking. That happens to be
right here, because it is a direct variation. In a time-and-work sum the same
reflex gives an answer in the wrong direction. Always ask first whether the
quantities rise together or move oppositely.
:::

## 6.2 The unitary method in inverse variation

When one quantity increases and the other decreases in proportion, find the
total (the constant) instead of the unit rate.

$$\text{total work}=\text{number of workers}\times\text{number of days}$$

$$\text{total distance}=\text{speed}\times\text{time}$$

::: example Men and days
**Problem.** 12 men can complete a piece of work in 10 days. How many days will
15 men take to do the same work?

**Solution.** Total work $=12\times10=120$ man-days.

With 15 men, the time is $120\div15=8$ days.

**Answer.** 8 days.

**Check.** $12\times10=15\times8=120$ ✓. The answer is smaller than 10, as it
must be when more men are put on the same work.
:::

::: example Men, days and hours
**Problem.** 8 men working 6 hours a day finish a job in 15 days. How many days
will 10 men take, working 9 hours a day?

**Solution.** Total work in man-hours:

$$8\times6\times15=720\text{ man-hours}$$

With 10 men at 9 hours a day, the daily work is $10\times9=90$ man-hours.

$$\text{days}=\frac{720}{90}=8$$

**Answer.** 8 days.

**Check.** $10\times9\times8=720$ ✓ — the same total work as
$8\times6\times15$.
:::

::: example Taps and time
**Problem.** Two taps fill a tank in 12 hours and 18 hours respectively. How
long will they take to fill it together?

**Solution.** Work in "tank per hour". The first tap fills
$\frac{1}{12}$ of the tank in an hour, the second $\frac{1}{18}$.

Together in one hour:

$$\frac{1}{12}+\frac{1}{18}=\frac{3}{36}+\frac{2}{36}=\frac{5}{36}$$

of the tank. So the whole tank takes

$$\frac{36}{5}=7\frac{1}{5}\text{ hours}=7\text{ hours }12\text{ minutes}$$

**Answer.** 7 hours 12 minutes.

**Check.** In $7.2$ hours the first tap supplies
$\frac{7.2}{12}=0.6$ of the tank and the second $\frac{7.2}{18}=0.4$, and
$0.6+0.4=1$ ✓. The answer must lie between 6 hours (if both were as fast as the
first) and 12 hours.
:::

## 6.3 Time, speed and distance

$$\text{speed}=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}},\qquad
\text{distance}=\text{speed}\times\text{time},\qquad
\text{time}=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}$$

Remember the unit conversions: $1$ km $=1000$ m, $1$ hour $=60$ minutes
$=3600$ seconds, so $1$ m/s $=3.6$ km/h.

::: example Converting a speed
**Problem.** A bus travels at 54 km/h. Express this in m/s and find how far it
goes in 25 seconds.

**Solution.** $54\text{ km/h}=54\times\frac{1000}{3600}=54\times\frac{5}{18}
=15$ m/s.

Distance in 25 s $=15\times25=375$ m.

**Answer.** 15 m/s; 375 m.

**Check.** $375$ m in 25 s is 15 m/s, and $15\times3.6=54$ km/h ✓.
:::

::: example A journey in two parts
**Problem.** A man walks 4 km at 5 km/h and then travels 12 km by bus at
40 km/h. Find his total time and his average speed for the whole journey.

**Solution.** Walking time $=\frac{4}{5}=0.8$ h.

Bus time $=\frac{12}{40}=0.3$ h.

Total time $=1.1$ h $=1$ hour 6 minutes.

Total distance $=16$ km, so

$$\text{average speed}=\frac{16}{1.1}=14.5\overline{45}\approx14.55\text{ km/h}$$

**Answer.** 1.1 hours; about 14.55 km/h.

**Check.** The average speed must lie between 5 and 40 km/h and must be much
closer to 5, because the walking took nearly three times as long as the bus
ride. It is 14.55, well below the midpoint of 22.5 ✓.
:::

::: caution Average speed is not the average of the speeds
For a journey of 4 km at 5 km/h and 12 km at 40 km/h, the average speed is
**not** $\frac{5+40}{2}=22.5$ km/h. Average speed is
$\frac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}}$. It equals the simple mean only
when the two speeds are used for equal **times**, which is rare.
:::

## 6.4 Direct and inverse variation side by side

The whole unitary method rests on one question: **does the second quantity rise
or fall when the first rises?** Answer that, and the arithmetic follows.

```figure caption="Direct and inverse variation. In direct variation the ratio y/x stays constant (a straight line through the origin); in inverse variation the product xy stays constant (a curve)."
x = np.linspace(1, 10, 100)
fig, (ax1, ax2) = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(5.0, 2.5))
ax1.plot(x, 2.0 * x, color=SERIES[0], linewidth=2)
ax1.set_title("Direct:  $y = kx$", fontsize=8.5, color=INK)
ax2.plot(x, 20.0 / x, color=SERIES[3], linewidth=2)
ax2.set_title("Inverse:  $xy = k$", fontsize=8.5, color=INK)
for ax in (ax1, ax2):
    ax.set_xlabel("x", color=MUTED, fontsize=8)
    ax.set_ylabel("y", color=MUTED, fontsize=8)
    ax.grid(color=GRID, linewidth=0.7)
    ax.set_axisbelow(True)
    for s in ("top", "right"):
        ax.spines[s].set_visible(False)
    ax.tick_params(colors=MUTED, labelsize=7.5)
fig.tight_layout()
```

| Situation | Type | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| More pens, more cost | direct | $\frac{\text{cost}}{\text{pens}}$ constant |
| More workers, less time | inverse | workers × time constant |
| More speed, less time (fixed distance) | inverse | speed × time constant |
| More days, more wages | direct | $\frac{\text{wages}}{\text{days}}$ constant |
| More taps, less filling time | inverse | taps × time constant |

::: example A two-step unitary problem
**Question.** 12 workers build 150 m of wall in 10 days. How many days will 15
workers take to build 225 m of the same wall?

**Answer.** Work with one worker and one day at a time.
In one day 12 workers build $\frac{150}{10} = 15$ m, so **one worker builds
$\frac{15}{12} = 1.25$ m a day**.
15 workers build $15 \times 1.25 = 18.75$ m a day.
Days needed $= \frac{225}{18.75} = \textbf{12 days}$.
**Check:** more workers but much more wall, so more days than 10 — the answer is
believable.
:::

::: note Activity — the price of a kilogram
Visit a shop and note the price of the same item in three pack sizes (say rice
in 1 kg, 5 kg and 25 kg). Work out the price **per kilogram** in each case.
**Discussion:** Which pack is cheapest per kilogram? Why do shops price it that
way? Is the biggest pack always the better buy for a small family?
:::

## Chapter summary

- In direct variation, find the value of one unit by division and multiply.
- In inverse variation, find the constant total (man-days, man-hours,
  speed × time) and divide by the new value.
- $\text{total work}=\text{workers}\times\text{days}$; with hours,
  $\text{work}=\text{workers}\times\text{hours per day}\times\text{days}$.
- For taps or pipes, work in "fraction of the tank per hour" and add the
  fractions; the time is the reciprocal of the sum.
- $\text{speed}=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$;
  $1$ m/s $=3.6$ km/h.
- Average speed $=\frac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}}$, never the
  simple average of the speeds.

## Practice questions

**Group A — Objective (1 mark each)**

1. If 6 books cost Rs 450, the cost of 1 book is
   <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Rs 60 (b) Rs 75 (c) Rs 90 (d) Rs 45
2. More men on the same job means less time. This is
   <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) direct variation (b) inverse variation (c) proportion (d) unitary
3. 36 km/h in m/s is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 10 (b) 12 (c) 6 (d) 3.6
4. If a man walks 3 km in 45 minutes, his speed is
   <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 3 km/h (b) 4 km/h (c) 5 km/h (d) 6 km/h
5. 10 workers finish a job in 8 days. 16 workers will take
   <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 4 days (b) 5 days (c) 6 days (d) 12.8 days
6. A car covers 150 km in 2.5 hours. Its speed is
   <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) 50 km/h (b) 60 km/h (c) 65 km/h (d) 75 km/h

::: note Answers to Group A
**1.** (b) — $450\div6=75$.
**2.** (b) — inverse variation.
**3.** (a) — $36\div3.6=10$.
**4.** (b) — 45 min $=\frac34$ h, and $3\div\frac34=4$ km/h.
**5.** (b) — total $=80$ man-days, and $80\div16=5$ days.
**6.** (b) — $150\div2.5=60$.
:::

**Group B — Short answer (2 marks each)**

1. If 9 kg of potatoes cost Rs 315, find the cost of 14 kg.
   <span class="marks">[2]</span>
2. 18 men can build a wall in 20 days. How many men are needed to build it in
   12 days? <span class="marks">[2]</span>
3. A train travels 240 km in 3 hours. Find its speed in km/h and in m/s.
   <span class="marks">[2]</span>
4. One tap fills a tank in 6 hours and another in 12 hours. How long will they
   take together? <span class="marks">[2]</span>

::: note Answers to Group B
**1.** 1 kg costs $315\div9=\text{Rs }35$; 14 kg cost $14\times35=\text{Rs }490$.
**2.** Total work $=18\times20=360$ man-days; men needed
$=360\div12=30$.
**3.** Speed $=240\div3=80$ km/h; in m/s, $80\div3.6=22.2$ m/s.
**4.** In one hour they fill $\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{12}=\frac{3}{12}=\frac{1}{4}$ of the
tank, so together they take 4 hours.
:::

**Group C — Long answer (4 marks each)**

1. 15 workers can dig a canal 90 m long in 12 days. How long will 20 workers
   take to dig a canal 120 m long, working at the same rate?
   <span class="marks">[4]</span>
2. A bus leaves a town at 7:00 a.m. and travels at 45 km/h. A car leaves the
   same town at 9:00 a.m. and travels along the same road at 60 km/h. At what
   time will the car catch the bus? <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers to Group C
**1.** This is a double unitary-method problem. First find the work per worker
per day: 90 m in $15\times12=180$ worker-days, so
$90\div180=0.5$ m per worker per day. For 120 m with 20 workers: the work needed
is $120\div0.5=240$ worker-days, and
$240\div20=12$ days.
**Check.** $20\times12=240$ worker-days, and $240\times0.5=120$ m ✓.
**2.** By 9:00 a.m. the bus has travelled $45\times2=90$ km and is 90 km ahead.
The car gains on the bus at the rate $60-45=15$ km/h. The time to close 90 km at
15 km/h is
$\frac{90}{15}=6$ hours. So the car catches the bus at 9:00 a.m. $+$ 6 hours
$=$ **3:00 p.m.**
**Check.** By 3:00 p.m. the bus has travelled 8 hours at 45 km/h $=360$ km, and
the car has travelled 6 hours at 60 km/h $=360$ km ✓. Both cover the same
distance, as they must at the meeting point.
:::

**Group C (4 marks)**

**13.** 20 workers can finish a job in 15 days. After 6 days, 5 workers leave. In
    how many more days will the remaining workers finish the job?

::: note Extra practice — answers
**11.** Inverse: $8 \times 18 = 12 \times t$, so $t = \frac{144}{12} = 12$ hours.
**12.** Direct: speed $= 60$ km/h, so distance $= 60 \times 5 = 300$ km.
**13.** Total work $= 20 \times 15 = 300$ worker-days.
Work done in 6 days $= 20 \times 6 = 120$ worker-days.
Work left $= 300 - 120 = 180$ worker-days.
Workers left $= 20 - 5 = 15$.
Days needed $= \frac{180}{15} = \textbf{12 days}$.
:::
