This is the opening chapter of the Zoology section, and it is the shortest unit
in the course — one hour. Do not mistake shortness for unimportance. It asks what
biology actually is, how it is divided, and how it leans on physics, chemistry
and mathematics. Every unit that follows is a branch on the tree this chapter
draws, and every career built on this subject — medicine, agriculture, forestry,
veterinary science, biotechnology, conservation — starts from the map on these
pages.

::: key What the examiner asks from this unit
Short questions, reliably: **define biology and name who coined the term**;
**define any four or five branches** (this is the commonest question in the
whole unit); **explain the relation of biology with physics / chemistry /
mathematics**; and **state the scope or importance of biology**. Definitions must
be one clean sentence each — vagueness costs marks here.
:::

## 6.1 Introduction to Biology

::: definition Biology
**Biology** is the branch of science that deals with the study of living
organisms — their structure, function, origin, growth, reproduction,
classification, behaviour, evolution and their relationship with the
environment. The word comes from the Greek *bios* (life) and *logos* (study or
discourse), and was brought into general use in **1802** independently by
**Gottfried Reinhold Treviranus** and **Jean-Baptiste Lamarck**.
:::

**Aristotle** (384–322 BC) is called the **Father of Biology** and also the
**Father of Zoology**, for his *Historia Animalium*, in which he described and
grouped about 500 kinds of animals. His pupil **Theophrastus** is the **Father of
Botany**, for *Historia Plantarum*.

### What makes something living

Biology's subject matter is defined by a short list of properties. A thing is
treated as alive if it shows:

- **cellular organisation** — it is built of one or more cells;
- **metabolism** — an ordered set of chemical reactions, anabolic and catabolic;
- **growth and development** — irreversible increase in mass from inside;
- **reproduction** — it makes more of its own kind;
- **irritability (response to stimuli)** — it reacts to light, heat, touch,
  chemicals;
- **homeostasis** — it holds its internal conditions steady;
- **adaptation and evolution** — its populations change over generations;
- **movement, respiration, excretion** and a finite **life span** ending in death.

A crystal grows, but from the outside and reversibly; a virus reproduces, but
only inside a host cell, and has no metabolism of its own. Both sit on the
boundary — which is exactly why the boundary is interesting.

### The branches of biology

Biology is divided in two different ways, and the exam expects you to know both.

**By the group of organisms studied:**

| Branch | What it studies |
|---|---|
| **Botany** | Plants |
| **Zoology** | Animals |
| **Microbiology** | Micro-organisms — bacteria, viruses, protists, microscopic fungi |
| **Mycology** | Fungi |
| **Phycology** | Algae |
| **Bacteriology** | Bacteria |
| **Virology** | Viruses |
| **Entomology** | Insects |
| **Ichthyology** | Fishes |
| **Herpetology** | Amphibians and reptiles |
| **Ornithology** | Birds |
| **Parasitology** | Parasites and their hosts |

**By the aspect of life studied:**

| Branch | What it studies |
|---|---|
| **Morphology** | External form and structure |
| **Anatomy** | Internal structure, as revealed by dissection |
| **Histology** | Tissues, using a microscope |
| **Cytology (cell biology)** | The structure and working of cells |
| **Physiology** | The functions and processes of living bodies |
| **Biochemistry** | The chemical substances and reactions in organisms |
| **Molecular biology** | Life at the level of DNA, RNA and protein |
| **Genetics** | Heredity and variation |
| **Embryology** | Development from zygote to a fully formed young |
| **Taxonomy and systematics** | Identification, naming and classification, and evolutionary relationships |
| **Evolution** | The origin of species and descent with modification |
| **Palaeontology** | Fossils — the life of past geological ages |
| **Ecology** | The relationship between organisms and their environment |
| **Immunology** | Immunity and defence against disease |
| **Pathology** | Diseases — their causes, course and effects |
| **Biotechnology** | The use of organisms or their parts to make useful products |
| **Genetic engineering** | Deliberate alteration of an organism's genetic material |

::: caution Two different questions, two different lists
"Name four branches of biology **based on the organisms studied**" wants
botany, zoology, microbiology, entomology. "Name four branches **based on the
aspect studied**" wants morphology, physiology, genetics, ecology. Students who
memorise one list and give it for both questions lose half the marks. Also note
that **microbiology is not a sub-branch of botany** — it cuts across the plant,
animal and non-plant-non-animal kingdoms alike.
:::

### The scope of biology

The reach of biology runs from the molecule to the biosphere. It gives
**medicine** its foundation; it raises crop and livestock yields through
**agriculture**, plant breeding and veterinary science; it manages **forests,
pastures, fisheries and wildlife**; it produces antibiotics, vaccines, insulin
and biofertilisers through **biotechnology**; it underlies **environmental
management** and pollution control; and through **conservation biology** it is
the reason there is still a rhinoceros in Chitwan. For a country whose economy
rests on farming, forests, water and tourism, biology is the applied science of
the national livelihood.

::: example Worked example 6.1
**Problem.** A compound microscope has a 10× eyepiece and objectives of 10×, 45×
and 100×. (a) Find the total magnification with each objective. (b) A cell
measured on a photomicrograph taken at ×600 is 30 mm across. Find its real
diameter in micrometres.

**Solution.**

(a) Total magnification is the product of the two lenses:

$$ M = M_{\text{eyepiece}} \times M_{\text{objective}} $$

$$ 10 \times 10 = 100\times, \qquad 10 \times 45 = 450\times,
\qquad 10 \times 100 = 1000\times $$

(b) Real size is the measured size divided by the magnification:

$$ \text{real size} = \frac{\text{image size}}{\text{magnification}}
= \frac{30\ \text{mm}}{600} = 0.05\ \text{mm} $$

$$ 0.05\ \text{mm} \times 1000 = 50\ \mu\text{m} $$

The cell is **50 μm** across — a typical plant cell.
:::

::: caution Magnification is not resolution
Magnification makes an image bigger; **resolution** is the smallest separation at
which two points still look like two points. A light microscope is limited to a
resolution of about **0.2 μm** by the wavelength of light, so magnifying beyond
about 1500× gives a bigger but blurrier picture — *empty magnification*. Seeing a
ribosome needs an **electron** microscope, not a stronger eyepiece.
:::

- **Physics** gave biology its instruments and much of its explanation. The
  compound and electron microscopes, the centrifuge and X-ray crystallography
  (the method that revealed the structure of DNA) are all physics. Nerve
  impulses are electrical, blood flow is fluid dynamics, the eye is an optical
  system, and X-ray, ultrasound and MRI imaging are physics applied to the body.
  The overlap is **biophysics**.
- **Chemistry.** Living matter is chemical: carbohydrates, lipids, proteins and
  nucleic acids. Respiration is a chain of oxidation–reduction reactions,
  photosynthesis a synthesis driven by light, enzymes are catalysts obeying
  ordinary kinetics, and blood pH is a buffer problem. The overlap is
  **biochemistry**.
- **Mathematics.** Mendel's ratios are arithmetic, gene frequencies obey the
  Hardy–Weinberg equation, populations grow exponentially and then logistically,
  and no experiment counts as evidence until its results have been tested
  statistically. The overlap is **biostatistics** and **biomathematics**.
- **Geology** supplies the fossil record, the dating of rock strata on which the
  whole history of life is hung, and the soils that vegetation grows in —
  **palaeontology** and **soil science**.
- **Geography** gives **biogeography**, the study of why species live where they
  do; **computer science** gives **bioinformatics**, without which no genome
  could be assembled.

::: example Worked example 6.2
**Problem.** An animal cell is about 20 μm across, a bacterium about 1 μm, and a
virus about 100 nm. Express each in metres in standard form, and find how many
times larger the animal cell is than the virus.

**Solution.** Using $1\ \mu\text{m} = 10^{-6}\ \text{m}$ and
$1\ \text{nm} = 10^{-9}\ \text{m}$:

$$ \text{cell} = 20 \times 10^{-6} = 2 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{m} $$

$$ \text{bacterium} = 1 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m} $$

$$ \text{virus} = 100 \times 10^{-9} = 1 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m} $$

$$ \frac{2 \times 10^{-5}}{1 \times 10^{-7}} = 2 \times 10^{2} = 200 $$

The animal cell is **200 times** wider than the virus and **20 times** wider than
the bacterium. Since the virus is well below the 0.2 μm resolution limit of a
light microscope, it cannot be seen with one at all.
:::

::: example Worked example 6.3
**Problem.** At a health camp in Chitwan, 18 of 250 schoolchildren screened were
found to be anaemic. (a) Find the percentage prevalence. (b) If the district has
4,000 children of the same age group, estimate how many are anaemic.

**Solution.**

(a) $$ \text{prevalence} = \frac{18}{250} \times 100 = 7.2\ \% $$

(b) $$ 4000 \times \frac{7.2}{100} = 288\ \text{children} $$

About **288** children in the district are likely to be anaemic. This is
biostatistics doing its ordinary job — turning a small sample into a public
health estimate. Note the assumption: the 250 screened must be representative of
the 4,000, or the estimate is worthless.
:::

## Chapter summary

- **Biology** is the science of living organisms; from Greek *bios* (life) and
  *logos* (study), brought into use in **1802** by **Treviranus** and
  **Lamarck**. **Aristotle** is the Father of Biology and of Zoology;
  **Theophrastus** the Father of Botany.
- Living things show cellular organisation, metabolism, growth, reproduction,
  irritability, homeostasis, adaptation and evolution, and a finite life span.
- Branches divide **by the group studied** — botany, zoology, microbiology,
  mycology, phycology, entomology, ichthyology, herpetology, ornithology,
  parasitology — and **by the aspect studied** — morphology, anatomy, histology,
  cytology, physiology, biochemistry, genetics, embryology, taxonomy, evolution,
  palaeontology, ecology, immunology, pathology, biotechnology.
- Biology overlaps **physics** (biophysics: microscopy, X-ray crystallography,
  nerve impulses, medical imaging), **chemistry** (biochemistry: enzymes,
  respiration, pH), **mathematics** (biostatistics: Mendelian ratios,
  Hardy–Weinberg, population models), **geology** (palaeontology, soils),
  **geography** (biogeography) and **computing** (bioinformatics).
- Total magnification of a compound microscope $=$ eyepiece $\times$ objective;
  real size $=$ image size $\div$ magnification. Light microscopes resolve to
  about **0.2 μm**; beyond about 1500× the extra magnification is empty.
- Applied biology supports medicine, agriculture, forestry, veterinary science,
  fisheries, biotechnology and conservation — the sectors that carry Nepal's
  economy.

**Group A — Multiple choice (1 mark each)**

1. The term "biology" is derived from the Greek words <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) *bios* and *logos* (b) *bio* and *logia* (c) *bios* and *graphos* (d) *vita* and *logos*
2. Aristotle is regarded as the <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) Father of Botany (b) Father of Zoology (c) Father of Genetics (d) Father of Taxonomy
3. The branch of biology that deals with the study of insects is <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) ichthyology (b) herpetology (c) entomology (d) ornithology
4. The study of fossils belongs to <span class="marks">[1]</span>
   (a) ecology (b) embryology (c) palaeontology (d) histology

::: note Answers to Group A
**1.** (a) — *bios* = life, *logos* = study or discourse.
**2.** (b) — Aristotle is the Father of Zoology (and of Biology as a whole); Theophrastus is the Father of Botany.
**3.** (c) — entomology; ichthyology is fishes, herpetology amphibians and reptiles, ornithology birds.
**4.** (c) — palaeontology, the branch that links biology to geology.
:::

**Group B — Short answer (4 marks each)**

1. Define biology, and name any four branches of biology based on the **aspect of
   life** studied, with one line on each. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
2. Explain the relation of biology with physics and chemistry, with two examples
   of each. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
3. A compound microscope has a 15× eyepiece and a 40× objective. (a) Find the
   total magnification. (b) A structure appears 24 mm long in the field of view
   at this magnification; find its real length in micrometres. (c) State one
   reason why this microscope cannot show a virus. <span class="marks">[4]</span>
4. State any four applied fields of biology and name, for each, one course or
   institution in Nepal where it is studied. <span class="marks">[4]</span>

::: note Answers to Group B
**1.** Biology is the science that deals with living organisms — their structure,
function, origin, growth, reproduction, classification, behaviour, evolution and
relation with the environment. Four aspect-based branches: **morphology**, the
external form and structure of organisms; **physiology**, the functions and
processes of the living body; **genetics**, heredity and variation;
**ecology**, the relation between organisms and their environment. (Anatomy,
cytology, embryology, taxonomy or biochemistry are equally acceptable.)

**2.** *Physics* supplies the instruments — the compound and electron
microscopes, the centrifuge, the X-ray crystallography that revealed the
structure of DNA — and explains the electrical nerve impulse, the fluid dynamics
of blood flow and the optics of the eye; medical imaging is applied physics. The
overlap is **biophysics**. *Chemistry*: living matter is carbohydrate, lipid,
protein and nucleic acid; respiration is a series of oxidation–reduction
reactions, enzymes are catalysts, and blood pH is held by buffers. The overlap is
**biochemistry**.

**3.** (a) $M = 15 \times 40 = 600\times$.
(b) Real length $= 24\ \text{mm} / 600 = 0.04\ \text{mm} = 40\ \mu\text{m}$.
(c) A light microscope cannot resolve detail finer than about 0.2 μm because of
the wavelength of light, and a virus is only about 0.1 μm across — so it lies
below the resolution limit, no matter how much the image is magnified.

**4.** Any four: **medicine** — MBBS or B.Sc. Nursing, entered through the
MECEE-BL examination; **agriculture** — B.Sc. Ag at Agriculture and Forestry
University, Rampur; **forestry** — B.Sc. Forestry at the Institute of Forestry,
Pokhara; **veterinary science** — B.V.Sc. & A.H. at AFU, Rampur;
**biotechnology / microbiology** — B.Sc. Microbiology at TU or B.Tech.
Biotechnology at Kathmandu University; **fisheries** — B.Sc. Aquaculture, AFU;
**conservation** — Environmental Science, with employers such as DNPWC, NTNC and
WWF Nepal.
:::
