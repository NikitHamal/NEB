Grammar II is the **largest English chapter** (our estimate: about 6 of 16 questions) and also the widest: sentence patterns and conditionals, parts of speech and the passive, infinitive / gerund / participle, punctuation and prepositions, vocabulary and idioms. The official model samples include a preposition item ("He is one person, I count ___ in times of difficulty": *on*) and a tense item, which tells you the level: single-fact recall with one tricky distractor.

::: key What the paper asks from this chapter
- The three real conditionals plus *wish*, *if only*, *had … would have*, and inverted conditionals.
- Passive voice of every tense and of questions; verbs that take a gerund or an infinitive.
- Prepositions with adjectives, verbs and nouns (*fond of, good at, depend on, count on*).
- Vocabulary: synonyms, antonyms, one-word substitutes, commonly confused words, idioms and phrasal verbs.
- Punctuation: comma, apostrophe, semicolon; parts of speech identified from use.
:::

## 2.1 Basic grammatical patterns and conditional sentences

**Sentence patterns:** SV (*Birds fly*), SVC (*She is a doctor*), SVO (*He reads books*), SVOO (*She gave me a pen*), SVOC (*They elected him captain*), SVA (*He lives in Pokhara*). Questions invert the auxiliary and subject; indirect questions do not.

| Type | If-clause | Main clause | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| zero | present | present | general truth: *If you heat ice, it melts* |
| first | present | will / can / may + verb | real future possibility: *If it rains tomorrow, we will stay at home* |
| second | past (**were** for all persons) | would / could + verb | unreal present: *If I were rich, I would help the poor* |
| third | past perfect | would have + past participle | unreal past: *If she had studied harder, she would have passed* |
| mixed | past perfect | would + verb | *If I had taken the job, I would be rich now* |

**Inverted conditionals** drop *if*: *Had he known, he would have come* (= If he had known); *Were I you, I would refuse*; *Should you need help, call me*. **Wishes:** *I wish I were a bird* (present); *I wish I had studied* (past); *If only I knew*. **Unless** = *if … not*. **Provided / as long as / in case** introduce conditions. Never put *will* or *would* in the *if*-clause of a real condition.

## 2.2 Parts of speech, active and passive voice

**Eight parts of speech** are decided by *function*: *light* is a noun (*the light*), an adjective (*light bag*) or a verb (*to light*). *She sings **beautifully*** — adverb; *He is a **fast** runner* — adjective; *He runs **fast*** — adverb. Articles *a, an, the*; **the** for something specific or unique (*the sun*), no article for plural general ideas, *an* before a vowel *sound* (*an hour, a university*). Pronoun cases: *I / me / my / mine*; relative pronouns *who* (subject), *whom* (object), *whose* (possession), *which* (things), *that*. **Conjunctions:** coordinating (*and, but, or, so*) and subordinating (*although, because, unless*); correlatives (*neither … nor, not only … but also, hardly … when, no sooner … than*).

**Passive voice:** object becomes subject; verb is *be* + past participle; the agent goes after *by*.

| Tense | Active → Passive |
|---|---|
| simple present | writes → is written |
| present continuous | is building → is being built |
| present perfect | has built → has been built |
| simple past | wrote → was written |
| past continuous | was writing → was being written |
| future | will write → will be written |
| modal | can write → can be written |

Questions: *Who wrote this book?* → *By whom was this book written?* Imperatives: *Open the door* → *Let the door be opened.* Verbs with two objects have two passives (*I was given a pen*). Intransitive verbs have no passive. *Someone stole my wallet* → *My wallet was stolen.*

## 2.3 Verbals: infinitives, gerunds and participles

- **Infinitive** (*to* + verb; bare after modals, *let, make, see, hear*): *He wants **to become** a doctor. They made him **work**.* After *too, enough, in order to*.
- **Gerund** (verb + *-ing* used as a noun): after prepositions (*I look forward **to seeing** you; She is fond of **singing***) and after *enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, practise, suggest, consider, keep, give up, can't help*: *She enjoys **reading** novels.*
- **Both with a change in meaning:** *stop to talk* (stop in order to talk) / *stop talking* (cease); *remember to lock* (not forget a task) / *remember locking* (a past memory); *try to* (attempt) / *try doing* (experiment).
- **Participles:** present (*a running horse*), past (*a broken window*), perfect (*Having finished, he left*). **Dangling participle** is an error: *Walking down the street, a dog bit me* → *Walking down the street, I was bitten by a dog.*
- *to* can be a preposition (followed by a gerund): *used to doing, look forward to, object to, devoted to*; but *used to do* means a past habit.

## 2.4 Punctuation and use of prepositions

**Punctuation:** full stop, question mark, exclamation; **comma** separates items in a list, non-defining clauses, and joins two main clauses only with a conjunction (*I like tea, but he likes coffee*; a comma alone is a *comma splice*); **semicolon** joins closely related main clauses; **colon** introduces a list or explanation; **apostrophe** for possession (*the boy's book, the boys' books, children's*) and contractions (*it's* = it is, *its* = of it); quotation marks for speech; hyphen for compounds (*well-known*).

**Prepositions of time and place:** *at* a point (*at 5 pm, at the station*), *on* days and surfaces (*on Monday, on the table*), *in* longer periods and enclosed spaces (*in 2020, in Kathmandu*); *for* a period (*for ten years*), *since* a point (*since 2015*); *by* a deadline, *until* a continuing time; *between* two, *among* many; *during* a period.

| Adjective / verb / noun | Preposition |
|---|---|
| fond, afraid, proud, jealous, capable, tired | **of** |
| good, bad, clever, quick, surprised | **at** |
| married, kind, similar, superior, addicted | **to** |
| interested, rich, believe | **in** |
| depend, rely, count, insist, congratulate (on) | **on** |
| agree, angry, pleased | **with** (person) / **about, at** (thing) |
| accuse, suspect | **of** |
| prevent, protect, differ | **from** |
| result **in**, die **of** (disease), die **from** (injury), blame … **for**, punish **for**, famous **for** | |

*He is one person I count **on** in times of difficulty.* *The meeting was postponed **owing to** rain.* *Different **from***; *prefer X **to** Y*; *comply **with***.

## 2.5 Vocabulary

Learn words in clusters. **Synonyms:** abundant = plentiful; candid = frank; diligent = hardworking; obsolete = outdated; reluctant = unwilling; scarce = rare; vague = unclear; frugal = thrifty. **Antonyms:** generous ≠ stingy; ancient ≠ modern; expand ≠ contract; victory ≠ defeat; transparent ≠ opaque. **One-word substitutes:** bibliophile (lover of books), philanthropist (lover of mankind who gives), misanthrope (hater of mankind), autobiography, orphan, omnipotent (all-powerful), omniscient (all-knowing), contemporary, amateur, nocturnal, herbivorous, edible, illegible (cannot be read), incorrigible (cannot be corrected), inevitable (cannot be avoided), anonymous (nameless), plagiarism (stealing others' writing), pedestrian. **Confusing pairs:** *affect* (verb) / *effect* (noun), *principal / principle*, *stationary / stationery*, *accept / except*, *advice / advise*, *complement / compliment*, *lose / loose*, *than / then*, *its / it's*, *emigrate / immigrate*. **Prefixes and suffixes:** *un-, in-, im-, il-, ir-, dis-, mis-* (negatives); *-able, -ful, -less, -ness, -tion, -ment*.

## 2.6 Idiomatic expressions

| Idiom | Meaning |
|---|---|
| bite the bullet | endure a painful situation bravely |
| a piece of cake | very easy |
| break the ice | start a conversation in an awkward situation |
| cry over spilt milk | regret something that cannot be changed |
| once in a blue moon | very rarely |
| let the cat out of the bag | reveal a secret |
| hit the nail on the head | say exactly the right thing |
| under the weather | slightly ill |
| burn the midnight oil | study or work late |
| kick the bucket | die |
| spill the beans | disclose a secret |
| the last straw | the final problem that makes things unbearable |
| a blessing in disguise | a good thing that seemed bad at first |
| in the nick of time | just in time |
| turn a blind eye | pretend not to notice |

**Phrasal verbs:** *call off* (cancel), *put off* (postpone), *look after* (take care of), *look into* (investigate), *give up* (stop), *carry out* (execute), *come across* (find by chance), *run out of* (exhaust), *take after* (resemble), *set off* (start a journey), *turn down* (reject), *bring up* (raise), *make up* (invent), *get over* (recover).

## Formula and fact sheet

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| first conditional | if + present, will + verb |
| second conditional | if + **were**, would + verb |
| third conditional | if + had + pp, would have + pp |
| gerund after preposition | *fond of reading* |
| passive | be + past participle |
| *for* / *since* | period / starting point |
| *affect / effect* | verb / noun |

## Chapter summary

- Match the conditional to the real or unreal time: present, future, past.
- The passive is *be* + past participle; progressive passives use *being*.
- A preposition is followed by a gerund, never a bare infinitive.
- Learn adjective–preposition and verb–preposition pairs as collocations.
- A comma cannot join two main clauses by itself.
- Idioms are learnt whole; a literal reading is nearly always the wrong option.

## Practice questions

**Set A — Recall (16 questions)**

1. If I ___ rich, I would help the poor.
   (a) am (b) was (c) were (d) had been
2. If it rains tomorrow, we ___ at home.
   (a) stayed (b) would stay (c) had stayed (d) will stay
3. He is married ___ a doctor.
   (a) to (b) with (c) by (d) for
4. She is afraid ___ dogs.
   (a) from (b) of (c) at (d) with
5. The letter ___ by him yesterday.
   (a) is written (b) has been written (c) is being written (d) was written
6. The passive of "Someone stole my wallet" is "My wallet ___ stolen."
   (a) was (b) is (c) has (d) were
7. I look forward to ___ you.
   (a) see (b) seeing (c) saw (d) have seen
8. He wants ___ a doctor.
   (a) becoming (b) become (c) to become (d) became
9. She enjoys ___ novels.
   (a) reading (b) to read (c) read (d) to reading
10. The word closest in meaning to "abundant" is
    (a) scarce (b) rare (c) tiny (d) plentiful
11. The opposite of "generous" is
    (a) kind (b) stingy (c) liberal (d) noble
12. A person who loves books is a
    (a) philanthropist (b) misanthrope (c) bibliophile (d) cynic
13. The idiom "to bite the bullet" means
    (a) to endure a painful situation bravely (b) to eat quickly (c) to shoot (d) to refuse
14. The idiom "a piece of cake" means
    (a) a dessert (b) dangerous (c) costly (d) very easy
15. Which sentence is correctly punctuated?
    (a) I like tea; but he likes coffee. (b) I like tea, but he likes coffee. (c) I like tea but, he likes coffee. (d) I like, tea but he likes coffee.
16. In "She sings beautifully", the word *beautifully* is
    (a) an adjective (b) a noun (c) an adverb (d) a preposition

::: note Answers and explanations — Set A
**1.** (c) — second conditional uses *were*.
**2.** (d) — first conditional: if + present, *will* + verb.
**3.** (a) — *married to*.
**4.** (b) — *afraid of*.
**5.** (d) — a past time word (*yesterday*) needs the simple past passive.
**6.** (a) — *was stolen*.
**7.** (b) — *to* here is a preposition, so a gerund follows.
**8.** (c) — *want* takes an infinitive.
**9.** (a) — *enjoy* takes a gerund.
**10.** (d) — abundant = plentiful.
**11.** (b) — generous ≠ stingy.
**12.** (c) — bibliophile.
**13.** (a) — to face something unpleasant courageously.
**14.** (d) — something very easy.
**15.** (b) — a comma before the conjunction *but* joining two main clauses.
**16.** (c) — it modifies the verb *sings*.
:::

**Set B — Understanding (10 questions)**

1. If she had studied harder, she ___ the exam.
   (a) would pass (b) will pass (c) would have passed (d) passed
2. I wish I ___ a bird.
   (a) were (b) am (c) was (d) will be
3. Had he known, he ___ come.
   (a) will have (b) would have (c) would (d) had
4. The passive form of "Who wrote this book?" is
   (a) By whom this book written? (b) Who was written this book? (c) By whom is this book written? (d) By whom was this book written?
5. He stopped ___ when the teacher entered the room.
   (a) to talk (b) talking (c) talk (d) to talking
6. She is the girl ___ won the first prize.
   (a) whom (b) whose (c) who (d) which
7. He has been living here ___ ten years.
   (a) for (b) since (c) from (d) in
8. He was punished ___ stealing.
   (a) of (b) by (c) for (d) with
9. "To cry over spilt milk" means to
   (a) waste food (b) be careless (c) complain loudly (d) regret something that cannot be changed
10. The word closest in meaning to "candid" is
    (a) secretive (b) frank (c) rude (d) shy

::: note Answers and explanations — Set B
**1.** (c) — third conditional: *had studied … would have passed*.
**2.** (a) — a wish about the present takes the past subjunctive *were*.
**3.** (b) — inverted third conditional (= *If he had known, he would have come*).
**4.** (d) — *By whom was this book written?*
**5.** (b) — *stop doing* means to cease; *stop to talk* would mean he paused in order to talk.
**6.** (c) — *who* is the subject of *won*.
**7.** (a) — *for* + period of time; *since* needs a starting point.
**8.** (c) — *punished for*.
**9.** (d) — regretting what is past and cannot be undone.
**10.** (b) — candid = frank and honest.
:::

**Set C — Application (6 questions)**

1. Hardly had he reached the station ___ the train left.
   (a) when (b) than (c) then (d) as
2. Not only did he fail, ___ he also lost his scholarship.
   (a) and (b) but (c) so (d) or
3. By the time we arrived, the film ___.
   (a) has started (b) started (c) had started (d) was starting
4. He suggested that she ___ a doctor.
   (a) sees (b) saw (c) see (d) seen
5. The man ___ car was stolen reported it to the police.
   (a) who (b) whom (c) which (d) whose
6. She would rather ___ at home than go out.
   (a) stays (b) stay (c) to stay (d) stayed

::: note Answers and explanations — Set C
**1.** (a) — the correlative is *hardly … when* (*no sooner … than*).
**2.** (b) — *not only … but also*.
**3.** (c) — *by the time* + past: the earlier action takes the past perfect.
**4.** (c) — *suggest that + subject + base verb* (subjunctive).
**5.** (d) — *whose* shows possession.
**6.** (b) — *would rather* + bare infinitive.
:::
