Phonetics carries about **2 of the 16 English questions** (our estimate), and the official model paper shows exactly what they look like: *"The word 'procrastination' has its stress on the … syllable"* and *"Which of the following pairs have the same initial vowel sound? (ago, alone)"*. Both are answerable with a short rule set and a little practice reading phonetic values aloud.

::: key What the paper asks from this chapter
- The 44 phonemes of Received Pronunciation: 20 vowels (12 pure + 8 diphthongs) and 24 consonants.
- Same-sound and different-sound word pairs (initial vowel, final consonant, silent letters).
- Number of syllables and the syllable that carries the primary stress.
- Stress shift between noun and verb, and sentence stress on content words.

## 3.2 Phonemes (vowels and consonants)

English spelling is not phonetic, so we count **sounds**, not letters. A *phoneme* is the smallest unit of sound that changes meaning (*ship* /ʃɪp/ versus *sheep* /ʃiːp/ — the pair is a **minimal pair**).

**Vowels (20).** Twelve **monophthongs** (single, steady sounds): short /ɪ e æ ʌ ɒ ʊ ə/ and long /iː ɑː ɔː uː ɜː/. Eight **diphthongs** (glides): /eɪ aɪ ɔɪ aʊ əʊ ɪə eə ʊə/. Examples:

| Sound | Words | Sound | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| /iː/ | seat, feel, key | /ɪ/ | sit, inn, village |
| /e/ | bed, head, said | /æ/ | cat, apple |
| /ɑː/ | car, father, calm | /ʌ/ | cup, up, love |
| /ɒ/ | hot, on, watch | /ɔː/ | all, caught, door, more |
| /ʊ/ | put, foot, good | /uː/ | food, blue, rule |
| /ɜː/ | bird, learn, her | /ə/ | **a**go, **a**lone, sof**a** (schwa) |
| /eɪ/ | day, break, eight | /aɪ/ | my, high, buy |
| /ɔɪ/ | boy, coin | /aʊ/ | now, **ow**l, house |
| /əʊ/ | go, home, boat | /ɪə/ | here, ear, beer |

The **schwa /ə/** is the commonest vowel sound in English and occurs only in unstressed syllables: *ago* /əˈgəʊ/ and *alone* /əˈləʊn/ begin with the same sound, while *owl* /aʊl/ and *all* /ɔːl/, *inn* /ɪn/ and *eel* /iːl/, *up* /ʌp/ and *on* /ɒn/ do not. Vowel *length* matters: *bit* /ɪ/ versus *beat* /iː/; *pull* /ʊ/ versus *pool* /uː/.

**Consonants (24).** Plosives /p b t d k g/; fricatives /f v θ ð s z ʃ ʒ h/; affricates /tʃ dʒ/; nasals /m n ŋ/; liquids /l r/; glides /j w/. Voiceless versus voiced pairs: /p b/, /t d/, /k g/, /f v/, /θ ð/, /s z/, /ʃ ʒ/, /tʃ dʒ/.
- /θ/ (voiceless) in **th**in, **th**ink, ba**th**; /ð/ (voiced) in **th**is, **th**en, ba**th**e.
- /ʃ/ in **sh**ip, na**ti**on, **s**ure; /ʒ/ in vi**si**on, mea**s**ure, plea**s**ure; /tʃ/ in **ch**air, **t**ure; /dʒ/ in **j**ug, **g**eneral, ba**dg**e.
- /ŋ/ in si**ng**, thi**n**k; /h/ in **h**at; /j/ in **y**es, **u**se.
- **Silent letters:** k in *knife, know*; b in *climb, comb, doubt, debt*; w in *write, answer*; p in *psychology, pneumonia*; g in *gnat, sign*; h in *hour, honest*; l in *walk, calm*; t in *listen, castle*; n in *autumn, column*.
- The same letter can give different sounds: *c* in *cat* /k/ and *city* /s/; *s* in *sun* /s/ and *rose* /z/; *-ed* is /t/ after voiceless sounds (*walked*), /d/ after voiced (*played*), /ɪd/ after /t d/ (*wanted*); *-s* is /s/, /z/ or /ɪz/ (*cats, dogs, buses*).

## 3.3 Syllables and stress (word and sentence)

A **syllable** has one vowel sound: *beau-ti-ful* (3), *ed-u-ca-tion* (4), *com-mu-ni-ca-tion* (5), *pro-cras-ti-na-tion* (5). Count vowel *sounds*: *the-ater* is 3, *name* is 1.

**Word stress** is the syllable pronounced louder, longer and higher. Rules of thumb:
- Words ending **-tion, -sion, -ic, -ical, -ity, -ify, -ian, -ious** stress the syllable **before** the ending: *edu-CA-tion, pro-cras-ti-NA-tion, eco-NOM-ic, ca-PA-ci-ty, pho-TOG-ra-pher*.
- Words ending **-graphy, -logy, -ate, -ment** (long words): *pho-TOG-ra-phy, bi-OL-o-gy, ECO-no-my* → *e-CON-o-my*.
- **Two-syllable nouns and adjectives** are usually stressed on the first syllable (*TAble, PREsent, REcord*); **two-syllable verbs** on the second (*preSENT, reCORD, deCIDE*). Stress shift marks the change of word class: *a PREsent* / *to preSENT*; *an INcrease* / *to inCREASE*; *a REcord* / *to reCORD*; *an OBject* / *to obJECT*.
- **Compound nouns** are stressed on the first part (*BLACKboard, GREENhouse*); compound adjectives and verbs on the second (*bad-TEMpered, underSTAND*).
- Prefixes are generally unstressed (*re-TURN, un-HAPpy*); suffixes *-ful, -less, -ness, -ing* do not move the stress (*BEAUtiful*).

**Sentence stress.** In connected speech, **content words** (nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs, negatives, question words) carry the stress; **function words** (articles, prepositions, auxiliaries, pronouns, conjunctions) are weak and their vowels reduce to /ə/. *I **want** to **go** to the **market**.* Stress changes the focus: *I didn't **steal** the money* (I did something else) versus *I didn't steal **the** money* (another money). **Intonation:** a falling tune for statements, wh-questions and commands; a rising tune for yes/no questions and uncertain lists (the last item of a list falls).

::: caution Phonetics traps
Count sounds, not letters (*knife* starts with /n/, *hour* with a vowel, so *an hour*). *-ed* has three sounds. Pairs that look alike do not sound alike: *cough, though, through, tough, thought*. The **stress** of a long word follows its *ending*, not its beginning.
:::

## Chapter summary

- English has 44 phonemes in RP; a silent letter adds no sound.
- Words ending in *-tion, -ic, -ity* stress the preceding syllable.
- Two-syllable nouns take first-syllable stress, verbs second.
- Weak function words reduce to schwa; content words carry sentence stress.

### Model answer D1: The English phonemes (10 marks)

**Introduction.** English spelling is not phonetic, so we count **sounds** and not letters. A **phoneme** is the smallest unit of sound that can change meaning: *ship* /ʃɪp/ and *sheep* /ʃiːp/ differ only in one phoneme. Received Pronunciation (RP) has **44 phonemes**: **20 vowels** and **24 consonants**.

**Vowels (20).**

1. **12 monophthongs (pure vowels):** short /ɪ e æ ʌ ɒ ʊ ə/ and long /iː ɑː ɔː uː ɜː/. Examples: *sit* /sɪt/, *bed* /bed/, *cat* /kæt/, *cup* /kʌp/, *hot* /hɒt/, *put* /pʊt/, *about* /əˈbaʊt/, *see* /siː/, *car* /kɑː/, *saw* /sɔː/, *blue* /bluː/, *bird* /bɜːd/.
2. **8 diphthongs (glides):** /eɪ aɪ ɔɪ əʊ aʊ ɪə eə ʊə/. Examples: *day* /deɪ/, *my* /maɪ/, *boy* /bɔɪ/, *go* /ɡəʊ/, *now* /naʊ/, *here* /hɪə/, *air* /eə/, *tour* /tʊə/.

**Consonants (24)** are described by **manner** and **voicing**.

| Manner | Voiceless | Voiced |
|---|---|---|
| Plosives | /p, t, k/ | /b, d, ɡ/ |
| Affricates | /tʃ/ (church) | /dʒ/ (judge) |
| Fricatives | /f, θ, s, ʃ, h/ | /v, ð, z, ʒ/ |
| Nasals | – | /m, n, ŋ/ |
| Lateral, approximants | – | /l, r, w, j/ |

**Notes.** A sound is **voiced** when the vocal cords vibrate (put a finger on the throat while saying /z/ and /s/). The fricatives /θ/ (*thin*) and /ð/ (*this*) are formed with the tongue tip between the teeth, and Nepali speakers often replace them with /t̪/ and /d̪/.

**Transcription practice.**

| Word | Transcription | Phonemes |
|---|---|---|
| thought | /θɔːt/ | 3 |
| measure | /ˈmeʒə/ | 4 |
| church | /tʃɜːtʃ/ | 3 |
| sing | /sɪŋ/ | 3 |
| judge | /dʒʌdʒ/ | 3 |
| box | /bɒks/ | 4 |

The word *box* has three letters but four sounds, while *sing* has four letters but three sounds, which shows that letters and phonemes are different.

**Conclusion.** The phoneme chart is the key to correct pronunciation and to dictionary symbols, and minimal pairs prove that each phoneme is distinct.

### Model answer D2: Syllables and stress (10 marks)

**Introduction.** A **syllable** is a unit of speech with one vowel sound, with or without consonants round it. *Book* has one syllable, *teacher* two (/ˈtiː.tʃə/), *information* four (/ˌɪn.fəˈmeɪ.ʃən/). **Stress** is the extra force given to a syllable.

**Word stress rules.**

1. **Two-syllable nouns and adjectives** are usually stressed on the first syllable: *TAble, PRESent* (noun), *HAPpy*.
2. **Two-syllable verbs** are usually stressed on the second: *preSENT* (verb), *deCIDE*, *beGIN*.
3. **Noun–verb pairs** change stress: *REcord* (noun) / *reCORD* (verb), *IMport* / *imPORT*.
4. **Compound nouns** take stress on the first part: *BLACKboard, GREENhouse*; **compound verbs** on the second: *underSTAND*.
5. **Words ending in -tion, -sion, -ic, -ical, -ial** are stressed on the syllable before the ending: *eduCAtion, ecoNOmic, poLItical, ofFIcial*.
6. **Words ending in -ity, -graphy, -logy** are stressed on the third syllable from the end: *aBILity, phoTOgraphy, bioLOgy*.
7. **Prefixes and suffixes** such as *un-, -ness, -ing* usually do not move the stress: *HAPpy → unHAPpy*.

**Sentence stress and rhythm.** In connected speech the **content words** (nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs) are stressed and the **function words** (articles, prepositions, auxiliaries) are weak and often contain /ə/. English is **stress-timed**, so the two sentences below, which have different numbers of syllables, take nearly equal time between the stressed words: *DOGS chase CATS.* *The DOGS are CHASing the CATS.*

**Intonation.** A **falling tone** is used for statements, commands and wh-questions (*Where do you live?*). A **rising tone** is used for yes/no questions (*Are you coming?*) and for lists that are not finished.

| Item | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun / verb | First / second syllable | *PREsent / preSENT* |
| -ic, -tion | Syllable before the ending | *ecoNOmic* |
| -ity | Third from the end | *aBILity* |
| Wh-question | Falling tone | *Where is it?* |
| Yes/no question | Rising tone | *Is it here?* |

**Conclusion.** Correct stress and rhythm help a listener understand speech and decide the meaning and the part of speech of a word.
