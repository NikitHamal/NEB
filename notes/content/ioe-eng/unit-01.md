Grammar I carries about **4 of the 16 English questions** (our estimate): agreement of the verb with its subject, the sequence of tenses, direct and indirect speech, and the transformation of sentences. The official model paper tests concord ("It ... two weeks since he left the city") and tense at both mark levels. The rules are few and rigid, so this is the most reliable part of the English paper to score in full.

::: key What the paper asks from this chapter
- Concord: collective and singular-looking subjects, *either … or*, *neither … nor*, *each / every / one of*, *a number of* versus *the number of*.
- Tense sequence: past in the main clause pulls the subordinate clause into the past; universal truths stay in the present.
- Indirect speech: pronoun, tense and time-word changes; the reporting verb for commands, requests, exclamations, questions.
- Transformation: simple ↔ compound ↔ complex, affirmative ↔ negative, assertive ↔ exclamatory ↔ interrogative, degrees of comparison.
:::

## 1.1 Sequence of tense and concord (agreement)

### Sequence of tenses

If the main verb is in the **past tense**, the verb in the subordinate clause is normally in a past form: *I knew that he **was** honest. She said that she **had finished**.* After a **present or future** main verb, any tense that suits the meaning may follow. **Exceptions** (the subordinate clause keeps the present): a universal truth (*The teacher said that the sun **rises** in the east*); a habitual fact still true; a comparison clause with *than* or *as* (*He loved her more than he **loves** me*); a conditional or purpose clause with the present (*She works hard so that she **may** succeed* becomes *so that she **might** succeed* after a past main verb).

| Main clause | Subordinate clause |
|---|---|
| He says that | he is / was / will be busy |
| He said that | he was / had been / would be busy |
| I have lived here since | 2020 (present perfect + *since* point, *for* period) |
| It is two weeks since | he **left** (simple past): *It is two weeks since he left*; *It has been two weeks since he left* |
| No sooner had he arrived | than it began to rain (*Hardly … when*) |
| By the time we arrived | the film **had** started |

### Concord

The verb agrees with its **subject**, not with the noun nearest to it.
- **Singular subjects:** *each, every, either, neither, one of, the news, mathematics, physics, economics, measles*, sums of money or distance treated as a unit (*Ten kilometres is a long walk*). *Each of the students **was** given a prize.* *Neither of the answers **is** right.*
- **Plural:** *the police, cattle, people, the poor*, *a number of* (*A number of students **are** absent*); but **the number of** is singular (*The number of applicants **is** increasing*).
- **Either … or, neither … nor, not only … but also:** the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject (*Either John or his brothers **are** responsible*).
- **Joined by *and***: plural (*Tea and coffee **are** …*) unless the pair is one idea (*Bread and butter **is** my breakfast*) or *each / every* precedes (*Every man and woman **has** …*).
- **With, as well as, along with, together with, besides** do not change the number: *The teacher, with his students, **is** coming.*
- **Collective nouns** (committee, team, jury, family) take a singular verb when the group acts as one, plural when members act separately.
- **Relative clauses:** *She is one of those girls who **are** always on time.* *He is the only one of the boys who **was** late.*
- *The* + adjective (*the rich, the young*) is plural. *More than one* + singular noun takes a singular verb.
- **Subject–verb inversion:** *There **are** two books on the table. Here **comes** the bus.*

::: caution Concord traps
A phrase between subject and verb ("of the students", "with his friends") is ignored when choosing the verb. *Neither* with two items takes a singular verb in formal English. *Pair of scissors / trousers* is plural; *a pair of shoes **is*** is singular.
:::

## 1.2 Direct and indirect speech

Remove the quotation marks and the comma, add **that** (or *if / whether* or a question word), then change the **tense, pronouns and adverbs** if the reporting verb is past.

| Direct | Indirect (past reporting verb) |
|---|---|
| simple present | simple past |
| present continuous | past continuous |
| present perfect / simple past | past perfect |
| will / can / may / shall | would / could / might / should |
| now, today, tomorrow, yesterday | then, that day, the next day / the following day, the day before |
| this, here, ago, last night | that, there, before, the previous night |

**Pronouns** shift by sense (first person → the speaker, second → the listener, third unchanged). Tense does **not** change for universal truths, or when the reporting verb is present or future.

- **Statements:** *He said, "I am busy now." → He said that he was busy then.*
- **Questions:** use *asked* and make the order of a statement, no auxiliary *do*: *She asked, "Where do you live?" → She asked me where I lived.* Yes/no: *if / whether*. *"Where have you been?" asked the teacher → The teacher asked where he had been.*
- **Commands and requests:** *told / ordered / asked / requested / advised* + object + *to*-infinitive (negative: *not to*): *"Please shut the door," he said to me → He requested me to shut the door.*
- **Exclamations and wishes:** *exclaimed with joy / sorrow / surprise that…*, *wished*, *prayed*: *She said, "Alas! I am ruined." → She exclaimed with sorrow that she was ruined.*
- **Suggestions:** *Let us go* → *He suggested that they should go* / *proposed that…*
- **Greetings:** *wished / greeted*; **thanks:** *thanked*.

## 1.3 Kinds of sentences and transformation of sentences

**Kinds by function:** assertive (statement), interrogative, imperative, exclamatory, optative. **By structure:** *simple* (one finite verb clause), *compound* (two or more independent clauses joined by *and, but, or, so*), *complex* (one independent + one or more dependent clauses: noun clause, adjective clause, adverb clause), compound-complex. *If you work hard, you will succeed* is complex.

| Transformation | Example |
|---|---|
| assertive → exclamatory | *It is a very hot day.* → *What a hot day it is!* / *How hot the day is!* |
| assertive → interrogative | *Nobody can deny it.* → *Who can deny it?* |
| affirmative → negative | *He is the best player.* → *No other player is as good as he.* |
| positive → comparative / superlative | *Few boys are as tall as he.* → *He is taller than most boys.* |
| *too … to* → *so … that* | *He is too weak to walk.* → *He is so weak that he cannot walk.* |
| complex → simple | *Because he was ill, he could not attend.* → *Because of his illness he could not attend.* |
| simple → compound | *Besides being rich, he is generous.* → *He is not only rich but also generous.* |
| complex → compound | *Though he is poor, he is honest.* → *He is poor but honest.* |
| *as soon as* → participial | *As soon as he saw the police, he ran away.* → *On seeing the police, he ran away.* |
| *if* → *unless* | *If you do not hurry, you will be late.* → *Unless you hurry, you will be late.* |

Tag questions reverse polarity: *She can swim, can't she?* / *He isn't coming, is he?*

## Formula and fact sheet

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| *the number of* / *a number of* | singular / plural |
| *either … or*, *neither … nor* | verb agrees with the nearer subject |
| *each, every, either, neither, one of* | singular |
| past main verb | past or past perfect in the clause (except universal truths) |
| indirect speech | *now → then; tomorrow → the next day; here → there* |
| command → indirect | *told / ordered + to-infinitive* |
| question → indirect | statement order; *if / whether* for yes–no |
| *too … to* | *so … that … cannot* |

## Chapter summary

- The verb agrees with the head noun; "with", "as well as" and "of …" phrases do not count.
- Universal truths keep the present tense in indirect speech.
- Indirect questions use statement order with no *do*.
- Pick the reporting verb that matches the force of the original (*ordered, requested, exclaimed*).
- Transformation keeps meaning exactly: only the form changes.

## Practice questions

**Set A — Recall (14 questions)**

1. Neither of the two boys ___ present.
   (a) were (b) are (c) is (d) have been
2. The news ___ true.
   (a) are (b) is (c) were (d) have been
3. He said, "I am busy now." He said that he ___ busy then.
   (a) was (b) is (c) were (d) will be
4. She asked, "Where do you live?" She asked me where ___.
   (a) do I live (b) did I live (c) I live (d) I lived
5. "Please shut the door," he said to me. He ___ me to shut the door.
   (a) requested (b) said (c) told that (d) wished
6. The exclamatory form of "It is a very hot day" is
   (a) How hot day it is! (b) What a hot day it is! (c) What hot day is it! (d) How a hot day it is!
7. "If you work hard, you will succeed" is a ___ sentence.
   (a) simple (b) compound (c) complex (d) compound-complex
8. Each of the students ___ given a prize.
   (a) were (b) are (c) have been (d) was
9. The sun ___ in the east.
   (a) rose (b) rises (c) has risen (d) is rising
10. I ___ here since 2020.
    (a) live (b) lived (c) have lived (d) am living
11. The police ___ looking for the thief.
    (a) is (b) was (c) has (d) are
12. He told me, "I will come tomorrow." He told me that he would come ___.
    (a) the next day (b) tomorrow (c) yesterday (d) today
13. Either John or his brothers ___ responsible.
    (a) are (b) is (c) was (d) has been
14. She can swim, ___?
    (a) can she (b) isn't she (c) doesn't she (d) can't she

::: note Answers and explanations — Set A
**1.** (c) — *neither of* + singular verb.
**2.** (b) — *news* is a singular uncountable noun.
**3.** (a) — present → past; *now → then*.
**4.** (d) — indirect question: statement order and past tense.
**5.** (a) — a polite request is reported with *requested*.
**6.** (b) — *What a + adjective + noun + subject + verb!*
**7.** (c) — one main clause plus an *if* adverbial clause.
**8.** (d) — *each* takes a singular verb.
**9.** (b) — a universal truth takes the simple present.
**10.** (c) — *since* + point of time needs the present perfect.
**11.** (d) — *the police* is plural.
**12.** (a) — *tomorrow → the next day*.
**13.** (a) — the verb agrees with the nearer subject, *brothers*.
**14.** (d) — positive statement, negative tag with the same auxiliary.
:::

**Set B — Understanding (8 questions)**

1. The number of applicants ___ increasing every year.
   (a) is (b) are (c) were (d) have been
2. He said, "Let us go for a walk." In indirect speech: He ___ that they should go for a walk.
   (a) said (b) ordered (c) proposed (d) asked
3. She said, "Alas! I am ruined." The best indirect form is: She ___ that she was ruined.
   (a) told (b) exclaimed with sorrow (c) said (d) asked
4. "She is too tired to work" means
   (a) She is very tired but she works (b) She is tired enough to work (c) She is too tired and works (d) She is so tired that she cannot work
5. Which is the simple form of "Because he was ill, he could not attend"?
   (a) He was ill, so he could not attend (b) He could not attend, being ill though (c) Because of his illness he could not attend (d) If he was ill he could not attend
6. I knew that he ___ a liar.
   (a) is (b) was (c) has been (d) will be
7. One of the books on the shelf ___ missing.
   (a) is (b) are (c) were (d) have been
8. Neither the manager nor the employees ___ aware of the change.
   (a) is (b) was (c) has been (d) were

::: note Answers and explanations — Set B
**1.** (a) — *the number of* is singular (*a number of* is plural).
**2.** (c) — *Let us* is reported as a proposal or suggestion.
**3.** (b) — "Alas!" expresses sorrow; the reporting verb is *exclaimed with sorrow*.
**4.** (d) — *too … to* means *so … that … cannot*.
**5.** (c) — the clause becomes a phrase: *because of* + noun.
**6.** (b) — past main verb, past subordinate verb.
**7.** (a) — the subject is *one*, not *books*.
**8.** (d) — nearer subject *employees*, plural, with a past-time context.
:::

**Set C — Application (6 questions)**

1. In "Each of the boys have done his work", the error is corrected by replacing *have* with
   (a) has (b) had (c) are (d) were
2. "Where have you been?" asked the teacher. The indirect form: The teacher asked where ___.
   (a) he has been (b) had he been (c) have he been (d) he had been
3. The teacher said to the class, "Do not make noise." The teacher ___ the class not to make noise.
   (a) said that (b) ordered (c) asked that (d) told that
4. "He is the best player in the team" is equivalent to
   (a) He is better than all players in the team (b) He is better than other player in the team (c) No other player in the team is as good as he (d) Some players are as good as he
5. Mathematics ___ my favourite subject.
   (a) are (b) were (c) have been (d) is
6. "Will you come to the party?" she asked him. → She asked him whether he ___ come to the party.
   (a) will (b) would (c) can (d) did

::: note Answers and explanations — Set C
**1.** (a) — *each* is singular.
**2.** (d) — past perfect in the clause, statement order.
**3.** (b) — a command is reported with *ordered / told ... not to*; options (a), (c) and (d) are ungrammatical with "that".
**4.** (c) — the superlative becomes a negative comparison with *as … as*.
**5.** (d) — *mathematics* is singular despite its *-s*.
**6.** (b) — *will* → *would*.
:::
