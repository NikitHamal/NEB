A **determiner** is the little word that opens a noun phrase and tells you how much
of the noun is meant and which one: *the, a, an, this, my, some, every, three, much*.
The three articles — *a*, *an* and **zero** (no article at all) — are the part
Nepali learners lose most marks on, because Nepali has no article system. NEB Q7
tests articles almost every year, usually as a fill-in-the-blank passage or as an
error-correction item where an article is missing or wrongly present.

::: key The two questions that settle almost every article
**(1) Is the noun countable and singular?** If yes, it *must* have a determiner —
*a/an*, *the*, *my*, *this*, *one*… A bare singular countable noun is almost always
an error. **(2) Does the listener already know which one?** If yes, use **the**;
if not, use **a/an**.
:::

## 23.1 The noun phrase and the order of determiners

Determiners come in three positions, and they always appear in this order before
the adjectives and the noun:

**pre-determiner → central determiner → post-determiner → adjective(s) → noun**

| Slot | Words | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-determiner | all, both, half, double, such, what | **all** the students |
| Central determiner | a, an, the, this, that, these, those, my, your, his, some, any, no, every, each, either, neither | all **the** students |
| Post-determiner | one, two, three…, first, next, last, many, few, several | all the **first ten** students |
| Adjective | any descriptive word | all the first ten **hard-working** students |

Two central determiners cannot stand together. You cannot write ~~the my book~~ or
~~a this pen~~; say *my book*, *this pen*, or *a book of mine*.

## 23.2 The indefinite article: *a* and *an*

Use *a/an* before a **singular countable noun** that is mentioned for the first
time or is one of many.

**The a/an rule is about sound, not spelling.** Use **an** before a vowel *sound*
and **a** before a consonant *sound*.

| Write *a* | Because the sound is | Write *an* | Because the sound is |
|---|---|---|---|
| a university | /juː/ — a *y* sound | an umbrella | /ʌ/ — a vowel |
| a European tourist | /jʊə/ | an hour | /aʊ/ — the *h* is silent |
| a one-rupee coin | /wʌn/ — a *w* sound | an honest officer | silent *h* |
| a useful book | /juː/ | an heir | silent *h* |
| a historic day | /h/ is pronounced | an MA in English | "em" — a vowel sound |
| a B.Ed. graduate | "bee" | an SLC certificate | "ess" |
| a unique idea | /juː/ | an X-ray machine | "eks" |

**Uses of *a/an*:**

1. **First mention.** *A tourist asked me for directions. **The** tourist was from
   Japan.*
2. **One of a class / a job.** *My sister is **an** engineer.* *Nepal is **a**
   landlocked country.*
3. **"One" with numbers and measures.** *Rs 500 **a** kilo*, *twice **a** week*,
   *80 kilometres **an** hour*.
4. **In exclamations with *what*.** *What **a** beautiful view of Machhapuchhre!*
5. **Before a singular noun standing for the whole class.** *A cow is a domestic
   animal.*

::: caution The missing article before a singular countable noun
A singular countable noun almost never stands alone.
~~He is teacher.~~ → **He is a teacher.**
~~I bought book yesterday.~~ → **I bought a book yesterday.**
~~She went to shop.~~ → **She went to a shop** / **to the shop.**
~~My father is farmer in Dang.~~ → **My father is a farmer in Dang.**
Before writing any singular countable noun, ask: *a, the,* or a possessive?
:::

## 23.3 The definite article: *the*

Use **the** when the listener can identify **which one** you mean. Ten situations
cover nearly every exam item.

1. **Second and later mentions.** *I saw a snake near the shed. **The** snake was
   nearly two metres long.*
2. **Only one of its kind in the world or in the context.** *the sun, the moon,
   the sky, the earth, the equator; **the** headmaster of our school*.
3. **Made definite by a following phrase or clause.** ***the** boy **who won the
   prize***, ***the** capital **of Nepal***.
4. **Superlatives and ordinals.** *Everest is **the** highest peak.* *She was **the
   first** woman to climb it.*
5. **Rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges, groups of islands, deserts, canals,
   gulfs.** *the Koshi, the Bagmati, the Bay of Bengal, the Himalayas, the
   Mahabharat range, the Sahara, the Maldives*.
6. **Countries whose names are plural or contain a common noun.** *the United
   States, the Netherlands, the United Kingdom, the Philippines, the Republic of
   India*.
7. **Newspapers, historic buildings, monuments, ships, organisations.** *The
   Kathmandu Post, the Dharahara, the Taj Mahal, the United Nations*.
8. **Nationalities and adjectives used as plural nouns.** *the Nepalese, the
   Chinese, the rich, the poor, the blind, the unemployed*.
9. **Musical instruments and inventions.** *She plays **the** madal.* ***The**
   telephone changed communication.*
10. **A singular noun standing for a whole class or species.** ***The** tiger is
    disappearing from the Terai.*

## 23.4 The zero article — where students actually lose the mark

The hardest part of the system is knowing when to use **no article at all**. Learn
these categories; they are what NEB error-correction items are built from.

| Use no article before | Examples | But note |
|---|---|---|
| Plural countable nouns in a general sense | *Students need books.* *Tigers are endangered.* | *the students* = particular ones |
| Uncountable nouns in a general sense | *Water is life.* *Honesty pays.* *She gave advice.* | *the water in this jug* |
| Names of people, most countries, cities, single mountains, single lakes, continents | *Sita, Nepal, Pokhara, Everest, Rara, Asia* | *the Himalayas, the Netherlands* |
| Languages and school subjects | *He speaks Nepali.* *She studies economics.* | *the Nepali of the hill region* |
| Meals | *We had lunch at one.* | *the dinner she cooked* |
| Days, months, festivals, seasons (general) | *on Friday, in Baisakh, during Tihar* | *the Dashain of 2021* |
| Games and sports | *They play volleyball.* | — |
| Means of transport with *by* | *by bus, by plane, by foot* (also *on foot*) | *on the 7 a.m. bus* |
| Places used for their **purpose** | *go to school, in hospital, at church, in bed, to court, in prison* | *go to the school* (= the building) |
| Titles followed by a name | *President Poudel, Doctor Sharma, Uncle Hari* | *the President of Nepal* |
| Abstract nouns in general | *Knowledge is power.* *Life is short.* | *the life of a porter* |
| Two nouns joined as a pair | *hand in hand, day by day, from door to door* | — |

::: caution "Go to school" versus "go to the school"
This pair is a favourite Q7 item.
*My brother goes to **school**.* — he is a student; the purpose is study.
*My father went to **the** school to meet the teacher.* — he went to the building.
The same contrast works for *hospital* (as a patient / to the building), *prison*,
*church*, *college* and *bed*.
:::

## 23.5 Demonstratives and possessives

| Determiner | Singular | Plural | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Near | this | these | *this book*, *these books* |
| Far | that | those | *that hill*, *those hills* |
| Possessive | my, your, his, her, its, our, their, Sita's | same | never with another central determiner |

*This/that* also refer to time: *this week* (now), *those days* (then).

## 23.6 Quantifiers

A quantifier tells you **how much** or **how many**. The choice depends on whether
the noun is countable, uncountable, or either.

### many / much / a lot of

- **many** + plural countable: *How **many** students appeared for the SEE?*
- **much** + uncountable: *There is not **much** water in the tank.*
- **a lot of / lots of / plenty of** + either, and preferred in positive statements:
  *We had **a lot of** rain in Shrawan.*

In modern English *much* and *many* are normal in questions and negatives, while
*a lot of* is normal in positive statements. *He has much money* sounds odd; write
*He has a lot of money*.

### few / a few / little / a little

This pair of pairs is tested constantly. The article changes the meaning.

| Form | Goes with | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| a few | plural countable | some, a small number — **positive** | *I have **a few** friends in Dharan, so I am never lonely.* |
| few | plural countable | hardly any — **negative** | ***Few** students passed, so the teacher was worried.* |
| a little | uncountable | some, a small amount — **positive** | *There is **a little** sugar left; we can make tea.* |
| little | uncountable | hardly any — **negative** | *There is **little** hope of finding them alive.* |

*Quite a few* means "a fairly large number", not a small one.

### some / any

Use **some** in positive statements and in offers or requests that expect *yes*;
use **any** in negatives and most questions.

- *I bought **some** apples at Kalimati.*
- *I did not buy **any** mangoes.*
- *Would you like **some** tea?* (offer)
- ***Any** student may enter the competition.* (= it does not matter which)

### each / every / all / both / either / neither

| Word | Noun form | Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| each | singular | singular | separately, of two or more |
| every | singular | singular | all together, of three or more |
| all | plural / uncountable | plural | the whole group |
| both | plural | plural | the two together |
| either | singular | singular | one or the other of two |
| neither | singular | singular | not one and not the other of two |

- ***Each** of the four houses **has** a separate gate.*
- ***Every** student **was** given a form.*
- ***Neither** of the answers **is** correct.*
- ***Both** the roads **lead** to Bhaktapur.*

::: caution "One of my friend"
After **one of / each of / either of / neither of / none of / some of**, the noun
must be **plural**, while the verb stays **singular**.
~~One of my friend lives in Butwal.~~ → **One of my friends lives in Butwal.**
~~Each of the boy were punished.~~ → **Each of the boys was punished.**
Also: *all/most/some of* **the** *students* — the *the* is needed after *of* when
you mean a particular group.
:::

::: tip How article items are set in Q7
Three shapes recur. (i) *Fill in the blanks with a/an/the or leave blank* — a short
passage of three or four sentences; the first mention takes *a/an* and every later
mention takes *the*. (ii) *Correct the sentence* — look first for a missing *a*
before a singular countable noun, then for a wrong *the* before a general plural.
(iii) *Choose the correct alternative* — usually *few/a few* or *much/many*. Read
the whole sentence for the positive-or-negative feel before choosing.
:::

::: example Worked transformation 23.1 — filling a short article passage
**Problem.** *Yesterday I saw ____ old man near ____ Bagmati bridge. ____ man was
carrying ____ heavy sack of rice.*

**Solution — step by step.**

1. **Blank 1.** *old man* is singular countable, first mention. Article needed. The
   sound at the start of *old* is a vowel → **an**.
2. **Blank 2.** *Bagmati* is a river, and rivers take **the**.
3. **Blank 3.** The man has already been mentioned, so the listener knows which man
   → **The**.
4. **Blank 4.** *sack* is singular countable, first mention; *heavy* begins with a
   consonant sound → **a**.

**Answer.** *Yesterday I saw **an** old man near **the** Bagmati bridge. **The**
man was carrying **a** heavy sack of rice.*
:::

::: example Worked transformation 23.2 — choosing between *few* and *a few*
**Problem.** Choose: *(Few / A few) people attended the meeting, so it was
cancelled.*

**Solution — step by step.**

1. **Check the noun.** *people* is plural countable → the pair is *few / a few*
   (not *little*).
2. **Read the result clause.** *so it was cancelled* tells us the number was
   **not enough** — a negative meaning.
3. **Match the meaning.** Negative, "hardly any" → **few**. *A few* would mean
   "some came", which does not explain a cancellation.

**Answer.** ***Few** people attended the meeting, so it was cancelled.*
:::

::: example Worked transformation 23.3 — correcting an article error
**Problem.** Correct: *The honesty is the best policy, and the poor of our country
need an information about their rights.*

**Solution — step by step.**

1. ***The honesty***. *Honesty* is an abstract uncountable noun used in a general
   sense → **no article**.
2. ***the best policy***. A superlative takes **the**. Correct as it stands.
3. ***the poor***. An adjective used as a plural noun for a class takes **the**.
   Correct.
4. ***an information***. *Information* is uncountable, so it can take no *a/an* and
   no plural → **information**, or **a piece of information** if one item is meant.

**Answer.** ***Honesty** is the best policy, and the poor of our country need
**information** about their rights.*
:::

## Chapter summary

- A singular countable noun needs a determiner: *a/an*, *the*, a possessive or a
  demonstrative. A bare singular countable noun is almost always an error.
- *a/an* = first mention or one of a class; *the* = the listener knows which one.
- *an* goes before a vowel **sound** (*an hour, an MA*), *a* before a consonant
  **sound** (*a university, a one-rupee coin*).
- *the* with: second mention, unique things, superlatives, ordinals, rivers, seas,
  mountain ranges, plural country names, newspapers, nationalities, instruments.
- **No article** with: general plurals, general uncountables, names, languages,
  subjects, meals, games, festivals, *by* + transport, and places used for their
  purpose (*go to school, in hospital, in bed*).
- *many/few/a few* + plural countable; *much/little/a little* + uncountable;
  *some/any/a lot of/plenty of* with both.
- *a few* and *a little* are positive ("some"); *few* and *little* are negative
  ("hardly any").
- After *one of / each of / neither of*, the noun is plural and the verb singular.

**Fill in the blanks with a, an, the or leave blank where no article is needed (1 mark each)**

1. My uncle is ____ honest officer in the district court. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
2. ____ Koshi floods the eastern Terai almost every monsoon. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
3. She is studying for ____ MA in English at Tribhuvan University. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
4. ____ tiger is disappearing fast from the Terai forests. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
5. My younger brother goes to ____ school at half past nine. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
6. Everest is ____ highest mountain in the world. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
7. We travelled to Janakpur by ____ bus. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
8. He earns about Rs 700 ____ day as a mason. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
9. ____ honesty is respected everywhere. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
10. There was ____ accident at Koteshwor, and ____ driver ran away. <span class="marks">[1]</span>

::: note Answers
**1.** an — *honest* begins with a vowel sound because the *h* is silent.
**2.** The — river names take *the*.
**3.** an — *MA* is read "em-ay", beginning with a vowel sound.
**4.** The — a singular noun standing for a whole species takes *the*.
**5.** (no article) — *go to school* for the purpose of studying.
**6.** the — superlatives take *the*.
**7.** (no article) — *by* + means of transport takes no article.
**8.** a — *a* here means "per".
**9.** (no article) — an abstract uncountable noun in a general sense.
**10.** an … the — first mention takes *an*; *the driver* is identified by the accident just mentioned.
:::

**Choose the correct alternative (1 mark each)**

11. Only (few / a few) candidates answered all the questions, so the pass rate was low. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
12. There is (little / a little) rice left, so we can cook khichadi tonight. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
13. How (much / many) sisters does Kamala have? <span class="marks">[1]</span>
14. He does not have (much / many) money to pay the hostel fee. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
15. (Each / Every) of the two candidates was given ten minutes. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
16. (Neither / None) of my two brothers has a driving licence. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
17. I did not buy (some / any) vegetables at the market. <span class="marks">[1]</span>
18. (All / Every) the students of Grade 11 attended the orientation. <span class="marks">[1]</span>

::: note Answers
**11.** few — the result clause is negative, so "hardly any".
**12.** a little — a positive meaning: enough to cook with.
**13.** many — *sisters* is plural countable.
**14.** much — *money* is uncountable, and the sentence is negative.
**15.** Each — *each of* is used with two or more; *every of* does not exist.
**16.** Neither — *neither* is used for exactly two.
**17.** any — negatives take *any*.
**18.** All — *every* cannot be followed by *the*; *all the students* is correct.
:::

**25.** Each of the students **was** given a certificate. — *each of* takes a singular verb.
**26.** I met **a** university professor … — *university* begins with the consonant sound /j/.
