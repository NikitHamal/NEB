Thirty years ago an office was paper, a typewriter and a ledger. Today it is a computer, a network, a printer and a software package, and the ledger is a database. **Information technology** has changed not only the speed of office work but the nature of it: a salary sheet that took three days is produced in an hour, a bank reconciliation that took a morning takes a minute, and the accounts of a whole country can be consolidated overnight. This unit explains what information technology is, the office packages and the internet, what the computer is used for in an office, its advantages and disadvantages, the software used in business accounting, and the information systems used in Nepal's government accounting.

## 15.1 Computer and information technology: introduction

A **computer** is an electronic device that accepts data, processes it according to a set of stored instructions, stores the result and gives it out in a usable form. Its working cycle is **input → process → store → output**, and its five physical parts are the input unit, the processing unit (the CPU), the memory, the output unit and the storage devices.

**Information technology (सूचना प्रविधि)** is the whole set of devices, software, networks and methods by which information is collected, stored, processed and communicated. Information technology is therefore wider than the computer: it includes the printer, the scanner, the telephone, the internet, the software and the people who use them.

**Main components.**

| Component | What it does | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Hardware** | The physical equipment | CPU, monitor, keyboard, mouse, printer, scanner, router |
| **Software** | The instructions that make the hardware work | Operating system (Windows, Linux), application packages (Word, Excel) |
| **Network** | The connection between computers | LAN in an office, the internet, Wi-Fi |
| **Data** | The raw facts the office works with | Names, amounts, dates, stock quantities |
| **People** | The users | Clerk, accountant, manager, computer operator |
| **Procedures** | The rules for using the system | Password policy, daily backup, entry checks |

An office needs all six. A computer with no trained operator, or with no rule about backup, is not an information system — it is a piece of furniture.

## 15.2 Office packages used in office operation

An **office package (अफिस प्याकेज)** is a set of application software that does the ordinary work of an office. The commonest is the **Microsoft Office** family, with free alternatives such as LibreOffice.

| Package | What it is for | An example of its use |
|---|---|---|
| **Word processor** (Word) | Typing, formatting and printing text | A letter, a notice, a report, a memorandum, a contract |
| **Spreadsheet** (Excel) | Calculations in rows and columns, with formulae, charts and sorting | A salary sheet, a budget, a stock register, a bank reconciliation |
| **Presentation** (PowerPoint) | Slide shows | A briefing to the office head, a training session, a project proposal |
| **Database** (Access) | Storing and querying large structured records | A staff database, a store register, a debtors list |
| **Accounting package** (Tally, Swastik) | Recording transactions and producing the accounts | Day books, ledgers, trial balance, final accounts |
| **E-mail and calendar** | Sending messages and fixing appointments | Official correspondence, meeting invitations |
| **Graphics and PDF tools** | Preparing and reading documents in a fixed form | Designing a notice, converting a report to PDF |

**Spreadsheet uses worth remembering for the exam.** In a spreadsheet the cell holds a value or a **formula**; a formula begins with `=` and refers to other cells (for example `=SUM(B2:B20)`); and when the figure in a cell changes, every result that depends on it updates by itself. That one feature — automatic recalculation — is why a spreadsheet is the office's most useful tool for salary sheets, budgets, stock registers and depreciation schedules.

## 15.3 Internet: introduction and use in an office

The **internet** is a worldwide network of computers that are connected to one another and can exchange information using common rules (protocols). The **World Wide Web** is the part of the internet that carries pages of information; **e-mail** carries messages; and other services carry files, voice and video.

**Uses of the internet in an office.**

1. **Communication** — e-mail, instant messaging and video meetings, which replace much of the post and much of the travel.
2. **Information** — searching for a rate, a rule, a circular, a standard, a supplier's catalogue, or a government form.
3. **Government services** — filing tax and VAT returns, registering a company, applying for a licence, checking a PAN, downloading an official form.
4. **Banking** — checking a balance, transferring funds, paying a bill; internet banking means the office does not have to visit the branch.
5. **Publishing** — an office's own website or social media page carries notices, results and tenders to the public at almost no cost.
6. **Cloud working** — documents and accounts kept on a remote server, so that staff in two districts can work on the same file.
7. **Procurement** — electronic government procurement (E-GP) publishes tenders and receives bids online.

The internet also brings risk: electronic fraud, phishing messages that look like a bank's, viruses, and the leakage of confidential information. An office therefore needs a written policy on what may be sent, which sites may be used and how passwords and backups are handled.

## 15.4 Use of computer technology in office operation

| Area of office work | How the computer is used |
|---|---|
| **Correspondence** | Letters, notices and reports typed in Word, stored in folders, mailed out and filed electronically |
| **Records and filing** | Scanning paper into electronic files; naming and storing them in a folder structure; searching by name, number or date instead of walking to the record room |
| **Accounts** | Day books, ledgers, trial balance and final accounts in an accounting package; automatic posting and automatic totals |
| **Payroll** | Salary computed from a spreadsheet or payroll software, with provident fund, income tax and other deductions calculated automatically |
| **Store and inventory** | Bin card and store ledger maintained in a database; the balance updates on every receipt and issue; a reorder alert when stock falls |
| **Budgeting and reporting** | Spreadsheets preparing the budget, comparing actual with budget, and drawing graphs for the monthly report |
| **Bank work** | Bank reconciliation and internet banking |
| **Communication** | E-mail, video meetings, shared calendars |
| **Data analysis** | Sorting and filtering data, preparing charts, finding an average or a total in a moment |
| **Security** | Passwords and user rights limiting who may see and change which record; audit trails showing who entered what |

The last row is important. A computer system can be **more** controlled than a paper one, because it can be made to record what it did and who did it. But that only happens if the office sets the user rights and reads the log.

## 15.5 Advantages and disadvantages of information technology

**Advantages.**

1. **Speed** — a calculation that took an hour is done in a moment.
2. **Accuracy** — the machine does not make arithmetic mistakes; errors come from the data entered, not from the adding.
3. **Storage and retrieval** — thousands of records in a small space, found in seconds.
4. **Automatic posting and updating** — one entry updates the ledger, the trial balance and the reports.
5. **Reporting** — statements and charts produced at any time, for any period, without rewriting anything.
6. **Communication** — instant, cheap and documented.
7. **Less paper and less space** — an electronic record needs no cabinet.
8. **Better control** — user rights, passwords and audit trails.
9. **Consistency** — the same format and the same calculation for everybody.

**Disadvantages.**

1. **Cost** — hardware, software, licences, maintenance, internet and training are expensive for a small office.
2. **Dependence** — if the system fails, the work stops; a paper office can still write with a pen.
3. **Data loss** — a hard disk failure, a virus or a fire can destroy everything unless there is a backup.
4. **Fraud and hacking** — electronic money can be moved in a moment and records can be altered; without controls the fraud is larger and harder to trace.
5. **Training** — staff must be trained, and staff change.
6. **Unemployment** — routine clerical jobs are reduced, which is a social cost.
7. **Health** — long hours at a screen cause eye strain, backache and repetitive strain.
8. **Garbage in, garbage out** — a computer is only as good as the data entered; a wrong entry produces wrong accounts faster than a clerk ever could.
9. **Loss of the paper trail** — unless the system keeps an audit trail, there is no signed voucher to check afterwards.
10. **Obsolescence** — hardware and software must be replaced every few years.

## 15.6 Use of information technology in business accounting

In a business, the computer has changed accounting from a manual task into a system.

**Recording.** Transactions are entered once in the accounting package from the voucher. The package posts the entry to the ledger automatically, so there is no separate posting stage and no posting error.

**Ledgers and trial balance.** The ledger, the debtors' and creditors' accounts and the trial balance are always up to date — not at the end of the month, but at the moment of entry.

**Final accounts.** The trading account, the profit and loss account and the balance sheet are produced for any period at the press of a key, together with the ratios, the graphs and the comparison with last year.

**Specific jobs.** Payroll with statutory deductions; inventory valuation by FIFO or weighted average; depreciation computed automatically from the asset register; bank reconciliation against an imported bank statement; VAT computation and the tax invoice; ageing of debtors; and the budget against actual statement.

**Controls in a computerised system.** The advantages come with duties: passwords and user rights so that only the authorised person may enter or change an entry; a **backup** taken daily and kept in a second place; the **audit trail** left on, so that every entry shows who made it and when; a check that the data entered agrees with the voucher; and a printed copy of the statements signed and filed, because an electronic file alone is not evidence.

**Accounting software used in Nepal.** For trading and small businesses: **Tally, Swastik, Easy Account, Quick Book**. For banks and financial institutions: **Pumori, Finacle, FlexCube, Globus**. For non-profit and social organisations: **FASS, Peach Tree**. The choice depends on the size of the business, the number of users and the reporting the law requires.

## 15.7 Information systems used in Nepal's government accounting

Government accounting has its own systems, because a government office must answer to the budget, the release, the chart of accounts and the auditor, and ordinary commercial software does not do that. Nepal has built a family of them.

| System | Full name | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **BMIS** | Budget Management Information System | Budget preparation, release and budget transfer; used in the Ministry of Finance |
| **LMBIS** | Line Ministry Budget Information System | Budget formulation and implementation by every ministry, department and government office; the provinces use **PLMBIS** |
| **FMIS** | Financial Management Information System | An integrated national financial reporting system; operated by the Financial Comptroller General Office |
| **TSA** | Treasury Single Account | The single account system through which the budget is released and spent, one account per district or area; operated by the Treasury and Comptroller Offices; in force at the federal and provincial levels |
| **CGAS** | Computerized Government Accounting System | Activity-based accounting system linked with LMBIS; records expenditure activity by activity and produces the reports; under the FCGO |
| **SUTRA** | Sub-National Treasury Regulatory Application | The integrated system for the local levels — budget preparation, execution, accounting and reporting |
| Others | **PAMS** public asset management, **PMIS** pension management, **E-GP** electronic government procurement, **ITS** integrated tax system, **RAS** revenue administration system | Asset records, pension records and payments, online tendering, tax administration and revenue collection |

**Why these systems matter.** Together they make the government's finances **visible**. The Ministry of Finance can see the budget it prepared being executed; the FCGO can see every office's expenditure as it happens instead of waiting for a monthly return; the Auditor General can test the data rather than only the paper; and a citizen can read a published financial statement. They also make **control** possible: a payment beyond the release or against a wrong head is refused by the system rather than discovered by the auditor two years later.

::: example Word or Excel?
**Problem.** Say which office package should be used for each job, and why: (a) typing an office order for the signature of the head of office; (b) preparing the salary sheet of 45 staff for the month; (c) preparing a stock register in which the balance must update whenever goods are received or issued; (d) preparing the slides for a training session; (e) recording the daily transactions of a business and producing the profit and loss account at the end of the year.

**Solution.**
Step 1 — (a) A letter or order is text, to be formatted and printed → **word processor (Word)**.
Step 2 — (b) A salary sheet is a calculation repeated over many rows, with totals and deductions → **spreadsheet (Excel)**, because the formulae update themselves when a figure changes.
Step 3 — (c) A stock register with an updating balance is a calculation over a list → **spreadsheet** again (or a small **database**, if the register is large and has to be queried).
Step 4 — (d) Slides for a presentation → **presentation package (PowerPoint)**.
Step 5 — (e) Recording transactions and producing the final accounts requires double entry, a ledger and reports → an **accounting package (Tally, Swastik, Easy Account)**, not a spreadsheet.

**Answer.** (a) Word, (b) Excel, (c) Excel or a database package, (d) PowerPoint, (e) an accounting package.
**What it means.** Choose the tool by the nature of the job: text by a word processor, calculation by a spreadsheet, double-entry accounting by an accounting package. Using a spreadsheet where an accounting package belongs is how businesses end up with a balance sheet that does not tally.
:::

::: example Advantages and disadvantages weighed
**Problem.** A small district office with five staff has to decide whether to computerise its accounts. Two staff have computer training and one does not. Advise the office.

**Solution.**
Step 1 — List what the office would gain. Speed and accuracy in the salary and the store registers; automatic totalling and reporting; the monthly statement of expenditure produced from the ledger; less paper and less filing space; and better control through passwords and an audit trail.
Step 2 — List what it would cost. A computer, a printer, a licensed accounting package, a backup drive and, above all, the training of the staff who have none.
Step 3 — Identify the real risks. The volume of work in a five-person office is modest, so the gain is smaller than in a large commercial office; if the one trained clerk leaves, the system may stop; and if no backup is taken, a single failure loses the year's accounts.
Step 4 — Decide. Computerise in stages: first the salary and the store register (the two jobs that repeat most), with a written backup routine and one more member of staff trained; keep a printed copy of every statement signed and filed; and move the accounts to an accounting package only when the volume justifies it and two staff can operate it.

**Answer.** Computerise by stages, train a second operator, take a daily backup and keep printed signed copies.
**What it means.** Information technology is not automatically an improvement. It is an improvement **if** the volume of work justifies it, the staff can run it and the controls — training, backup and audit trail — are in place.
:::

::: example Which government system?
**Problem.** Name the system used in each case: (a) a ministry preparing its budget proposal and sending it to the Ministry of Finance; (b) the Ministry of Finance preparing the budget and releasing money; (c) the Financial Comptroller General Office consolidating the national financial report; (d) a district office receiving a budget release and making payments from a single account; (e) a rural municipality preparing its budget and keeping its accounts.

**Solution.**
Step 1 — (a) A line ministry's budget formulation and implementation → **LMBIS** (in the provinces, PLMBIS).
Step 2 — (b) Budget preparation, release and transfer at the Ministry of Finance → **BMIS**.
Step 3 — (c) The integrated national financial reporting system run by the FCGO → **FMIS**; the activity-based accounting linked with LMBIS is **CGAS**.
Step 4 — (d) The single account through which the budget is released and spent, operated by the Treasury and Comptroller Office → **TSA** (Treasury Single Account).
Step 5 — (e) The integrated system for the local levels — budget, execution, accounting and reporting → **SUTRA**.

**Answer.** (a) LMBIS, (b) BMIS, (c) FMIS (with CGAS), (d) TSA, (e) SUTRA.
**What it means.** Each system covers one link of the same chain: the ministry plans, the Ministry of Finance budgets and releases, the FCGO consolidates, the district pays through the single account, and the local level runs its own treasury. They are integrated, so a figure entered at one end is visible at the other.
:::

::: caution The common mistake
"Computerisation automatically makes the accounts correct" is wrong, and examiners like to see the reason: a computer is only as good as the data entered — **garbage in, garbage out**. An office that enters a wrong amount gets wrong ledgers, a wrong trial balance and wrong final accounts, all of them faster and with more confidence than before. The controls — checking the entry against the voucher, user rights, the audit trail and the daily backup — are what make computerisation an improvement.
:::

## Chapter summary

- A **computer** accepts data, processes it by stored instructions, stores it and outputs it; its parts are the input unit, the processing unit, memory, the output unit and storage.
- **Information technology** is the whole set of hardware, software, networks, data, people and procedures by which information is collected, stored, processed and communicated.
- **Office packages** are the application software of an office: the word processor (letters and reports), the spreadsheet (salary sheets, budgets, registers), the presentation package (slides), the database (large structured records), the accounting package (double-entry accounts) and e-mail.
- In a **spreadsheet**, a formula refers to other cells and the results update automatically when a figure changes — which is why it is the office tool for repeated calculations.
- The **internet** is a worldwide network of computers using common rules. An office uses it for communication, information, government services, banking, publishing, cloud working and electronic procurement, and must guard against fraud, phishing, viruses and leakage.
- A computer is used in an office for correspondence, electronic filing, accounts, payroll, stores, budgeting, bank work, communication, data analysis and security.
- The **advantages** of information technology are speed, accuracy, storage and retrieval, automatic posting, instant reporting, communication, less paper, better control and consistency. The **disadvantages** are cost, dependence, data loss, fraud and hacking, training, unemployment, health, garbage in garbage out, loss of the paper trail and obsolescence.
- In **business accounting**, the computer records the transaction once, posts it automatically, keeps the ledgers and trial balance always current, and produces the final accounts and reports for any period. It needs controls: passwords and user rights, daily backup, the audit trail, and printed signed copies. Software used in Nepal includes **Tally, Swastik, Easy Account, Quick Book**; in banks, **Pumori, Finacle, FlexCube, Globus**.
- In **government accounting** Nepal uses **BMIS** (budget preparation, release and transfer in the Ministry of Finance), **LMBIS** (line ministry budget formulation and implementation; PLMBIS in the provinces), **FMIS** (integrated national financial reporting, FCGO), **TSA** (the single account through which the budget is released and spent, operated by the Treasury and Comptroller Offices), **CGAS** (activity-based accounting linked to LMBIS, under the FCGO) and **SUTRA** (the integrated system for local levels), besides PAMS, PMIS, E-GP, ITS and RAS.
- These systems make the government's finances visible and its controls automatic, and they are integrated, so a figure entered at one end is seen at the other.
