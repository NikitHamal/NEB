A government cannot run on goodwill. Roads, schools, police, pensions and the salaries of its own staff all have to be paid for, and the money comes mainly from **revenue**, of which **tax** is by far the largest part. In Nepal more than 85 per cent of total government revenue comes from tax. This unit explains where revenue comes from, what a tax is and what makes a good tax, how taxes are classified, and then two things every office worker in Nepal must know in practice — the **Permanent Account Number (PAN)** and **Value Added Tax (VAT)**.

::: key What the examiner tests here
The questions here are short and definitional: "What is indirect tax?", "Write the full form of VAT.", "What is PAN and how many digits does it have?". The numerical question, when it comes, is always the VAT chain — a producer, a wholesaler and a retailer, each selling at a higher price, and you are asked for the tax each pays and the total tax the government collects. Learn that one chain perfectly.
:::

## 3.1 Revenue: meaning and sources

**Revenue** (राजस्व) is the income a government raises to meet its public expenditure. The CDC textbook defines a tax as the money that the people are **compelled by law** to pay to the government for the public good, for which they do **not** receive a direct, individual benefit in return.

The sources of government revenue in Nepal fall into two groups.

| Group | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Tax revenue** | Compulsory payments, no direct return to the payer | Income tax, house and land tax, capital gains tax, value added tax, excise duty, customs duty, vehicle tax |
| **Non-tax revenue** | Income from services, property and other sources | Fees and charges for services, fines and penalties, registration fees, royalties from mines and forests, dividends from public enterprises, interest on loans, rent of government property, foreign grants and aid |

Tax revenue is further divided into **direct** and **indirect** taxes, and it is this division that Group A likes to test.

## 3.2 Tax: introduction, principles and objectives

A **tax** is a compulsory contribution imposed by the government on persons, goods, services and transactions, to meet the expenses of the state, without any direct and proportionate return to the person who pays it.

**Principles (canons) of taxation.** Adam Smith set out four canons in 1776 and they are still the standard answer.

1. **Equality or ability to pay.** Those who earn more should pay more; the burden should be distributed fairly.
2. **Certainty.** The taxpayer must know when, how much and where to pay. Nobody should be at the mercy of an official's guess.
3. **Convenience.** The tax should be collected at a time and in a manner that suits the taxpayer — which is why VAT is collected at the moment of sale and income tax through deduction at source.
4. **Economy.** The cost of collecting the tax must be small compared with the amount collected.

To these modern writers add **simplicity**, **flexibility** (the rate can be changed as the economy changes), **elasticity** and **neutrality** (the tax should not distort business decisions more than necessary).

**Objectives of taxation.**

- **To raise revenue** for public expenditure — the primary objective.
- **To redistribute income and wealth**, by taxing the rich at a higher rate and spending on the poor.
- **To control inflation**, by taking purchasing power out of the hands of consumers.
- **To protect domestic industry**, by placing a heavy customs duty on imported goods.
- **To discourage harmful consumption** — high excise duty on alcohol, tobacco and gambling.
- **To encourage saving and investment**, by giving concessions on amounts saved or invested.
- **To promote balanced regional development**, by giving tax holidays to industries set up in backward areas.

**Direct tax.** A direct tax is levied on a person's income or property and is paid by the person on whom it is legally imposed; the burden cannot be passed to anybody else. Dalton's words: *"A direct tax is really paid by the person on whom it is legally imposed."* For example, the tax on a salary is deducted from the salary of the person who earns it, and he cannot make anybody else bear it.

**Indirect tax.** An indirect tax is imposed on one person but borne by another. Dalton: *"An indirect tax is imposed to one person but paid partly or wholly by another."* A shopkeeper pays VAT to the government, but the money came from the customer who bought the goods. The tax is *shifted* along the chain of sale until it rests on the final consumer.

| Point | Direct tax | Indirect tax |
|---|---|---|
| Burden | Cannot be shifted | Can be shifted to the consumer |
| Levied on | Income, profit, property | Goods and services, transactions |
| Who collects | Government directly from the taxpayer | The seller, who pays it to the government |
| Example | Income tax, house rent tax, capital gains tax | VAT, excise duty, customs duty |
| Effect on prices | Does not directly raise prices | Raises the price of the goods |
| Nature | Usually progressive — the rate rises with income | Usually regressive in effect — the same rate hits the poor harder |

Taxes may also be classified as **progressive** (rate rises as income rises), **proportional** (same rate for all), **regressive** (rate falls as income rises) and **digressive**; and by the base on which they fall as taxes on income, on capital, on expenditure and on property.

## 3.4 Permanent Account Number (PAN)

A **Permanent Account Number (स्थायी लेखा नम्बर)** is a unique **nine-digit** number given by the **Inland Revenue Department** to a taxpayer and meant to last his whole working life. Once allotted, the same number stays with the taxpayer for ever — and that is why it is called *permanent*. In English it is the **Permanent Account Number**, shortened to **PAN**.

**Who must take a PAN.** Any person who carries on an industry, trade, business or profession, who is employed, and anyone who wishes to earn an assessable income or who must deduct tax at source, must obtain a PAN **before** doing so. Anyone else may also apply voluntarily.

**Two kinds of PAN.** A number taken by an individual is a **personal PAN**; a number taken for a firm or company is a **business PAN**.

**Documents needed.** For a personal PAN: name, surname, permanent address, father's name, date of birth, citizenship or identity card, and a passport-size photograph. For a business PAN: a copy of the firm's or company's registration certificate, the memorandum and articles of association or the constitution, the application form signed by the partners or directors, passport-size photographs of the signatories, copies of their citizenship certificates and any other prescribed documents.

**Why it matters.** The PAN must be quoted in bank transactions, in bills and invoices issued, in returns of income and in any other prescribed dealing. Without a PAN, a business cannot be registered properly, cannot deduct tax at source and cannot claim the benefits the tax law gives. The PAN is also the key by which the tax office can see all of a taxpayer's transactions in one place, which is exactly what makes tax evasion harder.

## 3.5 Introduction to Value Added Tax (VAT)

**Value Added Tax (मूल्य अभिवृद्धि कर)** is an **indirect tax** charged on the **value added** at each stage of production and distribution of goods and services. When a good travels from the producer to the wholesaler to the retailer to the final consumer, its value rises at every step; VAT is charged on that rise in value.

In Nepal, VAT has been in force from **1 Mangsir 2054 B.S.** It is governed by the **Value Added Tax Act, 2052** and the **Value Added Tax Rules, 2053**, and the present rate is a **single rate of 13 per cent**.

**How it works — the input-credit method.** Every registered dealer charges VAT on his sales (**output tax**) and pays VAT on his purchases (**input tax**). At the end of the period he deposits only the difference, *output tax minus input tax*, with the Inland Revenue Office. Because each dealer gets credit for the tax already paid by his supplier, no tax is charged twice on the same value. That is what makes VAT a tax only on the *value added* by each dealer.

A person whose annual turnover crosses the prescribed limit must register for VAT. A registered dealer must issue a tax invoice showing the seller's PAN, the buyer's PAN, the description, the quantity, the price, the rate and the amount of VAT, and must keep accounts and file returns at the prescribed intervals. Certain goods and services — basic agricultural produce, education and health services, and prescribed social goods — are exempt or zero-rated.

::: example The VAT chain, step by step
**Problem.** A producer makes goods worth Rs. 1,00,000 and sells them to a wholesaler for Rs. 1,00,000 plus VAT. The wholesaler sells the same goods to a retailer for Rs. 1,20,000 plus VAT, and the retailer sells them to the final consumer for Rs. 1,30,000 plus VAT. The rate of VAT is 13 per cent. Find the VAT each of the three dealers deposits and the total VAT the government receives. Show that this equals the VAT on the final sale price.

**Solution.**
Step 1 — Write down each dealer's sale and purchase and compute output and input tax.
Step 2 — **Producer:** output tax $= 13\% \times 1{,}00{,}000 = Rs.\ 13{,}000$; input tax $= 0$; tax deposited $= 13{,}000 - 0 = Rs.\ 13{,}000$.
Step 3 — **Wholesaler:** sale Rs. 1,20,000; output tax $= 13\% \times 1{,}20{,}000 = Rs.\ 15{,}600$; input tax $= Rs.\ 13{,}000$; tax deposited $= 15{,}600 - 13{,}000 = Rs.\ 2{,}600$.
Step 4 — **Retailer:** sale Rs. 1,30,000; output tax $= 13\% \times 1{,}30{,}000 = Rs.\ 16{,}900$; input tax $= Rs.\ 15{,}600$; tax deposited $= 16{,}900 - 15{,}600 = Rs.\ 1{,}300$.
Step 5 — Total deposited $= 13{,}000 + 2{,}600 + 1{,}300 = Rs.\ 16{,}900$.
Step 6 — Check: $13\% \times 1{,}30{,}000 = Rs.\ 16{,}900$. Equal.

| Stage | Sale price (Rs.) | Value added (Rs.) | Output tax (Rs.) | Input tax (Rs.) | Tax deposited (Rs.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Producer | 1,00,000 | 1,00,000 | 13,000 | — | 13,000 |
| Wholesaler | 1,20,000 | 20,000 | 15,600 | 13,000 | 2,600 |
| Retailer | 1,30,000 | 10,000 | 16,900 | 15,600 | 1,300 |
| **Total** | | **1,30,000** | | | **16,900** |

**Answer.** Producer Rs. 13,000, wholesaler Rs. 2,600, retailer Rs. 1,300; total Rs. 16,900, which is exactly 13 % of the final price of Rs. 1,30,000.
**What it means.** The final consumer bears the whole tax, but no dealer bears more than 13 % of the value he himself added. The tax is collected in instalments along the chain, which is why VAT is difficult to evade.
:::

::: example Direct tax on a salary
**Problem.** An employee's annual salary is Rs. 6,00,000. The tax-free threshold is Rs. 5,00,000 and the amount above it is taxed at 10 per cent. Find the tax payable and state whether the tax is direct or indirect, with a reason.

**Solution.**
Step 1 — Taxable income = salary − threshold $= 6{,}00{,}000 - 5{,}00{,}000 = Rs.\ 1{,}00{,}000$.
Step 2 — Tax $= 10\% \times 1{,}00{,}000 = Rs.\ 10{,}000$.
Step 3 — Classify. The employee who earns the salary is the person who pays the tax and he cannot pass the burden to anybody else.

**Answer.** Rs. 10,000; **direct tax**, because it is imposed on, and borne by, the same person.
:::

::: example Tax and non-tax revenue
**Problem.** A municipality expects the following receipts in a year: house and land tax Rs. 40,00,000; business tax Rs. 15,00,000; building-permit fees Rs. 8,00,000; fines for illegal construction Rs. 2,00,000; dividend from a municipal enterprise Rs. 5,00,000; and a grant from the provincial government Rs. 30,00,000. Find the total revenue, the tax revenue and the non-tax revenue, and the share of tax revenue.

**Solution.**
Step 1 — Tax revenue $= 40{,}00{,}000 + 15{,}00{,}000 = Rs.\ 55{,}00{,}000$.
Step 2 — Non-tax revenue $= 8{,}00{,}000 + 2{,}00{,}000 + 5{,}00{,}000 + 30{,}00{,}000 = Rs.\ 45{,}00{,}000$.
Step 3 — Total revenue $= 55{,}00{,}000 + 45{,}00{,}000 = Rs.\ 1{,}00{,}00{,}000$.
Step 4 — Share of tax revenue $= \frac{55{,}00{,}000}{1{,}00{,}00{,}000} \times 100 = 55\%$.

**Answer.** Total Rs. 1,00,00,000; tax Rs. 55,00,000; non-tax Rs. 45,00,000; tax share 55 %.
**What it means.** A local body that raises only 55 % of its money from its own taxes depends heavily on money from above — which is exactly why local governments are pressed to widen their own tax base.
:::

::: caution The common mistake
"Direct tax means paid directly to the government" is wrong. A shopkeeper pays VAT directly to the government too. The test is not *who hands over the money* but ***who bears the burden***. If the payer can pass it on to somebody else, the tax is indirect; if he cannot, it is direct.
:::

## Chapter summary

- **Revenue** is the income a government raises for public expenditure. It is **tax revenue** (compulsory, no direct return) or **non-tax revenue** (fees, fines, royalties, dividends, interest, rent, grants). In Nepal more than 85 % of revenue comes from taxes.
- A **tax** is a compulsory contribution imposed by law on persons, goods, services and transactions without a direct and proportionate return.
- Adam Smith's **canons**: equality (ability to pay), certainty, convenience and economy; modern writers add simplicity, flexibility, elasticity and neutrality.
- **Objectives of taxation**: revenue, redistribution of income, control of inflation, protection of domestic industry, discouragement of harmful consumption, encouragement of saving and investment, and balanced regional development.
- **Direct tax** is borne by the person on whom it is legally imposed and cannot be shifted (income tax, house and land tax, capital gains tax). **Indirect tax** is shifted to the final consumer (VAT, excise, customs).
- **PAN** is a unique **nine-digit**, permanent number issued by the **Inland Revenue Department**. It is compulsory for industry, trade, business, profession and employment. It is a **personal PAN** for an individual and a **business PAN** for a firm or company, and it must be quoted in bank transactions and in bills.
- **VAT** is an indirect tax on the value added at each stage of production and distribution. In Nepal it has applied from **1 Mangsir 2054 B.S.**, under the **VAT Act, 2052** and **VAT Rules, 2053**, at a single rate of **13 per cent**.
- A registered dealer deposits **output tax minus input tax**. The sum of the amounts deposited by all the dealers equals the VAT on the final sale price, so the whole tax is finally borne by the consumer.
