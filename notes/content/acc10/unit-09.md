A government office collects money as well as spends it. It receives tax, fees for a passport, examination fees, rent from government property, income from the sale of old goods. Every rupee of it must be entered against the right head, deposited into the consolidated fund through a bank on the same day as it is collected, and reported upwards. **Revenue accounting** is that side of government accounting. This unit covers what it is, the codes that name each revenue head, the formats used, the banking arrangement (why a government office may not keep cash), and how the monthly revenue statement is prepared.

## 9.1 Revenue accounting: introduction and objectives

**Revenue accounting (राजस्व लेखा)** is the branch of government accounting that records the **collection of government revenue**, its **deposit into the consolidated fund** through the bank, and its **reporting** to the Treasury and Comptroller Office and the Financial Comptroller General Office.

**Objectives.**

1. To record every rupee of revenue collected, with the evidence — the receipt, the voucher and the bank deposit slip.
2. To **deposit** the whole collection into the consolidated fund, so that no money lies with the office and none is spent before it is appropriated.
3. To show, head by head, how much revenue has been collected against the estimate — which is how the ministry knows whether the revenue target will be met.
4. To detect **leakage**: revenue collected but not deposited, or a receipt book missing a leaf.
5. To provide the figures for the **annual financial statement** of the government and for the audit.
6. To let the citizen see where the money goes: government revenue is public money and its account is public.

**Why the revenue must go to the bank at once.** A government office is not allowed to hold its collection as cash and spend out of it. Two things would go wrong if it were. First, expenditure would escape the budget, because the office would be spending money that no appropriation had authorised. Second, money would lie idle and unaccounted in thousands of drawers. So the rule is simple and absolute: **collect, deposit the same day into the government account in the bank, and record it in the bank cash book.** The only cash an office may hold is the petty cash expressly allowed to it.

## 9.2 Revenue accounting codes

Every revenue head has a **code (सङ्केत)** from the uniform chart of accounts, so that a rupee collected as an examination fee in Jhapa and a rupee collected as an examination fee in Kailali land in the same head and can be added together.

**Structure.** Government revenue is first divided into **tax revenue** and **non-tax revenue**, and each is then grouped and sub-grouped.

| Code | Head | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **11000** | कर (tax revenue) | The Tax group |
| 11111 | एकलौटी फर्म तथा व्यक्तिगत आयमा लाग्ने कर | Income tax on sole proprietorships and individuals |
| **13000 / 14000 / 15000** | गैरकर राजस्व (non-tax revenue) | The non-tax groups |
| 14151 | सरकारी सम्पत्तिको बहाल तथा अन्य आय | Rent of government property and other income |
| 14212 | सरकारी सम्पत्तिको बिक्रीबाट प्राप्त रकम | Receipts from the sale of government property |
| 14213 | अन्य बिक्रीबाट प्राप्त रकम | Receipts from other sales |
| 14216 | निजी धाराबापतको शुल्क | Fee for private use of a water source |
| 14221 | न्यायिक दस्तुर | Judicial fee |
| 14222 | हुलाक सेवा शुल्क | Postal service charge |
| 14223 | शिक्षा क्षेत्रको आम्दानी | Income from the education sector |
| 14224 | परीक्षा शुल्क | Examination fee |
| 14227 | राहदानी शुल्क | Passport fee |
| 14251 | कम्पनी रजिस्ट्रेसन दस्तुर | Company registration fee |
| 14252 | एजेन्सी रजिस्ट्रेसन दस्तुर | Agency registration fee |
| 14311 | न्यायिक दण्ड | Judicial fine |

The pattern is worth noticing: the first digit group tells you whether the money is tax or non-tax, the next digits the department or sector, and the last digits the individual head. A wrongly coded receipt is as bad as an unrecorded one, because the department's revenue statement will be wrong.

## 9.3 Formats used in revenue accounting

| Format | Used for |
|---|---|
| **Receipt (रसिद)** | Issued to the payer the moment money is received; pre-numbered and accounted for leaf by leaf |
| **आम्दानी भौचर (revenue voucher)** | Recording the revenue in the accounts, naming the head, the payer, the amount and the evidence |
| **Bank deposit slip (बैंक दाखिला फाराम)** | The paying-in slip by which the money reaches the government account in the bank |
| **Bank cash book (म.ले.प.फा.नं. २०९)** | The office's record of the money received and deposited, month by month |
| **Revenue statement (राजस्वको फाँटबारी)** | The monthly return sent to the Treasury and Comptroller Office |
| **Receipt and issue register** | The register in which the pre-numbered receipt books are issued to staff and returned |

The rule on formats is the same as everywhere in government accounting: only the prescribed form may be used, and every entry must name the head, the payer and the evidence.

## 9.4 Banking arrangement of revenue accounting

All government revenue is deposited into the **consolidated fund** through the government's banking arrangement with the **Nepal Rastra Bank** and the commercial banks.

1. **Every office has a designated bank account** — under the single account fund, one account for its transactions.
2. **Revenue is deposited the same day it is collected.** If the bank is closed or the office is far away, the deposit is made on the first working day and the reason is recorded.
3. **No revenue may be spent directly.** The office may not pay an expense out of the revenue it has collected; revenue and expenditure run through separate records and separate appropriations.
4. **Cheques are not accepted** for government revenue without the prescribed authority; the ordinary mode is cash or a direct transfer into the government account.
5. **The bank reconciliation** is prepared every month so that the office's bank cash book and the passbook agree.
6. **The Treasury and Comptroller Office** watches the deposits made by every office against the collections reported.

## 9.5 Reporting arrangement of revenue accounting

Revenue is reported upwards on a fixed timetable, and each report has its own form.

- **Daily.** The revenue collected is entered in the bank cash book the same day, and the receipt book's counterfoils are checked against the cash received.
- **Monthly.** The office prepares the **revenue statement (राजस्वको फाँटबारी)** for the month and sends it to the Treasury and Comptroller Office, together with the bank statement and the bank reconciliation.
- **Quarterly.** The Treasury and Comptroller Office and the ministry review the collection against the estimate and report any shortfall.
- **Annually.** The Financial Comptroller General Office consolidates every office's revenue into the government's annual financial statement, which is audited by the **Office of the Auditor General** and laid before Parliament.

The point of all the reporting is the same: by the time the money is a month old, three different offices know exactly how much was collected, under which head, and where it is.

## 9.6 Preparation of the revenue statement (फाँटबारी)

The **revenue statement** is the monthly return that shows, head by head, how much revenue the office has collected and how much of it has reached the consolidated fund.

**The columns.** The prescribed form carries the head code and description, the revenue collected up to the month, the amount deposited into the consolidated fund, and a column for the cash position — the balance of cash held and the amounts collected in earlier years but deposited late. The vertical check is the important one:

$$Revenue\ collected = Amount\ deposited + Cash\ still\ in\ hand$$

If that does not hold, revenue is lying somewhere — with a clerk, in a drawer, or in a receipt book whose counterfoil has not been accounted for.

::: example Prepare the revenue statement
**Problem.** During the month of Mangsir a District Education Office collected the following revenue. Prepare the revenue statement and show the amount to be deposited into the consolidated fund. It had no opening cash balance.

| Head | Description | Amount collected (Rs.) |
|---|---|---|
| 14223 | Income from the education sector | 4,42,600 |
| 14224 | Examination fee | 1,75,500 |
| 14227 | Passport fee | 60,000 |
| 14151 | Rent of government property | 2,38,800 |

Of the examination fee, Rs. 35,100 had not yet been deposited into the bank at the end of the month.

**Solution.**
Step 1 — Add the collection: $4{,}42{,}600 + 1{,}75{,}500 + 60{,}000 + 2{,}38{,}800 = Rs.\ 9{,}16{,}900$.
Step 2 — The undeposited amount is Rs. 35,100, so the deposit made in the month is $9{,}16{,}900 - 35{,}100 = Rs.\ 8{,}81{,}800$.
Step 3 — Check: collected = deposited + cash in hand → $9{,}16{,}900 = 8{,}81{,}800 + 35{,}100$. ✔
Step 4 — Write the statement head by head.

*Revenue statement for the month of Mangsir*

| Head code | Description | Revenue collected (Rs.) | Deposited into the consolidated fund (Rs.) | Cash in hand (Rs.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14151 | Rent of government property and other income | 2,38,800 | 2,38,800 | — |
| 14223 | Income from the education sector | 4,42,600 | 4,42,600 | — |
| 14224 | Examination fee | 1,75,500 | 1,40,400 | 35,100 |
| 14227 | Passport fee | 60,000 | 60,000 | — |
| | **Total** | **9,16,900** | **8,81,800** | **35,100** |

**Answer.** Total revenue collected Rs. 9,16,900; to be deposited Rs. 8,81,800 in the month; Rs. 35,100 still to be deposited on the first working day of the next month.
**What it means.** The last column is the whole purpose of the statement. A revenue statement with a cash-in-hand figure that is not explained is the first thing an auditor looks at.
:::

::: example Which head and which code?
**Problem.** Choose the correct revenue code for each of the following: (a) a fee charged to a private company for drawing water from a government spring; (b) the fee paid by a company when it is registered; (c) the rent received for a government building let to a bank; (d) a fine imposed by a court; (e) the fee paid by a candidate sitting an examination.

**Solution.**
Step 1 — (a) Private use of a water source → **14216 निजी धाराबापतको शुल्क**.
Step 2 — (b) Registration of a company → **14251 कम्पनी रजिस्ट्रेसन दस्तुर**.
Step 3 — (c) Rent of government property → **14151 सरकारी सम्पत्तिको बहाल तथा अन्य आय**.
Step 4 — (d) A court fine → **14311 न्यायिक दण्ड**.
Step 5 — (e) An examination fee → **14224 परीक्षा शुल्क**.

**Answer.** (a) 14216, (b) 14251, (c) 14151, (d) 14311, (e) 14224.
**What it means.** Every one of these is non-tax revenue — money the government earns from a service, a property or a penalty, not a compulsory contribution. That is why none of them falls under the 11000 tax group.
:::

::: example Revenue and the consolidated fund
**Problem.** An office collects Rs. 2,00,000 of revenue in a month. Its electricity bill for the month is Rs. 18,000. The head clerk proposes to pay the bill out of the money collected, "so that the office does not have to ask for a release". Say whether this is allowed, and what should be done instead.

**Solution.**
Step 1 — State the rule. All revenue must be deposited into the **consolidated fund**; money may be spent only under an **appropriation made by law**, through a payment order and a budget release.
Step 2 — Applying the rule, the proposal is **not allowed**. Paying an expense out of collected revenue would create expenditure outside the budget, and the Auditor General would report it as an irregular payment.
Step 3 — What should be done: deposit the whole Rs. 2,00,000 into the government account the same day, record it in the bank cash book and the revenue statement, and pay the electricity bill in the normal way — by issuing a payment order against the budget head for electricity, and entering the payment in the bank cash book when the release is received.

**Answer.** Not allowed. Deposit the revenue in full and pay the bill through the appropriation and the bank cash book.
**What it means.** The separation of revenue and expenditure is the whole foundation of legislative control. Once an office may spend what it collects, the budget has stopped being a law.
:::

::: caution The common mistake
"Non-tax revenue is not really revenue, so it may be kept for office expenses" is wrong. Non-tax revenue is government revenue: it must be receipted, coded, deposited into the consolidated fund and reported exactly like a tax. An office that treats a service fee as its own money has committed a serious irregularity.
:::

## Chapter summary

- **Revenue accounting** records the collection of government revenue, its deposit into the consolidated fund through the bank, and its reporting to the Treasury and Comptroller Office and the FCGO.
- Its **objectives** are to record every rupee with evidence, to deposit the whole collection into the consolidated fund, to compare collection with the estimate, to detect leakage, to supply the annual financial statement and to keep the account public.
- **Revenue must be deposited the same day.** A government office may not hold its collection as cash and may not spend out of it; expenditure requires a separate appropriation.
- Every revenue head carries a **code** in the uniform chart of accounts: **11000** is the tax group (for example **11111** income tax on sole proprietorships and individuals); **13000, 14000 and 15000** are the non-tax groups (for example **14224** examination fee, **14227** passport fee, **14251** company registration fee, **14221** judicial fee, **14151** rent of government property, **14311** judicial fine).
- The **formats** are the pre-numbered receipt, the **आम्दानी भौचर** (revenue voucher), the bank deposit slip, the **bank cash book (No. 209)**, the **revenue statement** and the receipt-and-issue register.
- Under the **banking arrangement** each office has one designated bank account, deposits revenue the same day, never pays an expense from it, reconciles the bank every month, and is watched by the Treasury and Comptroller Office.
- **Reporting** is daily (bank cash book), monthly (the revenue statement with the bank reconciliation), quarterly (review against the estimate) and annual (consolidation into the government's financial statement and audit by the Auditor General).
- The **revenue statement (फाँटबारी)** shows head by head the revenue collected, the amount deposited into the consolidated fund and the cash still in hand, and must satisfy **revenue collected = amount deposited + cash in hand**.
