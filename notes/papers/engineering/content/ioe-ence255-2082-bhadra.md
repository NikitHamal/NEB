@s 1
**Classification by the number of storeys and the height** (the usual practice; the by-laws and the codes of Nepal give the limits of the height with the road width and the seismic zone):

| Type | Storeys | Approximate height |
|---|---|---|
| **Low-rise** | 1 to 3 (4) | up to about 11 to 12 m |
| **Mid-rise** | 4 to 7 | about 12 to 35 m |
| **High-rise** | 8 and above (in Nepal a building above about 5 storeys or 17 m is called "high-rise" for the fire and the lift) | above about 35 m |
| **Skyscraper** | very tall | above about 100 m (supertall 300 m, megatall 600 m) |

The type decides the structural system, the lift, the fire escape, the fire-fighting and the seismic requirement.

**Structures that are not buildings (they have no roof and the occupancy or are not for the habitation):** a **bridge** and a **dam**; also the **retaining wall, the boundary wall, the tower and the chimney, the water tank, the tunnel, the culvert, the road** and the transmission pylon.

@s 2
**Lighting.** The provision of the **light (the natural daylight and the artificial light)** in a building so that the occupants can see, work and move safely and comfortably; the natural lighting is by the windows, the skylights, the ventilators and the light wells (the daylight factor 2 to 5 %), and the artificial lighting is by the lamps and luminaires (the incandescent, the fluorescent, the LED), measured as **illuminance in lux** (e.g. 100 to 150 lux for the living room, 300 to 500 lux for the office, 500 lux for the drawing office). It must be adequate, uniform and free from the glare.

**Types of mechanical ventilation.** Fans move the air when the natural ventilation is inadequate (the deep plan, the basement, the kitchen, the toilet, the hall):

1. **Extract (exhaust) ventilation:** the fans **remove the air** from the room, giving a **negative pressure** so that the fresh air enters through the openings (the kitchens, the toilets, the factory, the bathroom);
2. **Supply (plenum, the positive pressure) ventilation:** the fans **blow the fresh (filtered, and if necessary heated or cooled) air** into the room, and the air leaves through the openings (the clean rooms, the operating theatre, the boiler room, the hall);
3. **Balanced (combined supply and extract) ventilation:** both the supply and the extract fans work together, giving the best control (the **air conditioning**, the cinemas, the auditoria), with the heat recovery;
4. **Air-conditioning (the central HVAC) and the local (the window, the split) units:** the supply with the cooling, the heating and the humidity control.

**Acoustic defects other than the reverberation and the echo.**

- **Sound foci (the concave surfaces, the domes):** the curved surfaces concentrate the reflected sound at a point, giving the excessive loudness there and the **dead spots** elsewhere;
- **Dead spots (the sound shadow):** the places that get no or a weak sound, because of the reflection, the diffraction or the overhanging balcony;
- **Echelon effect:** the **rhythmic repetition** of the sound from the evenly spaced reflecting surfaces (the staircases, the railings);
- **Creeping (the whispering gallery):** the sound travels along the concave wall;
- **Resonance:** the **sympathetic vibration** of the panels or the air volume at the natural frequency, which makes some frequencies louder; **Flutter echo** (the repeated reflection between the parallel hard walls); and **the exterior noise and the insulation faults** (the lack of the sound insulation, and the **sound distortion** from the unequal absorption of the frequencies). The remedies: the splayed or the shaped surfaces, the absorbent materials, the diffusers and the proper room shape.

@s 3
**(a) Quarry-faced ashlar masonry.** The **ashlar masonry** is built of the **accurately dressed stones laid in the uniform courses with the thin joints (3 to 6 mm)** and the regular bond. In the **quarry-faced** type the face of the stone is **left as it comes from the quarry (rock-faced, the rough)**, with a **chisel-drafted margin about 25 mm wide** along the edges of the block, so that the wall has a rugged and a decorative appearance; the sides, the top and the bottom beds are properly dressed. It is used for the **fronts of the public buildings, the retaining walls, the abutments, the boundary walls and the plinth**; it is strong, durable and costly.

**Factors to consider while excavating trenches for the pipes.**

1. **Alignment and the depth:** the depth must give the **cover** (a minimum of 0.6 to 1 m under the road, 0.3 to 0.5 m in the field, below the frost depth and the traffic load), the **fall (the gradient)** for the sewers and the washout, and the clearance from the other utilities;
2. **Width:** the pipe diameter plus 0.2 to 0.3 m on both sides (the minimum 0.3 m clear), for the work and the jointing, the bedding and the compaction, but not too wide (the larger load on the pipe and the cost);
3. **Soil type and the water table:** the stability of the sides (the **side slope or the shoring** with the timber or the steel sheets in the soft soil and in the deep trench, more than 1.2 m), the **dewatering** for the high water table and the rock excavation (the blasting, the breaker);
4. **Safety and the surroundings:** the **excavated material** is kept 0.6 m or more from the edge, the barricades, the warning signs and the lights, the **traffic diversion**, the nearby building and the foundation (the underpinning), the **existing services** (the cables and the pipes), and the permission of the road authority; the bedding of the sand or the gravel (0.1 m), and the backfilling in the layers with the compaction;
5. the **economy** (the quantity of the excavation, the machine or the labour, the disposal of the surplus), the pipe material and the trench-box.

**(b) Concrete 3D printing.** An **additive manufacturing** process in which a computer-controlled robotic arm or the gantry **extrudes a special cementitious mortar/concrete (with the fine aggregate, the admixtures and the fibres) layer by layer (the layer 10 to 30 mm)** from a nozzle, following the digital (BIM/CAD) model, **without the formwork**. *Merits:* the **speed** (a house walls in a day or two), the **free-form and the complex geometry**, the less waste and labour, the low cost for the repetition, the reduced formwork; *demerits/critical points:* the **reinforcement** is difficult (the steel is placed manually, the structural and the seismic performance and the joints between the layers (the anisotropy) are not fully proven), the **special mix** with the controlled setting (the buildability), the equipment cost, the limited codes and standards, and the high cement content. Used for the housing, the **small buildings, the bridges (the pedestrian) and the architectural elements**; in Nepal it is at the research stage.

**Autoclaved aerated concrete (AAC) block.** A **lightweight precast** block made from the **fly ash or the sand, cement, lime, gypsum and the aluminium powder**, which reacts and forms the hydrogen gas bubbles (the cellular structure, 80 % air); the block is **cured in the autoclave (the high-pressure steam at 180 °C, 12 bar for 8 to 12 h)**. Density 450 to 650 kg/m³ (one-third of the concrete), the compressive strength 3 to 4.5 MPa, the thermal conductivity 0.1 to 0.16 W/m·K (a good thermal insulation), the fire resistance (the 2 to 4 h), the sound insulation, the easy cutting, and the large size block (the speed), the light dead load (the good for the earthquake); but the **low strength (the non-structural infill for the frame buildings)**, the high water absorption, the need for the thin-bed adhesive mortar and the plaster, and the cost.

**Expansion joint.** A **deliberately provided gap (20 to 40 mm) that separates the two parts of a structure**, completely through the structure from the roof to the top of the foundation, so that the parts can **move freely with the temperature change (the expansion and the contraction), the shrinkage, the creep and the differential settlement** without the cracking and the damage. It is placed at the intervals of about 30 to 45 m in the long building and the pavement, at the changes in the height, the plan and the foundation level, and at the junction of the old and the new part (the same joint also acts as a seismic gap, which is larger). It is **filled with the compressible filler (the bitumen-impregnated board, the cork or the polystyrene), and sealed on the surface** with the sealant, the metal cover plate or the water bar (the PVC) for the water retaining and the roofs, and the double column is used in the buildings.

@s 4
**(a) Ladder and staircase.**

| | Ladder | Staircase |
|---|---|---|
| Form | a **steep (60° to 90°)** set of the rungs between the two stiles, | a series of the **steps with the treads and the risers** (the slope 25° to 40°) and the landings |
| Use | for the occasional and the **temporary** access (the loft, the roof, the maintenance, the construction) | the **permanent**, regular and the safe circulation between the floors, and the escape |
| Safety, the comfort | the climbing facing the ladder, the less safe, no handrail | the comfortable, with the handrail and the balustrade, the width 0.9 to 1.5 m |
| Space, cost | the small space, cheap | the more space, the costlier |

**Types of doors based on the working mechanism, with their uses.**

1. **Swing (hinged) door:** the leaf turns on the hinges, in one direction (single-acting) or in both directions (**double-acting**, with the spring hinges); the most common, for the **rooms, the houses and the offices** (the double-acting in the hospitals and the restaurants);
2. **Sliding door:** the leaf **slides on the rollers along the track** (the top or the bottom), saves the swing space, for the **shops, the balconies, the wardrobes, the shop-fronts and the garages**;
3. **Folding (bi-fold) door:** the leaf is of the **hinged panels which fold** against each other, for the **wardrobes, the partitions and the wide openings**;
4. **Revolving door:** **three or four leaves rotate about a central pivot**, keeps the air-conditioned air in and always closed; for the **hotels, the banks and the shopping malls** (the busy entrances), with the side swing door for the emergency;
5. **Rolling shutter (roller) door:** the **steel slats roll up on the drum** above the opening; for the **shops, the garages and the warehouses (the security)**;
6. **Overhead (sectional, up-and-over) door:** the panels slide up on the horizontal track under the ceiling; for the **garages and the industrial buildings**;
7. **Collapsible (the steel) door**, the **automatic (the sensor) sliding door** (the hospitals, the airports, the malls) and the **fire-resisting doors** (the self-closing) at the fire escapes.

**(b) Suspended floor.** A floor that is **raised above the ground** and **supported on the walls or the sleeper walls**, with an **air space (the void)** below which is ventilated, so that the damp and the rot do not reach the timber; used for the timber floor of the ground storey in the damp site (and the RCC suspended floor over the basement or the hollow). The upper floors are all suspended floors supported by the beams and the walls.

**Components of a lightning arrester (protection) system.**

1. **Air terminal (the lightning rod, the finial) or the horizontal conductor and the mesh** at the highest points of the roof, the ridge and the parapet, which receives the stroke (the copper or the aluminium, 12 to 25 mm; the zone of protection is a cone of about 45° to 60°);
2. **Down conductor(s)**: the copper, the aluminium or the galvanised steel tape (25 × 3 mm) or the round rod, running **straight down the outside wall (the shortest path)** with the clips, at least two for a larger building (at the 20 m spacing), and the test joint at the 1.5 m above the ground;
3. **Earth termination (the earthing electrode):** the **plate (the 600 × 600 mm copper or GI plate), the pipe or the rod** buried in the **earth pit with the charcoal and the salt** to get a low earth resistance (below 10 Ω, preferably 5 Ω), kept moist (the watering pipe), and the earth resistance is tested;
4. **Bonding (equipotential) of the metal parts** (the metal roof, the pipes, the tanks) to the system, the **surge protective devices (SPD)** in the electric service, the **joints, the clips and the test points (the inspection chamber).**

@s 5
**(a) Damp-proofing methods.** The damp rises (the capillary) from the ground, or penetrates from the roof, the walls and the floors.

1. **Membrane damp-proofing (the DPC, the damp-proof course):** a **continuous layer of the impervious material** (the hot bitumen, the bituminous felt, the polythene sheet 1000 gauge, the lead sheet, the slate, the cement concrete 40 mm (1:2:4) with the water-proofing compound (the integral)) **at the plinth level (150 mm above the ground), in the walls and the floors, with the vertical DPC for the basement (the tanking)**;
2. **Integral damp-proofing:** the **water-proofing compounds** (the pudlo, the lime, the alum and the soap, the hydrophobic admixtures) mixed into the concrete and the mortar to make them impervious;
3. **Surface treatment:** the **water-repellent coats** (the cement plaster with the water-proofing compound, the bituminous paint, the silicone and the tiles) on the face of the wall;
4. **Cavity wall construction:** the **air gap (50 to 75 mm)** between the two leaves, which is not bridged, so the damp does not cross it (with the weep holes and the DPC trays);
5. **Guniting (the shotcrete) and the pressure grouting:** the dense mortar sprayed over the surface or injected into the cracks and the voids; also the **chemical DPC injection** into the old wall; the brick tile layer and the proper drainage (the French drain) around the building.

**Pre-construction anti-termite treatment (the soil poisoning).** The termite (the white ant) is controlled by creating a **chemical barrier in the soil** before the floor and the foundation are built (IS 6313, per the building code):

1. **Foundation trench:** clear the site of the stumps, the roots and the termite mounds (destroy the nest); the **bottom and the sides of the excavation are sprayed** with the chemical emulsion (**chlorpyrifos 20 % EC at 1 %, or the imidacloprid, the permethrin**, with the emulsion 5 L/m² of the horizontal surface and 7.5 L/m² per vertical surface in the 300 mm of the wall depth);
2. **Backfill:** the earth is treated at **7.5 L/m³** during the **backfilling** in 150 mm layers and compacted;
3. **Plinth filling and the floor:** the top surface of the **filling under the floor** is treated at 5 L/m², before the sub-floor concrete is laid;
4. **Junction of the wall and the floor and the around the building:** a **vertical barrier** is made along the plinth, the external perimeter is treated (the trench along the wall, 300 mm deep and the rod holes and the chemical), and the **service pipe openings** and the expansion joints are treated; the treatment is **not to be disturbed**, and is done when the soil is dry (not before the rain). The treated area is checked and the guarantee of 10 years is given by the contractor.

**(b) Retrofitting and repair.**

| | Repair | Retrofitting |
|---|---|---|
| Aim | restores the **damaged or deteriorated** element to its **original** condition (the cracks, the spalling, the leakage) | **upgrades** the existing structure (sound or the damaged) to a **higher performance level** than the original (the seismic, the load, the function) |
| Scope | local, and the cosmetic or the functional | global or the member-wise strengthening, the new codes |
| Examples | the patching, the epoxy injection, the re-plastering | the jacketing of the columns, the shear walls, the steel bracing, the FRP wrapping, the base isolation |

**Fire-resistant construction for the wall openings.** The openings (the doors, the windows, the ducts and the service openings) are the weak points of the **fire compartment wall**; to keep the **fire rating (the 1 to 4 h)** they are provided with:

- **fire-resisting doors** (the steel or the timber with the intumescent seals, the self-closing and the fire rating equal to the wall: the 1/2 to 2 h) and **fire-rated glazing** (the wired glass, the toughened, the ceramic or the intumescent gel glass) in the steel frame;
- **fire dampers** in the ventilation ducts, the **fire-stopping (the intumescent sealant, the collars, the mineral wool, the mortar)** around the pipes, the cables and the joints;
- the **limited size** of the opening in the fire wall (max 25 to 50 % of the wall area, or the opening of 2.1 × 1.2 m), the **protected lobby** and the **the lintels** with the sufficient concrete cover, the parapet and the spandrel between the floors, and the **sprinkler or the drencher** curtain for the glass walls.

@s 6
**Sustainable building.** A building which is **designed, constructed, operated and finally demolished in a way that minimises its impact on the environment and the resources over its whole life, while giving a healthy, comfortable, safe and economical environment to the occupants** and the society: the efficient use of the **energy, water and the materials**, the reduction of the waste and the carbon emission, the protection of the site ecology and the indoor air quality, i.e. meeting the needs of the present without harming the future (the triple bottom line: the environment, the economy and the society).

**Common rating (the assessment) systems.** The rating systems give the **points for the credits in the categories**, and certify the level.

| System | Country | Categories / levels |
|---|---|---|
| **LEED** (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design, USGBC) | USA (worldwide, the most used) | the site, the water, the energy and the atmosphere, the materials, the indoor quality, the innovation; Certified (40 to 49), Silver (50 to 59), **Gold (60 to 79), Platinum (80+)** points of 110 |
| **BREEAM** (Building Research Establishment Environmental Assessment Method) | UK (the oldest, 1990) | the management, the health, the energy, the transport, the water, the materials, the waste, the land use, the pollution; Pass, Good, Very good, Excellent, **Outstanding** |
| **GRIHA** (Green Rating for Integrated Habitat Assessment) and **IGBC** | India | the site planning, the energy, the water, the waste, the materials; one to five stars (GRIHA), Certified to Platinum (IGBC) |
| **Green Star** (Australia), **CASBEE** (Japan), **Estidama Pearl** (UAE), **Green Mark** (Singapore) and **EDGE** (IFC, the emerging markets: 20 % savings in the energy, the water and the embodied energy in the materials) | | similar |
| **Nepal:** the **Nepal National Building Code** and the green building (and the green-building and energy-efficiency guidelines of the government, still voluntary) with EDGE and LEED used in the private projects | | the voluntary criteria |

The rating improves the design at the early stage, makes the performance measurable and the comparison of the buildings possible, reduces the operating cost and gives the marketing value.
