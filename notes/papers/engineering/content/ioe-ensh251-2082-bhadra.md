**(a) Scope of technical communication for engineers.** Technical communication is the clear, accurate and efficient exchange of technical information with a particular audience. An engineer spends a large share of the working time on it, so its scope in engineering is wide:

1. **Written communication:** technical reports (feasibility, progress, inspection, final), proposals, tender and contract documents, specifications, standards, design calculations, manuals and instructions, data sheets, letters, memos, e-mails, minutes, résumés and applications, journal and conference papers, patents.
2. **Oral communication:** meetings, site briefings, presentations, project defences, telephone and video calls, interviews, negotiation, group discussion and public speaking.
3. **Visual and graphical communication:** drawings, CAD models, charts, graphs, flow charts, dashboards, photographs, posters and slides.
4. **Electronic and digital communication:** e-mail, project-management tools, websites, social media, online meetings and documentation platforms.
5. **Direction:** downward (instructions to technicians), upward (reports to managers), horizontal (between departments and disciplines) and external (clients, contractors, suppliers, government, public).
6. **Across the project life cycle:** from the feasibility study and the design to construction, operation, maintenance and closing.
7. **Value:** it avoids errors and accidents, saves cost and time, wins projects and funds, supports teamwork, and is a necessary skill for promotion and leadership; many jobs also need cross-cultural and ethical communication.

**(b) Plagiarism as a key concern in professional communication.** Plagiarism is presenting another person's words, ideas, data, designs or work as one's own without acknowledgement. It is a key concern because:

1. **Ethics and honesty:** it is a form of theft and lying; the engineers' codes of ethics require honesty and giving credit.
2. **Legal risk:** it can violate **copyright, patents and contracts**, leading to fines, lawsuits, withdrawal of a report or a lost contract.
3. **Credibility and reputation:** a discovered plagiarism destroys the trust in a person and a company; it can mean dismissal, loss of a licence or degree.
4. **Reliability of the technical record:** engineers must be able to trace data and design values to the original source to check them; false or hidden sources make decisions on safety unreliable.
5. **Fairness and innovation:** the real author loses credit; copying prevents original thinking and learning.
6. **Common forms:** copy-paste (verbatim), paraphrasing without credit, patchwork, self-plagiarism, false citation, using images, data or code without permission.
7. **Prevention:** cite every source in a standard style (APA, IEEE), use quotation marks for exact words, paraphrase correctly and still cite, keep a record of sources, ask permission and use plagiarism-checking software.

**(b) Roles of unity and coherence in effective technical writing.**

*Unity* means that **every sentence of a paragraph (and every paragraph of a document) supports one main idea**, the topic stated in the topic sentence or the thesis. *Coherence* means that the ideas are **logically ordered and connected** so that the reader moves smoothly from one idea to the next.

| | Unity | Coherence |
|---|---|---|
| Question answered | does everything belong here? | do the ideas follow and link properly? |
| How achieved | one controlling idea (topic sentence); remove irrelevant details; stick to the purpose; a title that fits | logical order (time, space, importance, cause to effect, general to specific); **transitions** (therefore, however, in addition, for example); repeated key words and pronouns; parallel structure; consistent tense and point of view; headings and numbering |
| Faults | irrelevant sentences, several topics in one paragraph | jumping ideas, missing links, illogical order |

*Roles in technical writing.*

1. They make the document **clear and easy to understand**, so that the reader finds the information quickly (important for manuals, reports and instructions).
2. They **prevent misunderstanding and errors**, since the steps of a procedure must follow exactly and no stray ideas may confuse them.
3. They **save the reader's time** and show respect for the reader; the document can be scanned using headings and topic sentences.
4. They support **persuasion and credibility:** an organised argument (problem, evidence, conclusion) convinces managers and clients.
5. They help the writer to **plan, check and revise** the document (outline, topic sentences, transitions).
6. They give the whole report a **professional structure** (introduction, body, conclusion) and make team-written documents consistent.

**(c) Corrected sentences.**

| No. | Answer | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| i | Neither Ram nor the girls **are** making presentation today. | with *neither ... nor* the verb agrees with the nearer subject, *girls* (plural) |
| ii | Your hair **has** turned grey. | *hair* (collective, uncountable) takes a singular verb |
| iii | The orchestra will give **its** final performance tonight. | the orchestra is a collective noun regarded as one unit, so *its* |
| iv | The word guitar is the antecedent of **it**. | *it* replaces the singular thing *guitar* (*him* refers to *he*) |
| v | I advise you to try, **but** you may not succeed. (or: *you may not succeed, yet I advise you to try.*) | the subordinate concessive clause becomes a co-ordinate clause |
| vi | **Although** he has discovered new facts, my opinion is unchanged. | *but* is replaced by the subordinating conjunction *although*: complex sentence |
| vii | The manager came **and** the staff became alert at once. | the subordinate clause (*as soon as*) becomes a co-ordinate clause |
| viii | **On being kicked**, the dog barked at him. (or: *The dog barked at him on being kicked.*) | one finite verb only: simple sentence |
| ix | **wants** | the finite verb (it shows tense and agrees with *she*); *to learn* is an infinitive |
| x | **They enjoy singing in the rain** | *singing* is a gerund, a non-finite verb form; *walks* in the other sentence is finite |

**(c) Research article: "Finding sustainable building design and construction" (abstract, findings, discussion).**

> **ABSTRACT**<br>
> Buildings account for about 35-40% of global energy use and nearly 40% of carbon dioxide emissions, and Nepal's rapid urbanisation, after the 2015 earthquake, makes the choice of building design and construction methods very important. This article reviews sustainable design and construction practices and assesses their performance and cost, using published studies and case examples from South Asia. The findings show that passive design, efficient insulation and lighting, local and low-carbon materials (compressed stabilised earth blocks, bamboo, fly-ash bricks, recycled steel), solar water heaters and rainwater harvesting can reduce the operational energy by 30-50% and the lifecycle emissions by 20-40%, with an extra initial cost of only 3-10% that is recovered in 5-8 years. The article concludes that sustainable and earthquake-resistant buildings are technically and economically feasible, and recommends codes, incentives and training to apply them.

> **FINDINGS**<br>
> 1. *Design:* orientation to the sun, natural ventilation and daylight, compact form and shading reduced the heating, cooling and lighting demand by 25-40% in comparable buildings.<br>
> 2. *Materials:* cement and steel give most of the embodied carbon; the use of fly ash, blended cement, recycled steel, local stone, compressed earth blocks and bamboo reduced it by 20-35%.<br>
> 3. *Systems:* LED lighting, efficient appliances, solar thermal and rooftop photovoltaic systems, and the harvesting and reuse of rainwater and grey water reduced the energy and water use by 30-50%.<br>
> 4. *Construction:* waste reduction, prefabrication and the reuse of demolition waste cut the material waste by up to 30%; the proper detailing of the structure kept the earthquake safety.<br>
> 5. *Cost:* the initial cost increases by 3-10% but the running cost falls so that the payback is 5-8 years.<br>
> 6. *Barriers:* lack of awareness, absence of enforced green codes, limited supply of skilled workers and materials, and the preference for the lowest first cost.

> **DISCUSSION**<br>
> The results confirm that the largest saving is obtained when sustainability is considered from the earliest design stage and over the whole life of the building (design, materials, construction, use and demolition), not by adding gadgets later. Passive and local solutions suit the climate and the economy of Nepal and are also safer when they follow the seismic codes. The slightly higher initial cost is the main barrier, which can be overcome by tax rebates, loans with a low interest, rating systems and the inclusion of the energy and environmental criteria in the National Building Code. Training for engineers, masons and contractors is equally important. The limitation of this study is that it relies on secondary data with different methods and climates; field measurements of the buildings in the Nepalese climate zones would give more reliable numbers.

**(a) Types of non-verbal communication for effective speaking.** Non-verbal communication is the part of the message that is sent without words. In speaking, it can **support, emphasise, or contradict** what is said, shows confidence and sincerity, and affects the way the audience reacts.

1. **Kinesics, body language:** *posture* (stand upright, relaxed shoulders, weight on both feet, no slouching), *movement* (purposeful steps, no pacing or swaying), *gestures* (open hand movements to illustrate and stress points; avoid pointing a finger, fidgeting, hands in pockets).
2. **Facial expression:** a natural smile and expressions matched to the content show warmth and honesty; a blank or tense face loses the audience.
3. **Eye contact (oculesics):** look at different parts of the audience for 3-5 seconds each; it builds trust and shows confidence; avoid reading the slides or looking at the floor.
4. **Paralanguage (vocalics):** *how* it is said: volume (loud enough for the last row), pitch variation, rate (about 120-150 words per minute), pronunciation and **pauses** for emphasis; avoid filler sounds ("um", "uh").
5. **Proxemics (space):** keep a suitable distance from the audience, move closer to involve them, avoid hiding behind the desk; arrange the seats to suit the purpose.
6. **Appearance and artefacts:** clean, formal, suitable dress and grooming and neat slides or props create credibility at the start.
7. **Haptics (touch):** a firm handshake at the beginning or end; avoid touching in an improper or culturally unsuitable way.
8. **Chronemics (time):** punctuality, keeping within the time and the pace of delivery.
9. **Silence:** a short pause before a key point or after a question gives time to think and adds impact.

**(b) Environmental and physical barriers of active listening.**

*Environmental (surrounding) barriers* come from the place and situation:

- **Noise:** traffic, machinery, other conversations, phones and notifications, a poor microphone or speaker.
- **Poor room conditions:** bad acoustics or echo, too hot, too cold or crowded, poor light and ventilation, uncomfortable seats.
- **Distractions and interruptions:** people entering, a messy surrounding, the use of mobile phones and computers.
- **Distance and arrangement:** the listener is too far or cannot see the speaker, the slides or the lips of the speaker; poor seating plan.
- **Remedies:** choose a quiet room, switch off the phones, sit near the speaker, reduce the noise and improve the sound system and the lighting, hold the meeting at a suitable time.

*Physical barriers* are the physical condition of the listener or the speaker:

- **Fatigue, hunger, sleeplessness, illness, headache** or pain reduce the attention.
- **Hearing or vision problems** and a weak speaker's voice or a very fast speech.
- **Physical discomfort,** time of day (after lunch), long meetings without breaks.
- **Technical or transmission problems** in telephone and online communication (weak signal, delay, bad quality of the line).
- **Remedies:** rest well and eat before, take breaks, use hearing aids or glasses, ask the speaker to speak louder or slower, use good equipment and a stable connection.

| Basis | Graph | Diagram |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | a visual that shows the **relationship between numerical variables** drawn on axes (scale) | a **drawing or sketch** that shows the structure, parts, working or process of an object or system |
| Based on | numerical data (measured or counted values) | structures, concepts and relationships (no scale is necessary) |
| Axes and scale | usually have x- and y-axes with scale and units | no axes; may or may not be to scale |
| Purpose | to show trends, comparisons, correlation and distribution | to explain how something looks, is built or works |
| Types | line graph, bar graph, histogram, pie chart, scatter plot | block diagram, circuit diagram, flow chart, schematic, cross-section, exploded view, organisation chart |
| Reading | read values from the scale | read the labelled parts and connections |
| Example | graph of the load against deflection of a beam | diagram of a water-treatment plant or a circuit |
