@s 1
**(a) Constituents of concrete and their functions.**

| Constituent | Function |
|---|---|
| **Cement** (OPC, PPC; 300 to 450 kg/m³) | the **binder**: reacts with water (the hydration) and forms the **paste (the C-S-H gel)** which coats and **binds the aggregate**, gives the strength and the setting |
| **Fine aggregate** (the sand, about 35 % of the aggregate) | **fills the voids between the coarse particles**, gives the **workability and cohesion**, reduces the shrinkage, the segregation and the cost |
| **Coarse aggregate** (the crushed stone or gravel, 10 to 40 mm) | the **bulk (60 to 75 % of the volume)**, gives the **strength, the volume stability, the economy** and the wear resistance; reduces the shrinkage and the heat |
| **Water** (clean, w/c 0.4 to 0.6) | takes part in the **hydration** of the cement, **lubricates** the mix (the workability), and is used for the **curing** |
| **Admixtures** (the plasticisers, the retarders, the accelerators, the air-entraining agents, the water-proofing) | **modify the properties**: workability at the lower water, setting time, durability and the strength (the optional) |
| **Reinforcement** (in RCC) | takes the tension |

**(b) Concrete as a three-phase system.** On the **micro-level** concrete is a composite of three phases:

1. **Aggregate phase:** the coarse and the fine particles, the strong and the inert, which are 70 to 75 % of the volume; their strength, the shape, the texture and the size control the strength, the modulus and the shrinkage;
2. **Hydrated cement paste (matrix) phase:** the **C-S-H gel, the calcium hydroxide, the calcium sulphoaluminates, the un-hydrated cement and the capillary and the gel pores**: the binding phase that bonds the aggregate;
3. **Interfacial transition zone (ITZ):** the thin (10 to 50 $\mu$m) **weak layer around the aggregate surface**, where the water collects (the bleeding), more porous, with the large crystals of the calcium hydroxide and the micro-cracks (the bond cracks); it is the **weakest link**, and decides the strength and the permeability and the failure of the concrete (the lower w/c and the silica fume make it denser).

(Another common meaning is **solid, liquid and gas (air)**: the solids (aggregate and cement), the water and the entrapped air voids; the concrete is the three-phase mixture in the fresh state.)

@s 2
**Methods for testing the workability** (IS 1199):

1. **Slump test:** the measure of the consistency (the plain concrete, the 25 to 125 mm);
2. **Compacting factor test:** the degree of the compaction under the standard work (the low workability, 0.7 to 0.95); $CF=\dfrac{W_{partially\ compacted}}{W_{fully\ compacted}}$;
3. **Vee-Bee consolometer test:** the time in seconds (the Vebe) for the cone to be remoulded to a cylinder by the vibration (the very dry, the low workability mix);
4. **Flow table test:** the percent spread of the concrete on the table after 15 drops (the high workability and the flowing concrete: 100 to 150 %);
5. **Kelly ball penetration test** (the depth of the 15 lb ball, the field test), the **K-slump** test, the **L-box, V-funnel and the slump-flow test** for the self-compacting concrete.

**Slump test (IS 1199:1959).** *Apparatus:* the **frustum of a cone** (the bottom diameter 200 mm, the top 100 mm, the height 300 mm), the 16 mm **tamping rod** (600 mm long, with the bullet end), the base plate (non-absorbent, level), and the scale.

*Procedure:* (1) the inside of the mould and the plate are cleaned and moistened; the mould is placed on the plate and held down by the foot-pieces; (2) the fresh concrete is filled in **4 layers of about one-quarter of the height** each, and **each layer is tamped by 25 strokes** of the rod uniformly over the section (the rod just penetrates the layer below); (3) the top is levelled with the trowel; (4) the mould is **lifted slowly and vertically** (within 5 to 10 s), and the concrete subsides; (5) the **slump = the vertical drop of the top of the concrete** (the highest point) from the top of the mould is measured to the nearest 5 mm.

*Interpretation:* the **true slump** (the uniform subsidence) is recorded; the **shear** slump (one half slides) and the **collapse** slump (the very wet) mean that the test is to be repeated and indicate the lack of the cohesion. The slump 0 to 25 mm (the very low, the roads), 25 to 75 (the low, the foundation, the mass concrete), 50 to 100 (the medium, the slabs, the beams and the columns), 100 to 150 (high, the congested bars), and above 150 (the flowing). The test is used at the site for the **uniformity** of the batches and the water control; it is not suitable for the very dry or the very wet mix.

@s 3
**Nominal mix and design mix.**

| | Nominal mix | Design (engineered) mix |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | the **fixed volumetric proportions** (1:1.5:3 (M20), 1:2:4 (M15), 1:3:6 (M10)) given by the code | **designed by the calculations and the trial** for the required strength, the workability and the exposure |
| Strength | the **assumed (the minimum guaranteed)**; no test is needed, but is **conservative** (the variable) | the **specified characteristic strength** is achieved, verified by the trials and the cubes |
| Cement | **more cement than needed (uneconomic)** | the **minimum cement** (the economy and the less heat) |
| Quality control | the simple, the volume batching, the small and the minor works | the **weigh batching, the lab control, the skilled** supervision, the mixes of M25 and above |
| Use | the small works, the M20 and below in IS 456 | the important and the large structures, the RMC |

**IS code method (IS 10262:2019) of the mix design.** Steps:

1. **Stipulations:** the grade, the max nominal aggregate size, the exposure (the min cement and the max w/c from IS 456 tables 4 and 5), the workability (the slump), the cement type, the specific gravity, the type of aggregate and the sand zone.
2. **Target mean strength:** $f_{target}=f_{ck}+1.65\,s$ (or $f_{ck}+X$, with $s$ from the table: M20 4.0, M25 4.0, M30 5.0 MPa; the table value $X$ is 5.0, 5.0 and 6.0 MPa).
3. **w/c ratio:** from the **curve of the strength and w/c** (the 28-day strength of the cement, the figure 1 in the code) for the target strength; the check with the limit of the durability, and the lower value is adopted.
4. **Water content:** the **maximum water for the 50 mm slump** from the table for the aggregate size (for the 20 mm angular aggregate, 186 kg/m³); **increase by 3 % for each 25 mm of the extra slump**; the superplasticiser reduces the water by 20 % or more.
5. **Cement content** $=\dfrac{\text{water}}{w/c}$, with the minimum for the exposure (e.g. 300 kg/m³ for the mild, 320 for the moderate, 360 for the severe) and the max 450 kg/m³ (the OPC).
6. **Proportion of the coarse aggregate:** the **volume of the coarse aggregate per unit volume of the total aggregate** from the table (the size, the zone of the sand, for the w/c = 0.5), corrected by $\pm0.01$ for each $\mp0.05$ change of the w/c (the pumpable concrete: reduced by 10 %); the fine aggregate $=1-$ the coarse.
7. **Mix calculations (the absolute volume method):** for $1\ \text{m}^3$ of the concrete, the volume of the entrapped air is taken as 2 % (0.02 m³) for the 20 mm aggregate, and

$$V_{agg}=1-\Big[\frac{C}{S_c\times1000}+\frac{W}{1000}+\frac{M_{ad}}{S_{ad}\times1000}+0.02\Big]$$

where $S_c$ is the specific gravity of the cement (3.15) and $M_{ad}$ the mass of the admixture. The masses of the aggregates are then

$$\begin{gathered}\text{fine}=V_{agg}\,(1-f_c)\,S_{fa}\times1000 \\ \text{coarse}=V_{agg}\,f_c\,S_{ca}\times1000\end{gathered}$$

with $f_c$ the volume fraction of the coarse aggregate from step 6.

8. **Trial mixes:** the trials with the corrected proportions for the workability and the 7- and 28-day strength, the **adjustment** (the water, the sand ratio, the admixture), the final mix and the **correction for the moisture** and the absorption at the site.

@s 4
**Gel/space ratio and the strength.** The **gel/space ratio $x$** (Powers) is the ratio of the **volume of the hydrated cement (the gel, including the gel pores) to the sum of the volumes of the hydrated cement and of the capillary pores** (i.e. the available space):

$$x=\frac{0.657\,\alpha}{0.319\,\alpha+w/c}\quad(\alpha=\text{degree of hydration, }0\ \text{to}\ 1)$$

(e.g. for $w/c=0.5$ and the full hydration $\alpha=1$: $x=0.657/(0.319+0.5)=0.80$; for $\alpha=0.7$: $x=0.46/(0.223+0.5)=0.64$.) The Powers' relation of the strength (found for the mortar cubes) is

$$f_c=f_{c0}\,x^{\,n}\ \ (n\approx3),\quad f_{c0}\approx234\ \text{MPa (the intrinsic strength of the gel)}$$

so the strength **rises steeply with the gel/space ratio**: for $x=0.64$, $f_c\approx234(0.64)^3\approx61$ MPa, and for $x=0.5$, $f_c\approx29$ MPa (the concrete values are lower because of the air voids and the aggregate-paste interface). At the higher gel/space ratio the **capillary pores (the voids, which limit the strength) are fewer** and the gel fills the space. **It explains the effect of the w/c, the age and the curing on the strength:** the lower w/c and the longer or better curing (the higher degree of hydration) both raise $x$ and the strength; the poorly cured concrete has the low $\alpha$ and $x$.

**Shrinkage.** The **volume decrease (the contraction) of the concrete due to the loss of the water, without the load**: the plastic shrinkage (the first hours), the **drying shrinkage** (the loss of the adsorbed and the capillary water, the strain 0.0003 to 0.0008, mostly in the first months, partly reversible), the **autogenous** and the **carbonation** shrinkage. It increases with the cement and the water content, the **fine cement**, the small aggregate and the dry hot air; it causes the cracks when restrained, the loss of the pre-stress and the warping. Control: the low water, the large and the stiff aggregate, the **curing**, the **movement joints and the shrinkage reinforcement (0.12 to 0.15 %)**, the shrinkage-compensating cement.

**Creep.** The **continuing increase of the strain with the time under the sustained load** (the creep coefficient $\phi=\varepsilon_c/\varepsilon_e$ = 1.4 to 2.2 for the loading at 28 days to 7 days, IS 456); the creep deformation is due to the **seepage of the gel water and the sliding of the gel particles**. It increases with the stress, the w/c, the paste content, the early age and the dry atmosphere, and falls with the stronger aggregate. The consequences: the **deflection**, the **loss of the pre-stress**, the transfer of the load to the steel in the columns and the redistribution of the stress; partly beneficial (the relief of the stress concentration).

@s 5
**Compressive strength (IS 516).** The **150 mm cubes (or 150 × 300 mm cylinders)** are cast in the oiled moulds in the 3 layers with the 35 tamping strokes (or the vibrator), kept for 24 h in the moist air at $27\pm2\,^\circ$C, demoulded and **cured in the water at $27\pm2\,^\circ$C** for 7 and 28 days. At the age, the cube is placed in the **compression testing machine** with the cast faces in the contact with the platens, the load is raised **at 140 kg/cm² per minute (about 0.23 N/mm² per s)** to the failure, and

$$f_c=\frac{P_{max}}{A}\ ,\quad A=150\times150=22\,500\ \text{mm}^2$$

the mean of **three** specimens (the rejected if the variation is more than $\pm15$ %) is the strength; the cylinder strength is about 0.8 of the cube.

**Tensile strength.** Since the direct tension test is difficult (the grips and the alignment), it is measured indirectly:

1. **Split cylinder (Brazilian) test (IS 5816):** the 150 × 300 mm cylinder is placed **horizontally** between the platens with the plywood strips and loaded in the diametral compression (the rate 1.2 to 2.4 N/mm²/min) to the splitting; $f_{st}=\dfrac{2P}{\pi\,L\,D}$ (typically $f_{st}\approx0.5$ to $0.6\sqrt{f_{ck}}$);
2. **Flexural (the modulus of rupture) test (IS 516):** the **beam 100 × 100 × 500 mm (or 150 × 150 × 700 mm)** on the two supports at the 400 mm (600 mm) span, loaded at the **third points**; $f_{cr}=\dfrac{PL}{bd^2}$ (if the crack is in the middle third), the **modulus of rupture** $=0.7\sqrt{f_{ck}}$ MPa (IS 456).

**Schmidt (rebound) hammer test (IS 13311 Part 2).** A **non-destructive surface-hardness test**. The **spring-loaded mass** is released and strikes the plunger on the concrete surface; the **rebound** of the hammer is read on the scale as the **rebound number $R$** (the larger R, the harder (the stronger) surface).

*Procedure:* the surface is **smoothed** (ground, the dry, the clean, away from the edges, the bars and the voids, about 20 mm from the edge); the hammer is held **at right angles** to the surface (the correction when it is inclined: the horizontal, the up and the down), pressed until the impact; **at least 9 to 12 readings** are taken on a 300 × 300 mm area at the spacing of 25 mm or more, the **outliers (more than 6 units from the average) are removed** and the mean $R$ is found; the **strength is read from the calibration curve** (the correlation, the R against the cube strength at the angle of the impact; the typical $R$ = 20 gives 15 MPa and 40 gives 40 MPa; the accuracy $\pm$ 20 to 25 %).

*Influences:* the carbonation of the surface (the over-estimates), the moisture, the surface texture, the age, the type of the aggregate and the cement, and the stiffness of the member. *Use:* the **uniformity of the concrete, the comparison of the parts, the detection of the weak zones, the quick assessment of the old structures**, to be calibrated against the cores.

@s 6
**Ferrocement.** A **thin reinforced-cement composite** made of the **rich cement-sand mortar (the cement : sand = 1 : 1.5 to 1 : 2.5, w/c 0.4 to 0.45) reinforced with the closely spaced layers of the continuous small-diameter wire mesh** (the hexagonal chicken mesh, the welded or the woven square mesh with the 0.5 to 1.5 mm wires) and the **skeletal steel (the 6 mm bars)**; the thickness is 10 to 50 mm; the volume fraction of the steel is 2 to 8 %, and the specific surface of the reinforcement is large (the 1 to 2 cm²/cm³).

*Behaviour and properties:* the **high tensile strength and the ductility, the excellent crack control (the fine well-distributed cracks)**, the high impact resistance and the toughness, the **light weight** (the thin sections: the 100 to 150 kg/m² less than the RCC), and it can be moulded into any shape; the **low cost (the local material and the unskilled labour, the no formwork or the simple)**; the disadvantages: the corrosion of the mesh if the cover is poor, the labour-intensive casting and the difficulty of the fixing the mesh, the lack of the code (the limited for the structures).

*Applications:* the **water tanks (the domestic, the ferro-cement tanks of 1 to 20 m³, the rainwater harvesting tanks), the grain silos and the bins**, the roof (the folded plates, the shells and the channels), the **boats, the barges and the pontoons**, the **precast panels, the permanent formwork and the wall partitions, the low-cost housing**, the **pipes and the sewers, the biogas plants, the toilets and the latrines (the rural water and sanitation)**, the **repair and the strengthening (the jacketing) of the RCC, the masonry and the earthquake retrofitting** of the old walls (the mesh and the mortar plaster) and the **sculpture and the architectural elements**.
