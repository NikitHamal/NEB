@s 1
**(a) Gradation and the shape of the coarse aggregate.** Coarse aggregate is graded (the particle size distribution by the sieves 80, 40, 20, 10, 4.75 mm, IS 383) as the **single-sized** (the nearly uniform, e.g. 20 mm), **graded** (the all-in, the continuous: e.g. 40 to 4.75 mm) and **gap-graded** (the intermediate size missing).

*Shape classes (IS 2386, BS 812):* **rounded** (the river gravel, the smooth, the low voids, the good workability, the poor bond), **irregular**, **angular (crushed)** (the rough, the good interlock and bond, the higher water demand), **flaky** (the thickness less than 0.6 of the mean size) and **elongated** (the length more than 1.8 of the mean size).

*Impact on the strength:* (1) the **angular, cubical, rough** aggregate gives the better mechanical interlock and the **bond**, so the 10 to 20 % higher compressive and the flexural strength than the rounded gravel at the same w/c (but needs more paste for the workability); (2) **flaky and elongated** particles pack badly, increase the voids and the water demand, **lower the strength** and the workability, **lie flat and trap the bleed water under them** (the weak zone): the flakiness plus the elongation is limited to 30 to 40 %; (3) the **well-graded aggregate** has the minimum voids, so the less paste, the **higher density, the strength and the durability**, with the less segregation, and the gap-graded gives the good strength with the low sand (the exposed aggregate), but segregates easily; (4) the **maximum size**: the larger size gives the higher strength for the lean mixes but the lower for the rich (the 10 to 20 mm for the high strength).

**(b) Bulking of sand.** The **increase in the volume of the fine aggregate (the sand) due to the moisture film** on the particles, which pushes them apart (the surface tension of the film): the bulking rises with the moisture to a maximum of about **25 to 30 % at 4 to 6 % moisture** (the fine sand bulks more), and falls to zero for the fully saturated (the inundated) sand.

$$\text{Bulking}\,(\%)=\frac{V_{moist}-V_{dry}}{V_{dry}}\times100=\frac{h_1-h_2}{h_2}\times100$$

(the field test: the sand is filled in the measuring cylinder to 100 mm (the height $h_1$ moist), the cylinder is filled with the water and stirred so that the sand is saturated, and the new height $h_2$ is measured; $\text{bulking}=\dfrac{h_1-h_2}{h_2}\times100$). Bulking matters because the **volume batching** of the moist sand gives **less sand** than the design (a lean, harsh mix), so the quantity of the sand is increased by the bulking percentage.

**Fineness modulus of the coarse aggregate (the sieve analysis, IS 2386 Part 1).** The oven-dried **sample (5 kg for the 20 mm, 10 kg for the 40 mm)** is shaken for 10 min through the **standard set of sieves 80, 40, 20, 10, 4.75, 2.36, 1.18 mm, 600, 300 and 150 $\mu$m**; the **mass retained on each sieve** is weighed and expressed as the percentage of the total, the **cumulative percentage retained** is found, and

$$FM=\frac{\sum\text{cumulative percentage retained on all the standard sieves}}{100}$$

*Example:* the cumulative percentages retained on 80, 40, 20, 10, 4.75, 2.36, 1.18 mm, 600, 300, 150 $\mu$m are 0, 5, 70, 95, 100, 100, 100, 100, 100, 100, so the sum is 770 and $FM=7.70$ (the coarse aggregate: 5.5 to 8.0, the 20 mm single size about 6.9 to 7.0; the fine sand 2.2 to 3.2). A larger FM means the coarser aggregate; it is used to check the **uniformity of the supply** and to combine the aggregates (the required FM of the mix).

@s 2
**Role of the water-cement ratio.** The strength of the concrete is governed mainly by the **w/c ratio (Abrams' law)**:

$$\begin{gathered}f_c=\frac{K_1}{K_2^{\,w/c}}\quad\text{(Abrams)} \\ \text{or in the gel/space form: }f_c=f_{c0}x^3\end{gathered}$$

For the fully compacted concrete, a **lower w/c gives the higher strength** (the less capillary porosity, the denser paste and the better bond); each 0.1 increase in w/c lowers the strength by about 10 to 15 MPa in the range 0.4 to 0.7. The water needed for the hydration is about 0.23 to 0.25 (the chemical combined 0.23 plus the gel water 0.15, total 0.38 for the full hydration without the capillary pores); the **excess water makes the capillary pores**, which lower the strength, the durability and the impermeability, and increase the shrinkage and the bleeding. Too low w/c (below 0.35 without the plasticiser) makes the mix unworkable (the honeycomb), so the **admixtures (the superplasticiser)** are used to give the workability at the low w/c. The w/c is also limited by IS 456 (e.g. 0.45 to 0.55 for the moderate to the severe exposure) for the durability.

**Segregation and bleeding.** *Segregation* is the **separation of the coarse aggregate from the mortar** in the fresh concrete (the causes: the harsh and lean mix, the wrong grading, the too much water, the drop of more than 1.5 m, the long chute, the over-vibration): the **honeycomb, the non-uniform strength, the poor bond, the porous layers**. *Bleeding* is the **rise of the mixing water to the top** of the placed concrete, because the solids settle (the causes: the high w/c, the coarse cement, the lack of the fines, the over-vibration): the **laitance and the weak top layer, the water pockets and the channels under the coarse particles and the bars (the poor bond), the permeable concrete** and the plastic settlement cracking. Both are reduced by the correct grading and the cohesive mix, the lower water, the fines, the air entrainment and the careful placing, the compaction and the finishing.

@s 3
**DoE method of the mix design** (the British DoE "Design of Normal Concrete Mixes"; the steps):

1. **Target mean strength:** $f_m=f_{ck}+1.64\,s$ (5 % defectives), $s$ from the test records (or 5 to 6 MPa for the standard).
2. **Free water/cement ratio** from the **figure of the strength against the w/c** (the 28-day value for the cement type and the aggregate type, by the interpolation between the lines), and the **maximum w/c** from the durability (IS 456 table).
3. **Free water content** from the **table** of the slump or Vebe, the maximum aggregate size (10, 20, 40 mm) and the aggregate type; for the mixed (the crushed and the uncrushed): $W=\tfrac23W_f+\tfrac13W_c$.
4. **Cement content** $=W/(w/c)$, and the check with the **minimum cement** (the durability) and the maximum limit (e.g. 450 kg/m³); if the cement is below the minimum, the minimum is used and the w/c is recalculated.
5. **Total aggregate content:** the **wet density of the concrete** from the figure (the free water and the relative density of the aggregate, 2.6 to 2.7): $\text{Aggr}=D-C-W$.
6. **Proportion of fine aggregate** (percent passing the 600 $\mu$m sieve, the grading zone, the w/c, the slump and the max size) from the figure: the fine and the coarse quantities (the coarse divided in the 10 and 20 mm fractions of 1/3 : 2/3).
7. **Adjust for the moisture and the absorption** (the batch weights), the **trial mix** (the 0.1 m³), the check of the workability and the 7- and 28-day strength, and the revision of the water and the aggregate.

*Illustration:* the M25: $f_m=25+1.64(5)=33.2$ MPa, $w/c\approx0.50$, the water 190 kg/m³ (the 20 mm crushed aggregate, the 30 to 60 mm slump), the cement 380 kg/m³, the wet density 2400, the total aggregate $2400-380-190=1830$ kg/m³, the fine 35 % = 640 and the coarse 1190 kg/m³ (the proportion about 1 : 1.7 : 3.1; w/c 0.50). The DoE method is more **rational** than the nominal mix, takes the strength, the workability and the **durability** into account and is the basis of the IS 10262 method.

@s 4
**Load-deformation (stress-strain) behaviour of the hardened concrete.** Concrete under the **uniaxial compression** gives the **non-linear** stress-strain curve:

- up to about **30 to 40 % of the ultimate** stress the curve is nearly **linear and elastic** (the micro-cracks at the aggregate-paste interface (the bond cracks) are stable): the modulus $E_c=5000\sqrt{f_{ck}}$ MPa (IS 456);
- from 40 to 75 % the **bond cracks grow and the curve bends** (the stable crack propagation, the mortar cracking starts at about 50 %);
- at 75 to 90 % the **mortar cracks join up** (the unstable crack growth, the volume starts to increase: the critical stress);
- the **peak (the ultimate) stress** $f_c$ is at the strain of about 0.002 and the **descending branch** follows (the strain softening) up to the crushing at the strain of 0.0035 (IS 456 design value $\varepsilon_{cu}=0.0035$). The higher the strength, the steeper the slope, the more linear and the **more brittle** (the lower failure strain). For the tension: the linear up to the failure, the strength about $0.7\sqrt{f_{ck}}$.

**Shrinkage.** The **contraction of the concrete (the volume reduction) with the loss of the moisture, with no load**: *plastic shrinkage* (in the first hours, the rapid surface evaporation, the cracks), *drying shrinkage* (the loss of the adsorbed water of the gel: about $0.0003$ to $0.0008$, which continues for months and years, partly reversible, the ends of the cracking when restrained), *autogenous shrinkage* (the self-desiccation at the low w/c) and *carbonation shrinkage*. It increases with the **water and cement content, the finer cement, the smaller aggregate, the dry and the hot atmosphere, the small section**; the cracks are controlled by the movement joints, the reinforcement, the curing and the low water.

**Creep.** The **time-dependent increase of the strain under the sustained stress** (the sketch: the instantaneous elastic strain, then the creep strain increasing with the decreasing rate to a limit ($\phi=\varepsilon_{creep}/\varepsilon_{el}$, the creep coefficient 1.6 at 28 days, 2.2 for the 7 days loading, IS 456), with the recovery of the part on the unloading). It increases with the stress/strength ratio, the earlier loading, the higher w/c, the paste volume and the dry environment, and falls with the strong dense aggregate and the large section. Effects: the **deflection (long-term) increases, the loss of the pre-stress, the stress transfer to the steel**, and the relaxation of the stress concentration. Creep and shrinkage are both related to the movement of the gel water and are not additive in the simple way.

@s 5
**Non-destructive testing (NDT)** assesses the **strength, the quality and the condition of the concrete in the structure without damaging it** (the existing structures, the doubtful concrete, the cubes that failed).

1. **Rebound (Schmidt) hammer (IS 13311 Part 2):** the spring-driven hammer's **rebound number** $R$ (the surface hardness) is read on the 12 points, and the strength is read from the calibration curve (the accuracy of $\pm25$ %); quick, cheap, to check the **uniformity and the comparison**, only the surface (the carbonated and the wet surface affects it).
2. **Ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV, IS 13311 Part 1):** the time $t$ of the ultrasonic pulse (54 kHz) over the path $L$ gives $v=L/t$ (the direct, the semi-direct and the indirect transmission): $v>4.5$ km/s is excellent, 3.5 to 4.5 good, 3.0 to 3.5 doubtful, below 3.0 poor; used for the **uniformity, the voids and the cracks (the depth), the honeycomb and the modulus**; with the rebound hammer (the **SONREB** method) for the strength.
3. **Penetration resistance (the Windsor probe)**, the **pull-out and the pull-off tests**, the **break-off test** and the **maturity method** (the semi-destructive) for the in-situ strength and the surface bond.
4. **Cover meter / profometer (the electromagnetic)** and the **ground penetrating radar (GPR)** to locate the **reinforcement, the cover and the spacing**; the **half-cell potential** and the **resistivity** for the **corrosion** of the steel; the **carbonation test** (the phenolphthalein spray: the pink colour in the alkaline); the **radiography, the infrared thermography and the impact echo** for the internal defects and the delamination; **permeability and the chloride tests**.

*Applications:* the **quality control** of the new structure (the early strength, the formwork removal), the **assessment of the old and damaged buildings, the bridges, the dams and the pavements** (the fire, the earthquake and the age), the **detection of the cracks, the voids and the honeycombing**, the corrosion survey, the **retrofitting decisions** and the dispute settlement where the cubes are not available; the results are combined with the **cores (the destructive, IS 516)** for the calibration.

@s 6
**Emerging technologies in concrete.**

1. **Self-compacting concrete (SCC)** with the superplasticisers and the viscosity-modifying agents: flows into the formwork and the congested bars without vibration.
2. **High-performance and ultra-high-performance concrete (HPC and UHPC)**: the low w/c, the silica fume and the fibres; 60 to 200 MPa, the very high durability.
3. **Fibre-reinforced concrete (steel, glass, polypropylene, basalt)** and the **engineered cementitious composites (the bendable concrete, ECC)**: the toughness and the crack control.
4. **Geopolymer concrete and the green concrete**: the cement-free (the fly ash or the slag with the alkali activator), the recycled aggregate, the low-carbon cement (LC3, the limestone calcined clay) to **reduce the $\text{CO}_2$ emission**.
5. **3D-printed concrete (additive manufacturing)**: the formwork-free layered printing of the building and the elements.
6. **Self-healing concrete** (the bacteria, the capsules, the crystalline admixtures), the **pervious concrete** (the drainage), the **light-weight and the foam concrete**, the **roller-compacted concrete** for the dams and the pavements, the **translucent concrete**, the **nano-modified and the smart (self-sensing) concrete** and the **carbon-capturing concrete**; the **ready-mix concrete (RMC)** with the automation, the maturity sensors and the IoT monitoring, and the **precast and the modular** systems.
