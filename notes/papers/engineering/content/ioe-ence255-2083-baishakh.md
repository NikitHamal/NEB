@s 1
**History of building technology.** Humans first used caves, then **mud, thatch, wood and stone** (huts, pit houses). Fired **brick** and lime mortar appeared in the Indus valley (about 2500 BC), **stone masonry** and the post-and-lintel in Egypt and Greece, the **arch, vault and concrete** (the pozzolanic Roman cement) in Rome. The Middle Ages brought the Gothic arch, the flying buttress and timber framing. The industrial revolution gave **cast iron, steel, Portland cement (1824), reinforced concrete (1850 to 1900) and the elevator**, and so the skyscraper; the 20th century added prestressed concrete, shell and the space structures, precast systems, and today the **green, prefabricated, 3D-printed and BIM-based** construction.

**Nepalese building.** Traditional buildings are of **mud mortar masonry with timber** (the Newar houses of brick, with carved timber windows, the pagoda temples of the Kathmandu valley, the 3 to 5 storey with sloping tiled roofs), the **stone and slate** houses of the hills and the mountains, the **thatch and bamboo** houses of the Terai, and the Rana palaces (neo-classical, 19th century, brick, lime and cast-iron). Modern construction started in the 1950s with the **RCC frame, the cement-sand masonry (the load-bearing)** and, after the 1988 and the 2015 Gorkha earthquakes, with the **National Building Code (NBC, prepared in 1994 and made mandatory through the Building Act 2055 and the municipal by-laws) with the earthquake-resistant design requirement**, the building by-laws, and the **earthquake-resistant** and retrofitting techniques.

@s 2
**Thermal comfort.** The condition of mind in which a person is **satisfied with the thermal environment**, neither too hot nor too cold, so that the body can lose its metabolic heat at the rate it is produced. It depends on the air temperature (the comfort zone 21 to 27 °C in the summer), the mean radiant temperature, the **humidity** (30 to 60 %), the **air velocity**, the clothing and the activity (the PMV/PPD index of Fanger, the effective temperature).

**Thermal performance of a building.** The ability of the building envelope (walls, roofs, floors, windows) to **control the heat flow** and to keep the inside temperature comfortable with the minimum use of the energy: it depends on the **U-value (the thermal transmittance, $U=1/\sum R$)**, the **thermal mass** (the time lag and the decrement factor), the orientation, the glazing and the shading, the air-tightness and the ventilation.

**Principles of planning of a building.**

1. **Aspect and prospect:** the rooms are placed so as to receive the morning sun and the breeze (the kitchen and the bedrooms to the east, the living to the south in Nepal) and to give a pleasant view;
2. **Orientation, sun and wind:** the long axis along the east-west, the shading of the south, and the cross-ventilation (the windows on the opposite walls);
3. **Privacy, grouping and the circulation:** the bedrooms are private, the rooms are grouped by their use (the kitchen near the dining), the circulation is short and without the cross-traffic (the corridors and the stairs);
4. **Roominess and sanitation:** the sizes and the proportions of the rooms (the minimum area and the height), the light and the ventilation, the **water and the sanitary** arrangement;
5. **Flexibility, furniture requirement and the elegance**, the **economy** (the compact plan, the repeating spans) and the **practical considerations**: the building by-laws and the codes (the ground coverage, the set-back, the height), the structural safety and the fire and the earthquake provision, the accessibility.

**Classification of sound.** (i) By the **frequency**: infrasonic (below 20 Hz), audible sound (20 Hz to 20 kHz) and ultrasonic (above 20 kHz). (ii) By the **source and the transmission** in buildings: **airborne sound** (the speech, the music, through the air and the partition) and **structure-borne (impact) sound** (the footsteps and the machine vibration through the structure). By the **nature**: the *noise* (the unwanted, irregular sound) and the *musical* sound (the regular). By the **effect of the room**: the *direct*, the *reflected (the echo, the reverberant)* and the *absorbed* sound.

@s 3
**(a) Rubble masonry.** Masonry built of **undressed or roughly dressed stones (the rubble) of irregular shapes and sizes**, laid in the **lime or cement mortar** (or the mud, in the hills): *random rubble* (uncoursed or coursed) and *square rubble*, with the **bond stones (through stones) at every 1 to 1.5 m** and the thickness at least 450 mm. It is cheap, the strength is moderate, and it is common for the foundation, the retaining and the boundary walls and the buildings in the hills of Nepal.

**Painting new woodwork.** (1) **Preparation:** the surface is cleaned with the sand paper, the dust is removed, the **knots** are covered by the knotting (shellac) and the **resinous** spots are treated; (2) **priming:** one coat of the **wood primer** (the red lead or the aluminium, or the oil primer) to seal the pores and to give a bond; (3) **stopping:** the holes and the cracks are filled with the putty (the linseed oil with the whiting) after the priming and then allowed to dry; (4) **rubbing** with the fine sand paper; (5) the **under-coat (the second coat)** and, after drying, (6) the **finishing coat(s)** (the enamel or the synthetic paint), applied in the uniform strokes, with the drying time (24 h) between the coats and the light rubbing before the last coat. Two to three coats are given.

**(b) Slip formwork.** A **continuously moving formwork** for tall, constant-section (or the slowly varying) structures such as the chimneys, silos, cores of high-rise, bridge piers and the water tower. The formwork (about 1.2 m high) is lifted by the **hydraulic jacks on the jack rods embedded in the concrete** at the rate of 150 to 300 mm/h, while the concrete is placed continuously (the 24 h work), and the reinforcement is added at the top. It is fast, gives a **joint-free** wall, with fewer labour, but the process cannot stop and needs a careful control of the plumb and the concrete setting time.

**Compressed stabilised earth block (CSEB) masonry.** Blocks formed by **compressing the moist soil (the sandy clay) stabilised with 5 to 10 % cement or lime** in a manual or hydraulic press (the pressure 2 to 10 MPa), cured for 28 days (not fired). Size such as 240 × 115 × 90 mm. It is laid in the **cement–soil mortar** (the thin joints), has a compressive strength of 3 to 7 MPa, is **low in energy and cost, eco-friendly, with a good thermal mass** (the cool in the summer), and uses the local soil; it needs protection against the water (the plinth, the projection of the roof) and the plaster or the water-proofing; the block is used for the walls of the low-rise and the **post-earthquake rural housing**.

**Precast and cast-in-situ construction.**

| | Precast | Cast-in-situ |
|---|---|---|
| Place | cast in a **factory or yard**, then transported and erected | cast **at the final position** on site |
| Quality | good (a controlled environment, the moulds) | variable (the site conditions) |
| Speed | fast (the parallel work, no curing at the site) | slow (the formwork, the curing) |
| Joints | many joints (the connection detail and the seismic care) | monolithic, continuous |
| Cost | economical for the repetition; needs the crane and the transport | economical for the small and the irregular works |
| Examples | the beams, the slab planks, the pile, the pipes, the wall panels | the footings, the columns, the slabs, the frames |

@s 4
**(a) Stairway and staircase.** The **stairway** is the *passage or the opening (the space or the room)* in which the stair is built, i.e. the whole enclosure of the stair; the **staircase** is the *structure*, i.e. the series of steps with the landings and the balustrade (the handrail) which connect the floors. (The staircase is in the stairway.)

**Terms of a roof.**

- **Pitch:** the **slope** of the roof: the ratio of the rise to the span (the rise/span, e.g. 1/4, 1/3), or the angle with the horizontal (e.g. 30°);
- **Eaves:** the **lower edge of the roof** that projects (overhangs) beyond the face of the wall to throw the rain water away from the wall (the projection 0.45 to 0.9 m);
- **Hip:** the **ridge formed by the intersection of two sloping surfaces** at an external angle (it runs from the ridge to the eaves at the corner of a hipped roof); the intersection at an internal angle is the **valley**;
- **Purlin:** the **horizontal timber, steel or RC member** laid across the principal rafters or the trusses (parallel to the ridge), which supports the common rafters or the roof covering (the CGI sheets) directly.

**(b) Fixing a door.**

1. The **door frame** (the chowkat: the head and the two posts, with the sill if used) is made and placed in the opening, **plumbed with a plumb bob and levelled** with the spirit level and temporarily braced. The frame is fixed in the masonry either by **building the frame in as the wall rises** (the frame fixed first, with the holdfasts (the iron lugs) of 250 to 300 mm embedded in the wall at 3 or 4 per post), or by **fixing the frame in a rough opening after the wall is built** with the **fox wedges, the rawl plugs and the screws, or the anchor bolts**. The gap between the frame and the wall is filled with the cement mortar.
2. The **shutter (the leaf)** is hung on the frame with at least **3 hinges** (the top, the middle and the bottom, at 150 mm from the ends), the fixed with the screws; the **tower bolts, the aldrop, the handle and the lock** are fixed, and the **clearance** of 3 to 5 mm all round (the bottom 10 to 12 mm) is kept so that the door swings freely.
3. The door is then checked for the plumb and the smooth swing, the stops and the architraves are fixed, the finish is given.

**Wiring systems in a building.**

1. **Cleat wiring:** the wires are held by the porcelain or wooden cleats (temporary, cheap, exposed);
2. **Casing-and-capping (the wooden batten) wiring:** the wires in the grooves of the wooden casing with a cover (the old systems, the fire risk);
3. **Batten wiring:** the wires on the teak battens with the clips (the exposed);
4. **Conduit wiring** (the most common and the standard): the wires run in the **PVC or the GI pipes**; (a) the **surface conduit** (fixed on the wall by the saddles) or (b) the **concealed conduit** (embedded in the walls and slabs, covered by the plaster); safe, the neat, and the wires can be replaced;
5. **Lead-sheathed (the metal-sheathed, the TRS/CTS) wiring**, the **MICC** wiring (the fire-resistant, in the industrial and the special buildings).

@s 5
**(a) Double scaffolding (the independent scaffolding) against single scaffolding.** Single scaffolding (the bricklayer's) has **one row of the standards** parallel to the wall at 1.2 m from it, with the putlogs resting in the **holes in the wall**; double scaffolding has **two rows of the standards** (the inner one is 200 mm from the wall), joined by the ledgers, the putlogs rest on both rows, and the **cross-braces and the guard rails** are provided.

Advantages of the double scaffolding:

1. **Stronger and more stable**, independent of the wall, so it is **suitable for stone masonry and the high walls**, where the holes cannot be left for the putlogs;
2. **no holes** are left in the wall (no later filling and no weakening), which is also necessary for the **finished wall, the plastering and the repair**;
3. it can carry the **heavier load** (the materials and the workers) with a better safety, and can be extended to **taller** buildings (the bracing and the ties);
4. it gives the **wide working platform** for the heavy stone work and the plastering.

(Single scaffolding is cheaper and faster, but it is limited to the brick masonry and the low heights.)

**Thermal insulation of exposed walls.** The heat flow through the walls is reduced by:

- **cavity walls** (the 50 to 75 mm air gap, with an insulation fill) and the **hollow blocks or the AAC (the aerated) blocks** which have the low conductivity;
- **insulation layers** of the low-conductivity material (the **expanded polystyrene EPS, the extruded polystyrene XPS, the mineral wool, the polyurethane foam, the cork, or the straw/hemp**) applied on the **outer face (the external wall insulation system EWIS, with the render)**, the inner face or in the cavity; the *outside insulation* keeps the thermal mass inside the building and avoids the condensation;
- the **reflective (the light-coloured) finish** and the paints with the low absorptivity, the **shading** (the overhang, the vegetation, the green wall), the thick **thermal mass** (the mud or the stone masonry) for the time lag, and the **double-glazed** windows with the air-tight joints.

**(b) Structural and non-structural cracks.**

| | Structural cracks | Non-structural cracks |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | **overloading, the poor design, the foundation settlement, the earthquake and the corrosion** (the loss of the structural capacity) | shrinkage, the **thermal movement**, the moisture, the chemical action, the poor workmanship |
| Location | in the **beams, columns, slabs and the load-bearing walls** (the diagonal at the support, the vertical at the mid-span, the wide cracks) | in the **plaster, the partition walls, the finishes**, the hairline (map pattern) |
| Danger | **endangers the safety**, grows with the time and the load | affects the appearance and the durability only |
| Remedy | strengthening: the epoxy injection, the jacketing, the underpinning | the repair by the patching, the sealants, the re-plastering |

**Methods of demolition.** (1) **Manual (the hand tools)**, piece by piece in the reverse order of the construction, for small buildings; (2) **mechanical:** the excavator with the breaker or the **crusher**, the crane with the **wrecking (demolition) ball**, the pusher arm and the hydraulic cutters (the pressure-based pulverisers); (3) **explosive (implosion)** with the controlled blasting of the tall structures; (4) **thermal and the cutting methods:** the gas or the thermic lance, the diamond **wire saw**, the water jet; (5) **chemical (the expansive agents)** and the **hydro-demolition** (the high-pressure water) for the concrete. The choice is based on the structure, the surroundings, the noise, the dust and the safety (the permission, the pre-demolition survey, the shoring, the salvage, the waste disposal).

@s 6
**Sustainable building** uses the resources and the energy efficiently over the **whole life** (the design, the construction, the use and the demolition), so as to **reduce the environmental impact and to give a healthy, economical and the socially responsible** building.

**Techniques and strategies.**

1. **Site and planning:** the minimum disturbance of the site, the **passive solar design** (the orientation, the daylight, the natural ventilation, the shading), the **compact form**, the use of the existing infrastructure, the public transport and the green space (the green roof, the vegetation);
2. **Energy efficiency:** the **high-performance envelope** (the insulation, the airtight, the thermal mass), the efficient lighting and the appliances (the LED), the heat pumps, and the **renewable energy** (the solar PV and the solar water heater, the biogas, the micro-hydro);
3. **Water efficiency:** the **rainwater harvesting**, the low-flow fixtures, the **grey-water recycling** and the reuse, the recharge pit, the dual-flush WC, and the drought-resistant planting;
4. **Materials and resources:** the **local and the low-embodied energy** materials (the CSEB, the bamboo, the timber from the sustainable source, the stone), the **recycled and the reused** materials (the fly ash cement, the recycled aggregate, the AAC), the durable materials and the **waste minimisation** at the site, the modular and the prefabricated design for the less waste;
5. **Indoor environmental quality:** the good light and the ventilation, the **low-VOC** paints and the finishes, the thermal and the acoustic comfort;
6. **Construction and the operation:** the efficient construction practice (the reduction of the dust and the noise, the safe labour), the commissioning and the maintenance, the **flexible and the adaptable** design for the long life and the **design for the deconstruction** at the end of the life;
7. **Disaster resilience** (the earthquake, the flood and the fire) and the use of the **life-cycle cost** and the rating systems (LEED, BREEAM, GRIHA, the Nepal green building criteria) to check the performance.
