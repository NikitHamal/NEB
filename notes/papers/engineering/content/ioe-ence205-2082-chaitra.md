@s 1
**(a) Basic requirements of aggregates (IS 383).**

*Coarse aggregate* (retained on the 4.75 mm sieve): (1) **strong, hard and durable** rock (the crushing value $\le45$ % for the ordinary and $\le30$ % for the wearing surface, the impact value $\le45$ %, the abrasion value $\le50$ %); (2) **clean**: free from clay, silt, organic matter, coal, mica and the soluble salts (the chlorides and the sulphates); (3) **good shape**: cubical or angular and rough-textured (for the bond), the **flakiness and the elongation index** limited (about 30 to 40 %); (4) **well graded** between the nominal sizes (e.g. 20 mm, 40 mm); (5) **sound (the soundness test)**, the water absorption low (below 2 %) and no reactive silica (the alkali-silica reaction), the specific gravity 2.6 to 2.9.

*Fine aggregate* (passing the 4.75 mm sieve; the sand): (1) **hard, clean, durable and inert** particles (the silt and clay below 3 to 5 %, the organic impurities by the colorimetric test), no salts; (2) **well graded** (the grading zones I to IV of IS 383, the fineness modulus 2.2 to 3.2), neither too coarse (the harsh mix) nor too fine (the high water demand); (3) the **rounded or cubical** shape for the workability and the **rough** surface for the bond; (4) **no excess of bulking** and the limited **deleterious** materials, the **specific gravity** 2.6 and the low water absorption.

**(b) Why grading information is necessary.** The grading (the particle size distribution, by the sieve analysis) tells the **proportion of each size**. It is needed because it controls: the **voids** (the well-graded aggregate has the minimum voids, so the **less cement paste** fills them, which is **economical**), the **workability** and the cohesion (the mix is neither harsh nor segregating), the **density and the strength and the durability** (the less permeable), the **water demand and the shrinkage**, and it is the basis of the **mix proportioning** (the combined grading of the fine and the coarse to the IS limits and the Fuller curve), the control of the supplies and the pumpability. The **fineness modulus** is obtained as $FM=\dfrac{\sum\text{cumulative \% retained}}{100}$.

@s 2
**(a) Segregation** is the **separation of the constituents of the fresh concrete** (the coarse aggregate separates from the mortar) so that the mix is no longer uniform. *Causes:* the wrong grading, too wet or too dry mix, a drop from a height (more than 1 to 1.5 m), the long chute or the barrow transport, the over-vibration, the rough handling. *Effects:* the **honeycombing, the weak and porous concrete, the poor bond with the steel, the non-uniform strength**, the low durability and the loss of the surface. Prevented by the proper grading, the controlled water, the careful transport and the placing, the limited drop.

**Bleeding** is the **upward movement of the mixing water** to the surface of the freshly placed concrete (a form of the segregation) because the solids settle. *Effects:* the **laitance** (the weak layer at the top, a poor bond with the next lift), the **water gain under the coarse aggregate and the reinforcement** (the voids and the weak bond), the **porous and the permeable** concrete, the plastic settlement cracks, the loss of the strength at the top of the columns. It is reduced by the **lower water content, the finer cement, the air entrainment, the proper grading and the fines**, and the finishing after the bleed water has gone.

**(b) Importance of curing.** Curing keeps the concrete **moist (and at a favourable temperature) for the first 7 to 14 days** (the minimum 7 days for the OPC and 10 days for the mineral admixtures, as the IS 456) so that the cement **hydration continues**. The reasons: (1) the **strength and the durability** develop fully (the unmoistened concrete may lose 30 to 50 % of the strength); (2) the **shrinkage and the plastic cracks are reduced** (the surface does not dry before the strength is gained); (3) the **permeability and the abrasion resistance** improve; (4) the **thermal cracking** is controlled. The methods: the ponding, the spraying, the wet gunny bags and the sand, the curing compound, the polythene sheets and the steam for the precast.

@s 3
**(a) DoE (British Department of the Environment) method of mix design** (the steps):

1. **Target mean strength:** $f_m=f_{ck}+k\,s$, with the margin $k s$ ($k=1.64$ for the 5 % defective, $s$ the standard deviation, e.g. 5 MPa for the M25, or from the previous test data).
2. **Free water–cement ratio:** from the **chart** (the relation of the strength and the w/c for the cement type, the aggregate and the age, e.g. 28-day), and check against the **maximum w/c** for the durability (the exposure, IS 456).
3. **Free water content:** from the **table** by the slump or Vebe, the maximum size and the type of aggregate (crushed or uncrushed), corrected for the mixed aggregates ($\tfrac23W_f+\tfrac13W_c$).
4. **Cement content** $=\dfrac{\text{free water}}{w/c}$, checked against the minimum (durability) and the maximum limits.
5. **Total aggregate content:** from the **estimated wet density** of the fresh concrete (the chart of the density against the free water and the specific gravity of the aggregate): $\text{aggregate}=\text{wet density}-\text{cement}-\text{water}$.
6. **Proportion of the fine aggregate:** from the chart (the maximum size, the slump, the w/c and the grading zone of the sand), giving the fine and the coarse aggregate (the 10 and 20 mm fractions).
7. **Trial mix**, the test of the slump and the cubes at 7 and 28 days and the adjustment of the water and the proportions to the target strength and the workability.

*Illustration:* the M25, $s=5$: $f_m=25+1.64(5)=33.2$ MPa; the chart gives $w/c\approx0.50$; free water 190 kg/m³ (the 20 mm crushed, the 30 to 60 mm slump); the cement $=190/0.50=380$ kg/m³; the wet density 2400 kg/m³; the total aggregate $=2400-380-190=1830$ kg/m³; with 35 % fines: the fine 640 and the coarse 1190 kg/m³ (the proportion about 1 : 1.7 : 3.1).

**(b) Why trial mixes are important.** The proportions from the charts and the tables are only the **first approximation**, because the actual cement, the aggregate shape, the grading, the moisture and the admixtures vary; the trial batches **check the workability, the strength at 7/28 days, the cohesion, the finishing and the economy** and allow the adjustment of the water and the proportions before the structure is cast, so that the specified strength (the characteristic) is attained with the minimum cement.

**Difference from the nominal mix.** The **nominal mix** has the **fixed volume proportions** (1 : 1.5 : 3, 1 : 2 : 4) for the ordinary works (M5 to M20) and the strength is not tested by trials, so it is wasteful (conservative and the variable), while the **designed mix** (DoE, IS 10262) is **proportioned by the trial for the required strength and the workability** with the **control of the weight batching** and the economy (the M25 and above).

@s 4
**(a) Effect of creep.** Creep is the **time-dependent increase of the strain under a sustained load** (it continues for years: about 50 % in 2 months and 75 % in 1 year, and it is mostly recoverable by 25 to 30 %). Effects: (1) the **increased deflections** of the beams and the slabs (2 to 3 times the elastic deflection in the long term); (2) the **loss of the pre-stress** in the pre-stressed concrete (a major cause); (3) the **redistribution of the stress** from the concrete to the steel in the columns (the steel may yield), **the reduction of the effective stiffness**, the cracking and the increased the buckling; (4) the **relief of the stress concentration and the shrinkage stress** (the favourable effect: the thermal and the differential settlement stresses are reduced), and the effect on the long-term behaviour of the tall structures (the shortening). Creep is more with the higher w/c, the less aggregate, the high stress and the earlier loading, and less in the humid atmosphere.

**(b) Effect of the size of the aggregate on the strength.** For the same w/c: (1) the **larger aggregate needs less water** (the lower surface area and the lower paste for the same workability), so the strength should rise; (2) but the **bond area is smaller** and the **bleeding water collects under the large particles** (the weaker interfacial zone) and the **stress concentrates** at the larger particles, with the thermal and the shrinkage differential stresses, so in the high-strength concrete the **strength is reduced** with the size above 20 mm (the optimum size of 10 to 20 mm for the strength above 40 MPa; for the lean mixes the larger size, up to 40 mm, gives the higher strength). The **maximum size** is limited by the spacing of the reinforcement, the cover and the section.

**(c) Maturity of concrete.** The maturity is the measure of the **combined effect of the time and the temperature on the strength gain** of the concrete (the strength of the same mix is the same function of the maturity). Nurse-Saul function:

$$M=\sum(T-T_0)\,\Delta t\quad(^\circ\text{C-hours or days})$$

with $T$ the average temperature in the period $\Delta t$ and $T_0$ the datum temperature (about $-10\,^\circ$C, the temperature at which the strength gain stops). A **strength-maturity curve** is made in the laboratory (the strength against the log of the maturity), and then the **in-situ strength is estimated from the temperature record** (thermocouple, maturity meter), which allows the **safe time to strip the formwork, to stress the tendons, to open the road to the traffic**, and to assess the cold and the hot weather concreting and the steam curing. (The equivalent age is $t_e=\sum e^{-E/R(1/T-1/T_r)}\Delta t$, the Arrhenius-type method.)

@s 5
**(a) Quality control (QC) and quality assurance (QA).** **Quality control** is the set of the **operational techniques and the inspections and tests used to check that the materials and the workmanship meet the specification** during the production and the placing (the sieve analysis, the slump test, the cubes, the batching accuracy, the temperature, the curing, the testing of the cement); it is **corrective**, done by the contractor or the site laboratory, based on the **statistical** acceptance (IS 456: the mean $\ge f_{ck}+0.825s$ and the individual $\ge f_{ck}-3$ MPa). **Quality assurance** is the **planned and the systematic management system (the quality plan and the audit)** that gives the confidence that the quality requirements will be met: the **procedures, the documentation, the training, the approved suppliers, the inspection and the test plan and the corrective actions** (ISO 9001), the preventive, covering the whole process from the design and the procurement to the handing over. QA is the system (the prevention), and QC is the checking (the detection) within it.

**(b) Testing of concrete for the compressive strength (IS 516).**

1. **Specimens:** the **150 mm cubes** (or the 150 × 300 mm cylinders) are cast from the representative sample: the mould (the steel, the oiled) is filled in the 3 layers (the 50 mm) and each is compacted by the **tamping rod (35 strokes of the 16 mm rod) or the vibrator**, the top is levelled; at least **3 cubes for each age** (the sample of 6 cubes for the 7 and the 28 days).
2. **Curing:** covered with the wet sacks at $27\pm2\,^\circ$C for 24 h, demoulded and **cured in the water at $27\pm2\,^\circ$C** until the test (3, 7, 28 days).
3. **Testing:** the cube is taken out, wiped, the **dimensions are measured**, placed in the **compression testing machine (CTM)** with the cast **sides as the loaded faces** (not the trowelled top) and centred; the load is applied **without shock at the rate of 140 kg/cm² per minute (about 14 N/mm² per minute = 0.23 N/mm² per s, i.e. about 5.2 kN/s for a 150 mm cube)** until the specimen fails; the **maximum load $P$** is noted.
4. **Calculation:** $f_c=\dfrac{P}{A}$ ($A=150\times150=22\,500$ mm²); the **average of 3 specimens** is the strength (if the individual value differs by more than 15 % from the average, it is rejected); the strength of the cylinder $\approx0.8\times$ the cube. The acceptance is by the characteristic strength criterion of the IS 456.

@s 6
**Emerging concrete technologies.**

1. **Self-compacting concrete (SCC):** the highly flowable concrete (the superplasticiser, the viscosity agent, the powder) which fills the formwork and the congested reinforcement **without the vibration**; the better finish, the fast, the less noise and the labour.
2. **High-performance and the ultra-high-performance concrete (HPC/UHPC):** the low w/c (0.2 to 0.3), the silica fume, the fibres; the compressive strength 60 to 200 MPa, the very low permeability and the long durability; the slender and the long-span members.
3. **Fibre-reinforced concrete (steel, glass, polypropylene, basalt fibres):** the improved tensile and the flexural strength, the toughness, the crack control, the impact (the industrial floors, the shotcrete, the tunnel lining).
4. **Geopolymer and the green concrete:** the **cement-free** binder from the **fly ash or the slag with the alkali activator**, or the recycled aggregate and the industrial waste (the less $\text{CO}_2$, up to 60 to 80 % less).
5. **3D-printed concrete:** the additive manufacturing without the formwork.
6. **Self-healing concrete** (the bacteria *Bacillus* with the calcium lactate, the capsules, the crystalline admixtures) that closes the cracks; **translucent, the light-weight (the foam and the aerated), the pervious (the porous) concrete** for the drainage and the **roller-compacted concrete** (the dams, the pavement); **smart/ the ultra-ductile (engineered cementitious composite, ECC) and the nano-modified concrete** with the carbon nano-tubes and the nano-silica for the strength and the sensing; the **ready-mix concrete (RMC) and the precast** with the automated batching, the maturity and the sensor monitoring (the IoT).
