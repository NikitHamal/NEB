### Group A

@s 1
Two key-handling points: **issue keys only to authorised persons after signing in a key register** and keep unused keys locked in a key cabinet; and **never announce the room number with the key** and use card keys that are reprogrammed at every check-out. Lost keys are reported at once.

@s 2
**Occupancy ratio** is the percentage of rooms occupied compared with the rooms available for sale in a given period:

$$\text{Occupancy \%} = \frac{\text{Rooms occupied}}{\text{Rooms available}}\times 100$$

@s 3
**MC** stands for **Master of Ceremonies**, the person who hosts the programme, introduces speakers and keeps the event on schedule.

@s 4
Two dishes with garnish: **clear soup with chopped parsley or croutons**, and **grilled fish or steak with a lemon wedge and tomato or cucumber slices**. (Biryani with fried onion and coriander is another.)

@s 5
**Roux** is a cooked mixture of **equal weights of fat (butter) and flour** (1 : 1), used to thicken sauces and soups. It is cooked to white, blond or brown stage.

@s 6
Two organisational events: **seminar or conference** and **annual general meeting** (also product launch, trade fair and training workshop).

@s 7
Two functions of minerals: they **build bones and teeth** (calcium, phosphorus), and they **regulate body processes** such as blood formation (iron), nerve and muscle action (sodium, potassium) and thyroid function (iodine).

@s 8
The **faucet** (tap) is installed at the **wash basin and bathtub or shower of the bathroom**, and the **cistern** (flush tank) is fixed on or behind the **water closet (toilet)**.

@s 9
The **egg yolk** is used in Hollandaise sauce: yolks are whisked over gentle heat and emulsified with melted butter, with lemon juice and seasoning.

### Group B

@s 10
**Food poisoning** is an illness caused by eating food contaminated by harmful micro-organisms, their toxins, chemicals or natural poisons. The main causes are:

- **Bacteria:** Salmonella (eggs, poultry), E. coli (beef, water), Staphylococcus aureus (from skin and nose of handlers), Clostridium and Bacillus cereus (rice).
- **Poor personal hygiene:** unwashed hands, sores or cuts, coughing and sneezing over food, and handlers working when ill.
- **Cross-contamination:** raw meat and cooked food sharing boards, knives, cloths or fridge shelves.
- **Improper temperature:** keeping food in the danger zone (5 °C to 63 °C), undercooking, slow cooling, and re-heating below 75 °C.
- **Chemical contamination:** pesticides on vegetables, cleaning agents, and metals from damaged utensils.
- **Natural toxins:** poisonous mushrooms, green potatoes, certain fish and beans.
- **Pests and dirty premises:** flies, rats and cockroaches, unclean water and surfaces.

Prevention follows from these causes: cook thoroughly, chill promptly, separate raw and cooked items and keep high personal hygiene.

@s 11
**Evening turndown service** is given to **occupied rooms**, especially **VIP and suite rooms and all occupied rooms of star hotels**, between about 5 and 9 p.m., to prepare the room for the night. It is not given to vacant rooms or when "Do Not Disturb" is displayed.

**Process:**
1. **Knock, announce** "Housekeeping, turndown service" and enter if there is no objection.
2. **Draw the curtains** and switch on the bedside lamps.
3. **Fold back the bedspread** in a neat triangle on the guest's side and **turn down the blanket** and top sheet; fluff the pillows.
4. **Place a chocolate or flower, and the next-day weather card or door hanger,** on the pillow, and put slippers and a bathrobe beside the bed.
5. **Tidy the room:** empty ashtrays and waste bins, remove used glasses and trays, and straighten the guest's belongings without disturbing them.
6. **Bathroom:** replace used towels, wipe the basin and floor, and refill amenities.
7. **Check lights and AC,** leave the TV remote ready, and **leave quietly,** recording the service on the sheet.

@s 12
**Flower arrangement** is the art of arranging cut flowers, leaves and branches in a container in a pleasing design to decorate the lobby, rooms, restaurant and event halls.

**Types (styles) of arrangement:**
1. **Mass arrangement:** many flowers packed closely to give a block of colour, such as in a round or oval bouquet; used in lobbies and banquets.
2. **Line arrangement:** a few flowers and branches placed to emphasise line and height, as in Japanese ikebana; simple and elegant.
3. **Mass-line arrangement:** a combination of mass in the focal area and a clear line to give height.
4. **Free-style (modern) arrangement:** abstract, using unusual containers and materials.
5. **Traditional shapes:** triangular, crescent, fan, vertical and horizontal (table) arrangements.
6. **Others:** floating flowers, garlands, and bouquets or corsages.

A flower arrangement should match the colour, scale and purpose of the place and be refreshed daily.

**Or (Q12). Cleaning of metals.** Metals (brass, copper, silver, stainless steel, chrome) tarnish and need regular cleaning.

| Metal | Agent | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Brass and copper | Brasso, or lemon and salt | Apply, rub with a soft cloth, wash, dry and buff |
| Silver | Silvo or silver dip, plate powder | **Polishing** by rubbing, or **dipping** in a tray of hot water with soda and aluminium foil, then wash and dry |
| Stainless steel | Mild detergent, vinegar | Wash, rinse and dry to avoid spots |
| Chrome | Soft damp cloth, mild cleaner | Wipe and polish; no abrasives |

**Process:** (1) remove dust and food stains; (2) apply the polish with a damp soft cloth; (3) rub along the grain; (4) rinse in clean water to remove the polish; (5) dry and buff to a shine; (6) store tidily. Use gloves and ventilation.

This question is personal; a model answer is given.

"I last dined out at a mid-priced restaurant in Kathmandu with my family for a birthday." I chose that restaurant for the following reasons:

- **Food quality and menu variety** suited every family member, including vegetarian dishes.
- **Cleanliness and hygiene** of the dining area, kitchen and washrooms.
- **Location and parking:** it was close and easy to reach.
- **Price and value for money** matched our budget, and the menu prices were clearly shown.
- **Service:** friendly, quick and knowledgeable staff.
- **Ambience:** comfortable seating, soft music and lighting.
- **Reputation and reviews** from friends and the internet.

In general, guests select a restaurant on the basis of food, price, service, ambience, hygiene and convenience, which is why a restaurateur must pay attention to all of these.

**Or (Q13). KOT format.** A **Kitchen Order Ticket (KOT)** is the written order passed from the waiter to the kitchen and bar. Items are written in the order of service: juice first, then the food, and the hot beverage last.

| KOT No. 041 | Table 6 | Covers 1 | Waiter: R. | Date / time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Juice (fresh orange) | 1 | To bar | |
| 2 | Omelette (masala) | 1 | To kitchen | |
| 3 | Tea (milk tea) | 1 | To bar | |

The original goes to the kitchen, the duplicate to the cashier and the triplicate remains in the waiter's pad. If the guest asks for tea with the omelette, the order follows the guest's request.

@s 14
**Mise en place** is a French term meaning "everything in its place": the preparation of the restaurant before service so that service is smooth and quick.

**Process:**
1. **Cleaning:** sweep, mop and dust the floor, tables, chairs and side stations.
2. **Table setting:** lay the cloth, covers, napkins, cutlery, glassware, menu cards, condiments and flower vases, as per the meal period.
3. **Side station:** stock spare cutlery, crockery, linen, trays, water jugs, ashtrays, bill folders and order pads.
4. **Equipment check:** test the hot plate, coffee machine, lights and music; polish glass and cutlery.
5. **Food and beverage preparation:** cut lemons, fill sauces and butter, prepare water and ice, and check the day's specials with the kitchen.
6. **Staff briefing and grooming:** the captain assigns stations and explains the menu and the reservations, and staff check their uniform and hygiene.
7. **Final check** of the room, then the doors open to the guests.

@s 15
**Definition.** A **sauce** is a thickened, flavoured liquid that is served with or used in a dish to add moisture, flavour, colour and richness and to improve the appearance and digestibility of food. A good sauce has the right consistency, a smooth texture, a balanced flavour and a shine.

**Mother sauces.** The five classical mother (leading) sauces are béchamel, velouté, espagnole, tomato and hollandaise. The **stock-based mother sauces** are **velouté** (white stock) and **espagnole** (brown stock); **tomato** sauce is made from tomato and stock too.

**(i) Velouté (1 litre).**

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| White stock (chicken, veal or fish) | 1 litre |
| Butter | 50 g |
| Flour | 50 g |
| Salt, white pepper | to taste |

Method: (1) melt the butter in a heavy-bottomed pan; (2) add the flour and cook for 4 to 5 minutes to a pale, sandy **blond roux**; (3) cool the roux slightly; (4) add the hot stock slowly, whisking continuously to avoid lumps; (5) bring to the boil, lower the heat and simmer for 30 to 40 minutes, skimming off the impurities; (6) season and strain through muslin. Derivatives: Allemande (egg yolk and cream), Suprême (cream), Normande (fish velouté with mushroom and cream). Used with chicken, veal and fish dishes.

**(ii) Espagnole or brown sauce (1 litre).**

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Brown stock | 1.5 litres |
| Butter or fat | 60 g |
| Flour | 60 g |
| Mirepoix (onion, carrot, celery) | 150 g |
| Tomato purée | 50 g |
| Bouquet garni, salt, pepper | to taste |

Method: (1) prepare a **brown roux** by cooking fat and flour slowly until nut brown; (2) in another pan brown the mirepoix, add the tomato purée and cook for a few minutes; (3) add the cooled roux and the hot brown stock gradually, stirring; (4) add the bouquet garni, simmer for 3 to 4 hours, skimming regularly; (5) strain, season and reduce to the coating consistency. Derivatives: demi-glace, Madeira, Chasseur and Robert sauces; served with red meat and game.

**Or (Q15). Stock.** **Stock** is a clear, thin, flavoured liquid made by simmering bones, meat, vegetables and herbs in water, and it forms the base of soups, sauces and gravies. The standard quantity is **1 litre of water for every 500 g bones and 100 g vegetables (mirepoix)**.

*White stock.* Ingredients (for 4 litres): 4 litres cold water, 2 kg raw chicken or veal bones (blanched), 400 g mirepoix (onion, carrot, celery, leek), a bouquet garni and 10 peppercorns. Method: (1) chop the bones and **blanch** them to remove the blood; (2) place them in cold water and bring slowly to the boil; (3) skim, and add the mirepoix and bouquet garni uncoloured; (4) simmer gently for 4 to 6 hours for beef bones (2 to 3 hours for chicken, 30 to 40 minutes for fish); (5) skim regularly and never allow it to boil hard; (6) strain, cool quickly, label and refrigerate.

*Brown stock.* Ingredients: the same quantities, but using beef or veal bones and **tomato purée 100 g**. Method: (1) **roast the bones** in an oven at about 200 °C until brown; (2) brown the mirepoix in a pan and add the tomato purée; (3) put the bones, vegetables and cold water in a stock pot and bring to the boil; (4) skim, add the bouquet garni and simmer for 6 to 8 hours; (5) skim, strain and use or chill. The roasting gives brown stock its colour and deeper flavour and it is used for espagnole and brown soups.

@s 16
**Modes of payment** used in star hotels are: (1) **cash** in local or foreign currency; (2) **credit and debit cards** (Visa, Mastercard and others); (3) **travellers' cheque**; (4) **personal or company cheque and bank draft**; (5) **city ledger (direct billing) to companies** under credit agreement; (6) **vouchers** from travel agents and tour operators; (7) **digital wallets and QR payments** such as eSewa, Khalti and mobile banking, and (8) **advance deposit or bank transfer**.

**Procedure for taking payment: cash**
1. Present the **itemised bill** to the guest and check that all charges are included.
2. Receive the cash and **count it in front of the guest**; check the notes for forged ones and for foreign currency verify the day's exchange rate.
3. Enter the amount in the **folio and the cash register**, and give the **change**.
4. Issue a **receipt** (official invoice with the PAN and VAT) and thank the guest.
5. At the end of the shift, the cashier deposits the cash and balances the account.

**Procedure for taking payment: credit card**
1. Present the bill and ask the guest for the card; **check the name, signature, expiry date and the card type** that is accepted by the hotel.
2. **Swipe or insert the card** in the POS machine and enter the exact amount; the guest enters the PIN.
3. Wait for **authorisation** from the bank; if declined, politely ask for another card.
4. Print the **slip, let the guest sign it,** and compare the signature with the card.
5. Return the card with the guest's copy of the slip and the invoice, and file the merchant copy for settlement with the bank.
6. Never leave the card out of the guest's sight, and keep the card details confidential.

Careful handling of payment avoids loss to the hotel and protects the guest's trust.
