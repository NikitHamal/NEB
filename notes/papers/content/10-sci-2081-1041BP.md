### Section A — answer key

| Q | 1(a) | 1(b) | 1(c) | 1(d) | 1(e) | 1(f) | 1(g) | 1(h) | 1(i) | 1(j) |

**1(a). 46.** A human body cell (somatic cell, such as a skin cell) is diploid, $2n = 46$, that is 23 pairs. Options 40, 42 and 44 are not the human chromosome number; 23 is the number in a gamete.

**1(b). $P = 1/f$.** The power of a lens is the reciprocal of its focal length in metres, so $f = 1/P$. $M = v/u$ (not $v = M/u$) and $M = I/O$ are magnification relations, and $f = v/u$ is not a lens relation (the lens formula is $1/f = 1/v - 1/u$). The print of this stem is small and its wording is ambiguous; option (iii) is the only one that connects $f$ correctly, so it is the key.

**1(c). Tricuspid valve.** The valve between the right auricle (atrium) and right ventricle has three flaps and is the tricuspid valve. It lets blood pass between these two chambers and prevents back-flow. The bicuspid valve is on the left side, and the aortic and pulmonic valves guard the exits of the ventricles. The stem is printed with the direction reversed relative to normal flow; the valve between the two chambers is the tricuspid in any case.

**1(d). Asiatic pennywort (Ghodtapre).** It is the plant of the list that is taken for digestive complaints and has antimicrobial action. Neem is an antiseptic for skin, Gurjo is used for fever and immunity, and Tulsi for cough and cold. This key rests on the textbook uses of the four plants; no official key is published.

**1(e). Ethylene glycol.** A di-hydric alcohol has two –OH groups: $\mathrm{HOCH_2CH_2OH}$. Ethanol and methanol have one –OH, glycerol has three (tri-hydric).

**1(f). Increases.** $g = GM/R^2$. If the mass stays the same and the radius shrinks to the Moon's size, $R$ falls and $g$ rises (by a factor $(R_E/R_M)^2 \approx 13.5$). It cannot be zero or unchanged.

**1(g). Electric motor changes electrical energy into kinetic energy.** The motor converts electrical to mechanical (kinetic) energy; the generator does the reverse. They work on different principles (force on a current-carrying conductor in a field versus electromagnetic induction), so the other three statements fail.

**1(h). Nature of matter.** Specific heat capacity is a property of the substance (for water 4200 J kg$^{-1}$ °C$^{-1}$); it does not depend on mass, volume or temperature change. Mass and temperature affect the heat required, not $c$.

**1(i). $\mathrm{kg\,m^{2}s^{-2}}$.** $E = F\times d = (\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}})(\mathrm{m}) = \mathrm{kg\,m^{2}s^{-2}}$. The others are the units of force ($\mathrm{kg\,ms^{-2}}$), momentum ($\mathrm{kg\,ms^{-1}}$) and a quantity of different dimensions.

**1(j). 875 N.** Pascal's law: pressure is the same, $F_B/A_B = F_A/A_A$, so $F_B = 500\times 350/200 = 875$ N. The other values correspond to wrong area ratios.

**s-block elements** (groups 1 and 2, valence electrons in the s sub-shell, $ns^{1\text{ to }2}$): (1) they are soft, highly reactive metals with low melting points and densities, and are strongly electropositive; (2) they lose their valence electrons to form $+1$ or $+2$ ions and form ionic compounds (e.g. NaCl, CaO).

**p-block elements** (groups 13 to 18, valence electrons filling the p sub-shell, $ns^2np^{1\text{ to }6}$): (1) the block contains metals, non-metals and metalloids, and includes all the noble gases; (2) most of them form covalent compounds and show more than one oxidation state (e.g. nitrogen, phosphorus, halogens).

(i) Advantages: (1) the semen of one superior (high-yielding or disease-resistant) male can be used to fertilise many females, which improves the breed quickly; (2) it avoids the cost and trouble of keeping and transporting a male and prevents the spread of sexually transmitted disease between animals; it also allows breeding when the male is far away or injured.
