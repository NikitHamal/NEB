### Group A

@s 1
Two responsibilities of a financial manager: **estimating the funds required and raising them** from the best sources, and **allocating funds to profitable investments** while managing working capital. Distributing profit as dividend and managing financial risk are also his duties.

@s 2
The **statement of financial position (balance sheet)** shows the financial position of a business, that is, its assets, liabilities and owners' equity, on a particular date.

@s 3
The **fixed assets turnover ratio** (Sales ÷ Net fixed assets) shows how many rupees of sales the firm generates from each rupee invested in fixed assets, and so measures how efficiently fixed assets are used.

@s 4
Two advantages of short-term debt: it is **cheaper and quicker to obtain** (lower interest, fewer conditions), and it is **flexible**, because it can be repaid when the need is over so the firm does not pay interest on idle funds.

@s 5
Features of an optimal capital structure: it **minimises the overall cost of capital** and so **maximises the market value of the firm**; it also keeps **risk and control** within acceptable limits and leaves **flexibility** to raise funds in future.

@s 6
Two differences: (i) **preferred stock** receives a **fixed dividend**, whereas the dividend on **common stock** varies with profit; (ii) preferred stockholders have a **prior claim** on income and assets, and normally no voting right, whereas common stockholders are the residual owners with voting rights.

@s 7
Two characteristics of a bond: it is a **long-term debt security** with a **fixed coupon (interest) rate** paid periodically on the par value, and it has a **maturity date** at which the par value is repaid. Bondholders are creditors and have no voting right.

@s 8
**Investment decision** (capital budgeting decision) is the decision on how to invest the firm's funds in long-term assets or projects so that they earn a return higher than the cost of funds and increase the value of the firm.

@s 9
A **forward rate** is the exchange rate fixed today, in a forward contract, for the purchase or sale of a foreign currency at a **specified future date**. It protects the parties from exchange-rate fluctuation.

@s 10
Short-term source is 40% of total debt, so total debt = Rs. 3,00,000 ÷ 0.40 = **Rs. 7,50,000** (long-term debt = Rs. 4,50,000).

@s 11
Dollars needed = Rs. 68,900 ÷ Rs. 130 = **USD 530**.

### Group B

@s 12
**Economics** studies how society allocates scarce resources, while **business finance** applies those ideas to the raising and use of funds in a firm. The link is close:

- **Microeconomics** (demand, supply, cost and price) helps the finance manager to forecast sales, cost and profit and to price products.
- **Macroeconomics** (inflation, interest rate, exchange rate, GDP growth, monetary and fiscal policy) shapes the cost of funds, the demand for the firm's products and the risk of investment.
- **Marginal analysis** is used in finance: a project is accepted if marginal benefit exceeds marginal cost, and the optimum capital structure is found where the marginal cost of capital is lowest.
- **Time value and opportunity cost,** key ideas of economics, underlie discounting, NPV and the cost of capital.
- **Efficient allocation.** Financial markets, which are studied in economics, channel savings to the best investments and thus link the firm to the national economy.

So economics provides the theory and the environment, and finance applies them to value-maximising decisions.

@s 13
The **income statement** (profit and loss account) shows the revenue, expenses and net profit of a firm for a period. It is prepared from the **trial balance** with adjustments.

**Steps:** (1) Start with **net sales**. (2) Deduct **cost of goods sold** to get **gross profit**. (3) Deduct **operating expenses** (selling, administrative, depreciation) to get **operating profit (EBIT)**. (4) Add other income and deduct **interest** to get **profit before tax (EBT)**. (5) Deduct **income tax** to get **net profit after tax**, which is then divided between dividends and retained earnings.

**Example (Rs.):**

| Particulars | Rs. |
|---|---:|
| Net sales | 10,00,000 |
| Less: Cost of goods sold | 6,00,000 |
| **Gross profit** | **4,00,000** |
| Less: Operating expenses | 1,50,000 |
| **EBIT** | **2,50,000** |
| Less: Interest | 50,000 |
| **EBT** | **2,00,000** |
| Less: Tax at 25% | 50,000 |
| **Net profit** | **1,50,000** |

@s 14
Terms **4/15, net 35**: a 4% discount if paid within 15 days; otherwise the whole amount is due on day 35. By not taking the discount the buyer gets 20 more days of credit (35 − 15). With a 360-day year:

$$\text{APC} = \frac{4}{96}\times\frac{360}{35-15} = 0.041667\times 18 = 0.75 = \mathbf{75\%}$$

$$\text{EAR} = \left(1+\frac{4}{96}\right)^{360/20}-1 = (1.041667)^{18}-1 = 1.0851$$

The **annual percentage cost is 75%** and the **effective annual rate is about 108.51%**. Since this exceeds any bank interest rate, the firm should take the discount, borrowing from a bank if it must.

@s 15
$$\text{BEP (units)} = \frac{\text{Operating fixed cost}}{P-V}$$

Given $P = 60$, $V = 40$, BEP = 8,000 units.

$$\text{BEP in rupees} = 8{,}000\times 60 = \mathbf{Rs.\ 4{,}80{,}000}$$

$$\text{Contribution per unit} = 60-40 = 20$$

$$\text{Operating fixed cost} = 8{,}000\times 20 = \mathbf{Rs.\ 1{,}60{,}000}$$

Check: sales Rs. 4,80,000 − variable cost Rs. 3,20,000 = contribution Rs. 1,60,000, which equals the fixed cost, so profit is nil. The contribution margin ratio is 20/60 = 33.3%, so BEP in rupees = 1,60,000 ÷ 0.3333 = Rs. 4,80,000 as well.

@s 16
**Types of investment proposals** (projects) are classified according to their purpose:

1. **Replacement investment.** Old, worn-out or obsolete machinery is replaced to keep the present level of operations, so risk is low.
2. **Expansion investment.** Capacity is increased, or new branches are opened, to meet growing demand for existing products.
3. **New product (diversification) investment.** Funds are put into new products or markets, which raises both return and risk.
4. **Cost reduction (modernisation) investment.** Better technology or automation is bought to lower unit cost and raise quality.
5. **Mandatory (welfare and safety) investment.** Projects required by law, such as pollution control, safety equipment and staff welfare, which yield no direct return.
6. **Mutually exclusive, independent and contingent projects** (classification by relationship): acceptance of one rules out the other, or has no effect on the others, or depends on another project.

@s 17
Sales Rs. 8,00,000; inventory turnover 8 times; receivable collection period 24 days; payable deferral period 18 days; 340 working days.

**a) Inventory conversion period**

$$\text{ICP} = \frac{340}{8} = \mathbf{42.5\ days}$$

**b) Cash conversion cycle**

$$\text{Operating cycle} = \text{ICP} + \text{RCP} = 42.5 + 24 = 66.5\ \text{days}$$

$$\text{CCC} = 66.5 - 18 = \mathbf{48.5\ days}$$

The firm must finance its operations for 48.5 days between paying suppliers and collecting cash from customers. The sales figure itself is not needed because the turnover ratio is given directly.

@s 18
**Factors affecting dividend decision:**

1. **Earnings and profitability.** Dividends are paid out of profit, so stable and high earnings allow higher payout.
2. **Liquidity.** Cash dividend needs cash in hand; a profitable firm short of cash may pay a stock dividend instead.
3. **Growth and investment opportunities.** A growing firm with good projects keeps more profit and pays less.
4. **Legal restrictions.** Company law forbids dividends from capital, and loan agreements may restrict them.
5. **Shareholders' preference and tax.** Investors who need regular income prefer high dividends, and tax rates on dividend and capital gain change the preference.
6. **Stability of earnings and past policy.** Firms with stable earnings pay a steady dividend, and a cut is read as bad news.
7. **Access to capital markets and control.** Firms that can borrow easily can pay more; firms that fear dilution of control retain earnings.

@s 19
A **multinational corporation (MNC)** is a company that owns and runs business in two or more countries. Its role in a host country's development:

- **Capital and investment.** It brings foreign direct investment, which fills the gap between domestic saving and investment needs.
- **Technology and know-how.** It transfers modern technology, management and marketing skills to local firms and workers.
- **Employment and income.** It creates direct and indirect jobs and raises incomes and skills.
- **Exports and foreign exchange.** It opens world markets for local products and earns foreign currency, improving the balance of payments.
- **Infrastructure and competition.** It builds roads, power and communication, and pushes local firms to raise quality and efficiency.
- **Government revenue.** It pays taxes, customs duty and royalty.

For this reason developing countries like Nepal try to attract MNCs, while regulating them to avoid profit repatriation without reinvestment and harm to local industry.

**Or (Q19). Cross rate AUD/USD.** 1 SGD = 1.8484 AUD and 1 SGD = 1.3748 USD, so both currencies are expressed in the common currency SGD:

$$1\ \text{AUD} = \frac{1.3748}{1.8484}\ \text{USD} = \mathbf{0.7438\ USD}$$

The inverse is $1\ \text{USD} = 1.8484/1.3748 = \mathbf{1.3445\ AUD}$. The cross rate between the Australian dollar and the American dollar is therefore **AUD 1 = USD 0.7438** (USD 1 = AUD 1.3445).

@s 20
**Working figures.** Current assets = cash 1,00,000 + debtors 2,00,000 + stock 50,000 = Rs. 3,50,000. Current liabilities = creditors 80,000 + overdraft 1,20,000 = Rs. 2,00,000. Debt = debenture + creditors + overdraft = Rs. 3,00,000. Equity = capital + retained earnings = Rs. 4,50,000. Total assets = Rs. 7,50,000 (both sides agree).

**a) Current ratio** $= \dfrac{3{,}50{,}000}{2{,}00{,}000} = \mathbf{1.75:1}$

**b) Liquid ratio** $= \dfrac{\text{Current assets} - \text{Stock}}{\text{Current liabilities}}$

$$= \frac{3{,}50{,}000-50{,}000}{2{,}00{,}000} = \mathbf{1.5:1}$$

**c) Inventory turnover ratio** $= \dfrac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Inventory}}$

$$= \frac{15{,}00{,}000}{50{,}000} = \mathbf{30\ times}$$

(Cost of goods sold is not given, so the sales basis is used.)

**d) Debt-equity ratio** $= \dfrac{\text{Total debt}}{\text{Equity}}$

$$= \frac{1{,}00{,}000+80{,}000+1{,}20{,}000}{3{,}00{,}000+1{,}50{,}000} = \frac{3{,}00{,}000}{4{,}50{,}000} = \mathbf{0.67:1}$$

**e) Total assets turnover** $= \dfrac{15{,}00{,}000}{7{,}50{,}000} = \mathbf{2\ times}$

**f) Return on capital employed.** Capital employed = equity + long-term debt (debenture) = 4,50,000 + 1,00,000 = Rs. 5,50,000. Only net profit is given (no interest or tax), so net profit is used:

$$\text{ROCE} = \frac{1{,}00{,}000}{5{,}50{,}000} = 0.1818 = \mathbf{18.18\%}$$

**g) Return on equity** $= \dfrac{1{,}00{,}000}{4{,}50{,}000} = 0.2222 = \mathbf{22.22\%}$

**h) Net profit ratio** $= \dfrac{1{,}00{,}000}{15{,}00{,}000} = 0.0667 = \mathbf{6.67\%}$

**Comment.** A current ratio of 1.75 and liquid ratio of 1.5 show adequate short-term solvency (the usual standards are 2 : 1 and 1 : 1). Assets are used well (turnover 2 times) and the 22.22% return on equity is good, although the net margin of 6.67% is thin.

@s 21
**Meaning.** An **ordinary (common) share** is a share whose holders are the **real owners of the company**. They have a residual claim on profit and assets, since they are paid only after creditors and preference shareholders, and they take the main risk of the business.

**Five features of ordinary shares:**

1. **Ownership and control.** Holders have voting rights at the general meeting and elect the board, so they control the company.
2. **Residual claim on income.** Dividend is paid only from profit after interest and preference dividend, and it is not fixed; it may be high in good years and nil in bad years.
3. **Residual claim on assets.** At liquidation they are repaid last, after all creditors and preference shareholders.
4. **Limited liability.** The loss of a shareholder is limited to the unpaid amount on shares held, and personal property is safe.
5. **Pre-emptive right.** Existing shareholders have the first right to buy new shares in proportion to their holdings, protecting their control and ownership share.
6. **No maturity and free transferability.** Ordinary share capital is never repaid in the life of the company, but the shares can be sold on the stock exchange.
7. **Capital gain and bonus.** Shareholders earn from rising share price and bonus shares as well as dividend.

Ordinary shares are the safest source of funds for a company because they carry no fixed charge, but the cost of equity is the highest.

**Or (Q21). a) Perpetual bond.** Coupon = 12% × 1,000 = Rs. 120 a year for ever, required return = 15%:

$$V_0 = \frac{\text{Interest}}{r_d} = \frac{120}{0.15} = \mathbf{Rs.\ 800}$$

The bond sells below par because the coupon rate (12%) is less than the required return (15%).

**b) Constant-growth model.** $D_0 = 20$, $r = 14\%$, $g = 8\%$:

$$D_1 = 20\times 1.08 = 21.60$$

$$P_0 = \frac{D_1}{r-g} = \frac{21.60}{0.14-0.08} = \mathbf{Rs.\ 360}$$

If the projects are mutually exclusive, choose A.
