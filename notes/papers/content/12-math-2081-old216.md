**(b)** Let $\mathbf{Q}^+$ be the set of positive rational numbers and $a\circ b = \dfrac{ab}{4}$. We verify the group axioms and commutativity.

1. *Closure.* For $a, b \in \mathbf{Q}^+$, the product $ab$ is a positive rational and so is $\dfrac{ab}{4}$. Hence $a\circ b \in \mathbf{Q}^+$.
2. *Associativity.* $(a\circ b)\circ c = \dfrac{ab}{4}\circ c = \dfrac{abc}{16}$ and $a\circ(b\circ c) = a\circ\dfrac{bc}{4} = \dfrac{abc}{16}$. They are equal.
3. *Identity.* Let $e$ satisfy $a\circ e = a$. Then $\dfrac{ae}{4} = a$, so $e = 4 \in \mathbf{Q}^+$. Also $e\circ a = a$ by the same working.
4. *Inverse.* For $a \in \mathbf{Q}^+$ let $a'$ satisfy $a\circ a' = 4$. Then $\dfrac{aa'}{4} = 4$, so $a' = \dfrac{16}{a} \in \mathbf{Q}^+$, and $a'\circ a = 4$ too.
5. *Commutativity.* $a\circ b = \dfrac{ab}{4} = \dfrac{ba}{4} = b\circ a$.

All five conditions hold, therefore $(\mathbf{Q}^+, \circ)$ is an abelian group.

**Or (the group-theory proof).** Let $e$ be the identity of the group $(G, *)$.

(ii) By definition $a^{-1}*a = e = a*a^{-1}$. This says exactly that $a$ is an inverse of $a^{-1}$. By uniqueness of the inverse, $(a^{-1})^{-1} = a$.

In the expansion of $\left(x - \dfrac1x\right)^{2n}$ the number of terms is $2n + 1$, which is odd, so there is exactly one middle term, the $(n+1)$th term.
$$T_{r+1} = {}^{2n}C_r\,x^{2n-r}\left(-\frac1x\right)^r = {}^{2n}C_r\,(-1)^r\,x^{2n-2r}.$$
Put $r = n$:
$$T_{n+1} = {}^{2n}C_n\,(-1)^n\,x^{0} = (-1)^n\,\frac{(2n)!}{n!\,n!}.$$
Now write $(2n)!$ by separating the odd and even factors:
$$(2n)! = [1\cdot3\cdot5\cdots(2n-1)]\times[2\cdot4\cdot6\cdots2n].$$
The even factors are $2\cdot4\cdots2n = 2^n(1\cdot2\cdots n) = 2^n\,n!$. Therefore
$$(2n)! = [1\cdot3\cdot5\cdots(2n-1)]\;2^n\;n!.$$
Substituting:
$$T_{n+1} = (-1)^n\,\frac{[1\cdot3\cdot5\cdots(2n-1)]\,2^n\,n!}{n!\,n!} = \frac{1\cdot3\cdot5\cdots(2n-1)}{n!}\,(-1)^n\,2^n = \frac{1\cdot3\cdot5\cdots(2n-1)}{n!}(-2)^n.$$

**Definition.** The vector (cross) product of two vectors $\vec a$ and $\vec b$ inclined at an angle $\theta$ $(0\le\theta\le\pi)$ is the vector
$$\vec a\times\vec b = ab\sin\theta\;\hat n,$$
where $\hat n$ is the unit vector perpendicular to both $\vec a$ and $\vec b$, in the direction in which a right-handed screw advances when turned from $\vec a$ to $\vec b$. **Sine rule by the vector method.** Let the sides of the triangle $ABC$ be $\vec{BC} = \vec a$, $\vec{CA} = \vec b$, $\vec{AB} = \vec c$, taken in order round the triangle, so
$$\vec a + \vec b + \vec c = \vec 0.$$
Cross multiply by $\vec a$ on the left; since $\vec a\times\vec a = \vec 0$:
$$\vec a\times\vec b + \vec a\times\vec c = \vec 0\ \Longrightarrow\ \vec a\times\vec b = -\,\vec a\times\vec c = \vec c\times\vec a.$$
Cross multiply the same relation by $\vec b$: $\vec b\times\vec c + \vec b\times\vec a = \vec 0$, so $\vec b\times\vec c = \vec a\times\vec b$. Hence
$$\vec a\times\vec b = \vec b\times\vec c = \vec c\times\vec a.$$
Equate the magnitudes. The angle between $\vec a$ and $\vec b$ is $180^\circ - C$, between $\vec b$ and $\vec c$ is $180^\circ - A$, between $\vec c$ and $\vec a$ is $180^\circ - B$, and $\sin(180^\circ - \theta) = \sin\theta$:
$$ab\,\sin C = bc\,\sin A = ca\,\sin B.$$
Divide throughout by $abc$:
$$\frac{\sin C}{c} = \frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{\sin B}{b}.$$
Hence proved. (All three products equal $2\times$ the area of the triangle.)

**Or.** *Kinetic energy* is the energy a body has because of its motion: $K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ for mass $m$ moving with speed $v$. *Potential energy* (gravitational) is the energy a body has because of its position: $U = mgh$ for mass $m$ at height $h$ above the reference level (the ground), where $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity.

*Proof for a freely falling body.* Let a body of mass $m$ be released from rest at a height $H$ above the ground. After it has fallen a distance $s$ it is at height $h = H - s$ and its speed is $v$, given by $v^2 = u^2 + 2gs = 2gs$ (since $u = 0$).
$$K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{1}{2}m(2gs) = mgs,$$
$$U = mgh = mg(H - s).$$
$$K + U = mgs + mg(H - s) = mgH.$$
This is independent of $s$, so the total energy $K + U = mgH$ is the same at every point of the motion. At the start ($s = 0$): $K = 0$, $U = mgH$. At the ground ($s = H$): $K = mgH$, $U = 0$. Midway ($s = H/2$): $K = U = \frac{1}{2}mgH$. The loss of potential energy is exactly the gain of kinetic energy, so the sum remains constant (the law of conservation of mechanical energy).
