### Group A

@s 1
**(a) (3 marks).** $|u|\le k$ means $-k\le u\le k$. So $|2x-5|\le3\iff-3\le2x-5\le3$. Add 5 to every part: $2\le2x\le8$. Divide by 2: $1\le x\le4$. The inequality is **$1\le x\le4$**, that is $x\in[1,4]$.

**(b) (3 marks).** For $R=\{(1,1),(2,4),(3,9),(4,16)\}$ the domain is the set of first elements and the range is the set of second elements.

- Domain $=\{1,2,3,4\}$
- Range $=\{1,4,9,16\}$

(The relation is $y=x^2$ on the domain $\{1,2,3,4\}$.)

@s 2
**(a) (3 marks).** The yearly deposits $500,1000,1500,\dots$ form an A.P. with $a=500$, $d=500$ and $n=16$.
$$S_{16}=\frac{n}{2}\bigl[2a+(n-1)d\bigr]=8\bigl[1000+15\times500\bigr]=8\times8500.$$
He will have saved **Rs. 68,000** (the sixteenth deposit is $500\times16=8000$; check: $8\times(500+8000)=68000$). *Print reading:* the question asks for the total deposited, so no interest is added.

**(b) (3 marks).** $5\dfrac1{16}=\dfrac{81}{16}$. Inserting 4 G.M.s makes a G.P. of 6 terms with $a=\dfrac23$ and $ar^5=\dfrac{81}{16}$.
$$r^5=\frac{81}{16}\div\frac23=\frac{81}{16}\times\frac32=\frac{243}{32}\ \Rightarrow\ r=\frac32.$$
The means are $\dfrac23\times\dfrac32=1,\ \ 1\times\dfrac32=\dfrac32,\ \ \dfrac94,\ \ \dfrac{27}{8}$. So the four geometric means are **$1,\ \tfrac32,\ \tfrac94,\ \tfrac{27}8$**. Check: $\tfrac{27}8\times\tfrac32=\tfrac{81}{16}$.

@s 3
**(a) (3 marks).** A plate uses 3 different digits out of 6, with order mattering: $^6P_3=6\times5\times4=\mathbf{120}$ license plates.

**(b) (3 marks).** Use $|AB|=|A|\,|B|$.
$$|A|=(-3)(3)-(-2)(5)=-9+10=1,\qquad|B|=(3)(3)-(2)(-5)=9+10=19.$$
So $|AB|=1\times19=\mathbf{19}$. Direct check: $AB=\begin{pmatrix}1&-12\\0&19\end{pmatrix}$, whose determinant is $19$.

**(b) (3 marks).** Direct substitution gives $\tfrac00$, so factorise: $x^3-27=(x-3)(x^2+3x+9)$ and $x^2-5x+6=(x-3)(x-2)$.

@s 6
**(a) (3 marks).** A function is continuous at $x=a$ if $f(a)$ exists, $\lim_{x\to a}f(x)$ exists and the two are equal.

- $f(4)=\dfrac{16-16}{4-4}=\dfrac00$, so $f(4)$ is **not defined**.
- For $x\ne4$, $f(x)=\dfrac{(x-4)(x+4)}{x-4}=x+4$, so $\lim_{x\to4}f(x)=8$.

The limit exists but $f(4)$ does not, so $f$ is **discontinuous at $x=4$**. The discontinuity is removable: defining $f(4)=8$ would make it continuous.

**(b) (3 marks).** $x=2a(t+3)\Rightarrow\dfrac{dx}{dt}=2a$, and $y=a(t^2+7)\Rightarrow\dfrac{dy}{dt}=2at$.
$$\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{dy/dt}{dx/dt}=\frac{2at}{2a}=\mathbf{t}.$$

@s 11
Let $\Delta$ be the given determinant. Apply $C_1\to C_1+C_2+C_3$. Every entry of the new first column becomes $(1+a)+1+1=a+3$:
$$\Delta=\begin{vmatrix}a+3&1&1\\a+3&1+a&1\\a+3&1&1+a\end{vmatrix}=(a+3)\begin{vmatrix}1&1&1\\1&1+a&1\\1&1&1+a\end{vmatrix}.$$
Now apply $R_2\to R_2-R_1$ and $R_3\to R_3-R_1$:
$$\Delta=(a+3)\begin{vmatrix}1&1&1\\0&a&0\\0&0&a\end{vmatrix}.$$
The determinant is upper triangular, so it equals the product of the diagonal entries $1\cdot a\cdot a=a^2$. Therefore
$$\Delta=(a+3)\,a^2=a^2(a+3).\qquad\blacksquare$$

@s 12
Let $f(x)=\dfrac1{1+x}$. By the definition, $f'(x)=\displaystyle\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$.
$$f(x+h)-f(x)=\frac1{1+x+h}-\frac1{1+x}=\frac{(1+x)-(1+x+h)}{(1+x+h)(1+x)}=\frac{-h}{(1+x+h)(1+x)}.$$
Divide by $h$ and let $h\to0$:
$$f'(x)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{-1}{(1+x+h)(1+x)}=-\frac1{(1+x)^2}.$$
So $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\dfrac1{1+x}\right)=\mathbf{-\dfrac1{(1+x)^2}}$, valid for $x\ne-1$.

@s 13
Profit $\pi=R-C=(Q-3Q^2)-(Q^2-2Q)=3Q-4Q^2$.

First-order condition: $\dfrac{d\pi}{dQ}=3-8Q=0\Rightarrow Q=\dfrac38$. Second-order condition: $\dfrac{d^2\pi}{dQ^2}=-8<0$, so profit is a maximum.

At $Q=\dfrac38$: $\pi=3\cdot\dfrac38-4\cdot\dfrac9{64}=\dfrac98-\dfrac9{16}=\dfrac9{16}$. The maximum profit is **Rs. $\tfrac9{16}=0.5625$** (at $Q=0.375$).

@s 14

For the region to exist we need $6-3x\le x+6$, which forces $x\ge0$; then $y\ge6-3x\ge0$ as well (because $x\le2$). So $x,y\ge0$ come out automatically even though they are not printed.
