### Group A

@s 1
A statutory company is a company created by a special Act of Parliament (for example Nepal Electricity Authority, Nepal Rastra Bank), whose powers and duties are fixed by that Act.

@s 2
Two types of preference share: (i) cumulative and non-cumulative preference shares; (ii) redeemable and irredeemable (also participating and convertible) preference shares.

@s 3
Two items of fixed assets: land and building, and plant and machinery (also furniture, vehicles).

@s 4
Two limitations of financial accounting: (i) it records only the monetary transactions and gives the total cost, not the cost per unit; (ii) it does not give the cost control, wastage and efficiency information and is historical in nature.

@s 5
Fixed overhead is the indirect expense that remains constant in total within a period whatever the volume of production, for example factory rent, insurance and the salary of the manager.

@s 6
Centralized store means one common store for the whole organisation, in which all materials are received, kept and issued to the departments by one storekeeper.

@s 7
Two advantages of the time rate system: (i) it is simple to understand and to calculate, and a worker gets a fixed wage for the time worked; (ii) quality of work is good because the worker is not in a hurry to produce more.

@s 8
MS-Excel is a spreadsheet software of Microsoft. It works in rows and columns (cells) and is used in accounting to enter data, make calculations with formulas and functions, prepare ledgers, tables and charts.

@s 16
**What is cost (2).** Cost is the amount of expenditure, actual or notional, incurred on or attributable to a given thing (a product or service); it is the sacrifice in terms of money made to acquire goods or services. For example, the cost of a chair is the money spent on wood, labour and other expenses of making it.

**Classification on the basis of element (3).** Cost is divided into three elements:
1. **Material cost.** Cost of the materials used in the product. Direct material (wood in a chair) is traced to the product; indirect material (nails, glue, oil) is shared.
2. **Labour cost.** Wages paid to the workers. Direct labour (the carpenter making the chair) is traceable to the product; indirect labour (supervisor, storekeeper) is a part of overhead.
3. **Expenses.** All other costs: direct expenses (hire of a special machine, royalty for the product) and indirect expenses (rent, insurance, power, depreciation).

Direct material + direct labour + direct expenses = **prime cost**; all indirect material, labour and expenses together are the **overheads**, which are factory, office and administration, and selling and distribution.

@s 17
**(I) Material codification (2).** Material codification is the system of giving each item of material a symbol, number or letters (a code) according to its group, type and size, so that each material can be identified without confusion. It avoids duplicate names, makes the stores ledger, requisition and purchase work quick, and helps the use of the computer. For example, 1-01-05 may mean group 1 (steel), type 01 (rod), size 05 mm.

@s 19
**Meaning (2).** Accounting software is a computer program (for example Tally, QuickBooks, Peachtree) that records the transactions, posts the ledgers and prepares the trial balance, financial statements and reports automatically from the data entered once.

**Three advantages (3).**
1. **Speed and accuracy.** Calculations, posting and totalling are automatic, so the books and statements are prepared in seconds without arithmetical error.
2. **Easy storage, search and reports.** A very large number of vouchers is stored in little space, and any ledger or report (debtors list, stock, profit and loss) is produced at any date.
3. **Control and integration.** One entry updates all related books; passwords and audit trails protect the data, and the management gets timely information for decisions.
