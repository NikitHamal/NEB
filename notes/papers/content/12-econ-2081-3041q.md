### Group A

@s 1
Scarcity is the condition in which human wants are unlimited but the resources (land, labour, capital, time) that can satisfy them are limited and have alternative uses, so choice has to be made. It is the root problem of every economy.

@s 2
Total profit = TR − TC = (5Q² − 4Q + 10) − (4Q² − 6Q + 20) = **Q² + 2Q − 10**.

@s 3
Internal borrowing is the loan that a government raises from sources inside the country, such as treasury bills, development bonds, citizen saving bonds, the central bank and commercial banks, to finance a budget deficit. It is repaid in the domestic currency.

@s 4
Balance of trade records only the export and import of visible goods, while balance of payments records all economic transactions with the rest of the world (goods, services, transfers and capital movements). Hence balance of trade is only a part of balance of payments.

@s 5
Two measures to reduce poverty: (i) generating productive employment through agriculture, small industry and skill training; (ii) targeted programmes such as social security allowance, microfinance and free basic education and health for the poor.

@s 6
Two principles of the WTO: (i) non-discrimination (most-favoured-nation treatment and national treatment); (ii) freer and more predictable trade through the gradual lowering of tariffs and other trade barriers by negotiation. (Fair competition and special treatment for developing countries are also principles.)

@s 7
Two advantages of remittance in Nepal: (i) it is a large source of foreign exchange that finances the trade deficit and strengthens the balance of payments; (ii) it raises household income and consumption, which reduces poverty and pays for education, health and housing.

@s 8
Sustainable Development Goal 10, "Reduced Inequalities", aims to reduce inequality within and among countries.

@s 9
In the singular sense, statistics means a numerical measure or a single figure calculated from a sample or a set of observations, such as the mean, the standard deviation or the correlation coefficient. (In the plural sense it means the collection of numerical data.)

@s 10
Primary data are collected afresh by the investigator through surveys, questionnaires or interviews, which needs enumerators, travel, printing and time. Secondary data already exist in published form, so collecting them costs far less. (Any one such reason earns the mark.)

@s 11
The empirical relation is Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean, so
$$\text{Median} = \frac{\text{Mode} + 2\,\text{Mean}}{3} = \frac{25 + 2(22)}{3} = \frac{69}{3} = 23.$$
The median is **23**.

### Group B

@s 12
**Economic system adopted by Nepal (1 mark).** Nepal has adopted a **mixed economic system**, in which the private sector, the public sector and the cooperative sector work side by side. The constitution of Nepal (2072) names these three pillars of the national economy.

**Four features (4 marks).**
1. **Co-existence of three sectors.** Public, private and cooperative sectors all operate. The state owns electricity, water supply, the airline, the oil corporation and similar enterprises, while private firms run most industry, trade and services.
2. **Private property and freedom of choice.** Citizens may own property, start business and choose their occupation, and consumers choose what to buy, as in a market economy.
3. **Government regulation and planning.** The government guides the economy through periodic plans, the annual budget, monetary policy and regulatory bodies, and corrects market failures (for example it sets minimum wages and controls price of essentials).
4. **Role of the market together with welfare aims.** Prices are largely set by demand and supply, yet the state provides social security, public education and health, and tries to reduce poverty and inequality.
Other features: foreign aid and investment, and a large share of agriculture in employment.

**Or (Q12). Specialization (1 mark).** Specialization means that each person, firm, region or country concentrates on the work or production in which it is most efficient, and exchanges the surplus for what it does not produce. Division of labour is its commonest form.

**Four advantages (4 marks).**
1. **Higher productivity and output.** Repetition of one task raises speed and skill, so more is produced with the same resources.
2. **Lower cost of production.** Better use of machines and less wasted time reduce average cost and give the benefit of large scale.
3. **Better quality.** Workers who do one job repeatedly become experts and make fewer errors.
4. **Encourages invention and trade.** Simple repeated tasks are easy to mechanise, and specialisation creates the need for exchange between regions and countries, so trade expands (comparative advantage).

@s 13
**Ricardian theory of rent.** David Ricardo (1817) defined rent as *"that portion of the produce of the earth which is paid to the landlord for the use of the original and indestructible powers of the soil"*. According to him rent arises because land differs in fertility and is limited in supply, and it is a differential surplus.

**Assumptions.** (i) Land differs in fertility and situation; (ii) land is limited in supply; (iii) the law of diminishing returns operates; (iv) the market is perfectly competitive; (v) cultivation begins on the best land and then extends to inferior land as population grows; (vi) rent is determined only by the margin of cultivation.

**Explanation.** Suppose three grades of land A, B and C each receive the same dose of capital and labour. Grade A yields 40 quintals, B yields 30 and C yields 20. When only A is cultivated no rent is paid, since land is abundant. As demand for food rises, B is brought under the plough; the price is now set by the cost of producing on B, so A earns a surplus of 10. Later C is cultivated and becomes the **no-rent (marginal) land**: rent of A = 40 − 20 = 20 and rent of B = 30 − 20 = 10. Thus rent = produce of the given land − produce of the marginal land.

```figure caption="Ricardian rent: surplus of each grade over the no-rent marginal land (grade C)."
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(5.4,2.6))
vals=[('Grade A',40),('Grade B',30),('Grade C (marginal)',20)]
for i,(n,v) in enumerate(vals):
    ax.bar(i,20,color='#cfd8e6',ec=INK,lw=0.8,width=0.55)
    if v>20: ax.bar(i,v-20,bottom=20,color='#fbe3c8',ec=INK,lw=0.8,width=0.55)
    ax.text(i,v+1,'output '+str(v),ha='center',fontsize=7.5)
    if v>20: ax.text(i,20+(v-20)/2,'rent '+str(v-20),ha='center',va='center',fontsize=7.5,color=ACCENT)
ax.text(2,10,'cost / no rent',ha='center',va='center',fontsize=7)
ax.set_xticks(range(3)); ax.set_xticklabels([v[0] for v in vals],fontsize=7.5)
ax.set_ylabel('quintals per acre',fontsize=7.5); ax.set_ylim(0,48)
ax.tick_params(labelsize=7)
```

**Criticisms.** Rent also arises on the best land by scarcity; land does have some alternative uses; the "original and indestructible powers" are not truly indestructible; cultivation does not always start from the best land. Despite this, the theory explains well why rents differ between plots.
