### Group A

@s 1
Two uses of the internet: **communication** (email, messaging, video calls) and **information search and online business** (e-commerce, banking, education and entertainment).

@s 2
**Green marketing** is the marketing of products that are environmentally safe, and the use of environment-friendly production, packaging and promotion, so as to satisfy customers while protecting nature.

@s 3
**Salesmanship** is the skill or ability of persuading prospective customers to buy goods and services by satisfying their needs through personal selling.

@s 4
Two trade promotion tools: **trade discounts and allowances** to dealers, and **dealer contests with incentives** (also trade fairs, display materials and free goods).

@s 5
**E-commerce** is the buying and selling of goods and services, and the transfer of money and data, through the internet and electronic networks, for example online shopping and e-payment.

@s 6
The **concept of marketing** is that the success of an organisation depends on finding out customers' needs and wants and satisfying them more effectively than competitors, with profit as the reward (customer orientation, integrated marketing and profit through satisfaction).

@s 7
Two examples of accessory equipment: **office equipment** (computers, desks) and **hand tools or portable machines** such as drills.

@s 8
For medicine I would use **informative labels** (name, composition, dose, batch, dates and price) and **warning labels** on side effects and storage, along with a brand label, as required by law.

@s 9
Types of discount: **cash discount** (for prompt payment), **trade (functional) discount** for the middlemen, **quantity discount** for bulk purchase, and **seasonal discount** in the off-season.

@s 10
**Distribution** is the set of activities that make the goods and services available to the target customers at the right place, time and quantity, through channels and physical movement of goods.

@s 11
Four roles of salesmanship: (i) it **creates demand** and increases sales for the firm; (ii) it **informs and guides customers** to choose suitable goods; (iii) it **gives feedback** to the producer about the market; and (iv) it **provides employment** and raises the standard of living. It also builds goodwill.

### Group B

@s 12
**Installations** are the **major capital goods** of an industrial buyer, such as buildings, land, large machines, generators, mainframe computers and production lines. They are the important, costly equipment that directly affects the buyer's production and are not part of the finished product.

**Features:**
1. **High price and long life,** so they are bought rarely and are depreciated over many years.
2. **Negotiation and top-level decision.** Purchase decisions are taken by top management after long study and bargaining.
3. **Direct selling** by the producer's own technical salespersons, with few or no middlemen.
4. **Customised design.** They are often made to order to fit the buyer's needs.
5. **Services are vital:** installation, training, maintenance, spare parts and guarantee.
6. **Leasing and credit** are common, and promotion relies on personal selling and trade fairs more than on advertising.

@s 13
**Importance of pricing to consumers:**
1. **Measure of value and quality.** Consumers use the price to judge what they get, especially for new or unfamiliar goods.
2. **Decision on purchase.** The price decides whether a product is within the budget and which of the alternatives will be bought.
3. **Allocation of income.** It helps consumers spend limited income among many wants.
4. **Protection from exploitation.** Competitive and fair prices keep the cost of living low and give choice.
5. **Satisfaction and status.** A price that matches the benefits brings satisfaction, and a high price of luxury goods gives prestige.
6. **Savings and planning.** Stable prices allow consumers to plan spending and savings.
7. **Benefits of competition,** such as discounts, offers and better quality, flow from price rivalry.

@s 14
**Retailers** are the last link in the channel who sell goods in small quantities directly to final consumers. Their role in the distribution channel:
1. **Link with consumers.** They bring the product to the place where consumers live and shop.
2. **Breaking bulk.** They buy in large lots from wholesalers and sell in small units.
3. **Assortment.** They keep different brands and goods under one roof, so customers can choose.
4. **Storage and inventory.** They hold stock near the customers and so ensure immediate availability.
5. **Credit and service.** They give credit to regular customers, home delivery, repairs and after-sale service.
6. **Market information.** They convey customers' tastes, complaints and the competitors' moves to wholesalers and producers.
7. **Promotion.** Display, window decoration, demonstration and personal selling at the point of purchase, which make impulse sales.
8. **Risk bearing,** by taking the risk of price fall, damage and unsold goods.

@s 15
**Meaning.** Publicity is the non-paid, non-personal communication of news about a company, its products or its activities through the mass media, which the sponsor does not control. It is a part of public relations.

**Tools of publicity:**
1. **News releases (press releases)** sent to newspapers, radio, TV and online portals about new products or events.
2. **Press conferences** to announce major events and answer questions.
3. **Feature articles and interviews** of the organisation's leaders.
4. **Events and sponsorship,** such as sports, concerts, seminars, trade fairs and exhibitions.
5. **Social and community activities:** donations, blood donation camps, scholarships and tree plantation.
6. **Speeches and public talks** by the managers.
7. **Printed and audio-visual material:** annual reports, brochures, newsletters, films and the company website and social media.
8. **Celebrity and personality endorsement** and lobbying.

@s 16
A successful salesperson needs these qualities:
1. **Physical:** a neat, healthy and pleasing appearance, a good voice and confident manner.
2. **Mental:** intelligence, alertness, imagination, memory, and quick judgement to handle objections.
3. **Social:** courtesy, politeness, friendliness, cooperation and the ability to mix with people.
4. **Character:** honesty, integrity, loyalty and a sense of responsibility.
5. **Knowledge:** thorough knowledge of the product, the company, the competitors, customers and selling techniques.
6. **Communication skill** and the art of listening and persuasion.
7. **Enthusiasm, self-confidence and persistence:** the ability to face rejection and keep trying.
8. **Discipline and time management,** and willingness to learn and use technology.

@s 17
**Meaning.** Televised (television) marketing means promoting and selling products through television, using commercials, sponsored programmes, infomercials and home-shopping channels, which combine sight, sound and motion.

**Features:**
1. **Audio-visual appeal.** Sight, sound and movement make the message vivid and lasting.
2. **Wide reach.** It reaches a mass audience across the country at the same time.
3. **High cost** of airtime and production, though the cost per viewer is low.
4. **Emotional impact and demonstration** of the product in use.
5. **Limited message time,** and the message is brief and passes quickly, so repetition is needed.
6. **Selective by time and programme,** which lets advertisers target particular audiences.
7. **Less selective** than the internet in geography, and feedback is delayed.

**Or (Q17). Internet marketing.** Yes. Internet marketing is becoming popular because internet and smartphone use has spread widely; it is low in cost and reaches local and global customers at any time; it is interactive, with instant feedback and personalised offers; its results can be measured through clicks, views and sales; e-payment (eSewa, Khalti, banking apps) and home delivery make buying easy; and small firms can compete with large firms. Limits include security fears, weak internet literacy in rural areas and the lack of trust in online sellers, but the growth supports the statement.

@s 18
**Social marketing** is the use of marketing methods to change attitudes and behaviour of people for the benefit of society, for example campaigns for family planning, vaccination, tobacco control and cleanliness. Its functions are:
1. **Identify the social problem and the target group** through research.
2. **Set the social objective,** such as raising the immunisation rate.
3. **Develop the "product"** as the idea, behaviour or service being promoted.
4. **Set the "price,"** the cost (time, effort, money, social cost) for the adopter, and try to lower it.
5. **Make it available (place),** through health posts, schools and community centres.
6. **Promote it** through media, folk songs, street drama and volunteers, with simple and persuasive messages.
7. **Partnership and mobilisation** of government, NGOs, media and community leaders.
8. **Monitoring and evaluation** of behavioural change.

**Or (Q18). Ignoring social responsibility and goodwill.** Goodwill is the favourable image and trust of a company among its customers and the public. When a company ignores its social responsibility, it harms goodwill in these ways: (i) **selling harmful, adulterated or substandard goods** destroys the trust of customers, who stop buying; (ii) **false or misleading advertising and cheating** make people regard the firm as dishonest; (iii) **pollution and damage to the environment** raise public anger, protests and legal action; (iv) **exploiting workers** (low pay, unsafe conditions, child labour) attracts criticism of the media and the loss of good employees; (v) **hoarding, black-marketing and tax evasion** bring penalty and a bad name; (vi) **boycotts, fines and loss of licence** and loss of investor confidence follow. As a result, sales, profit and the long-term survival of the company suffer, and rebuilding goodwill takes years.

@s 19
**Salesmanship is an art.** An art is the skilful and creative use of knowledge and personal ability to achieve a desired result, and it is developed by practice. Salesmanship fits this definition:
1. **Personal skill.** Persuasion, voice, gestures and humour differ from person to person, and success depends on the individual's talent.
2. **Creativity.** A salesperson creates a unique approach and demonstration for each customer and finds new ways to close the sale.
3. **Understanding human behaviour.** He or she reads the customer's mood, needs and objections and adjusts the talk.
4. **Practice and experience.** Skill improves by repeated selling, as an artist improves by practice.
5. **Result-oriented.** The art is judged by the result: the sale and a satisfied customer.
6. **Emotional appeal and relationship building.** A good salesperson builds trust, not just a transaction.

Although it also uses scientific methods and knowledge, the personal skill and creativity are the heart of selling, so salesmanship is an art.

### Group C

@s 20
**Packaging** is the set of activities of designing and producing the container or wrapper for a product. It protects the goods and also promotes them. There are three levels: **primary package** (the immediate container, for example a bottle), **secondary package** (the carton that holds several units) and **shipping package** (the outer crate for transport).

**Importance of packaging:**
1. **Protection** against damage, spoilage, leakage, dust and theft in transport, storage and handling.
2. **Promotion.** An attractive package works as a "silent salesperson" and attracts attention on the shelf.
3. **Identification and differentiation.** The brand name, colour and design distinguish the product from rivals.
4. **Information.** The label gives contents, price, dates, directions for use and warnings.
5. **Convenience.** Easy to carry, open, store and use (small pouches, spray bottles, resealable bags).
6. **Hygiene and safety.** Sealed and tamper-proof packs for food and medicine.
7. **Higher price and profit.** Good packaging adds value, so a higher price can be justified.
8. **Easy handling and storage** and lower transport cost; and **reuse** value, for example jars.

**Qualities of good packaging:**
1. **Protective:** strong enough for the safety of the product.
2. **Attractive:** good colour, shape and design that suit the product and target group.
3. **Informative:** clear, truthful labels in a readable language.
4. **Convenient:** easy to handle, open and store.
5. **Economical:** low cost compared with the value of the goods.
6. **Environment-friendly:** recyclable and biodegradable materials.
7. **Distinctive and consistent** with the brand image, and **legal** with all the required details.
8. **Suitable in size and shape** for shelves and transport.

@s 21
**Channel structure** shows the number of levels of intermediaries between the producer and the final user.

**Consumer product channels** (goods bought by households for personal use):
1. **Producer to consumer** (direct): own outlets, door-to-door, mail order and online sale.
2. **Producer to retailer to consumer:** used by large retailers or when the producer sells in bulk to shopping malls.
3. **Producer to wholesaler to retailer to consumer:** the traditional channel for convenience goods such as soap, biscuits and noodles.
4. **Producer to agent to wholesaler to retailer to consumer:** used when the producer is small or the market is wide, where the agent finds the buyers.

**Industrial product channels** (goods bought by organisations for production or operation):
1. **Producer to industrial user** (direct): the main channel for heavy machinery, installations and large orders, because buyers are few and need technical service.
2. **Producer to industrial distributor to industrial user:** for standard supplies, tools, spare parts and small accessories.
3. **Producer to agent (manufacturer's representative) to industrial user:** used by small firms or for new markets.
4. **Producer to agent to industrial distributor to industrial user:** used for wide and scattered markets.

The main difference is that industrial channels are **shorter** because buyers are fewer, orders are large and technical, and direct selling with after-sale service is needed, while consumer channels are **longer** because buyers are many and scattered.

**Or (Q21). Agriculture marketing** is all the activities of moving farm products from the farm to the consumer, including grading, packing, storage, transport, processing, finance and selling, as well as supply of inputs.

**Prospects:** (i) most people depend on agriculture, which gives a large base of supply; (ii) growing urban and hotel demand and the food-processing industry; (iii) export potential in tea, coffee, cardamom, ginger, honey and herbs, and a diverse climate for off-season crops; (iv) government support through subsidy, cold storage, collection centres, cooperatives and agricultural strategy; (v) agro-processing and commercialisation, and improved roads, mobile and internet; and (vi) tourism and organic farming.

**Problems:** (i) **small, scattered holdings** and subsistence farming, with low surplus; (ii) **poor storage and cold chain,** so perishable crops are wasted; (iii) **weak roads and transport** in hills, with high cost; (iv) **middlemen's domination** and low price to farmers; (v) **lack of market information, grading and standards;** (vi) **inadequate credit** and inputs, and a low level of technology; (vii) **price fluctuation and import competition,** particularly from India; and (viii) **sanitary and quality barriers** to export and weak branding. Solving these through cooperatives, storage, market information, standards and price support will realise the prospects.

@s 22
**Personal selling** is the oral presentation of the product in a face-to-face conversation with one or more prospective buyers for the purpose of making sales. It is a two-way personal communication between the salesperson and the customer.

**Features:**
1. **Personal contact.** It is a face-to-face or voice-to-voice interaction.
2. **Two-way communication.** The message is adjusted at once to the customer's questions and reaction.
3. **Persuasion and demonstration.** The product is shown and explained and objections are answered.
4. **Relationship building.** It develops a long-term bond and loyalty.
5. **Immediate feedback** about the customer's attitude and the market.
6. **Selective targeting.** Only prospects with real interest are approached, which reduces waste.
7. **Higher cost per contact** than advertising, but a higher chance of closing the sale.
8. **Aims at action** (the order), not just awareness.

| Basis | Personal selling | Advertising |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Personal, face-to-face | Impersonal, through media |
| Communication | Two-way, with feedback | One-way, no immediate feedback |
| Audience | Few selected persons | Mass audience |
| Cost | High cost per customer | Low cost per person reached |
| Flexibility | Message adapts to each customer | Same fixed message for all |
| Payment | Salary or commission to salesperson | Payment to the media |
| Result | Immediate sale likely | Creates awareness and interest, effect is slow |
| Best use | Industrial and high-value products | Convenience and mass-market goods |
