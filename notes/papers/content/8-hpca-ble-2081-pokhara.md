@s 1

@s 3

### Group 'B' — Short Answers

@s 11
**Ovaries** are the pair of almond-shaped primary female reproductive organs (gonads) situated on either side of the uterus inside the lower pelvic cavity. They perform two essential functions: [[2]]
1. **Gametogenesis (Oogenesis):** They produce and release mature female sex cells (**ova / eggs**), usually one ovum every $28\text{ days}$ from puberty to menopause.
2. **Endocrine function:** They secrete the female sex hormones **oestrogen** and **progesterone**, which regulate secondary sexual characteristics, the menstrual cycle and pregnancy.

@s 12
Any two importances of a **school health screening test**: [[2]]
1. **Early identification of health problems:** Routine checks of eyesight, hearing, dental hygiene, height and weight detect refractive errors, ear infections or malnutrition at an early stage so parents and doctors can treat them promptly.
2. **Better academic performance and healthy habits:** Students who receive timely corrective care stay active, attend classes regularly and learn lifelong personal hygiene habits.

@s 13
Any two measures to prevent **goitre** (enlargement of the thyroid gland due to iodine deficiency): [[2]]
1. **Regular use of iodized salt:** Always use packet iodized salt (*two-child logo salt*) in daily meals, store it in a closed airtight container away from heat, and add it towards the end of cooking so iodine is not lost by evaporation.
2. **Consume iodine-rich foods:** Include natural sources of iodine such as dairy products, eggs, green leafy vegetables and fish in the diet, and avoid excessive raw goitrogenic foods.

@s 14
Two measures to stay safe on the **school playground**: [[2]]
1. **Perform warm-up exercises and wear proper sports attire:** Always warm up before playing, wear well-fitted sports shoes and protective gear, and follow the rules of the game and the teacher's instructions.
2. **Inspect the ground and play with discipline:** Ensure the playing field is free from stones, broken glass, slippery mud or damaged equipment, and avoid pushing, rough tackling or unsafe stunts.

@s 15
Any two importances of **Drill (*Kawaj*)** for students: [[2]]
1. **Develops discipline, alertness and teamwork:** Responding simultaneously to commands (*Attention, Stand at Ease, Quick March*) builds concentration, obedience, coordination and group unity.
2. **Improves physical posture and endurance:** Regular drill exercises strengthen muscles, correct body posture and build stamina and self-confidence.

@s 16
A **relay race** is a track-and-field team running event in which a team of **4 athletes** ($4\times100\text{ m}$ or $4\times400\text{ m}$) run equal preset distances one after another, passing a hollow rod from one runner to the next within a marked $20\text{ m}$ exchange zone. [[2]]
- **Main tool used:** **Baton** (a smooth, hollow tube of wood, metal or plastic, $28\text{–}30\text{ cm}$ long and weighing at least $50\text{ g}$).

@s 17
Any two advantages of practising **Halasana** (Plough Pose): [[2]]
1. **Improves spinal flexibility and posture:** It stretches the shoulders and vertebral column, relieving backache and stiffness in the neck and spine.
2. **Stimulates abdominal organs and the thyroid gland:** It massages the digestive organs to relieve indigestion and constipation, and regulates metabolism by stimulating the thyroid gland.

@s 18
A list of **eight technical terms** used in music: [[2]]
1. **Swara (Note):** Musical pitch (the seven pure notes *Sa, Re, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, Ni*).
2. **Saptak (Octave):** The series of seven successive swaras (*Mandra, Madhya* and *Taar Saptak*).
3. **Alankar (Palta):** Ordered ornamental patterns of swaras practised for voice and instrument training.
4. **Laya (Tempo):** The uniform speed or pace of music (*Vilambit* slow, *Madhya* medium, *Drut* fast).
5. **Taal (Rhythmic cycle):** A fixed cycle of beats divided into sections (*vibhags*), such as *Dadra, Rupak, Kaharwa* and *Jhyaure*.
6. **Matra (Beat):** The smallest unit of time measurement in a taal.
7. **Sam (X):** The first and most accented beat of a taal where the rhythm cycle begins and resolves.
8. **Khali (0):** The unaccented beat of a taal indicated by a silent wave of the hand.

@s 20
**Jhyaure dance** is one of the most popular lively folk dances of Nepal, performed across the hilly and mid-hill regions (especially in Gandaki and Lumbini provinces) by young men and women to the brisk $6\text{-beat}$ (*3 + 3*) rhythm of **Jhyaure Taal** played on the **Madal, Khaijadi, Bansuri and Mujura**. Starting at a gentle pace and building to a fast, energetic tempo with joyful footwork, hand gestures and expressive facial movements, it celebrates love, harvest, youth and festive gatherings. ### Group 'C' — Long Answers

@s 21
**"The condition of mental health in Nepal is worsening day by day."** Four major causes behind the rising mental health problems in Nepal are: [[4]]
1. **Unemployment, economic hardship and youth migration:** Lack of job opportunities at home forces millions of youths into foreign employment, causing financial stress, family separation, loneliness and depression among both migrants and families left behind.
2. **Excessive social-media use and digital addiction:** Unchecked screen time, cyberbullying, online comparison and disturbed sleep cycles among adolescents and youth are increasing anxiety, irritability and social isolation.
3. **Academic, family and social pressure:** High expectations in examinations, family disputes, domestic violence, substance abuse and rapid urban lifestyle changes create chronic psychological stress.
4. **Social stigma and lack of mental health services:** Mental illness is still widely misunderstood or hidden out of fear of social stigma, and trained counsellors and psychiatric services remain scarce outside major cities.

@s 22
Four major **modes of transmission of HIV / AIDS** (caused by the Human Immunodeficiency Virus): [[4]]
1. **Unprotected sexual contact:** Having unprotected sexual intercourse with an HIV-infected partner is the most common route of transmission.
2. **Transfusion of unscreened infected blood:** Receiving whole blood, plasma or organ transplants that have not been screened for HIV transmits the virus directly into the bloodstream.
3. **Sharing contaminated needles, syringes or sharp instruments:** Sharing unsterilized needles among intravenous drug users or using unsterilized tattooing, piercing or surgical instruments spreads HIV.
4. **Mother-to-child (vertical) transmission:** An HIV-positive mother can transmit the virus to her baby during pregnancy (across the placenta), during childbirth or through breastfeeding if not under antiretroviral medical care.

@s 23
The **menstrual cycle** is the natural, recurring monthly cycle of physiological changes in the ovaries and uterus of a reproductive-age female (starting at *menarche*, around age $11\text{–}14$, and repeating roughly every **$28\text{ days}$**) that prepares the uterus for a possible pregnancy. [[4]]

**Four stages of the menstrual cycle:**
1. **Menstrual (Bleeding) phase (Days 1 to 5):** If the ovum is not fertilized, the thickened inner lining of the uterus (*endometrium*) breaks down and is discharged through the vagina along with blood and mucus for $3\text{ to }5\text{ days}$.
2. **Follicular (Proliferative) phase (Days 6 to 13):** Under the influence of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and oestrogen, a primary ovarian follicle matures into a Graafian follicle and the uterine endometrium regenerates and thickens.
3. **Ovulatory phase (Around Day 14):** A surge of luteinizing hormone (LH) causes the mature Graafian follicle to rupture and release the mature ovum into the fallopian tube (**ovulation**).
4. **Luteal (Secretory) phase (Days 15 to 28):** The ruptured follicle turns into the *corpus luteum*, which secretes progesterone to maintain the thick, blood-rich uterine lining; if fertilization does not occur, the corpus luteum degenerates and the next cycle begins.

@s 24
*(All three alternatives answered.)* [[4]]

**Option 1 — Short method of playing the Volleyball game:**
1. **Court, teams and toss:** Volleyball is played on an $18\text{ m} \times 9\text{ m}$ rectangular court divided by a central net between two teams of **6 players** each. The game begins with a coin toss to decide service and court side.
2. **Service and rally:** The server stands behind the end line and hits the ball over the net into the opponent's court. The receiving team uses up to **three touches** — typically a **dig (forearm pass)** by the back row, a **set (overhead finger pass)** by the setter, and an **attack hit (spike)** — to send the ball back over the net.
3. **Scoring and rotation:** Under the **rally point system**, every rally awards $1\text{ point}$ to the winning side. When the receiving team wins the rally, its players rotate **one position clockwise** before serving.
4. **Winning a set and match:** The first team to reach **25 points** with at least a **2-point lead** wins the set, and the match is won by the first team to win **2 out of 3 sets** (or **3 out of 5 sets**, with the deciding set played to $15\text{ points}$).

**Or Option 2 — Four basic skills of Football and brief description of one:**
- **Four basic skills of football:** 1. **Kicking**, 2. **Dribbling**, 3. **Passing and Trapping (Ball control)**, and 4. **Heading** (plus *Tackling* and *Goalkeeping*).
- **Description of Dribbling:** Dribbling is the fundamental skill of moving and controlling the ball close to one's feet while running past defenders using gentle touches with the **inside, outside or instep** of both feet, keeping the head up to observe teammates and opponents.

**Or Option 3 — Four rules to be followed by the active chaser in Kho-Kho:**
1. **No crossing the central lane:** The active chaser must not touch or cross the central lane (*chaser line*) between the two posts while chasing.
2. **Direction of movement:** The active chaser must move only in the direction they first face and turn after taking a *Kho*; they cannot turn their shoulder back to reverse direction until they either go around a pole or give a legal *Kho*.
3. **Giving a proper Kho:** To pass the chase to a sitting teammate, the active chaser must touch the sitting chaser from behind on the back with the hand while clearly calling the word **"Kho"** aloud at the same time.
4. **Stepping out of the box only after Kho:** A sitting chaser becomes the new active chaser only after receiving a legal *Kho*, and the previous active chaser must immediately sit down in the vacated square facing the opposite cross-lane.
