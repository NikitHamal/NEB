@s 20
**(a) First-order linear equation with constant coefficient and constant term (3 marks).** The general form is $\dfrac{dy}{dx}+ay=b$ with constants $a,b$. Take the example
$$\frac{dy}{dx}+2y=6.$$
Here the integrating factor is $e^{\int 2\,dx}=e^{2x}$. Multiply through:
$$\frac{d}{dx}\left(y\,e^{2x}\right)=6e^{2x}\ \Rightarrow\ y\,e^{2x}=3e^{2x}+C.$$
$$\boxed{y=3+Ce^{-2x}}$$
Check: $y'=-2Ce^{-2x}$, so $y'+2y=-2Ce^{-2x}+6+2Ce^{-2x}=6$. In general, $y=\dfrac ba+Ce^{-ax}$, and the constant solution $y=b/a$ is the equilibrium level that all solutions approach when $a>0$.

**(b) $f'(x)$ is decreasing on $(-8,0)$ (3 marks).** A derivative is decreasing on an interval if its own derivative, $f''(x)$, is negative there.
Here $f(x)=\dfrac1x$, so $f'(x)=-\dfrac{1}{x^2}$, and differentiating once more gives $f''(x)=\dfrac{2}{x^3}$.

For every $x\in(-8,0)$, $x$ is negative, so $x^3<0$ and therefore $f''(x)=\dfrac{2}{x^3}<0$. Hence $f'(x)$ is decreasing on $(-8,0)$.

Numerical confirmation: $f'(-8)=-\tfrac{1}{64}\approx-0.016$, $f'(-4)=-\tfrac1{16}=-0.0625$, $f'(-1)=-1$ and $f'(x)\to-\infty$ as $x\to0^-$. The values keep falling as $x$ moves right. (Also $f'<0$ everywhere, so $f$ itself is decreasing, and $f''<0$ means the graph is concave down on this interval.)

**(c) Derivative and anti-derivative are inverse operations (2 marks).** If $F'(x)=f(x)$ then $F$ is an anti-derivative of $f$, written $\int f(x)\,dx=F(x)+C$. Differentiating undoes integrating, and integrating undoes differentiating (up to the constant $C$).

*Example.* Let $f(x)=x^2$.

- Integrate first: $\int x^2\,dx=\dfrac{x^3}{3}+C$. Differentiate the result: $\dfrac{d}{dx}\left(\dfrac{x^3}{3}+C\right)=x^2$, which returns $f(x)$.
- Differentiate first: $\dfrac{d}{dx}(x^3)=3x^2$. Integrate: $\int3x^2\,dx=x^3+C$, which returns $x^3$ up to a constant.

So $\dfrac{d}{dx}\int f(x)\,dx=f(x)$ and $\int f'(x)\,dx=f(x)+C$: the two operations are inverses of each other, the only loss being the constant $C$, which a derivative always removes.

**(a) Line of regression (3 + 3 marks).** $n=5$. Let $X$ = age (months) and $Y$ = weight (kg).

| $X$ | $Y$ | $XY$ | $X^2$ | $Y^2$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 5 | 5 | 1 | 25 |
| 2 | 7 | 14 | 4 | 49 |
| 3 | 8 | 24 | 9 | 64 |
| 5 | 10 | 50 | 25 | 100 |
| 6 | 12 | 72 | 36 | 144 |
| **17** | **42** | **165** | **75** | **382** |

$\bar X=3.4$, $\bar Y=8.4$.

*Weight at 4 months* needs the regression of $Y$ on $X$:
$$b_{yx}=\frac{n\sum XY-\sum X\sum Y}{n\sum X^2-(\sum X)^2}=\frac{5(165)-17(42)}{5(75)-17^2}=\frac{825-714}{375-289}=\frac{111}{86}=1.2907.$$
Substituting into $Y-\bar Y=b_{yx}(X-\bar X)$ gives the line $Y=4.0116+1.2907X$.
At $X=4$: $Y=4.0116+5.1628\approx\mathbf{9.17\ \text{kg}}$.

*Age at 6 kg* needs the regression of $X$ on $Y$ (the age is the variable being estimated):
$$b_{xy}=\frac{111}{n\sum Y^2-(\sum Y)^2}=\frac{111}{5(382)-42^2}=\frac{111}{1910-1764}=\frac{111}{146}=0.7603.$$
From $X-\bar X=b_{xy}(Y-\bar Y)$: $X=3.4+0.7603(Y-8.4)$. At $Y=6$ this gives $X=3.4-1.8247\approx\mathbf{1.58\ \text{months}}$.
Using the $Y$ on $X$ line backwards would give $X=(6-4.0116)/1.2907=1.54$, a slightly different value, because the two lines differ unless $r=\pm1$. Here $r=\sqrt{b_{yx}b_{xy}}=\sqrt{1.2907\times0.7603}=0.99$, so the lines are nearly the same.
