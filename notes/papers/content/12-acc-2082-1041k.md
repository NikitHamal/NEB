### Group A

@s 1
Two features of a public company: (i) it needs at least seven shareholders and has no upper limit on their number; (ii) its shares are freely transferable and it may invite the general public to subscribe to its shares and debentures.

@s 2
Authorized capital is the maximum amount of share capital that a company is permitted to issue, as stated in its Memorandum of Association (MOA). It is also called registered or nominal capital.

@s 3
Two items of assets: land and building, and machinery (also furniture, debtors, inventory, cash and bank balance).

@s 4
Two importance of cost accounting: (i) it helps to control and reduce cost by showing the wastage and inefficiency of materials, labour and overheads; (ii) it helps the management to fix the selling price of a product on the basis of its ascertained cost.

@s 5
On the basis of control, overheads are classified as (i) controllable overheads, which a manager can influence within a period (for example indirect material, supervision), and (ii) uncontrollable overheads, which he cannot influence (for example factory rent, depreciation).

@s 6
Decentralized store means a system in which each department, plant or branch keeps its own separate store and keeps its own record of receipt and issue, instead of one central store for the whole organisation.

@s 7
A time card is a record of the daily time of arrival and departure of a worker (time keeping). It is the basis for preparing the payroll and for computing the wages of a time-rate worker and the overtime.

@s 8
Two elements of a computer system in accounting: (i) hardware (computer, monitor, keyboard, printer) and (ii) software (accounting package such as Tally or Quickbooks). People, data and procedures are the other elements.

@s 16
**Meaning (2).** Cost accounting is the process of recording, classifying, allocating and summarising the costs of materials, labour and overheads of producing a product or providing a service, and of analysing them to ascertain the cost per unit and to help the management in cost control and decision making. It looks at the past cost as well as the planning of the future cost.

**Limitations (3).**
1. **Not a complete system.** It is an addition to financial accounting, not a substitute; it cannot be used alone to find the true financial position of the business, because it does not record the full transactions such as capital and loans.
2. **Costly and time consuming.** A separate cost department, staff, forms and records are needed. A small firm often cannot bear this expense, so the benefit may be less than the cost of the system.
3. **Based on estimates and assumptions.** Overheads are shared by apportionment and absorption on arbitrary bases, and the cost may differ between firms. The result is therefore not an exact figure and can mislead if the base is wrong.

Other limitations are that it has no uniform standard method, that it needs cooperation of all departments, and that its reports are of little use when they are not used by the management.

@s 17
**(I) Advantages of centralized purchase (2).**
1. **Lower price through bulk buying.** All the requirements of the departments are bought by one department in large quantity, so the firm gets quantity and trade discount and a better bargaining power over the suppliers.
2. **Better control and uniform quality.** One expert purchasing officer buys, so duplication, over-buying and wastage of funds are avoided and the same quality and standard of materials are kept for all departments.

**(II) FIFO under periodic inventory system (3).** Under FIFO the earliest units bought are assumed to be sold first, so the closing stock consists of the latest purchases.

@s 19
Importance of computer system in accounting (any five):
1. **Speed.** A computer processes a large number of vouchers, posts the ledgers and prepares the trial balance and final accounts within seconds, which saves a lot of time compared with manual books.
2. **Accuracy.** Once data is correctly fed, calculations such as depreciation, interest and totals are done without arithmetical mistakes. Posting and balancing errors of the manual system are removed.
3. **Storage and retrieval.** Thousands of records are stored in a small space (disk or cloud). Any old entry or ledger can be searched and retrieved in a moment.
4. **Up-to-date reports.** Financial statements, aging of debtors, stock and cash reports can be generated at any date, so the management gets timely information for decisions.
5. **Reduced cost and labour.** A few staff can do the work of many clerks, and the paper, stationery and storage cost is saved in the long run.
6. **Security and control.** Passwords, access levels and audit trail protect data from unauthorized change and fraud, and backup protects it from loss.
7. **Integration.** One entry updates sales, stock, receivable and the ledgers together, so the records stay consistent.
