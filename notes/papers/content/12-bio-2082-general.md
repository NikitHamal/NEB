@s 8
*Process:* **linkage and crossing over (recombination)**. Genes located on the same chromosome tend to be inherited together (linkage), giving parental combinations; crossing over between non-sister chromatids in prophase I of meiosis breaks the linkage and produces non-parental (recombinant) combinations. The closer the genes, the stronger the linkage.
*Example (Morgan's experiment on* Drosophila*):* a grey-bodied, long-winged female (BBVV) was crossed with a black-bodied, vestigial-winged male (bbvv). The F₁ were all grey, long. A test cross of the F₁ female with a bbvv male gave: grey long 965, black vestigial 944 (parental, 83%) and grey vestigial 206, black long 185 (recombinant, 17%). If the genes were independent the ratio would be 1:1:1:1; the excess of parental types shows linkage and the 17% recombinant types show crossing over (17% recombination frequency = 17 map units).

### Section I: Botany, Group C

@s 10
*Pigments:* chlorophyll *a* (blue-green, the primary pigment in the reaction centres P680 and P700) absorbs mainly red (about 680–700 nm) and blue-violet light; chlorophyll *b* (yellow-green) and the carotenoids (carotene, xanthophyll) are accessory pigments that absorb other wavelengths (green-blue, 450–500 nm), pass the energy to chlorophyll *a*, widen the range of light used, and protect chlorophyll from photo-oxidation. Together they form light-harvesting antenna complexes that funnel energy to the reaction centre.

*Non-cyclic photophosphorylation* (both photosystems, Z-scheme): light excites P680 of PS II; the electron goes through pheophytin, plastoquinone, the cytochrome b₆f complex and plastocyanin to PS I. The hole in P680 is filled by electrons from the photolysis of water (2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂). Light excites P700 of PS I; the electron goes through ferredoxin and NADP reductase to reduce NADP⁺ to **NADPH**. The proton gradient built across the thylakoid drives ATP synthase, giving **ATP**. Products: ATP + NADPH + O₂.
*Cyclic photophosphorylation* (PS I only): the excited electron of P700 goes to ferredoxin, then back to P700 through plastoquinone, cytochrome b₆f and plastocyanin. Only **ATP** is made; there is no photolysis of water, no O₂ and no NADPH.

@s 11
*Inheritance:* red-green colour blindness is controlled by a recessive gene $c$ on the **X chromosome** (the Y chromosome has no allele). Normal vision is $X^{C}$, colour blindness is $X^{c}$. A female is colour blind only if she is $X^{c}X^{c}$ (two copies); a male is colour blind with a single $X^{c}$ ($X^{c}Y$), so the condition is much commoner in males. A heterozygous female $X^{C}X^{c}$ is a normal-vision carrier.

*Crosses:*

| Cross | Parents | Gametes | Offspring | Phenotypes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Carrier female × normal male: $X^{C}X^{c}\times X^{C}Y$ | $X^{C}, X^{c}$ × $X^{C}, Y$ | $X^{C}X^{C}$, $X^{C}X^{c}$, $X^{C}Y$, $X^{c}Y$ | Daughters all normal (half carriers); sons 1 normal : 1 colour blind |
| 2 | Normal female × colour-blind male: $X^{C}X^{C}\times X^{c}Y$ | $X^{C}$ × $X^{c}, Y$ | $X^{C}X^{c}$, $X^{C}Y$ | All daughters normal carriers; all sons normal |
| 3 | Carrier female × colour-blind male: $X^{C}X^{c}\times X^{c}Y$ | $X^{C}, X^{c}$ × $X^{c}, Y$ | $X^{C}X^{c}$, $X^{c}X^{c}$, $X^{C}Y$, $X^{c}Y$ | 1 carrier : 1 colour-blind daughter; 1 normal : 1 colour-blind son (50% affected overall) |
| 4 | Colour-blind female × normal male: $X^{c}X^{c}\times X^{C}Y$ | $X^{c}$ × $X^{C}, Y$ | $X^{C}X^{c}$, $X^{c}Y$ | All daughters normal carriers; all sons colour blind |

*Criss-cross inheritance:* the gene passes from the father to his daughter (who is a carrier and appears normal) and then from her to her sons: from male to female to male (cross 2 then cross 1). In cross 4 the mother's trait appears in all sons, while her daughters are carriers. A man never passes the gene to his son (he gives the Y), and a son always gets his X from his mother. The trait therefore crosses over between the sexes in alternate generations.

**Or.** The three models were conservative, semi-conservative and dispersive. The biologically feasible model, shown by Meselson and Stahl (1958, using ¹⁵N/¹⁴N and density-gradient centrifugation: after one generation all DNA was of intermediate density, after two generations half intermediate and half light), is the **semi-conservative** model: each daughter DNA has one old (parental) strand and one newly made strand.

*Mechanism (S-phase):*
1. **Initiation:** replication starts at the origin; **helicase** unwinds and separates the strands (breaking hydrogen bonds) forming a replication fork; **single-strand binding proteins** keep the strands apart; **topoisomerase (gyrase)** relieves the supercoiling ahead of the fork.
2. **Priming:** **primase** synthesises a short RNA primer on each template.
3. **Elongation:** **DNA polymerase III** adds nucleotides to the 3′ end of the primer, always in the 5′→3′ direction. On the template 3′→5′ the **leading strand** is made continuously; on the other template the **lagging strand** is made discontinuously as short **Okazaki fragments**, each needing its own primer.
4. **Termination:** **DNA polymerase I** removes the RNA primers and fills the gaps with DNA, and **DNA ligase** seals the nicks to join the fragments.

@s 19
*Neurulation* is the formation of the neural tube (the future central nervous system) after gastrulation, in the late gastrula stage of the frog.
1. The ectoderm lying above the notochord thickens to form the **neural plate**.
2. The edges of the plate rise as the **neural folds**, with a **neural groove** between them.
3. The folds approach each other and **fuse** in the mid-dorsal line, closing the groove into a hollow **neural tube** with a cavity (neurocoel), which sinks below the surface ectoderm.
4. The anterior part of the tube enlarges into the brain vesicles, the rest forms the spinal cord. Cells at the crest of the folds form the **neural crest**, which gives sensory ganglia and other structures; the outer ectoderm closes over to form the epidermis.

*Causative agent* (1): the bacterium *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* (tubercle bacillus).
*Mode of transmission* (2): by droplet infection. An infected person releases droplets containing the bacilli when coughing, sneezing, talking or spitting, and others inhale them. *Female reproductive system* (4): it consists of a pair of ovaries, a pair of fallopian tubes, the uterus, the vagina and the external genitalia (vulva), with the mammary glands as accessory organs.
- **Ovaries:** almond-shaped organs on each side of the lower abdomen; they produce ova (eggs) and the hormones oestrogen and progesterone.
- **Fallopian tubes (oviducts):** about 10 cm long; each begins as a funnel, the infundibulum, with finger-like fimbriae that catch the ovum, followed by the ampulla (the usual site of fertilisation) and the narrow isthmus joining the uterus. Cilia and peristalsis move the ovum along.
- **Uterus (womb):** a pear-shaped muscular organ with fundus, body and cervix; its wall has perimetrium, myometrium (smooth muscle) and endometrium (lining where the embryo implants). It nourishes the embryo during pregnancy.
- **Vagina:** a muscular tube from the cervix to the outside; the birth canal and the organ that receives the sperm.

*Ovulation* (1): under the influence of FSH, a primary follicle grows into a mature Graafian follicle. A surge of LH about the 14th day of the cycle makes the follicle wall rupture and the secondary oocyte (arrested in metaphase II) is released from the ovary into the peritoneal cavity, from where it is caught by the fimbriae of the fallopian tube. The empty follicle turns into the corpus luteum, which secretes progesterone.

**Or.** *Structure of the nephron* (4): each nephron has a **renal (Malpighian) corpuscle**, made of the cup-shaped **Bowman's capsule** around a tuft of capillaries, the **glomerulus**, and a **renal tubule**: the proximal convoluted tubule (PCT), the loop of Henle (descending and ascending limbs), the distal convoluted tubule (DCT), which opens into a collecting duct.

*Function* (3): blood is filtered in the glomerulus (ultrafiltration); the PCT reabsorbs glucose, amino acids, most salts and about 65% of the water; the loop of Henle builds the concentration gradient of the medulla; the DCT and collecting duct adjust water and salt under the control of ADH and aldosterone, and tubular secretion adds H⁺, K⁺ and drugs; the collecting duct finally carries the urine to the renal pelvis.
*Insufficient ADH* (1): the DCT and collecting duct become less permeable to water, so much less water is reabsorbed. A large volume of dilute urine is passed (polyuria), causing dehydration and excessive thirst; this condition is called **diabetes insipidus**.
