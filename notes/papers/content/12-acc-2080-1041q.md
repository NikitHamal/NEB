### Group A

@s 1
Two contents of the memorandum of association: (i) the name clause (name of the company, ending with "Limited" for a public company); (ii) the objects clause, which states the purposes for which the company is formed. (The registered office, liability and capital clauses are the others.)

@s 2
Calls in advance means the money received from a shareholder on shares before it has been called up. It is a liability of the company (Calls in Advance A/c, credit) and is adjusted when the call falls due.

@s 3
Current liabilities are the obligations that the company expects to settle within one year or its normal operating cycle, for example creditors, bills payable, outstanding expenses and bank overdraft.

@s 4
Process costing is the method of cost accounting used where production is continuous and passes through a series of processes. The cost of each process is collected separately and the cost per unit is found by dividing the process cost by the output (for example in textiles, sugar and chemicals).

@s 5
Semi-variable overhead is the indirect cost that has a fixed part and a variable part, so it changes with output but not in the same proportion. Examples are telephone and electricity charges (fixed rent plus charge per unit used).

@s 6
Material control is the systematic control of the purchase, receipt, storage and issue of materials so that the required materials are available at the right time, in the right quantity and quality, at the lowest cost, with no wastage.

@s 7
In the piece rate system wages are paid according to the number of units (pieces) produced, at a fixed rate per unit: wages = units produced × rate per unit. The worker is therefore paid for output and not for time.

@s 8
Computer software is the set of programs and instructions that tells the computer hardware what to do. It is of two kinds: system software (operating system, utilities) and application software (accounting packages, word processors, spreadsheets).

@s 16
**Meaning.** Cost accounting is the branch of accounting that classifies, records, allocates and analyses the cost of products, jobs, processes or services, so that the cost per unit can be found and controlled, and the cost information can be given to management for pricing, planning and decision making.

**Three limitations**

1. **Not a statutory requirement for most firms.** The law makes financial accounting and audit compulsory, but cost accounting is optional for most businesses, so its records are often not kept uniformly.
2. **Based on estimates and allocations.** Overheads are apportioned on arbitrary bases (floor area, machine hours), so two accountants can arrive at different unit costs; the results are only as accurate as the assumptions.
3. **Costly and complex.** Keeping detailed records of material, labour and overhead needs trained staff, forms and software. For a small firm the expense can be more than the benefit.

(Other limitations: it has no uniform method, it is not an exact science, and it covers only cost, not the whole of the business such as capital and financing.)

@s 17
**(I) Advantages of centralized purchasing**

1. **Quantity discount and a lower price.** All the purchases of the organisation are placed together in bulk with the supplier, so a trade discount and better credit terms are obtained.
2. **Uniform quality and better control.** One expert purchasing department buys, so standard quality is maintained, duplication and over-stocking are avoided and the cost of purchase is controlled.

**(II) LIFO, periodic inventory system.** In the periodic system the closing inventory is valued at the end of the period, and under LIFO the units that remain are taken from the earliest purchases (the latest are assumed sold first).

| Particulars | Units | Rate (Rs.) | Amount (Rs.) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Opening inventory (1 Magh) | 300 | 5 | 1,500 |
| Purchase (15 Magh) | 500 | 6 | 3,000 |
| Purchase (25 Magh) | 400 | 7 | 2,800 |
| **Goods available for sale** | **1,200** | | **7,300** |

Closing units = 1,200 − 900 sold = 300 units. Under LIFO these are the oldest 300 units, i.e. the opening inventory: **closing inventory = 300 × 5 = Rs. 1,500**.

**Cost of goods sold = 7,300 − 1,500 = Rs. 5,800.** Check: the 900 units sold are the 400 at Rs. 7 (2,800) plus the 500 at Rs. 6 (3,000) = Rs. 5,800.

@s 19
A computerised accounting system (CAS) is a system in which accounting data is recorded, processed and reported with the help of computer hardware and accounting software such as Tally or QuickBooks. Its main features are:

1. **Speed.** Entries, ledger posting and the preparation of trial balance and statements are done in seconds, so reports are available whenever management wants them.
2. **Accuracy.** Arithmetic is done by the program, so errors of totalling and posting are removed; a debit entry must have an equal credit entry, which keeps the books balanced.
3. **Automatic posting and integration.** A voucher entered once updates the journal, ledger, trial balance, stock and the final accounts together, with no repetition of work.
4. **Large storage and easy retrieval.** Years of data are stored in a small space and any voucher or ledger can be searched and printed in a moment.
5. **Reports and analysis.** Financial statements, ageing of debtors, cash flow, ratio analysis and graphs are generated on demand; data can be exported to Excel or PDF.
6. **Security and control.** Passwords and user rights protect the data, and backup and audit-trail features guard against loss and unauthorised change.
7. **Reliability and integration with other modules** such as payroll, inventory and VAT, so one package serves the whole business. Its limits are the cost of software and training, dependence on power and virus risks.
