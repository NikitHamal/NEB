@s 15
**(a)** Marginal revenue is the derivative of total revenue: $MR=\dfrac{d(TR)}{dQ}$; conversely $TR=\int MR\,dQ$ (with $TR=0$ at $Q=0$).

**(b)** A *stationary point* of $f$ is a point $x=c$ in the domain where $f'(c)=0$ (the tangent is horizontal). It may be a local maximum, a local minimum or a point of inflection.

**(c)** A *monotonic function* is one that moves in one direction only on its domain: either $f'(x)\ge0$ for all $x$ (monotonically increasing) or $f'(x)\le0$ for all $x$ (monotonically decreasing). For example $f(x)=x^3$ is monotonic increasing.

**(d)** If $f'(x)=0$ and $f''(x)>0$ at the point, the curve is concave up there, so $f(x)$ has a **local (relative) minimum** value at that point.

**(e)** *Fundamental theorem of calculus:* if $f$ is continuous on $[a,b]$ and $F$ is an antiderivative of $f$ ($F'=f$), then
$$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a).$$
(The first part states that $\dfrac{d}{dx}\int_a^x f(t)\,dt=f(x)$.)

**(a) Consumer and producer surplus (4 marks).** At market equilibrium $P_d=P_s$:
$$40-x^2=3x^2+8x+8\ \Rightarrow\ 4x^2+8x-32=0\ \Rightarrow\ x^2+2x-8=0\ \Rightarrow\ (x+4)(x-2)=0.$$
Quantity cannot be negative, so $x_0=2$ and $P_0=40-4=36$. Equilibrium: **quantity 2 units, price Rs. 36**.

Consumer surplus (CS) is the gain to buyers who would have paid more than $P_0$:
$$CS=\int_0^{2}(40-x^2)\,dx-P_0x_0=\Bigl[40x-\tfrac{x^3}{3}\Bigr]_0^2-72=\Bigl(80-\tfrac83\Bigr)-72=\frac{16}{3}\approx5.33.$$
Producer surplus (PS) is the gain to sellers who would have accepted less than $P_0$:
$$PS=P_0x_0-\int_0^{2}(3x^2+8x+8)\,dx=72-\bigl[x^3+4x^2+8x\bigr]_0^2=72-(8+16+16)=32.$$
**Nature:** CS $=\tfrac{16}{3}\approx$ Rs. 5.33 and PS $=$ Rs. 32. Both are positive, as they must be in a competitive equilibrium. Producer surplus is about six times consumer surplus, because the supply curve starts at Rs. 8 (a long way below Rs. 36) while the demand curve falls only from Rs. 40 to Rs. 36 over the same range: sellers gain much more than buyers from trading at Rs. 36. Total surplus is $\tfrac{16}{3}+32=\tfrac{112}{3}\approx37.33$.

**(b) Homogeneous differential equation (2 marks).** A first-order, first-degree equation $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=f(x,y)$ is *homogeneous* if $f(x,y)$ can be written as a function of $y/x$ alone, i.e. $M\,dx+N\,dy=0$ where $M$ and $N$ are homogeneous functions of the same degree. Example:
$$(x+y)\,dx-x\,dy=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{x+y}{x}=1+\frac yx.$$
*Solution.* Put $y=vx$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=v+x\dfrac{dv}{dx}$. Then $v+x\dfrac{dv}{dx}=1+v\Rightarrow x\dfrac{dv}{dx}=1\Rightarrow dv=\dfrac{dx}{x}$. Integrating, $v=\ln x+C$, and replacing $v=y/x$,
$$\boxed{y=x\,(\ln x+C)}$$

**(c) Price elasticity, demand and total revenue (2 marks).** $TR=P\cdot Q$, so $MR=\dfrac{d(TR)}{dQ}=P\Bigl(1-\dfrac1{e}\Bigr)$ where $e=\Bigl|\dfrac{dQ}{dP}\cdot\dfrac PQ\Bigr|$ is the price elasticity of demand. The sign of $MR$ decides how a price change moves total revenue:

| Elasticity | $MR$ | If price falls | If price rises |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elastic, $e>1$ | $>0$ | $TR$ rises | $TR$ falls |
| Unit elastic, $e=1$ | $=0$ | $TR$ unchanged ($TR$ is at its maximum) | $TR$ unchanged |
| Inelastic, $e<1$ | $<0$ | $TR$ falls | $TR$ rises |

So elasticity tells a firm whether a price cut or a price rise will increase its revenue, and revenue is greatest at the point of unit elasticity. This justifies the statement.
