### Group A

@s 1
Styles of fried egg: **sunny side up** (fried on one side, yolk liquid), **over easy** (turned, yolk runny), **over medium** (yolk partly set), **over hard** (yolk fully cooked), and **basted** (hot fat spooned over the egg).

@s 2
Two fire safety measures: install **smoke detectors, sprinklers and fire extinguishers** at proper places; and keep **clear fire exits, alarm points and an evacuation plan** with regular staff training and fire drills.

@s 3
**A/V** stands for **Audio-Visual** (equipment such as a projector, screen, microphone and speakers used in an event).

@s 4
The **credit control section** (also called accounts receivable) follows up and collects the hotel's outstanding amounts from guests and companies.

@s 5
Two importance of event: it **promotes the hotel's image and brings in revenue** (food, beverage and room sales), and it **brings people together** for business, learning or celebration and creates employment for local workers.

@s 6
Silver is polished with **'Silvo'** (silver polish), or by the silver dip method; Brasso is used for brass and copper.

@s 7
Both **mirepoix and bouquet garni are aromatic flavouring bases**: vegetables (mirepoix: onion, carrot, celery) and herbs (bouquet garni: parsley stalks, thyme, bay leaf) are added to stocks, soups and sauces to give flavour and aroma, and are removed or strained out before service.

@s 8
**Salmonella** and **E. coli** are **harmful bacteria** found in raw meat, eggs, poultry and contaminated water or food. They cause **food-borne illness (food poisoning)** with vomiting, diarrhoea and fever.

@s 9
Béchamel sauce: **milk, white roux (butter and flour)**, an onion studded with clove and bay leaf (onion piqué), and seasoning of salt, pepper and nutmeg.

### Group B

@s 10
**Balanced diet** is a diet that contains all nutrients (carbohydrate, protein, fat, vitamins, minerals, water and fibre) in the right proportion to meet the body's needs.

**Needs and importance:**
- **Growth and repair.** Protein, calcium and minerals build and repair tissue, bone and teeth, especially in children.
- **Energy.** Carbohydrate and fat provide energy for work and body heat.
- **Protection from disease.** Vitamins and minerals strengthen immunity and prevent deficiency diseases (night blindness, rickets, anaemia).
- **Regulation.** Water, fibre and minerals regulate digestion, blood and body temperature.
- **Weight and health.** It prevents obesity, malnutrition, heart disease and diabetes, and improves productivity.
- **Hotel relevance.** Menu planning in a hotel offers a variety of balanced meals for health-conscious guests.

**Or (Q10). Causes of food poisoning.** Food poisoning is illness caused by eating contaminated food. The causes are: (1) **bacteria** such as Salmonella, E. coli, Staphylococcus and Clostridium, from raw or undercooked meat, eggs and milk; (2) **poor personal hygiene** of food handlers (unwashed hands, cuts, coughing over food); (3) **cross-contamination** between raw and cooked food, boards and knives; (4) **improper temperature control**: storing food in the danger zone (5 °C to 63 °C), inadequate cooking or slow cooling and re-heating; (5) **chemicals** such as pesticides, cleaning agents, and metals from poor utensils; (6) **natural toxins** in poisonous mushrooms, sprouted potato and some fish; (7) **pests** (flies, rats, cockroaches) and unclean water or surfaces.

@s 11
Hard floors (marble, tile, granite, terrazzo, vinyl) are cleaned daily to keep them safe and attractive.

| Item | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Equipment** | Broom, dust pan, dust-control (treated) mop, wet mop and bucket with wringer, squeegee, wet-floor sign, scrubbing machine, vacuum |
| **Agents** | Neutral detergent, floor cleaner, disinfectant, polish or sealer, water |

**Process:** (1) Place a **wet-floor sign** and remove furniture. (2) **Sweep or dust-mop** to remove loose dirt. (3) Prepare the **cleaning solution** in a bucket in the right dilution. (4) **Damp-mop** in an S pattern, moving backwards, rinsing the mop often. (5) Scrub stains and corners by hand or machine. (6) **Rinse** with clean water and squeegee or dry-mop. (7) Allow the floor to **dry**, apply polish if required, and replace the furniture. (8) Clean and store the equipment.

@s 12
An **occupied room** has a guest registered in it, so the room attendant must respect privacy.

**Steps:**
1. **Check the room status** on the work list and collect the cart and supplies.
2. **Knock** at the door and announce "Housekeeping" in a clear voice. Wait for a reply, repeat once.
3. If there is no answer, **open the door and announce again**, then enter. If the guest is in the room, ask politely if it is convenient or come back later; if the "Do Not Disturb" sign is on, do not enter.
4. **Leave the door open** and cart across the doorway while working.
5. **Open the curtains**, remove waste and used glasses, and **make the bed**. Do not disturb the guest's belongings.
6. **Clean the bathroom,** replace towels and amenities, then dust and vacuum the room.
7. **Replenish supplies,** check the minibar and lights, and report any damage or lost property.
8. **Leave the room neat,** close the door softly and update the status sheet.

@s 14
**(a) Coffee shop: counter or waiter (plate) service in a casual, quick style.** A coffee shop gives fast, informal meals for a wide menu with many guests, so the order is taken at the table, served on the plate and the bill is paid quickly. It is cheap and turns tables fast.

**(b) Cafeteria: self-service (counter and cafeteria style).** The guest takes a tray, selects ready food from the counter and pays at the cashier. It needs few staff, is fast and economical, suitable for staff and students at lunch hours.

**Or (Q14). Red wine versus white wine.**

| Basis | Red wine | White wine |
|---|---|---|
| Grapes | Black or red grapes | White (green) or red grapes without skin |
| Skin | Fermented with skins and seeds | Skins removed before fermenting |
| Colour | Ruby to deep red | Pale yellow to gold |
| Taste | Fuller, tannic, dry | Light, crisp, fruity, acidic |
| Serving temperature | Room temperature, 16–18 °C | Chilled, 8–12 °C |
| Food match | Red meat, cheese | Fish, chicken, salads |

### Group C

@s 15
**Definition.** **Soup** is a liquid food, served hot or cold, made by cooking meat, fish, poultry, vegetables or pulses in stock, water or milk, and usually served at the start of a meal to stimulate the appetite. A good soup is well flavoured, correct in consistency, served at the right temperature and neatly garnished.

**Types of soup.** Soups are classified by their consistency and the thickening used:

| Class | Type | Description and examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Clear soups** | Clear thin | Broth, bouillon, consommé: strained stock; clear, light. E.g. chicken consommé |
| | Clear thickened | Clear stock with vegetables and pasta or rice. E.g. minestrone, vegetable soup |
| **Thick soups** | Purée | Cooked vegetables, pulses or meat rubbed through a sieve; thickened by the starch of the main item. E.g. lentil, carrot, tomato soup |
| | Cream | Purée or velouté finished with cream or milk. E.g. cream of mushroom, cream of tomato |
| | Velouté | Stock thickened with white roux, finished with egg yolk and cream. E.g. velouté of chicken |
| | Bisque | Thick, rich shellfish soup. E.g. lobster bisque |
| | Chowder | Thick soup of fish, clam or vegetables with potato and milk. E.g. clam chowder |
| **Special soups** | Cold soups | Served chilled in summer. E.g. gazpacho, vichyssoise |
| | National soups | Famous local soups. E.g. minestrone (Italy), borscht (Russia), mulligatawny (India), thukpa and gundruk soup (Nepal) |

**Points of service.** Hot soup is served at about 85 °C in a heated soup plate or cup and cold soup is chilled; the portion is 200 to 250 ml with accompaniments such as croutons, bread roll or grated cheese. Clear soups use a bouillon cup and thick soups a soup plate on an underplate.

**Principles of soup making:** use good stock, cook slowly, skim the fat and foam, season only at the end, and keep the colour and consistency right. Hence soup is a light but nourishing starter that prepares the guest for the main course.

@s 16
**Hotel safety** protects guests, staff and property from accident, fire and illness; **security** protects them from theft, violence and unauthorised entry.

**A. Safety methods**
1. **Fire safety:** smoke and heat detectors, sprinklers, extinguishers (water, foam, CO2, dry powder), fire alarm, clear exits and assembly points, fire drills and an evacuation plan displayed on every floor.
2. **Accident prevention:** non-slip floors, wet-floor signs, good lighting, safe stairs, guard rails and trained handling of hot and sharp kitchen equipment.
3. **First aid and medical help:** first-aid boxes, trained staff and a link to a hospital and ambulance.
4. **Food and health safety:** hygiene (HACCP), clean water and pest control.
5. **Natural disaster plan** for earthquake and flood, such as safe open spaces and emergency lights.

**B. Security methods**
1. **Guest room security:** electronic card keys, door viewer and chain, safe deposit box, and controlled key issue and recording.
2. **CCTV and alarm systems** at entrances, corridors, lifts, parking and cash points.
3. **Security staff:** uniformed guards at gates, patrol rounds and bag checks.
4. **Access control:** registration of every guest, visitor log, and restricted staff and service entrances.
5. **Lost property and incident record,** and cooperation with the police.
6. **Staff training and key control:** screening of new staff, ID cards and training on handling suspicious persons and bomb threats.

Together these measures give guests a feeling of safety, protect the hotel's reputation and meet legal requirements.

**Or (Q16). Roux-based mother sauces.** **Roux** is a cooked mixture of equal weights of fat (butter) and flour, used to thicken sauces. The three roux-based mother sauces are:

1. **Béchamel** (white sauce): white roux and milk.
2. **Velouté:** blond roux and white stock (chicken, veal or fish).
3. **Espagnole** (brown sauce): brown roux and brown stock with browned mirepoix and tomato purée (**demi-glace** is made from it).

**Recipe and method of two sauces**

*(i) Béchamel (1 litre).* Ingredients: milk 1 litre, butter 50 g, flour 50 g, onion piqué (1 small onion, 1 clove, 1 bay leaf), salt, white pepper and nutmeg. Method: (1) heat the milk with the onion piqué, bring to a simmer and keep warm; (2) melt the butter in a heavy pan, add the flour and cook for 2 to 3 minutes without colour to make a **white roux**; (3) cool the roux slightly and add the hot milk slowly, stirring to avoid lumps; (4) simmer for 15 to 20 minutes; (5) season, strain through a fine sieve and cover with butter to prevent a skin. Uses: lasagne, macaroni cheese, gratin. Derivatives: Mornay (cheese), cream sauce.

*(ii) Velouté (1 litre).* Ingredients: white stock 1 litre, butter 50 g, flour 50 g, salt, white pepper. Method: (1) prepare a **blond roux** by cooking butter and flour for 4 to 5 minutes until sandy in colour; (2) cool, then whisk in the hot white stock gradually; (3) simmer for 30 to 40 minutes, skimming impurities; (4) season and strain through muslin. Uses: chicken and fish dishes. Derivatives: Allemande, Suprême, Normande.
