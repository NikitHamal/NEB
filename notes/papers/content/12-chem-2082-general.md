@s 19
*Rusting* is the slow corrosion of iron in the presence of moist air, forming reddish-brown hydrated ferric oxide, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O.
*Electrochemical theory:* impure iron with moisture, dissolved CO₂/O₂ forms tiny galvanic cells. At the **anode** (pure iron): Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻. At the **cathode** (impurity or strained region): O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O (or O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻). The Fe²⁺ ions react: Fe²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Fe(OH)₂, then 4Fe(OH)₂ + O₂ + 2H₂O → 4Fe(OH)₃, which loses water to give Fe₂O₃·xH₂O (rust).
*Prevention (any two):* painting or oiling the surface; galvanising (zinc coating); electroplating with Cr/Ni/Sn; cathodic protection with a sacrificial metal such as Mg or Zn.

**Or (a)** *Solubility product:* a salt is precipitated when the ionic product exceeds its $K_{sp}$. *Common ion effect:* an added common ion lowers the solubility and ionisation, so only the least soluble salts precipitate. In group analysis: Group I (Pb²⁺, Ag⁺, Hg₂²⁺) are precipitated as chlorides by dil. HCl since their $K_{sp}$ is exceeded.
