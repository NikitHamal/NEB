**(a)** The *binomial expansion* is the expansion of a power of a binomial (sum of two terms) as a sum of terms. For a positive integer $n$,
$$(a + b)^n = {}^nC_0a^n + {}^nC_1a^{n-1}b + {}^nC_2a^{n-2}b^2 + \dots + {}^nC_nb^n = \sum_{r=0}^{n}{}^nC_r\,a^{n-r}b^r$$
It has $n + 1$ terms and the general term is $T_{r+1} = {}^nC_r\,a^{n-r}b^r$.

**(b)** For $(2 + 3x)^{30}$, $n = 30$ is even, so there is one middle term, the $\left(\dfrac{30}{2} + 1\right)$th $= 16$th term, $r = 15$:
$$T_{16} = {}^{30}C_{15}\,2^{15}(3x)^{15} = {}^{30}C_{15}\,6^{15}x^{15}$$
With ${}^{30}C_{15} = 155\,117\,520$, the middle term is $155\,117\,520\times6^{15}x^{15}$.

**(c)** Putting $x = 1$ in $(1 + x)^n = {}^nC_0 + {}^nC_1x + {}^nC_2x^2 + \dots + {}^nC_nx^n$:
$$2^n = {}^nC_0 + {}^nC_1 + {}^nC_2 + \dots + {}^nC_n$$
So the sum of the binomial coefficients is $2^n$.

**(d)** For a complex number $z = x + iy$ plotted as the point $P(x, y)$ in the Argand plane, the *argument* (amplitude) is the angle $\theta$ that the line $OP$ makes with the positive real axis, measured anticlockwise: $\theta = \arg z = \tan^{-1}\dfrac{y}{x}$ (the quadrant of $P$ decides the value). The principal argument lies in $-\pi < \theta \le \pi$.

**(a)** Let $P(n)$: $1^2 + 2^2 + \dots + n^2 = \dfrac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$.

*Step 1 (basis).* For $n = 1$: LHS $= 1$ and RHS $= \dfrac{1\cdot2\cdot3}{6} = 1$. So $P(1)$ is true.

*Step 2 (hypothesis).* Assume $P(k)$ is true for some positive integer $k$:
$$1^2 + 2^2 + \dots + k^2 = \frac{k(k+1)(2k+1)}{6}$$

*Step 3 (induction step).* Add $(k+1)^2$ to both sides:
$$1^2 + \dots + k^2 + (k+1)^2 = \frac{k(k+1)(2k+1)}{6} + (k+1)^2 = \frac{(k+1)\left[k(2k+1) + 6(k+1)\right]}{6}$$
$$= \frac{(k+1)(2k^2 + 7k + 6)}{6} = \frac{(k+1)(k+2)(2k+3)}{6}$$
This is exactly $\dfrac{(k+1)\left[(k+1)+1\right]\left[2(k+1)+1\right]}{6}$, the statement $P(k+1)$.

*Conclusion.* $P(1)$ is true and $P(k)\Rightarrow P(k+1)$, so by the principle of mathematical induction $P(n)$ is true for all $n\in\mathbf{N}$.

**(a)** Let $h$ be the depth of water at time $t$. The rate at which the depth increases is proportional to $\sqrt{h}$:
$$\frac{dh}{dt} = k\sqrt{h}$$
where $k$ is the constant of proportionality, with $h = n$ at $t = 0$.

**(b)** Separate the variables in $\dfrac{dh}{dt} = k\sqrt{h}$:
$$\frac{dh}{\sqrt{h}} = k\,dt$$
Integrate: $\displaystyle\int h^{-1/2}dh = k\int dt\;\Rightarrow\;2\sqrt{h} = kt + C$.
Initially $t = 0$ and $h = n$: $2\sqrt{n} = C$. Therefore
$$2\sqrt{h} = kt + 2\sqrt{n}\;\Rightarrow\;\sqrt{h} = \frac{kt}{2} + \sqrt{n}$$

**(c)** Put $h = 16$ and $t = 6$ in the result:
$$\sqrt{16} = \frac{6k}{2} + \sqrt{n}\;\Rightarrow\;4 = 3k + \sqrt{n}\;\Rightarrow\;\sqrt{n} = 4 - 3k$$
Squaring, $n = (4 - 3k)^2$. ∎
