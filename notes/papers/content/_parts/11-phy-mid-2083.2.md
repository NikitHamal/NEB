**(a) Projectile motion (4 marks).** A body thrown into the air that moves under gravity alone (air resistance neglected) is a projectile, and its motion is projectile motion.

*Path is a parabola.* Let the body be projected from the origin with speed $u$ at angle $\theta$ above the horizontal. The horizontal component $u\cos\theta$ stays constant, and the vertical component $u\sin\theta$ changes with acceleration $-g$. At time $t$:

$$x = u\cos\theta\; t$$
$$t = \frac{x}{u\cos\theta}$$
$$y = u\sin\theta\; t - \tfrac12 g t^{2}$$

Eliminating $t$:

$$y = x\tan\theta - \frac{g}{2u^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}\,x^{2}$$

This has the form $y = bx - cx^{2}$ with constants $b$ and $c$, the equation of a parabola. So the trajectory is a parabola.

**(b) Numerical (4 marks).** $u = 20$ m/s, $\theta = 30^\circ$, $g = 10$ m/s$^2$.

$$T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g} = \frac{2\times20\times0.5}{10} = 2\ \text{s}$$
$$H = \frac{u^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{2g} = \frac{400\times0.25}{20} = 5\ \text{m}$$
$$R = \frac{u^{2}\sin2\theta}{g} = \frac{400\times0.866}{10} = 34.64\ \text{m}$$

Check: $R = u\cos\theta\times T = 17.32\times2 = 34.64$ m.

**(a) Conservation of mechanical energy (4 marks).** *Law:* when only conservative forces (such as gravity or a spring force) do work, the total mechanical energy, the sum of kinetic and potential energy, of a system remains constant.

*Verification for free fall.* A body of mass $m$ is released from rest at height $H$. At a point $P$ at height $h$ above the ground it has fallen $x = H - h$, so $v^{2} = 2gx = 2g(H-h)$.

At the start: $K = 0$, $U = mgH$, so $E = mgH$.

At $P$:

$$K = \tfrac12 mv^{2} = mg(H-h)$$
$$U = mgh$$
$$E_P = mg(H-h) + mgh = mgH$$

Just before the ground: $h = 0$, $K = mgH$, $U = 0$, so again $E = mgH$. The sum is the same at every height: potential energy lost equals kinetic energy gained.

**(b) Vertical circle (4 marks).** $m = 0.5$ kg, $r = 0.8$ m, $v_L = 7$ m/s, $g = 10$ m/s$^2$.

*At the lowest point* the tension acts upward and the weight downward, and their difference gives the centripetal force:

$$T_L - mg = \frac{mv_L^{2}}{r}$$
$$T_L = 5 + \frac{0.5\times49}{0.8} = 35.6\ \text{N}$$

*Speed at the highest point* by conservation of energy (height gained $2r = 1.6$ m):

$$v_H^{2} = v_L^{2} - 4gr = 49 - 32 = 17\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$$

*At the highest point* both the tension and the weight point down:

$$T_H + mg = \frac{mv_H^{2}}{r}$$
$$T_H = \frac{0.5\times17}{0.8} - 5 = 5.6\ \text{N}$$

Since $T_H > 0$ (and $v_H^{2} = 17 > gr = 8$), the string stays taut and the stone completes the circle. The tension is $35.6$ N at the bottom and $5.6$ N at the top.
