**Error identification and corrected paragraph** $(10\times 0.5=5)$ [[5]]

<div style="border:0.35mm solid #cfd5de; padding:2.5mm 4mm; margin:1.5mm 0 2mm; background:#fbfcff;">
<b>"<u>Y</u>ou</b> are a good <b>man<u>,</u>"</b> he <b>said<u>,</u></b> "and I want to help <b>you<u>.</u></b> <b><u>Y</u>ou</b> were kind to me when <b><u>I</u></b> was ill and <b>alone<u>.</u></b> The money is for you and your family. <b><u>T</u>ake</b> <b>it<u>.</u>"</b>
</div>

| # | Printed error | Correction | Rule applied |
| :-: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | `"you` | **`"You`** | Capitalise the first word of a direct quotation. |
| 2 | `man"` | **`man,"`** | Put a comma before closing the first part of the quote. |
| 3 | `he said "` | **`he said, "`** | Place a comma after the reporting verb `said`. |
| 4 | `help you you` | **`help you.`** | End the first complete sentence with a full stop. |
| 5 | `you were kind` | **`You were kind`** | Begin the new sentence after the full stop with a capital `Y`. |
| 6 | `when i was` | **`when I was`** | The first-person singular pronoun **`I`** is always capitalised. |
| 7 | `and alone The` | **`and alone. The`** | End the clause with a full stop before `The money...`. |
| 8 | `family. take` | **`family. Take`** | Capitalise `Take` at the start of the imperative sentence. |
| 9 | `take it"` | **`Take it."`** | Put a full stop before the closing quotation mark. |
| 10 | Split speech | **`"..." he said, "..."`** | Keep both halves of the split direct speech properly punctuated. |

**Interpretation of the pie-chart: Crop Production of Dang in 2071** [[5]]

<p><b>Crop Production of Dang District in 2071 BS</b></p>
<p>The given pie-chart illustrates the percentage distribution of five major crops—Mustard, Paddy, Wheat, Maize, and Barley—produced in Dang district in the year 2071 BS.</p>
<p>According to the chart, <b>mustard</b> accounts for the largest share of agricultural output at <b>30%</b> (nearly one-third of total production). <b>Paddy</b> is the second largest crop, making up <b>25%</b> (exactly one-fourth) of the district's total crop yield, while <b>wheat</b> ranks third with <b>20%</b> (one-fifth). Together, mustard, paddy, and wheat represent three-fourths (<b>75%</b>) of Dang's harvest. Meanwhile, <b>maize</b> contributes <b>15%</b>, and <b>barley</b> occupies the smallest share at just <b>10%</b> of total production.</p>
<p>In conclusion, oilseeds (mustard) and staple food grains (paddy and wheat) dominated crop production in Dang in 2071 BS, whereas barley was cultivated the least.</p>

**Essay: Advantages and Disadvantages of Using Mobile Phones** [[10]]

**Advantages and Disadvantages of Using Mobile Phones**

A mobile phone is one of the most influential inventions of modern science and technology; today it has evolved from a simple calling device into a pocket-sized computer used by students, professionals, and homemakers alike, bringing both remarkable advantages and serious disadvantages.

On the positive side, mobile phones have made communication instant, affordable, and borderless through voice calls, text messages, and video calls with family and friends anywhere in the world. For students, smartphones serve as portable libraries providing access to online classes, educational videos, digital textbooks, and dictionaries, while also helping during emergencies, digital banking, and GPS navigation.

On the other hand, excessive and careless use of mobile phones causes several problems: many students waste valuable study hours on social media and online games, leading to poor academic results, while prolonged screen time causes eye strain, headaches, sleep disturbances, physical inactivity, and exposure to cyberbullying or online scams.

In conclusion, a mobile phone is a wonderful servant but a dangerous master; used with self-discipline and a clear purpose, it offers immense educational and practical benefits without harming our health or studies.

**Sentence transformation (Grammar)** $(5\times 0.5=2.5)$ [[2.5]]

- **(a)** She cut her fingers, **<u>didn't she</u>**? [[0.5]] *(With singular "She", present would be "cuts", so "cut" is simple past $\rightarrow$ `didn't she?`)*
- **(b)** **<u>Sulav did not (didn't) buy any apples yesterday.</u>** [[0.5]] *(Simple past `bought` $\rightarrow$ `did not buy`, and `some` $\rightarrow$ `any`.)*
- **(c)** **<u>Many essays were written by Devkota.</u>** [[0.5]] *(Active past `wrote` + plural object `many essays` $\rightarrow$ passive `were written`.)*
- **(d)** Don't make noise! The baby **<u>is sleeping</u>**. [[0.5]] *(`Don't make noise!` signals an action happening right now $\rightarrow$ present continuous.)*
- **(e)** **<u>The teacher said that the sun rises in the east.</u>** [[0.5]] *(Universal truth keeps present simple `rises` in indirect speech.)*

| Blank | Correct Answer | Grammar Rule |
| :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **(a)** | **the** | Superlative adjective (*"most important"*) takes the definite article **`the`**. |
| **(b)** | **both** | Correlative conjunction **`both ... and`** pairs *"physically and mentally"*. |
| **(c)** | **to** | The verb *"help"* takes the to-infinitive **`to keep`**. |
| **(d)** | **look** | Plural subject (*"the players"*) with *"always"* takes the plural base verb **`look`**. |
| **(e)** | **don't** | Present conditional (*"we become ... and can't"*) takes present simple negative **`don't`**. |
