# PART: Botany
## 1. Biomolecules and Cell Biology [15]
- Biomolecules: carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, nucleic acids, minerals, enzymes and water
- Cell: prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells; cell wall, cell membrane, mitochondria, plastids, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, ribosomes, nucleus, chromosomes, cilia, flagella and cell inclusions
- Cell division: cell cycle, amitosis, mitosis and meiosis
## 2. Floral Diversity [30]
- Introduction: three domains of life, binomial nomenclature, five-kingdom classification and flora status of Nepal
- Fungi: general characters, mycelium, nutrition, reproduction, classification (Zygomycetes, Ascomycetes, Basidiomycetes, Deuteromycetes), *Mucor*/*Rhizopus*, *Yeast*, *Agaricus* and economic importance
- Lichen: symbiotic nature, crustose, foliose and fruticose growth forms, internal structure, reproduction and ecological/economic importance
- Algae: Chlorophyceae, Phaeophyceae and Rhodophyceae; *Spirogyra* filament structure, conjugation and economic importance
- Bryophyta: general characters, alternation of generations, *Marchantia* and *Funaria* morphology, gametophyte, sporophyte and economic importance
- Pteridophyta: general characters, homospory and heterospory, *Dryopteris* (fern) sporophyte, sorus, prothallus, life cycle and economic importance
- Gymnosperm: general characters, *Pinus* morphology, coralloid and mycorrhizal roots, male and female cones, pollination and economic importance
- Angiosperm: morphology of root, stem, leaf, inflorescence, flower and fruit; taxonomic hierarchy; artificial, natural and phylogenetic classification; Brassicaceae, Fabaceae, Solanaceae and Liliaceae
## 3. Introductory Microbiology [5]
- Monera: general characters, bacterial cell structure, shapes, nutrition, reproduction, bacterial growth, Cyanobacteria (*Nostoc*) and economic importance of bacteria
- Virus: living and non-living characters, bacteriophage and plant/animal virus structure, lytic and lysogenic cycles, viral diseases
- Impacts of biotechnology in microbiology: fermentation, antibiotics, vaccines, biofertilizers and bioremediation
## 4. Ecology [11]
- Ecosystem ecology: biotic and abiotic components, pond and forest ecosystems, food chain, food web, trophic levels, 10% energy flow law, ecological pyramids, biogeochemical cycles (carbon and nitrogen) and ecological succession
- Ecological adaptation: hydrophytes, xerophytes, mesophytes and halophytes with morphological, anatomical and physiological adaptations
- Ecological imbalances: greenhouse effect, global warming, climate change, ozone layer depletion, acid rain and deforestation
## 5. Vegetation [3]
- Vegetation: factors affecting vegetation, tropical to alpine vegetation zones of Nepal, flora diversity of Nepal, in-situ (national parks, wildlife reserves, conservation areas) and ex-situ conservation
- Natural environment, vegetation and human activities: human dependence on vegetation, impacts of deforestation/overgrazing/developmental activities, and community forestry restoration
# PART: Zoology
## 6. Introduction to Biology [1]
- Introduction to Biology: characteristics of life, branches and scope of biology, relation with other sciences and career fields
## 7. Evolutionary Biology [15]
- Life and its Origin: special creation, spontaneous generation, biogenesis, Oparin–Haldane chemical evolution, Miller–Urey experiment and geological time scale
- Evidences of evolution: homologous, analogous and vestigial organs, atavism, connecting links (*Archaeopteryx*), embryological, palaeontological, biogeographical and biochemical evidences
- Theories of evolution: Lamarckism, Darwinism (natural selection), mutation theory, Neo-Darwinism, Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium, genetic drift and speciation
- Human evolution: primate characters, Miocene apes (*Dryopithecus*, *Ramapithecus*), *Australopithecus*, *Homo habilis*, *Homo erectus*, Neanderthal, Cro-Magnon and modern *Homo sapiens*
## 8. Faunal Diversity [34]
- Protista: classification of Protista, *Paramecium caudatum* (structure, locomotion, nutrition, osmoregulation, binary fission and conjugation) and *Plasmodium vivax* (schizogony and sporogony life cycle in human and female *Anopheles*)
- Animalia: levels of organization, body plan, symmetry, germ layers, coelom, segmentation and diagnostic characters with classification of nine phyla (Porifera to Chordata)
- Earthworm (*Pheretima posthuma*): habit and habitat, external morphology, body wall, coelom, locomotion, digestive, blood vascular, respiratory, excretory, nervous, sense organs, reproductive system and economic importance
- Frog (*Rana tigrina*): habit and habitat, external morphology, digestive, respiratory, blood vascular, portal systems, excretory, nervous, sense organs, reproductive system, metamorphosis and economic importance
## 9. Biota and Environment [10]
- Animal adaptation: aquatic (primary and secondary), terrestrial (cursorial, arboreal, fossorial, volant), desert (xeric) and high-altitude/cold adaptations
- Animal behavior: innate (taxis, reflexes, instincts) and learned behavior, honeybee communication dance, social behavior, biological rhythms, fish and bird migration, and interspecific interactions
- Environmental Pollution: sources, effects and control of air, water, soil, noise and radioactive pollution; eutrophication, BOD, bioaccumulation and biomagnification
## 10. Conservation Biology [4]
- Conservation Biology: genetic, species and ecosystem biodiversity, Simpson and Shannon diversity indices, HIPPO threats to biodiversity, in-situ and ex-situ conservation and protected areas of Nepal
- Wildlife: IUCN Red List categories, protected and endangered mammals, birds and reptiles of Nepal, CITES, and wildlife conservation strategies in Nepal
