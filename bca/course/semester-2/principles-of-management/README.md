# Principles of Management — BCA 156 (Semester II, 1 credit)

The functions of management — planning, organising, leading, controlling — plus the evolution of management thought, the business environment, quality and the Nepali context. Written to be studied end to end; a 1-credit subject, so shorter than the 3-credit books.

| Unit | Title |
|---|---|
| 1 | Organisations and Introduction to Management |
| 2 | Evolution of Management Thought and the Environment |
| 3 | Planning and Decision Making |
| 4 | Organising |
| 5 | Leading: Leadership, Motivation and Communication |
| 6 | Controlling, Quality and the Global Context |
| 7 | IT in Management, Forms of Business, Job Design and Leadership Approaches (revised-outline topics) |

## Verification

`bca/tools/check_management.py` recomputes every number in the book: break-even and target-profit units, expected monetary value, the five decision criteria under uncertainty (maximax, maximin, Laplace, Hurwicz, minimax regret), expected value of perfect information, decision-tree expected values, critical path and PERT times, Graicunas relationships, managers needed for a given span, expectancy motivation, budget variance, control limits, DPMO, EOQ and Pareto percentages. It caught two of the author's own slips (EVPI and EOQ), which were corrected before the text was written.

## Source and honesty

**The revised BCA 156 syllabus itself was not retrievable, and it is a 1-credit subject.** The unit structure is built from the published older TU BCA course "Introduction to Management" (CAMG304; the same topics appear in the TU "Principles of Management 156" listing on collegenote.net: nature of organisations, introduction to management, evolution of management thought, environmental context, planning and decision making, organising, leadership and conflict, motivation, communication, control and quality management, global context, management trends and scenario in Nepal), cross-checked against the TU BIM Principles of Management outline (benchpartner.com). Those twelve topics are grouped here into six units. If your college's 1-credit syllabus is shorter, treat Units 1, 3, 4, 5 and the control section of Unit 6 as the core and the rest as background. A page fetched from collegenote.net during this work returned unrelated spam, so only the search-result excerpt of it was used.
