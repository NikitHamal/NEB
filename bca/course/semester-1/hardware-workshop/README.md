# Hardware Workshop — BCA 106

Semester I · 1 credit · practical subject. This book is the written companion to the lab work and follows the **nine-unit** TU syllabus (unit titles from the published course outline, mirrored on hamronotes.com).

| Unit | Title | What it adds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction to Hardware (Electronic Components) | Ohm's law, resistors, capacitors, diodes, transistors, ICs, datasheets, testing |
| 2 | Introduction to Networking Components | Modem, NIC, hub, switch, router, AP, UTP/coax/fibre, T568A/B, crimping, testing |
| 3 | Desktop and Laptop Hardware Identification and Handling | Safety, toolkit, components, RAM and ROM types, buses, storage interfaces, I/O, PSU testing |
| 4 | PC and Laptop Assembly | Step-by-step build, POST and beep codes, BIOS/UEFI, partitions, OS installation, drivers, printer install, commands |
| 5 | Basic Network Devices and Deployment | Device identification, topologies, file/folder/printer sharing, printer technologies and maintenance |
| 6 | Basic Networking Configuration | IP classes, subnetting, router/switch/AP configuration, ping, tracert, ipconfig, netstat, arp, nslookup, netsh |
| 7 | Problem Solving Techniques | Identify-Test-Isolate-Fix-Verify, visual inspection, elimination, troubleshooting log |
| 8 | Maintenance and Upgrade | Upgrade planning, RAM/SSD/GPU/CPU, cleaning, thermal paste, battery, BIOS flashing, SMART |
| 9 | Final Practical Assessment | Five stations, checklists, sample marking scheme, 40 viva questions |

## Verification

Bandwidth, capacity, PSU/UPS, battery, wattage, cost-per-page and link-count figures were recomputed before publishing, and every subnetting example was checked with Python's `ipaddress` module. The procedures follow vendor manuals and the CompTIA A+ troubleshooting method; nothing here has been bench-tested by a TU technician.

## Source and honesty

Units 1 to 9 follow the published syllabus topic lists; session 19 replaced the earlier reconstructed four-unit outline. The syllabus does not state hours per unit and the marking scheme in Unit 9 is a practice model, not the official split. Beep codes, header layouts and menu names differ between manufacturers: always follow your lab's safety rules and the component manuals.
