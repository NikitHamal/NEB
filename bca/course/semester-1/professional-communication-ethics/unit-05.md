Most professional communication today is digital: email, chat, video calls, shared documents, social media posts, dashboards and slide decks. Digital messages travel fast, last long, can be copied without limit and are read by people you never meet. This unit covers virtual etiquette (netiquette), online professionalism, communication through social media and collaborative tools, plagiarism and online plagiarism, and the use of visuals (maps, tables, charts, infographics, icons, photographs and diagrams). It ends with vocabulary and grammar (social media vocabulary; prepositions of time, place and direction) and two prescribed readings: "Cat Pictures Please" by Naomi Kritzer and "ChatGPT May Be Eroding Critical Thinking Skills, According to a New MIT Study".

## 5.1 Virtual etiquette (netiquette)

**Netiquette** (network etiquette) is the set of accepted rules of polite and sensible behaviour in online communication. Virginia Shea's classic *Core Rules of Netiquette* (1994) begins with "Remember the human": behind every screen is a person who can be hurt, who may read your words at a bad moment, and who cannot see your face.

### Why netiquette matters

Online messages lack tone of voice and body language, so jokes read as insults and short replies as anger. Digital messages are also **permanent** (screenshots, backups), **searchable**, **forwardable** and **public by default**. A careless line can end a job offer, an internship or a friendship.

### Core rules

1. **Remember the human.** Write what you would say face to face to that person.
2. **Be clear and concise.** A meaningful subject line, short paragraphs, the action required and the deadline in the first lines.
3. **Use the right channel.** Quick question: chat. Formal request or record: email. Sensitive or emotional: call or meet. Complex discussion: meeting.
4. **Mind your tone.** Do not write in ALL CAPITALS (shouting); avoid sarcasm; use emojis sparingly and only when the relationship allows; always greet and sign off.
5. **Respond promptly,** and say when you will reply if you need time ("I will answer by Thursday").
6. **Respect privacy.** Do not forward private emails or screenshots without permission; use **Bcc** for large lists so that addresses are not exposed; do not add people to groups without asking.
7. **Do not spam or "reply all"** unless everyone needs the answer.
8. **Proofread** before you send: names, dates, attachments and links. Misspelling a client's name is an avoidable insult.
9. **Share only what is true.** Check facts before posting or forwarding; do not spread rumours or fake news.
10. **Respect copyright and give credit** (see 5.4).
11. **Be forgiving of others' mistakes.** Correct privately and politely; do not mock in public.
12. **Do not use workplace systems for private business** or inappropriate content.

### Email etiquette

- Subject line states the topic and, if needed, the action: *Action by 5 pm Friday: BCA Project I proposal*.
- Salutation: "Dear Mr Sharma" (formal), "Hello Sita" (colleague); closing: "Regards" or "Yours sincerely".
- One topic per email; use bullet points for lists; attach files and mention them in the body.
- Use **To** for those who must act, **Cc** for those who should know, **Bcc** for hidden recipients.
- Add an **out-of-office reply** when away; use a professional signature (name, position, phone).
- Do not email in anger; wait a day for strongly emotional replies.

### Chat and messaging etiquette

Start with the real message ("Hi Ram, the build failed on step 3; log attached"), not just "Hi"; avoid late-night messages unless urgent; use threads; mute channels, not people; and never type confidential data (passwords, citizenship numbers) in chat.

### Video-meeting etiquette

Test audio and camera in advance; join two minutes early; **mute when not speaking**; keep the camera on when bandwidth permits; use a plain, well-lit background; dress as for a face-to-face meeting; look at the camera; do not multitask visibly; use the "raise hand" feature; share the agenda and record minutes; and end on time. For a long power cut or poor connection, say so early and offer to switch to audio.

::: example Worked example: improving a chat message
*Poor:* "hi" ... "r u there" ... "need the file" ... "??".
*Better:* "Hi Sita, could you please send me the latest ER diagram when you have a moment? I need it for the 3 pm review. Thanks!"
The better message greets, states the request, the reason and the deadline in a single message, so Sita can act without a second exchange.
:::

## 5.2 Online professionalism

**Online professionalism** means behaving on the internet in a way that protects your reputation and the reputation of your institution or employer. Employers, universities and clients routinely search names before hiring or admitting; your **digital footprint** (everything you post, like, comment on, and everything others post about you) is a CV you did not write.

### Active and passive digital footprint

- **Active:** posts, comments, uploads, profile fields, reviews, forum answers.
- **Passive:** location data, cookies, browsing history, tags by others, app data collected without your effort.

### Building a professional online presence

1. **Use a professional email address** (name-based, not "cutiepie2060@..."), a clear photograph, and a consistent name across platforms.
2. **Create a LinkedIn profile** (headline, summary, projects, skills, education) and a **GitHub** profile with clean, documented repositories.
3. **Separate personal and professional accounts;** use privacy settings; remember that "private" posts can be screenshotted.
4. **Search yourself** on Google every few months and remove or fix harmful results.
5. **Think before you post:** the "newspaper test" (would I be comfortable seeing this on the front page of a newspaper with my name?).
6. **Be accurate and credible:** do not exaggerate skills, claim fake certificates or share unverified news.
7. **Keep emotions out of public disputes.** Complain through the proper channel, not on a public wall.
8. **Protect confidential information:** never post client data, internal documents, unreleased products, exam papers or screenshots of private chats.
9. **Respect others' consent** before tagging or sharing their photographs.
10. **Secure your accounts:** strong unique passwords, a password manager, two-factor authentication, and care with public Wi-Fi.

### Professionalism in written style online

Use correct grammar and spelling, avoid slang and text-speak in professional contexts, write complete sentences, keep a respectful tone even when you disagree, and acknowledge others' contributions.

### Cyberbullying, trolling and harassment

**Cyberbullying** is the use of digital media to hurt, humiliate or threaten a person repeatedly. **Trolling** is posting provocative or insulting comments to cause reaction. Respond by not engaging, **saving evidence** (screenshots, links, dates), blocking and reporting to the platform, informing a teacher or supervisor, and, where there are threats or private images, reporting to the **Nepal Police Cyber Bureau**. Do not take part in pile-ons; sharing is also harming.

::: key Quick checks before you post
- Is it true? Is it kind? Is it necessary? Would I say it to this person's face?
- Does it reveal someone's private information or mine?
- Could it be misread in another culture or by a future employer?
- Do I have the right to use this image, text or code?
:::

## 5.3 Communicating via social media and collaborative tools

### Social media

**Social media** are web and mobile platforms that let users create and share content and interact in networks. In the workplace they serve marketing, recruitment, customer service, public relations, learning and community building.

| Platform type | Examples | Professional use | Style |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Professional network** | LinkedIn | job search, recruiting, industry news | formal, achievement-oriented |
| **General social network** | Facebook, Viber/WhatsApp groups | community pages, customer support, events | friendly, local language and English |
| **Microblog** | X (Twitter), Threads | news, quick updates, thought leadership | short, direct |
| **Visual** | Instagram, Pinterest, TikTok, YouTube | brand stories, demos, tutorials | visual, informal |
| **Q and A / forums** | Stack Overflow, Reddit, Quora | help, knowledge sharing | precise, evidence-based |
| **Code and knowledge** | GitHub, Medium, dev.to | portfolio, documentation | technical, clear |

**Benefits:** wide reach at low cost; two-way feedback; quick news; networking; visibility for small businesses (a Pokhara homestay or a Kathmandu online shop can reach customers directly). **Risks:** misinformation, loss of privacy, cyberbullying, addiction and distraction, security attacks (phishing links), brand damage from a single bad post, and unclear boundaries between private and professional life.

**Guidelines for organisations:** a written **social media policy** (who may post, approval steps, what is confidential), an authorised person to speak for the organisation, quick and polite responses to complaints, an apology and correction when wrong, and a plan for crisis messages.

**Guidelines for individuals:** declare "views my own" where appropriate; do not argue with trolls; keep promotional posts honest; follow platform terms; tag and credit others.

### Collaborative tools

**Collaborative tools** let people work on shared content and tasks at the same time or at different times.

| Category | Examples | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Messaging and team chat** | Slack, Microsoft Teams, Google Chat, Discord | channels, threads, quick coordination |
| **Video conferencing** | Zoom, Google Meet, Teams | meetings, webinars, classes |
| **Shared documents** | Google Docs/Sheets/Slides, Microsoft 365, Notion | co-editing, comments, version history |
| **Project and task management** | Trello, Jira, Asana, GitHub Projects | boards, sprints, assignment, status |
| **Code collaboration** | GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket | version control, pull requests, code review |
| **Whiteboards and design** | Miro, Figma, Jamboard | brainstorming, UI design |
| **File sharing** | Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox | storage and permissions |

**Good practice with collaborative tools:** agree one tool per purpose and write it in the team charter; use clear names for files and channels; assign owners and due dates to tasks; use comments and "suggesting" mode instead of overwriting others; keep **version history** and back up; set **permissions** (viewer, commenter, editor) with care; summarise decisions in the channel or wiki; avoid sending the same message in five places; and review access when a member leaves.

::: example Worked example: choosing the channel
A team of five BCA students must (a) ask a quick question, (b) agree on a database design with changes by everyone, (c) report a serious misunderstanding with a teammate, (d) show the supervisor weekly progress. **(a)** a chat message; **(b)** a shared diagram with comments (Figma or Google Drawings), with decisions written on the project board; **(c)** a voice call or face-to-face talk first, not a group chat; **(d)** a short email or a shared progress document with a link to the board.
:::
