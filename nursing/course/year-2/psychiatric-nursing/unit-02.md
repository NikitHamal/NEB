## 2.1 What schizophrenia is and how it is diagnosed

**Schizophrenia** is a chronic brain disorder with disturbed thinking, perception, emotion and behaviour, with clear consciousness and usually intact orientation. The word means "split mind" but it is **not** split personality. Onset is usually between **15 and 35 years**, earlier in males. Risk factors include a **family history** (about 10% risk with an affected parent, about 40-50% for an identical twin), **pregnancy and birth complications**, **cannabis use in adolescence**, childhood trauma, urban birth and migration stress. The **neurodevelopmental** and **stress-vulnerability** models explain it: a vulnerable brain plus stress or drugs leads to illness.

**Diagnosis (DSM-5-TR):** two or more of delusions, hallucinations, disorganised speech, grossly disorganised or catatonic behaviour and negative symptoms for much of **one month** (at least one must be delusions, hallucinations or disorganised speech), with **signs of disturbance for at least six months** and a fall in functioning. **ICD-11** needs at least two symptoms for one month. Substance, medical and mood causes must be excluded. In primary care **mhGAP** asks about delusions, hallucinations, odd behaviour and self-neglect and uses the module "psychoses".

| Cluster | Meaning | Examples a nurse sees |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** (added) | Experiences not seen in health | **Delusions** (persecution, reference, control, grandeur); **hallucinations** (voices commenting or commanding, rarely visual) |
| **Negative** (lost) | Loss of normal function | **Alogia** (little speech), **avolition** (no drive), **anhedonia**, **flat affect**, social withdrawal |
| **Disorganised** | Chaotic thought and behaviour | Loose associations, word salad, neologisms, silly laughter, odd dress |
| **Cognitive** | Poor thinking skills | Poor attention, memory and planning (the best predictor of daily function) |
| **Catatonic / motor** | Abnormal movement | Stupor, mutism, posturing, waxy flexibility, excitement |

**Schneider's first-rank symptoms** strongly suggest schizophrenia: audible thoughts, voices arguing or commenting on the person, **thought insertion, withdrawal and broadcasting**, delusional perception, and feelings of **passivity** (made acts, made feelings). **Bleuler's four As** describe the core: loosening of **A**ssociation, flat **A**ffect, **A**mbivalence and **A**utism. Always exclude **organic causes** (thyroid disease, epilepsy, head injury, infection such as neurocysticercosis, cerebral malaria, HIV), alcohol, cannabis and amphetamine, and **delirium**, which has clouded consciousness.

## 2.2 The dopamine hypothesis and the brain

The **dopamine hypothesis** says that **overactivity of dopamine in the mesolimbic pathway** produces positive symptoms, while **low dopamine in the mesocortical pathway** produces negative and cognitive symptoms. The **glutamate (NMDA) hypothesis** adds that reduced NMDA receptor function (as with ketamine) disturbs this balance, and brain imaging shows **enlarged ventricles and reduced frontal and temporal volume**. Most antipsychotics **block D2 receptors** (Plate 2.2); this explains both their benefit and their side effects, because the same block in the **nigrostriatal** pathway causes movement disorders and in the **tuberoinfundibular** pathway raises **prolactin**.

## 2.3 Eight points of nursing care in schizophrenia

1. **Build trust slowly.** A suspicious patient may watch you closely, so be honest, consistent and predictable. Say what you will do before you do it and keep your promises. Sit at an angle, keep a distance and avoid whispering in front of the patient.
2. **Respond to delusions with care, not argument.** Do not agree and do not argue; say, "I know this feels very real and frightening to you, but I do not see it that way." Focus on the feeling, such as fear or anger, and on safe, real topics. Observe whether the delusion leads to danger, such as refusing food or fear of a neighbour.
3. **Respond to hallucinations.** Ask what the voices say, especially if they command harm. Say that you do not hear them but that you believe the person does, and help with distraction, music, walking, humming and a regular routine. Commands to harm must be reported at once and the observation level raised.
4. **Protect safety.** Assess suicide risk (about 5% of people with schizophrenia die by suicide, mostly early in the illness) and risk of violence every shift. Observe at the level ordered, keep the environment free of sharp objects and cords, and use de-escalation first. Check that tablets are swallowed (mouth check).
5. **Meet physical needs.** Many patients forget to eat, bathe and sleep. Encourage meals, fluids, hygiene and sleep, and weigh weekly. Check for constipation, dental problems and injury, since self-neglect is part of the illness.
6. **Reduce stimulation and give structure.** A quiet room, a daily timetable and simple one-step instructions lower confusion. Avoid crowds and loud noise. Short group tasks such as carrom, gardening or cleaning rebuild skills.
7. **Teach the patient and family.** Explain that schizophrenia is a brain illness, not a curse, and that medicine must continue for years. Teach **early relapse signs** (poor sleep, withdrawal, suspicion returning), side effects to report and a clear plan to reach the health post. Family psychoeducation lowers relapse, as does a calm home with low **expressed emotion** (criticism, hostility, over-involvement).
8. **Rehabilitate and link with the community.** Plan from admission for follow-up, a medicine supply (free at government facilities), a disability card where eligible, and work or study support. Social skills training, supported employment and community groups help recovery. Avoid cannabis and alcohol, which cause relapse.

## 2.4 Antipsychotic drugs: classes, doses and equivalents

**Antipsychotics** treat psychosis by blocking D2 receptors. They calm agitation within hours to days, but delusions and voices take **2-6 weeks** to fade, so the nurse must not judge a drug after three days. **First-generation (typical)** drugs (chlorpromazine, haloperidol, fluphenazine) cause more movement side effects. **Second-generation (atypical)** drugs (risperidone, olanzapine, aripiprazole, quetiapine, clozapine) cause fewer movement effects but more weight gain and metabolic change, except aripiprazole which is more neutral. **Clozapine** is for treatment-resistant illness after two adequate trials.

| Drug | Usual adult oral range | Notable points for the nurse |
|---|---|---|
| **Chlorpromazine** | 300-800 mg/day in divided doses | Very sedating; hypotension, photosensitivity, anticholinergic effects; cheap and in the essential list |
| **Haloperidol** | 5-15 mg/day (IM 2.5-5 mg for agitation) | Strong EPS, little sedation; check QTc; IM ampoule 5 mg/mL |
| **Risperidone** | 2-6 mg/day | Raises prolactin; EPS above 6 mg; orthostatic hypotension |
| **Olanzapine** | 10-20 mg/day (start 5-10 mg) | Weight gain, high glucose and lipids; sedating |
| **Aripiprazole** | 15-30 mg/day | Less weight gain; akathisia, insomnia |
| **Clozapine** | 300-450 mg/day after slow titration from 12.5 mg | Agranulocytosis (blood counts), myocarditis, drooling, seizures, constipation |
| **Fluphenazine decanoate** (depot) | 12.5-100 mg IM every 2-5 weeks (25 mg/mL) | For patients who stop tablets; deep IM, Z-track; EPS |

Doses of different drugs are compared by the **chlorpromazine equivalent (CPZ-eq)**: roughly **chlorpromazine 100 mg ≈ haloperidol 2 mg ≈ risperidone 2 mg ≈ olanzapine 5 mg ≈ aripiprazole 7.5 mg** (approximate values, from published equivalence tables). Total CPZ-eq above about **1,000 mg/day** is "high dose" and needs a doctor's review and an ECG.

```python
halo_mg, olz_mg = 10, 10                         # haloperidol and olanzapine in one day
cpz_halo = halo_mg / 2 * 100                     # 2 mg haloperidol = 100 mg chlorpromazine
cpz_olz = olz_mg / 5 * 100                       # 5 mg olanzapine = 100 mg chlorpromazine
total = cpz_halo + cpz_olz
im_mg, strength = 2.5, 5.0                       # haloperidol IM order, 5 mg/mL ampoule
vol = im_mg / strength
depot_mg, depot_strength = 37.5, 25.0            # fluphenazine decanoate 25 mg/mL
depot_ml = depot_mg / depot_strength
tabs = 3 * 28                                    # trihexyphenidyl 2 mg three times a day for 28 days
print(f"CPZ-eq: haloperidol {cpz_halo:.0f} + olanzapine {cpz_olz:.0f} = {total:.0f} mg ({'above' if total > 1000 else 'below'} 1,000 mg)")
print(f"Haloperidol {im_mg} mg from {strength:.0f} mg/mL = {vol:.1f} mL")
print(f"Fluphenazine decanoate {depot_mg} mg from {depot_strength:.0f} mg/mL = {depot_ml:.1f} mL")
print(f"Trihexyphenidyl 2 mg x 3 x 28 days = {tabs} tablets")
```

Haloperidol 2.5 mg from 5 mg/mL = 0.5 mL
Fluphenazine decanoate 37.5 mg from 25 mg/mL = 1.5 mL

A 2.5 mg IM order from a 5 mg/mL ampoule is **0.5 mL**; a 37.5 mg depot dose from 25 mg/mL is **1.5 mL**; and trihexyphenidyl 2 mg three times daily for 28 days needs **84 tablets**.

| Effect | Features | Nursing action and treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Acute dystonia** | Painful spasm of neck, jaw, eyes (oculogyric crisis), tongue; frightening, may affect breathing | Emergency: call doctor; **anticholinergic IM** (promethazine 25 mg or biperiden); reassure; then review dose |
| **Parkinsonism** | Tremor, rigidity, mask face, shuffling, drooling | Report; lower dose or switch; trihexyphenidyl 2 mg short course if ordered |
| **Akathisia** | Inner restlessness, cannot sit still; may be mistaken for agitation and lead to a higher dose | Report; reduce dose; propranolol may be ordered; do **not** raise the antipsychotic |
| **Tardive dyskinesia** | Involuntary lip, tongue and limb movements after months or years; may be irreversible | Screen every 6 months with **AIMS**; report early; anticholinergics make it worse |
| **Hyperprolactinaemia** | Breast milk, missed periods, low libido, erectile problems | Ask sensitively; report; change drug if bothersome |
| **Metabolic syndrome** | Weight gain, high glucose and lipids, hypertension | Baseline and regular checks, diet, exercise, switch if needed |

**NMS versus serotonin syndrome.** **Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (NMS)** develops over **days**, with **lead-pipe rigidity, slow reflexes**, fever above 38 °C, altered consciousness, sweating, unstable pulse and BP and a raised **creatine kinase (CK)**; treatment is to **stop the antipsychotic**, give IV fluids and cooling, monitor in intensive care and use dantrolene or bromocriptine as ordered. **Serotonin syndrome** comes within **hours** of a serotonergic drug, with **clonus, hyper-reflexia, diarrhoea and wide pupils**. Both are emergencies; the nurse acts on the signs without waiting for the diagnosis.

```python
w0, w1, ht = 62.0, 67.0, 1.60                    # baseline and 12-week weight, height in metres
gain = (w1 - w0) / w0 * 100
bmi0, bmi1 = w0 / ht ** 2, w1 / ht ** 2
aims = {"face": 2, "lips": 2, "jaw": 1, "tongue": 2, "arms": 1, "legs": 0, "trunk": 1}
aims_total = sum(aims.values())
days, taken = 30, 25
mpr = taken / days * 100
print(f"Weight gain = ({w1:.0f} - {w0:.0f}) / {w0:.0f} = {gain:.1f}% ({'significant, 7% or more' if gain >= 7 else 'below 7%'})")
print(f"BMI {bmi0:.1f} -> {bmi1:.1f} kg/m2")
print(f"AIMS items 1-7 total = {aims_total}; medication possession = {taken}/{days} = {mpr:.1f}%")
```

BMI 24.2 -> 26.2 kg/m2

### Antipsychotics: eight points of nursing responsibility

1. **Check the order and calculate the dose.** Confirm the patient, drug, dose, route, time and allergies, then compute tablets or millilitres on paper. A tenfold error is possible with 0.5 and 5 mg/mL strengths. Record the dose and the time.
2. **Make sure the drug is swallowed.** Patients with psychosis may hide tablets in the cheek. Watch the swallow and check the mouth, and use liquid or a depot if needed. Report refusal at once and do not hide tablets in food.
3. **Take baseline and routine measurements.** Record weight, BMI, waist, BP, pulse and temperature before starting, and fasting glucose and lipids at baseline, 12 weeks and yearly. An ECG is needed for haloperidol, high doses and heart disease. Plot weight on a chart weekly for six weeks.
4. **Watch for movement disorders.** Ask about stiffness, tremor, restlessness and twisting movements at every shift, and use the **AIMS** every six months. Teach the patient to report them and not to stop the drug alone. Early recognition prevents distress and refusal.
5. **Recognise emergencies.** Know the signs of acute dystonia, NMS, severe hypotension, seizure and agranulocytosis (sore throat, fever, mouth ulcers with clozapine). Check temperature and pulse when the patient is rigid or drowsy. Call the doctor at once and stop nothing without an order, except in suspected NMS as directed.
6. **Reduce common effects.** For postural hypotension, rise slowly and sit before standing. For dry mouth and constipation, give fluids, fibre and sugar-free sweets. Advise sun protection with chlorpromazine and no driving when drowsy.
7. **Give depot injections correctly.** Use a deep IM injection with a large-bore needle in the gluteal muscle, Z-track technique, rotate sides, record the date and book the next injection. A missed depot is an early sign of relapse, so follow up at once.
8. **Teach the patient and family.** The drug takes weeks to work and must be continued for at least 1-2 years after a first episode (often longer). Explain side effects, avoid alcohol and cannabis, and warn women of prolactin effects and pregnancy risk. Give a written plan, the follow-up date and the health post contact.

## Formula and fact sheet

- CPZ-eq: chlorpromazine 100 mg ≈ haloperidol 2 mg ≈ risperidone 2 mg ≈ olanzapine 5 mg ≈ aripiprazole 7.5 mg; above 1,000 mg/day is high dose.
- Volume (mL) = dose ÷ strength (mg/mL); tablets = daily dose ÷ tablet strength × days; weight change % = (new − old) ÷ old × 100 (7% or more is significant).
- Diagnosis: two or more core symptoms for one month and six months of disturbance (DSM-5-TR); onset 15-35 years; about 1 in 300 worldwide.
- NMS: days, rigidity, fever, high CK; serotonin syndrome: hours, clonus. Dystonia: IM anticholinergic.

## Chapter summary

- Schizophrenia causes delusions, voices, withdrawal and disorganisation, is linked to dopamine, glutamate and brain development and is treatable.

She is diagnosed with first-episode schizophrenia and started on risperidone 2 mg twice a day. On day 3 her neck twists to the side and she is very frightened. Later she often forgets tablets and is changed to fluphenazine decanoate 25 mg IM every 2 weeks; at 3 months she weighs 63 kg. Calculate and write the nursing care plan.**

*Calculations.* Risperidone 4 mg/day equals **200 mg** chlorpromazine; for 28 days she needs **56 tablets** of 2 mg. If trihexyphenidyl 2 mg three times a day is ordered for 28 days the pharmacy supplies **84 tablets**. A 25 mg depot dose from a 25 mg/mL ampoule is **1.0 mL**, and in 12 weeks she needs **6 injections**. Her weight rises from 58 to 63 kg, a gain of **8.6%** (7% or more is significant); BMI rises from **24.1** to **26.2** kg/m².

*Care.* On day 3 recognise **acute dystonia**: stay with her, reassure, call the doctor, give promethazine or biperiden IM as ordered and review the dose. Respond to the voice with calm, honest words and do not argue about the aunt's food; offer sealed or shared meals. Check the mouth after tablets and then plan the depot injection with consent, using Z-track and rotating sides. Weigh weekly and check BP, glucose and lipids at 12 weeks; give diet and exercise advice and encourage walking. Teach the family that the illness is a brain disorder, that medicine takes weeks to work, that stopping it brings relapse and that criticism or shouting raises relapse risk. Give the relapse signs (poor sleep, withdrawal, suspicion) and the health post and hospital numbers; arrange for the FCHV or health post to remind her of the injection date and for her to return to study or work step by step.
