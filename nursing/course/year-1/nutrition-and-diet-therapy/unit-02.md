Micronutrient deficiency is called **hidden hunger**, because a child may eat enough to fill the stomach and still lack vitamin A, iron or iodine. In Nepal 43% of children aged 6-59 months and 34% of women aged 15-49 are anaemic (NDHS 2022), and the government answers with vitamin A capsules twice a year, iron-folic acid tablets, iodised salt, Baal Vita micronutrient powder and food fortification. Nurses give these supplements, spot the signs early and teach families which local foods prevent them. ## 2.1 Vitamins: fat-soluble and water-soluble

**Vitamins** are organic substances needed in tiny amounts that the body cannot make in enough quantity. The four **fat-soluble** vitamins (A, D, E, K) are absorbed with fat, stored in the liver and fat, and can build up to toxic levels. The **water-soluble** vitamins (the B group and C) are not stored much, are lost in cooking water and urine, and need regular intake.

| Vitamin | Good sources | Main function | Deficiency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** (retinol) | Liver, egg, milk, ghee, carrot, pumpkin, green leaves, mango | Vision (rhodopsin), epithelium, immunity | Night blindness, xerosis, Bitot's spot, keratomalacia |
| **D** | Sunlight on skin, fish, egg, fortified oil | Calcium and phosphate absorption, bone | Rickets (child), osteomalacia (adult) |
| **E** | Vegetable oils, nuts, seeds | Antioxidant | Haemolysis in newborn, neuropathy |
| **K** | Green leaves, gundruk, gut bacteria | Clotting factors II, VII, IX, X | Bleeding, newborn haemorrhage |
| **B1** thiamine | Whole grain, pulses, nuts | Carbohydrate metabolism | Beriberi, Wernicke's |
| **B2** riboflavin | Milk, egg, green leaves | Energy metabolism | Cheilosis, glossitis |
| **B3** niacin | Meat, groundnut, whole grain | NAD, NADP | Pellagra: dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia |
| **B9** folate | Green leaves, pulses, liver | DNA synthesis | Megaloblastic anaemia, neural tube defect |
| **B12** | Animal foods only | Red cell and nerve | Pernicious anaemia, neuropathy |
| **C** | Amla, guava, citrus, tomato, green chilli | Collagen, iron absorption | Scurvy: bleeding gums, bruising |

**Vitamin A** is the programme vitamin of Nepal. Capsules of **100,000 IU for children 6-11 months and 200,000 IU for 12-59 months** are given every six months by health workers and FCHVs in the national vitamin A programme, which has been linked to a large fall in child deaths from diarrhoea and measles. For a municipality with 840 births a year, about **420 children** are aged 6-11 months and **3,360** are aged 12-59 months, so one round needs 420 blue and 3,360 red capsules, **7,560 capsules a year** for two rounds, delivering 714,000,000 IU in each round.

The WHO classifies **xerophthalmia** as **XN** night blindness, **X1A** conjunctival xerosis, **X1B** Bitot's spot, **X2** corneal xerosis, **X3A/X3B** corneal ulcer or keratomalacia (less or more than one third of the cornea) and **XS** corneal scar. The first symptom is **night blindness**, noticed by a mother as a child who cannot find the way at dusk. Treatment by the WHO schedule is **200,000 IU orally on day 1, day 2 and day 14** for a child over 12 months (100,000 IU for 6-11 months, 50,000 IU under 6 months), so a 3-year-old receives **600,000 IU** in three capsules; give the same schedule to measles patients in areas of deficiency. A pregnant woman must not take high-dose vitamin A (above 10,000 IU daily), because it is teratogenic.

**Niacin** shows why diet pattern matters: 60 mg of tryptophan can be turned into 1 mg of niacin, so the **niacin equivalent (NE)** of a diet is niacin plus tryptophan ÷ 60. A diet with 12 mg niacin and 600 mg tryptophan gives 12 + 10 = **22 NE**. Maize has its niacin bound and little tryptophan, so people who live mainly on maize without pulses or milk can develop pellagra; Nepali hill diets of maize *dhido* need pulses, milk or egg and nixtamalised or soaked maize for protection.

## 2.2 Minerals and the three big deficiencies: iodine, iron, calcium

| Mineral | Sources | Function | Deficiency or excess |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Iodine** | Iodised salt, sea fish, milk | Thyroxine and tri-iodothyronine | Goitre, cretinism, miscarriage |
| **Iron** | Liver, meat, green leaves, pulses, jaggery | Haemoglobin, myoglobin, enzymes | Iron-deficiency anaemia |
| **Calcium** | Milk, curd, small fish, ragi, green leaves | Bone, teeth, clotting, nerve and muscle | Rickets, osteoporosis, tetany |
| **Zinc** | Meat, pulses, nuts, whole grain | Enzymes, growth, wound healing | Growth failure, diarrhoea, delayed healing |
| **Fluoride** | Water, tea, fish | Enamel | Dental caries; excess gives fluorosis |
| **Sodium, potassium** | Salt; fruit, vegetables | Fluid balance, nerve, muscle | Hypertension (sodium excess) |

**Iodine.** The thyroid needs iodine; when it is lacking, the pituitary raises TSH and the gland enlarges to form a **goitre**. Severe deficiency in pregnancy damages the foetal brain (cretinism), and mild deficiency lowers the IQ of a whole population. Nepal's hills were a goitre belt, and the Universal Salt Iodisation programme with **iodised salt at 30 ppm (30 mg iodine per kg of salt)** brought the problem under control. An adult who eats the WHO limit of 5 g salt a day takes in 5 × 30 mg ÷ 1,000 = **150 µg of iodine**, exactly the adult need of 150 µg. A pregnant woman needs 250 µg, which would need 8.3 g of salt: too much salt, so she is advised to use **iodised salt in small amounts, and supplements or iodine-rich food** instead. Keep salt in a covered, dry container away from the stove, and add it after cooking, since iodine is lost by heat, light and damp.

**Iron.** Haem iron from meat and liver is absorbed 15-35%; non-haem iron from plants 2-10%, and **vitamin C raises** while **tea, coffee and phytate lower** this absorption. The Nepal national programme gives **iron-folic acid (IFA) tablets of 60 mg elemental iron and 400 µg folic acid, one daily for 180 days in pregnancy** and for 45 days after delivery, weekly iron to adolescent girls and Baal Vita sprinkles to children 6-23 months. The **WHO limits** for anaemia are haemoglobin below **12 g/dL** in non-pregnant women, **11 g/dL** in pregnancy and in children 6-59 months, and **13 g/dL** in men; in pregnancy 10-10.9 is mild, 7-9.9 moderate and below 7 severe.

A 52-kg woman with Hb 8 g/dL needs an iron deficit of 52 × (12 − 8) × 2.4 + 500 = **999 mg**. Since 60 mg of oral iron gives about 6 mg absorbed each day, the time to replace is about **167 days**: this is why IFA must be taken for the whole 180 days and for at least 3 months after Hb is normal. The 180-tablet course holds **10,800 mg** of iron. The calcium need of 1,000 mg is met by about **833 mL of milk** a day, so curd, small fish eaten with bones, finger millet and green leaves are added. Vitamin D 400 IU equals **10 µg**, and a 300,000 IU stoss dose equals 7,500 µg.

## 2.3 The nurse's role in preventing micronutrient deficiency: eight points

1. **Give vitamin A capsules correctly and on time.** Give 100,000 IU at 6-11 months and 200,000 IU at 12-59 months, every six months, and record on the child card and register. Do not give a double dose if a round was missed. A well-kept record lets the next health worker know when the next capsule is due.
2. **Supply and teach iron-folic acid.** Give 60 mg iron with 400 µg folic acid daily for 180 days in pregnancy, and teach the woman to take it at night or after food with citrus or amla. Tell her the stools will turn black, and to avoid tea one hour before and after. Many women stop because of nausea or fear, so counsel at every visit.
3. **Promote iodised salt.** Teach the family to buy packed iodised salt, store it in a dry covered jar and add it at the end of cooking. Check with the test kit at the health post or school. Warn that rock salt and unlabelled salt carry no iodine.
4. **Promote dietary diversity from local foods.** Advise dark green leaves, orange fruit, egg, pulses, small fish, milk, amla and guava each week. These foods are cheaper than tablets and also bring protein and other vitamins. Kitchen gardens and small poultry help poor families.
5. **Detect early signs on every contact.** Ask about night blindness, look for Bitot's spot, pale conjunctiva, cheilosis, bleeding gums, goitre and bowed legs. Weigh and measure the child. Early signs are cheap to treat and late signs may mean blindness.
6. **Give treatment doses by protocol.** Treat xerophthalmia with 200,000 IU on days 1, 2 and 14, and refer corneal signs urgently. Treat anaemia by Hb level, give calcium and vitamin D for rickets, and give vitamin C in scurvy. Always check dose, age and pregnancy status before giving.
7. **Protect special groups.** Pregnant and lactating women, infants, adolescent girls, the elderly, strict vegetarians and the poor have higher risk. Give each group its own package: IFA and calcium for pregnancy, Baal Vita at 6-23 months, weekly iron for girls. Check the vegan mother's infant for vitamin B12 lack.
8. **Educate, record and report.** Use pictures and local words, and teach with cooking demonstrations. Record supplements in HMIS and DHIS2, and report any outbreak such as scurvy or pellagra in a hostel or camp. Review coverage every month with the ward FCHVs.

## Formula and fact sheet

- Fat-soluble A, D, E, K; water-soluble B group and C. Niacin equivalent = niacin + tryptophan ÷ 60.
- Vitamin A: 100,000 IU at 6-11 months, 200,000 IU at 12-59 months every 6 months; treatment 200,000 IU on days 1, 2 and 14.
- Iodised salt 30 ppm; adult need 150 µg (5 g salt gives 150 µg); pregnancy 250 µg.
- IFA 60 mg iron + 400 µg folic acid, 180 days in pregnancy; anaemia limits 12, 11, 13 g/dL for non-pregnant women, pregnancy or child, and men.
- Iron deficit = weight × (target − actual Hb) × 2.4 + 500. Vitamin D: 40 IU = 1 µg.

## Chapter summary

- Vitamins A, D, E, K are stored and can be toxic; B group and C need daily intake.
- Vitamin A deficiency begins with night blindness and ends in corneal blindness; the capsules every six months prevent it.
- Iodine deficiency causes goitre and brain damage, prevented by iodised salt; iron deficiency is treated for months, not days.
- The nurse gives supplements, finds early signs and teaches the use of local foods.

3. A family uses salt with 15 ppm iodine; an adult eating 5 g a day takes in: (a) 15 µg (b) 75 µg (c) 150 µg (d) 300 µg.
4. A diet gives 8 mg niacin and 300 mg tryptophan; its niacin equivalent is: (a) 8 NE (b) 13 NE (c) 38 NE (d) 308 NE.
5. A pregnant woman has haemoglobin 8.6 g/dL; the anaemia is: (a) mild (b) moderate (c) severe (d) not anaemia.

::: note Answers and rationales
**1. (c).** 3 × 200,000 = 600,000 IU; the three doses (day 1, 2, 14) clear stores and prevent relapse, and 1,200,000 is four doses.
**2. (b).** 200 × 0.33 = 66 mg of elemental iron; doses are always judged by elemental iron, not salt weight.
**3. (b).** 5 g × 15 mg/kg = 0.075 mg = 75 µg, half the need, which is why 30 ppm is the production standard.
**4. (b).** 8 + 300 ÷ 60 = 13 NE; the tryptophan portion is divided by 60, not added directly.
**5. (b).** In pregnancy Hb 7-9.9 is moderate, 10-10.9 mild and below 7 severe; she needs treatment doses, a check for causes and follow-up.

**Short answer (3 marks): Vitamin A prophylaxis in Nepal.** Under the national vitamin A programme every child aged 6-59 months receives a capsule every six months, in Baisakh and Kartik rounds, through health workers and FCHVs. The dose is 100,000 IU (blue capsule) at 6-11 months and 200,000 IU (red capsule) at 12-59 months. It prevents night blindness, xerophthalmia and deaths from infection. The nurse records the dose on the child's card and in the register, never doubles a missed dose, and teaches mothers to give vitamin A-rich foods daily.

**Medium answer (5 marks): Describe iodine deficiency disorders and their prevention (Figure 2.2).** Low dietary iodine reduces thyroid hormone, the pituitary secretes more TSH and the thyroid enlarges as a **goitre**. The spectrum of **iodine deficiency disorders** includes goitre, hypothyroidism, miscarriage and stillbirth, congenital anomalies, **cretinism** (mental and growth retardation, deaf-mutism) and lower IQ in children. Hill and mountain soils are low in iodine, so the disease is endemic there. Prevention is by **universal iodisation of salt at 30 ppm** so that 5 g a day gives 150 µg; the nurse teaches the family to buy packed iodised salt, store it dry and covered and add it after cooking, and checks with salt-test kits. Iodised oil injection or capsule may be used in severe pockets; urinary iodine and goitre surveys monitor the programme.

**Long answer (10 marks): Maya, 3 years old, weighing 11.5 kg, is brought from a remote hill village to the health post. Her mother says she bumps into things at dusk for two months. Both eyes show dry, white, foamy patches beside the cornea and the conjunctiva is dull. She eats rice and thin dal, no vegetable, egg or ghee. She is pale and has had diarrhoea. Identify the condition, manage her and write the nursing care plan.**

*Diagnosis.* Night blindness with conjunctival xerosis and Bitot's spots is **vitamin A deficiency with xerophthalmia (XN and X1B)**; the corneas are clear so she has no sight-threatening stage, but she is at high risk of progressing to corneal ulcer within days if untreated. Diarrhoea and a poor diet worsen the deficiency; pallor suggests co-existing anaemia.

*Management.* Give **vitamin A 200,000 IU orally on day 1, again on day 2 and again on day 14 = 600,000 IU** (age over 12 months). Give ORS and zinc 20 mg daily for 10 days for the diarrhoea, following IMNCI, and check for pneumonia and dehydration. Give albendazole 400 mg once if the child is 24 months or older, and start iron-folic acid or Baal Vita according to the protocol after the acute illness. Refer to the hospital eye department if the cornea shows haze or ulcer. Advise the mother to feed dark green leaves, mango, papaya, pumpkin, egg, milk and ghee, cooked with a little oil, and to continue breastfeeding if she is still feeding. Enrol Maya in growth monitoring and give vitamin A every six months from the next round. Ask about the other children in the household.
