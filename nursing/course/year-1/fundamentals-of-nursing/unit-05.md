Giving the right drug to the right patient is the nurse's last safety check before harm. This unit teaches the rights of medication, the routes and injection techniques, IV cannulation, and the arithmetic of doses, drip rates and paediatric dosing, all computed and verified by program.

::: key What this unit gives you

- Dose calculation: tablets, liquids, injections, weight-based, Young's rule and BSA.

## 5.1 The drug order

A valid order has: patient's name and age, date, drug (generic name), **dose, route, frequency**, duration and the prescriber's signature with registration number. Verbal orders are accepted only in emergencies, repeated back, and signed by the prescriber later. Common abbreviations: **OD** once daily, **BD** twice, **TDS** three times, **QID** four times, **SOS/PRN** when needed, **STAT** immediately, **AC/PC** before/after meals, **HS** at bedtime, **NBM** nothing by mouth. Narcotics (such as morphine and pethidine) are stored under double lock, signed out in a register and checked by two nurses.

## 5.2 The rights of medication

Check **right patient** (two identifiers: name and hospital number, never the bed), **right drug**, **right dose**, **right route**, **right time**, **right documentation**, **right reason**, **right response** (monitor effect), **right to refuse**, and **right education**. Check the label **three times**: when taking it from the shelf, before preparing, and before giving or discarding the container. For high-alert drugs (insulin, heparin, potassium chloride, opioids) have a second nurse independently verify.

## 5.3 Routes and injection techniques

| Route | Angle | Needle | Volume | Typical sites |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intradermal (ID) | 10-15°, bevel up | 26 G, 3/8 in | 0.1 mL | inner forearm (Mantoux, BCG) |
| Subcutaneous (SC) | 45° (90° with short needle) | 25 G, 5/8 in | up to 1-2 mL | abdomen, upper arm, thigh |
| Intramuscular (IM) | 90° | 21-23 G, 1-1.5 in | up to 3 mL (deltoid 1-2 mL) | ventrogluteal, vastus lateralis, deltoid |
| Intravenous (IV) | 15-30° | cannula 18-24 G | per order | forearm, back of hand |

**IM technique:** wash hands, check the five rights, draw up, choose the site (**ventrogluteal** is safest in adults; the old dorsogluteal site risks the sciatic nerve and is discouraged), clean skin and let it dry, stretch the skin, insert quickly at 90°, aspirate if the policy requires, inject slowly (10 s per mL), withdraw, press with a swab (do not rub), discard the needle unrecapped, record. **Z-track** for irritant drugs.

**Intravenous cannulation.** The gauge is colour-coded: **14 G orange, 16 G grey, 18 G green, 20 G pink, 22 G blue, 24 G yellow**. A 20-22 G cannula suits most adults; 24 G for children and the elderly; 16-18 G for blood or trauma. Choose a distal vein, apply the tourniquet, clean for 30 seconds with alcohol and let it dry, insert at 15-30° bevel up, advance the catheter off the stylet, release the tourniquet, attach a flush, secure, date the dressing. Replace on signs of phlebitis (pain, redness, swelling, a palpable cord) or per hospital policy.

## 5.4 Calculations

**Basic formula:** Dose to give = (Desired ÷ Have) × Vehicle.

::: example Worked examples: dose calculations
1. **Tablets.** Ordered 500 mg; stock 250 mg tablets: 500 ÷ 250 = **2 tablets**.
2. **Liquid.** Ordered 125 mg; stock 250 mg per 5 mL: 125 ÷ 250 × 5 = **2.5 mL**.
3. **Injection.** Gentamicin 80 mg ordered; vial 40 mg/mL: 80 ÷ 40 = **2.0 mL**.
4. **Weight-based (child).** Paracetamol 15 mg/kg for a 12 kg child = 15 × 12 = **180 mg**; syrup 120 mg per 5 mL: 180 ÷ 120 × 5 = **7.5 mL**.
5. **Young's rule (age 6, adult dose 500 mg):** age ÷ (age + 12) × adult dose = 6 ÷ 18 × 500 = **166.7 mg**.
6. **Body surface area (Mosteller)** for a child of 110 cm and 18 kg: √(110 × 18 ÷ 3600) = **0.74 m²**.
:::

**IV drip rate.** Drops per minute = (volume in mL × drop factor) ÷ time in minutes. Macrodrip sets deliver **20 drops/mL** in most Nepali hospitals (some 15); **microdrip sets deliver 60 drops/mL**, so drops per minute equals mL per hour.

::: example Worked example: drip rate and time
**(a)** 1000 mL of normal saline over 8 hours with a 20 drops/mL set: 1000 × 20 ÷ (8 × 60) = 41.67, so **42 drops/min**. **(b)** 125 mL/h on a microdrip set (60 drops/mL) is **125 drops/min**. **(c)** 500 mL at 100 mL/h lasts 500 ÷ 100 = **5 hours**.
:::

```python
vol, hours, df = 1000, 8, 20
print("macro gtt/min", round(vol * df / (hours * 60)))
print("micro gtt/min", round(125 * 60 / 60))
print("paracetamol mg", 15 * 12)
print("syrup mL", round(15 * 12 / 120 * 5, 1))
print("BSA m2", round((110 * 18 / 3600) ** 0.5, 2))
```

## 5.5 Nursing responsibilities in medication administration: eight points

1. **Know the drug.** Read the action, usual dose, side effects, interactions and nursing implications before giving any unfamiliar medicine. Use the hospital formulary or the Nepal national list of essential medicines. Never give a drug you cannot explain.
2. **Verify the order and the patient.** Check the order against the chart, ask the patient to state their name, and compare it with the wristband. Question any order that is unclear, illegible or unusual. Do not give a drug on a guess.
3. **Check allergies and contraindications.** Ask about allergy to penicillin and other drugs, pregnancy and kidney or liver disease, and look at the allergy band. A repeat reaction can be fatal. Report any doubt to the doctor before giving.
4. **Calculate carefully.** Do the sum twice, use units correctly (mg, mcg, mL) and have a second nurse check high-risk doses. A decimal slip gives a ten-fold error. Write the calculation on the chart where policy allows.
5. **Prepare and give with asepsis.** Wash hands, use a clean tray, a new needle and syringe, and prepare the dose just before giving. Never use a drug without a label or after expiry. Stay until the patient swallows an oral medicine.
6. **Observe the response and side effects.** Check vital signs when required, for example BP before an antihypertensive, pulse before digoxin. Watch for rash, vomiting, drowsiness or breathlessness. Report adverse reactions and record them in the pharmacovigilance form.
7. **Document immediately.** Sign the time, dose, route and site after giving, and also record refusals, omissions and the reason. Never sign before giving or for another nurse. An unrecorded dose may be repeated by the next nurse.
8. **Teach the patient.** Explain the name, purpose, timing, common side effects and what to do for a missed dose, and complete antibiotic courses and TB DOTS regimens. In Nepal many patients stop tablets when they feel better, so teaching is as important as giving.

## Formula and fact sheet

- Dose = (Desired ÷ Have) × Vehicle.
- gtt/min = (mL × drop factor) ÷ (hours × 60). Microdrip: gtt/min = mL/h.
- Infusion time (h) = volume ÷ rate. Child dose by weight = mg/kg × kg.
- Young's rule = age ÷ (age + 12) × adult dose. BSA = √(height cm × weight kg ÷ 3600).
- Cannula colours: 14 orange, 16 grey, 18 green, 20 pink, 22 blue, 24 yellow.

**MCQs (with reasons)**

1. Ordered 500 mg; tablets are 250 mg. The nurse gives: (a) half tablet (b) 1 tablet (c) 2 tablets (d) 4 tablets.
2. A 20 drops/mL set, 1000 mL over 8 hours, runs at about: (a) 21 (b) 42 (c) 83 (d) 125 drops/min.
3. A pink IV cannula is: (a) 14 G (b) 18 G (c) 20 G (d) 24 G.
4. The safest IM site in an adult is: (a) dorsogluteal (b) ventrogluteal (c) rectus femoris (d) calf.
5. The Mantoux test is given at: (a) 90° (b) 45° (c) 10-15° (d) 30°.

::: note Answers and rationales
**1. (c).** 500 ÷ 250 = 2. Giving one tablet underdoses; four overdoses.
**2. (b).** 1000 × 20 ÷ 480 = 41.67, rounded to 42. 83 is the rate for a 4-hour run, and 21 for a 16-hour run.
**3. (c).** 20 G is pink. 14 is orange, 18 green and 24 yellow.
**4. (b).** The ventrogluteal area is away from major nerves and vessels. The dorsogluteal risks the sciatic nerve.
**5. (c).** Intradermal injections lie almost flat to the skin so a small bleb forms.
:::

**Short answer (3 marks): Calculate the syrup volume for a 12 kg child ordered paracetamol 15 mg/kg, stock 120 mg per 5 mL.** The dose is 15 × 12 = 180 mg. Volume = (180 ÷ 120) × 5 = 7.5 mL. Measure it with an oral syringe, not a household spoon, and confirm the interval (usually 6-hourly) and the maximum daily dose.

**Medium answer (5 marks): Describe the procedure for giving an intramuscular injection in the ventrogluteal site (Figure 5.1).** Check the order and the rights, explain the procedure and obtain consent, and perform hand hygiene. Draw up the drug with a sterile needle, change to a new needle of 21-23 G, 1-1.5 inches. Position the patient on the side with the knee flexed. Locate the site: place the heel of the opposite hand on the greater trochanter, the index finger on the anterior superior iliac spine, spread the middle finger back along the iliac crest to form a V; the injection goes into the centre of the V. Clean with an alcohol swab and let it dry. Stretch the skin, insert at 90° quickly, aspirate if policy requires, inject slowly, withdraw and press with a swab. Discard the needle in the sharps box without recapping, make the patient comfortable and document the drug, dose, time, site and any reaction.

**Long answer (10 marks): Mrs Kumari Tamang, 45, admitted to the medical ward with severe dehydration from gastroenteritis, is ordered 1000 mL of Ringer's lactate over 8 hours via an IV cannula, with gentamicin 80 mg IV (vial 40 mg/mL). Calculate and describe your nursing management, with a care plan.**

*Calculations.* Drip rate with a 20 drops/mL set = 1000 × 20 ÷ 480 = 41.67, i.e. **42 drops/min**. Gentamicin volume = 80 ÷ 40 = **2.0 mL**, diluted and given slowly as the protocol directs. *Procedure.* Check the rights, wash hands, select a distal forearm vein, insert a 20 G (pink) cannula under asepsis, fix and date the dressing, prime the set, connect, set the rate by counting drops for one minute, and label the bottle with the start time and the hourly volume goal (125 mL/h). Monitor the site every hour for pain, redness and swelling (phlebitis), infiltration, and watch for fluid overload (cough, breathlessness, raised pulse). Record intake and output.
