## 3.1 Menstrual disorders and PCOS

A **normal cycle** is **21-35 days**, bleeding lasts **2-7 days** and blood loss is under **80 mL** (about 6-8 pads soaked). Terms: **amenorrhoea** (primary: no period by 15 years with normal growth, or by 13 years with no breast development; secondary: no period for 3 months in a woman who had regular cycles, or 6 months if irregular), **oligomenorrhoea** (cycle over 35 days), **menorrhagia** or **heavy menstrual bleeding** (over 80 mL), **metrorrhagia** (bleeding between periods), **dysmenorrhoea** (painful periods) and **postmenopausal bleeding** (always investigate for endometrial cancer).

**Abnormal uterine bleeding (AUB)** is classified by the FIGO **PALM-COEIN** system: **P**olyp, **A**denomyosis, **L**eiomyoma, **M**alignancy and hyperplasia (the structural causes) and **C**oagulopathy, **O**vulatory dysfunction, **E**ndometrial, **I**atrogenic, **N**ot yet classified. The commonest in young women is **ovulatory dysfunction** (PCOS, thyroid disease, high prolactin). In teenagers think of **immaturity of the HPO axis** and bleeding disorders. First tests: **pregnancy test**, **haemoglobin**, **TSH**, **ultrasound**, and endometrial biopsy after 45 years or with risk. Treatment: **tranexamic acid** 1 g three times daily during the period, **NSAIDs** (mefenamic acid 500 mg three times a day), **COC**, **cyclic progestins** (norethisterone 5 mg three times daily for 21 days) or the **LNG-IUS**; surgery for fibroids or polyps; **iron** for anaemia. **Primary dysmenorrhoea** is due to prostaglandins and is relieved by **NSAIDs, heat and the COC**; **secondary** dysmenorrhoea is due to endometriosis, adenomyosis or PID.

**PCOS** affects about **6-13%** of women (global estimates) and is the commonest cause of anovulatory infertility. The **Rotterdam criteria** need **two of three**: (1) **oligo- or anovulation**, (2) **clinical or biochemical hyperandrogenism** (hirsutism, acne, raised testosterone), (3) **polycystic ovaries** on ultrasound (**20 or more follicles of 2-9 mm in one ovary**, or ovarian volume over 10 mL; recent guidelines allow AMH), after excluding thyroid disease, high prolactin and congenital adrenal hyperplasia. The pathway (Figure 3.1) is **insulin resistance → high insulin → ovary makes androgens and liver lowers SHBG → anovulation, acne, hirsutism**, with long-term risks of **diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidaemia, sleep apnoea, endometrial hyperplasia and depression**. Management: **weight loss of 5-10%** (it restores ovulation in many women), exercise 150 minutes a week, **COC** (for cycle control and hirsutism, with a period at least every 3 months to protect the endometrium), **metformin** 500 mg increasing to 1-1.5 g daily (insulin sensitiser), **letrozole** or **clomiphene** for ovulation, **laparoscopic ovarian drilling** in resistant cases, and screening for diabetes (OGTT) and mood.

## 3.2 Infertility

**Infertility** is the failure to conceive after **12 months of regular unprotected intercourse** (or **6 months if the woman is 35 or older**). **Primary** infertility means no previous pregnancy; **secondary** means a previous pregnancy. About **1 in 6 couples** are affected worldwide (WHO 2023). Causes: **female factors about 35%** (ovulatory disorders such as PCOS, thyroid disease and high prolactin; **tubal damage** from PID, tuberculosis or surgery; endometriosis; fibroids; cervical factors), **male factors about 30%** (low sperm count or motility, varicocele, infection, hormonal problems, drugs, heat, tobacco and alcohol), **both about 20%** and **unexplained 10-15%**. A woman's fertility falls from about 32 years and sharply after 37, so age matters for urgency. In Nepal infertility brings **stigma, family pressure, and even divorce or a second wife**, so the nurse must counsel with care and include the **husband**. The Safe Motherhood and Reproductive Health Rights Act 2075 lists infertility services as part of reproductive health care; specialised ART (IUI, IVF and ICSI) is mostly in private centres of Kathmandu and a few large hospitals.

**Investigations (done in both partners):** a full history (cycle, pelvic infection, TB, previous surgery, intercourse frequency, drugs), BMI, hirsutism, thyroid and breast examination, pelvic examination. **Semen analysis** after 2-7 days of abstinence (WHO 2021 lower reference limits: **volume 1.4 mL, concentration 16 million/mL, total number 39 million, total motility 42%, progressive motility 30%, normal forms 4%**). **Ovulation:** regular cycles, **mid-luteal progesterone** (about 7 days before the expected period; a level above 30 nmol/L suggests ovulation), ultrasound follicle tracking. **Tubes:** **hysterosalpingography (HSG)** in the first half of the cycle (after the period ends and before ovulation, with pregnancy excluded, with antibiotics if risk) or sono-HSG; **laparoscopy and dye test** when needed. Also **TSH, prolactin, AMH and FSH** by indication, and **rubella and HIV** screening.

## 3.3 Treatment of infertility

**Lifestyle first:** stop tobacco and alcohol, reach a **BMI of 19-25**, take **folic acid 400 micrograms daily** from before conception, have intercourse **every 2-3 days** through the cycle (not only on one "fertile day"), and keep the man's testes cool. **Ovulation induction:** **letrozole 2.5 mg daily on days 3-7** of a cycle (increase to 5-7.5 mg if no ovulation) is now preferred in PCOS; **clomiphene citrate 50 mg daily on days 2-6** (up to 150 mg) is cheaper and widely used. Follicles are tracked by ultrasound on days 11-14, and intercourse or **IUI** is timed when the lead follicle is about 18 mm. Side effects: hot flushes, headache, visual symptoms (stop clomiphene), **multiple pregnancy (about 5-8%)** and ovarian cysts. **Gonadotrophin injections** carry a higher risk of **ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS)**: abdominal distension, nausea, rapid weight gain, scanty urine, ascites; it needs admission, fluids and thrombosis prevention. **IUI** (washed sperm placed in the uterus) suits mild male factor and unexplained infertility. **IVF** (egg retrieval about 36 hours after the trigger, fertilisation in the laboratory, embryo transfer on day 3 or 5, pregnancy test 14 days later) and **ICSI** (one sperm injected into one egg) are used for blocked tubes, severe male factor or failed IUI. **Surgery:** tubal surgery, hysteroscopic removal of a septum or polyp, **varicocele repair**, laparoscopy for endometriosis. **Adoption** and **child-free living** are explained as honest options, under the Nepal adoption rules.

### Eight points of nursing responsibility for the infertile couple

1. **Receive the couple with respect and without blame.** Meet both partners together and apart, in a private room. Say that infertility has a medical cause in men and women and is no one's fault. This lowers shame and brings the man for testing.
2. **Take a full history and assess.** Ask about cycles, intercourse frequency, pelvic infection, TB, surgery, drugs, smoking, alcohol and work. Measure height, weight and BMI and note hirsutism or goitre. These details guide the choice of tests and show causes that can be changed.
3. **Teach the timing of intercourse and healthy living.** Advise intercourse every 2-3 days, folic acid daily, a balanced diet and 150 minutes of walking a week, no tobacco or alcohol. Overweight women who lose 5-10% of weight often ovulate again. Simple advice is the first treatment.
4. **Prepare the man for semen analysis.** Ask for 2-7 days of abstinence, collection in a sterile wide-mouth container by masturbation (no lubricant or ordinary condom), delivery to the laboratory within 1 hour kept at body temperature, and repeat after 3 months if abnormal. A wrong collection gives a false result and a wrong plan.
5. **Prepare the woman for HSG and other tests.** Fix the date after the period ends and before ovulation, confirm she is not pregnant, explain cramping, give analgesia and antibiotics as ordered, and watch for fever and pain afterwards. Right timing avoids irradiating an early pregnancy.
6. **Give drugs and teach their use.** Explain the dose and the days (for example letrozole 2.5 mg days 3-7), the scans, and warning signs such as visual change, severe abdominal swelling or rapid weight gain. Early reporting prevents OHSS and unplanned multiple pregnancy.
7. **Give emotional and social support.** Screen for stress and low mood, offer counselling, correct harmful beliefs and involve the family where the couple agrees. Infertility treatment causes anxiety, cost and guilt. Support helps the couple to continue and to cope with failure.
8. **Explain options, referral and records.** Tell them about IUI, IVF, adoption and the cost, refer to the specialist centre in time (especially above 35 years), and keep all records confidential in line with the NNC Code of Ethics. Honest information lets the couple decide freely.

## 3.4 Menopause

**Menopause** is the permanent end of menstruation, confirmed after **12 months of amenorrhoea** with no other cause; it reflects the loss of ovarian follicles. The **average age is 48-51 years** (about 48 in several Nepal studies). **Perimenopause** is the 4-8 years before, with irregular cycles and symptoms. **Premature ovarian insufficiency** is loss of function before **40 years** and early menopause is at 40-45 years. Causes of early menopause include **bilateral oophorectomy**, chemotherapy, pelvic radiotherapy, smoking and autoimmune disease.

**Symptoms and effects.** **Vasomotor** hot flushes and night sweats (in about 3 of 4 women), sleep disturbance, palpitations, irritability and low mood; **genitourinary syndrome of menopause** (vaginal dryness, dyspareunia, urgency and recurrent urinary infection); **bone loss** of 1-2% a year in the first 5-10 years, leading to **osteoporosis**, and **cardiovascular risk** rising to match men's. Prolapse and stress incontinence become common in Nepal after menopause because of repeated childbirth and heavy work.

**Management.** *Lifestyle:* weight-bearing exercise, no tobacco, less alcohol, calcium **1,000-1,200 mg** a day (milk, dahi, small fish eaten whole, green leaves, soybean) and vitamin D **800-1,000 IU** with sunlight. *Non-hormonal:* cool clothing, paracetamol, SSRIs or gabapentin for hot flushes, **vaginal moisturisers and low-dose vaginal oestrogen** for dryness, **pelvic floor exercises**. *Menopausal hormone therapy (MHT):* oestrogen (with a progestogen if the uterus is present) for **moderate to severe flushes, urogenital atrophy, premature ovarian insufficiency** (until about 51 years) and bone protection when other drugs are unsuitable; start **before 60 years or within 10 years** of menopause at the **lowest effective dose** for the **shortest time**, review yearly. **Contraindications:** **breast cancer**, **endometrial cancer**, **undiagnosed vaginal bleeding**, **active or past venous thrombosis**, **active liver disease**, recent heart attack or stroke, and pregnancy. Bone: **DEXA T-score -2.5 or lower is osteoporosis**; treat with calcium, vitamin D and **alendronate 70 mg once a week** (taken upright with a full glass of water, 30 minutes before food).

### Seven points of health education for menopause

1. **Explain menopause as a natural stage.** Say it is not a disease and that symptoms vary and fade in a few years. Fear and silence make women suffer alone. Understanding calms them.
2. **Teach the cooling and sleep measures.** Wear layers of cotton, keep the room cool, avoid spicy food, hot drinks, alcohol and tobacco before bed, and practise slow breathing. These steps reduce hot flushes without drugs.
3. **Protect the bones.** Advise calcium-rich food, 30 minutes of sunlight, walking, light weights and no smoking. Warn about slippery floors and poor light at home. Bone loss is silent, and a hip fracture can end independence.
4. **Care for the heart and weight.** Check blood pressure, sugar and cholesterol every year; advise less salt and ghee, more vegetables, fibre and fish, and a waist under 80 cm. Cardiovascular disease is the main killer of older women.
5. **Care for the vagina and bladder.** Teach pelvic floor exercises (squeeze 10 seconds, 10 times, three times a day), water-based lubricant, and regular emptying of the bladder. Local oestrogen is safe for most women. Report prolapse, leakage or burning urine early.
6. **Keep up screening.** Cervical screening (VIA or HPV test) every 5 years until 60, a yearly breast examination, mammography as advised from 40-50 years, and a doctor's review of any bleeding after menopause. Early cancers are curable.
7. **Look after mind and relationships.** Encourage talking with family, social groups, yoga and meditation, and counsel about sexual comfort and contraception for 12 months after the last period (24 months if under 50). Support from the family is the best medicine.

## 3.5 Calculations

**BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²**. **Ovulation day ≈ cycle length - 14** (the luteal phase is fairly constant at about 14 days). **Total sperm count = volume × concentration**. **Ovarian volume = 0.5 × length × width × thickness (cm)**. **Cumulative chance of pregnancy = 1 - (1 - p)ⁿ** for a chance p per cycle over n cycles. **T-score = (patient's BMD - young adult mean) ÷ SD**.

```python
h, w = 1.57, 72
bmi = w / h ** 2
print(f"Radha's BMI = 72 / 1.57 squared = {bmi:.1f} kg per m2 (overweight)")
for pct in (5, 10):
    nw = w * (1 - pct / 100)
    print(f"A {pct}% weight loss = {w * pct / 100:.1f} kg, new weight {nw:.1f} kg, new BMI {nw / h ** 2:.1f}")
tw = 25 * h ** 2
print(f"Weight for a BMI of 25 = {tw:.1f} kg, a loss of {w - tw:.1f} kg")
print(f"Ovulation day: a 35-day cycle ovulates near day {35 - 14}; a 60-day cycle near day {60 - 14}; a 28-day cycle near day {28 - 14}")
vol, conc = 3.0, 10
print(f"Dilip: volume {vol} mL x concentration {conc} million per mL = {vol * conc:.0f} million total sperm, below the WHO 2021 limit of 39 million")
print(f"Ovarian volume = 0.5 x 4.5 x 3.0 x 2.5 = {0.5 * 4.5 * 3.0 * 2.5:.1f} mL, above the 10 mL PCOS cut-off")
print(f"Chance of pregnancy at 15% per cycle over 12 cycles = {(1 - 0.85 ** 12) * 100:.1f}%; at 10% per IUI over 6 cycles = {(1 - 0.9 ** 6) * 100:.1f}%")
print(f"Letrozole 2.5 mg on days 3 to 7 = {2.5 * 5:.1f} mg per cycle; clomiphene 50 mg on days 2 to 6 = {50 * 5} mg per cycle")
t = (0.62 - 1.00) / 0.12
print(f"T-score = (0.62 - 1.00) / 0.12 = {t:.1f}, which is osteoporosis (-2.5 or lower)")
print(f"Calcium gap = 1,200 mg needed - 700 mg in food = {1200 - 700} mg to be added")
```

## Formula and fact sheet

- **Cycle** 21-35 days; bleeding 2-7 days; loss under 80 mL. **Amenorrhoea:** primary none by 15 years (13 without breast development); secondary 3 months. **PALM-COEIN** causes of AUB.
- **PCOS (Rotterdam):** 2 of 3: oligo- or anovulation, hyperandrogenism, polycystic ovaries (20 or more follicles per ovary or volume over 10 mL). Weight loss 5-10%, COC, metformin, letrozole.
- **Infertility:** 12 months (6 months if 35 or older). Causes: female 35%, male 30%, both 20%, unexplained 10-15%. **WHO 2021 semen limits:** 1.4 mL, 16 million/mL, 39 million total, 42% motile, 30% progressive, 4% normal forms.
- **Letrozole** 2.5 mg days 3-7; **clomiphene** 50 mg days 2-6; **folic acid** 400 micrograms; HSG after the period and before ovulation.
- **Menopause:** 12 months without a period; age 48-51; **T-score -2.5 or less** osteoporosis; calcium 1,000-1,200 mg and vitamin D 800-1,000 IU; **MHT contraindicated** in breast cancer, undiagnosed bleeding, VTE, active liver disease.

## Chapter summary

- Normal and abnormal bleeding are defined by cycle length, duration and volume; PALM-COEIN classifies causes; PCOS needs 2 of 3 Rotterdam criteria and is treated first with weight loss.
- Infertility is no pregnancy after 12 months (6 months from age 35), and both partners are tested; semen analysis, ovulation tests and HSG are the core tests.
- Ovulation induction with letrozole or clomiphene, then IUI and IVF or ICSI, form the ladder; OHSS and multiple pregnancy are the main risks.
- The nurse protects the couple from stigma, teaches timing, healthy living and test preparation, and records confidentially.
- Menopause is confirmed after 12 months of amenorrhoea; care includes lifestyle, calcium and vitamin D, screening, local oestrogen and selected MHT.

**7. (b).** 1,200 - 750 = 450 mg. A larger dose adds cost and constipation without benefit.
**8. (c).** Hormone-sensitive cancer makes MHT unsafe. Hot flushes, premature ovarian insufficiency and vaginal dryness are indications.

**Short answer (3 marks): List the causes of infertility in the female.** Ovulatory disorders (PCOS, thyroid disease, high prolactin, premature ovarian insufficiency); tubal damage after PID, tuberculosis or surgery; uterine causes (fibroids, polyps, septum, adhesions); endometriosis; and cervical factors. Age over 35 and obesity or underweight also lower fertility. Both partners must be tested, because about 30% of cases are male and 20% involve both.

**Short answer (3 marks): State the indications and contraindications of menopausal hormone therapy.** Indications are moderate to severe vasomotor symptoms, genitourinary syndrome of menopause, premature ovarian insufficiency until about 51 years and bone protection when other drugs are unsuitable. Contraindications are breast or endometrial cancer, undiagnosed vaginal bleeding, active or past venous thrombosis, active liver disease and recent heart attack or stroke. The woman starts before 60 years or within 10 years of menopause at the lowest effective dose and is reviewed every year.

**Medium answer (5 marks): Explain the pathophysiology and management of PCOS (Figure 3.1).** PCOS begins with insulin resistance, often with obesity. High insulin and raised LH make the ovary produce androgen, and the liver makes less SHBG, so free testosterone rises. Follicles stop at 2-9 mm and no egg is released, which causes oligomenorrhoea or amenorrhoea, acne, hirsutism and infertility. The diagnosis needs two of three Rotterdam criteria after excluding thyroid disease, high prolactin and congenital adrenal hyperplasia. Management is first a 5-10% weight loss with 150 minutes of exercise a week. A combined pill gives cycle control and lowers androgen effects; metformin improves insulin sensitivity; letrozole 2.5 mg on days 3-7 or clomiphene 50 mg on days 2-6 induces ovulation; laparoscopic ovarian drilling is for resistant cases. The nurse teaches diet, checks blood sugar, blood pressure and mood yearly and warns that a period at least every 3 months is needed to protect the endometrium.

**Long answer (10 marks): Radha Thapa, 32, married for 4 years, has cycles of 35-60 days, acne, facial hair, weight 72 kg and height 1.57 m. Her husband's semen has a volume of 3.0 mL and a concentration of 10 million/mL. Assess the couple, explain the diagnosis, and write the nursing care plan.**

*Calculations.* Radha's **BMI is 29.2 kg/m²** (overweight). A **5% loss (3.6 kg)** gives 68.4 kg and a BMI of 27.7; a **10% loss (7.2 kg)** gives 64.8 kg and a BMI of 26.3. A BMI of 25 would need 61.6 kg, a 10.4 kg loss. With a 35-day cycle she ovulates near **day 21 at best**, and with a 60-day cycle near day 46, so timed intercourse is unreliable. Dilip's **total sperm count is 3.0 × 10 = 30 million**, below the **WHO 2021 limit of 39 million**, and his concentration of 10 million/mL is below 16; the test is repeated after 3 months with proper collection. Radha meets **three of three Rotterdam criteria** (anovulation, hyperandrogenism and, on scan, an ovarian volume of 16.9 mL), so the diagnosis is **PCOS with a mild male factor**. The cumulative chance of pregnancy at 15% per cycle over 12 cycles is about 86%, but her anovulation lowers that sharply, so treatment is needed.

*Management.* The nurse sees both partners kindly, removes blame, and explains that infertility is a medical condition shared by the couple. She teaches weight loss of 5-10% by diet (less rice and sweets, more vegetables and lentils) and 150 minutes of walking weekly, folic acid 400 micrograms, no tobacco or alcohol for Dilip, loose underwear and avoiding hot baths. Blood sugar (OGTT), TSH, prolactin and an HSG after her next period are arranged. Treatment after counselling is **letrozole 2.5 mg on days 3-7 (12.5 mg per cycle)** with ultrasound on day 12-14 and timed intercourse every 2 days around the lead follicle; metformin may be added. The nurse explains side effects, multiple pregnancy and OHSS signs, gives written dates, and refers to IUI or IVF after 3-6 cycles or sooner if Dilip's repeat test remains abnormal. She screens for depression, addresses the family pressure with a joint family session where the couple agrees, and keeps the records confidential.
