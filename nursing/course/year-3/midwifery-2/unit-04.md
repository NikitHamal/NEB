## 4.1 Abnormal progress of labour

Normal labour follows the **Labour Care Guide (LCG)** thresholds from Midwifery I (active phase from 5 cm; thresholds 5 cm 6 h, 6 cm 5 h, 7 cm 3 h, 8 cm 2.5 h, 9 cm 2 h). When a threshold is passed, the midwife asks **the three Ps**: **Power** (weak contractions: fewer than 3 in 10 minutes, each under 40 seconds), **Passenger** (big baby, malposition, malpresentation) and **Passage** (small or deformed pelvis, cervical or pelvic tumour). **Slow progress** with weak contractions in a woman with a normal pelvis is treated with **amniotomy, mobilisation, hydration and then oxytocin** (if no obstruction); slow progress with a **caput, moulding and a tight head** means **obstruction**, and oxytocin is dangerous.

| Abnormal pattern | Features | First action |
|---|---|---|
| **Prolonged latent phase** | Contractions for over 8-12 h with cervix under 5 cm | Rest, fluids, analgesia; review in 4 h; no early intervention |
| **Slow active phase** | LCG threshold passed (e.g. 7 cm for 3 h) | Reassess three Ps; amniotomy; oxytocin only if powers are weak and no obstruction |
| **Hypotonic uterine action** | Weak, infrequent contractions | Exclude obstruction; oxytocin infusion |
| **Hypertonic or incoordinate action** | Painful, frequent, ineffective contractions; cervix does not dilate | Analgesia, rest, hydration; exclude malposition |
| **Prolonged second stage** | Over 3 h (first labour), 2 h (later labours) | Assess descent; assisted birth or caesarean |
| **Precipitate labour** | Birth within 3 h of onset | Prepare for tears, PPH and a cold baby |

## 4.2 Malpresentation and malposition

| Condition | Definition | Key risks | Management |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Occipito-posterior (OP)** | Occiput towards the sacrum | Long labour, backache, deflexed head | Upright or hands-and-knees, patience; most rotate; assisted birth or CS if stuck |
| **Breech** | Buttocks or feet first; 3-4% at term | Cord prolapse, head entrapment, birth asphyxia | **ECV at 36-37 weeks**, planned CS or skilled vaginal breech birth |
| **Face** | Head extended, chin leads (mento-anterior can deliver) | Facial oedema, mento-posterior is obstructed | Mento-anterior: vaginal; mento-posterior: CS |
| **Brow** | Partially extended head (diameter 13.5 cm) | **Obstructed** unless it flexes | CS |
| **Transverse or oblique lie** | Fetal long axis across the mother | **Cord prolapse, obstructed labour, uterine rupture** | CS (ECV in early labour only by an expert) |
| **Compound** | Hand or foot next to the head | Cord prolapse | Observe; CS if no progress |

**Breech.** Types: **frank** (hips flexed, knees extended: about 65%), **complete** (hips and knees flexed, about 25%) and **footling** (one or both feet below the buttocks, about 10%). Causes include prematurity, twins, polyhydramnios, praevia, fundal fibroids and uterine anomalies. **External cephalic version (ECV)** at 36-37 weeks (by a trained doctor with a scan, CTG and theatre standby; anti-D if Rh negative) succeeds in about 40-60% of cases. Contraindications: placenta praevia, APH, ruptured membranes, previous CS (relative), multiple pregnancy, fetal anomaly, abnormal CTG and hypertension. For **vaginal breech birth** by a skilled birth attendant: allow the baby to deliver to the umbilicus **unaided ("hands off the breech")**, then deliver the legs (Løvset for arms, **Mauriceau-Smellie-Veit** manoeuvre for the head with flexion by two fingers on the maxilla) and **never pull**. The "**head-to-body**" interval must be short (under 5 minutes).

## 4.3 Obstructed labour

**Obstructed labour** is failure of the presenting part to descend despite **strong uterine contractions**, because of a **mechanical barrier**. It causes about **8% of maternal deaths worldwide** and is the leading cause of **obstetric fistula**; in Nepal it is a prime example of the "three delays" (delay in deciding, delay in reaching a facility, delay in receiving care). **Causes:** **cephalopelvic disproportion (CPD)** (small pelvis in a short woman under 145 cm, or a macrosomic baby), **malpresentation** (brow, mento-posterior face, shoulder), **fetal anomaly** (hydrocephalus), and **pelvic or cervical tumours**.

**Recognition (maternal signs):** a long labour with a **distressed, dehydrated, febrile woman**; **rapid pulse**, dry tongue and ketones in urine; **tender, tightly contracted uterus** with a **retraction ring (Bandl's ring)** rising towards the umbilicus; **oedematous cervix and vulva**; **large caput and severe moulding**; **head high** and not engaged; **hot, dry vagina** with scanty, meconium-stained or foul fluid; and a **distended bladder**. **Fetal signs:** distress and later death.

**Complications:** **uterine rupture** (sudden severe pain, loss of contractions, bleeding and shock, fetal parts under the skin), **obstetric fistula** (vesicovaginal or rectovaginal; continuous leakage after pressure necrosis), **sepsis**, **PPH** (atony after exhaustion), **fetal death or asphyxia**, **neurological injury (foot-drop)** and **chorioamnionitis**. A woman with an obstetric fistula is often abandoned by her family, so counselling and surgical repair at a fistula centre are vital.

**Management:** *resuscitate* (IV fluids Ringer's lactate 1 L fast, then 125 mL/h; analgesia; antibiotics: **ampicillin 2 g IV every 6 hours + gentamicin 5 mg/kg IV once daily + metronidazole 500 mg IV every 8 hours**); *catheterise* (bladder emptied and kept for 10-14 days after a long obstruction to help fistula prevention); *deliver* by **caesarean section** if the baby is alive; **craniotomy or destructive delivery** only if the baby is dead and the doctor is skilled; then a **uterotonic and PPH watch**. *Prevention:* use the LCG from 5 cm, identify **short women** and send them to a CEONC before labour, transport early, and run **Aama** free transport and delivery support.

### Eight points of nursing care in obstructed labour

1. **Recognise the signs early and call for help.** A rising pulse, high temperature, Bandl's ring, oedematous cervix and large caput all warn of obstruction. Tell the doctor at once. Do not give oxytocin.
2. **Resuscitate with fluids and oxygen.** Insert two IV lines, send blood for group, Hb and cross-match, and run Ringer's lactate. A woman who has laboured long is dehydrated and ketotic, and she needs 2-3 litres over several hours. Oxygen is given if the fetus is distressed.
3. **Give antibiotics and analgesia.** Start ampicillin, gentamicin and metronidazole IV as ordered, because infection is already likely. Give analgesia (for example IV paracetamol or morphine as prescribed) and reassure her. Pain and fear raise her pulse.
4. **Insert a catheter and measure urine.** The bladder is often swollen and pressed by the head. A Foley catheter relieves it, allows hourly urine charting (30 mL/h or more) and protects against fistula. Record colour: blood in the urine means bladder injury.
5. **Monitor mother and baby closely.** Check pulse, BP, temperature, respiration, contractions and the retraction ring every 15-30 minutes. Mark the ring height on the abdomen. Listen to the fetal heart every 15 minutes.
6. **Prepare for caesarean section.** Explain and take consent, shave if needed, give nil by mouth, cross-match 2 units, and call the anaesthetist and the newborn team. Warm the theatre and the resuscitation corner.
7. **Watch for rupture and PPH.** Sudden collapse, severe pain, vaginal bleeding or a flattening of the contraction pattern means rupture, which needs immediate laparotomy. After birth give oxytocin, massage the uterus and measure blood loss. Keep her in high dependency for 24 hours.
8. **Give long-term care and support.** Keep the catheter for 10-14 days if obstruction was prolonged, with a high fluid intake. Support grieving if the baby has died, and explain danger signs, family planning and the need to give birth in a CEONC next time. Refer any woman who leaks urine to a fistula centre, and fill the MDR or PDR form where applicable.

## 4.4 Shoulder dystocia and cord prolapse

**Shoulder dystocia** (1 in 150 births) is failure of the shoulders to deliver after the head, with the "turtle sign" (the head retracts against the perineum). **Risk factors:** macrosomia (over 4 kg; the risk rises sharply with diabetes), previous shoulder dystocia, maternal obesity, prolonged second stage and assisted delivery. It is unpredictable in half the cases, so every birth attendant must know the **HELPERR** drill (Figure 4.2) and practise it. Complications: **brachial plexus injury (Erb's palsy)**, clavicle or humerus fracture, **hypoxic injury** and PPH. **Do not pull the head, do not push the fundus, and do not cut the cord early.**

**Cord prolapse** is descent of the cord beside or ahead of the presenting part after rupture of membranes (about 1 in 300 births; breech, transverse lie, polyhydramnios, preterm and high presenting part are the risk factors). The cord is compressed and the baby may die within minutes. **Action:** call for help; **relieve pressure** by pushing the presenting part up with two fingers in the vagina (do not handle the cord, which causes spasm); place the mother in the **knee-chest or exaggerated Sims position**; **fill the bladder with 500-750 mL of saline** through a catheter; and arrange the **fastest route to birth, usually caesarean section**. If fully dilated and the head is low, an assisted vaginal birth is faster.

## 4.5 Induction of labour

**Induction** is starting labour artificially when the risks of continuing pregnancy exceed the risks of birth. **Indications:** post-term (41 weeks or more), **pre-eclampsia**, term rupture of membranes without labour, diabetes, fetal growth restriction, reduced fetal movements, intrauterine death. **Contraindications:** placenta or vasa praevia, transverse lie, **cord prolapse**, prior classical caesarean or two or more caesareans (relative for misoprostol), active genital herpes, pelvic obstruction.

**Methods** (WHO 2022): check the **Bishop score** (8 or more is favourable; Midwifery I). *Unfavourable cervix:* **Foley catheter** (30 mL balloon, inserted beyond the internal os, left 12-24 hours) or **misoprostol 25 mcg vaginally every 4-6 hours** (or **25 mcg orally every 2 hours**); *favourable cervix:* **amniotomy** and **oxytocin**. **Oxytocin ladder (WHO):** 5 IU in 500 mL (10 mU/mL); start at **2 mU/min** and double every 30 minutes (2, 4, 8, 16, 32 mU/min) until 3-4 contractions in 10 minutes are achieved. **Hyperstimulation** (more than 5 contractions in 10 minutes, or a contraction over 2 minutes with FHR change) needs the infusion stopped, left lateral position, oxygen and, if needed, terbutaline 0.25 mg SC.

## 4.6 Instrumental (assisted) vaginal birth

**Indications:** prolonged second stage, **fetal distress in the second stage**, maternal exhaustion, and to avoid pushing in cardiac disease or severe hypertension. **Prerequisites:** the cervix is fully dilated, membranes are ruptured, the head is **engaged** (not more than one-fifth palpable above the pelvic brim, station at or below the ischial spines), the **position is known**, the bladder is empty, analgesia and consent are given, and an operator who can proceed to **caesarean** if it fails is present.

| Feature | **Vacuum (ventouse)** | **Forceps** |
|---|---|---|
| Action | Suction cup on the flexion point 3 cm in front of the posterior fontanelle; traction with contractions | Two blades cradle the head; traction in the line of the pelvic curve |
| Limits | Up to 3 pulls, 2 pop-offs, under 20 minutes, not under 34 weeks | Rotational use needs expertise |
| Baby risks | **Chignon, cephalhaematoma, subgaleal haemorrhage, retinal bleeding** | Facial nerve palsy, skull fracture, scalp marks |
| Mother risks | Fewer perineal tears | More **3rd and 4th degree tears** and vaginal trauma |
| Nepal | Metallic or soft cup at CEONC and some BEONC (signal function: assisted vaginal delivery) | CEONC |

## 4.7 Caesarean section

**Caesarean section (CS)** is birth through an abdominal and uterine incision (usually a **lower-segment transverse** incision). WHO says rates above about **10-15%** do not reduce maternal and newborn deaths, and unnecessary CS adds risks (infection, haemorrhage, placenta accreta and uterine rupture in later pregnancies). In Nepal the rate has risen quickly, is highest in urban and private hospitals, and is **lowest where women need it most**, in remote districts: a sign of both over-use and under-access (NDHS 2022). A **CEONC** offers CS and blood transfusion; a **BEONC** provides the seven signal functions below.

### Seven indications for caesarean section

1. **Obstructed labour or CPD.** If the head will not descend despite good contractions, a vaginal birth would crush the pelvic tissues and rupture the uterus. CS is the only safe way. The baby is born alive if the section is done in time.
2. **Fetal distress.** A heart rate under 110 or over 160, late decelerations, or thick meconium with abnormal trace means poor oxygen supply. CS is used when a vaginal birth cannot be achieved within minutes. Every minute of delay increases the risk of brain damage.
3. **Placenta praevia and severe antepartum haemorrhage.** A placenta over the cervix blocks the baby's way and bleeds when the cervix opens. CS is done at 36-37 weeks, or immediately for heavy bleeding or shock. Abruption with fetal distress also needs it.
4. **Malpresentation.** A transverse lie, brow presentation and mento-posterior face cannot be born vaginally. A breech needs CS after a failed ECV or if the pelvis is small. Cord prolapse needs CS in minutes.
5. **Severe pre-eclampsia, eclampsia or HELLP with an unfavourable cervix.** The woman must be delivered after stabilisation, and induction may take too long. CS gives a fast, controlled birth. MgSO4 is continued during and after the operation.
6. **Previous uterine scars.** Two or more previous caesareans, a classical incision or a myomectomy through the cavity carry a risk of scar rupture. A planned repeat CS is done at 38-39 weeks. A woman with one previous lower-segment scar may attempt a trial of labour in a CEONC.
7. **Maternal conditions and failed induction.** Active genital herpes, some heart or eye conditions, a mass blocking the pelvis, or failure of induction with ruptured membranes are indications. Fetal anomalies, such as large hydrocephalus, also need CS. The woman gives informed consent after explanation of the risks.

**Nursing care.** *Before:* explain, take consent, nil by mouth (antacid), catheter, IV line, cross-match, shave if needed, **antibiotic prophylaxis (cefazolin 1-2 g IV within 60 minutes before the incision, WHO)**, fetal heart check and theatre checklist. *After:* position flat or tilted with the head slightly raised, vitals every 15 minutes for 2 hours then hourly, **oxytocin infusion**, fundal and lochia checks, **urine output (catheter out at 12-24 h)**, analgesia (paracetamol, NSAID), early oral fluids, **mobilise at 6-12 hours**, thromboprophylaxis if risk, early breastfeeding, wound care and counselling about the next birth (CEONC, spacing 18-24 months).

## Formula and fact sheet

- **LCG thresholds** (5 cm 6 h, 6 cm 5 h, 7 cm 3 h, 8 cm 2.5 h, 9 cm 2 h). **Breech:** frank 65%, complete 25%, footling 10%; ECV at 36-37 weeks; never pull. **Shoulder dystocia:** HELPERR; McRoberts, suprapubic pressure, posterior arm; deliver in 5 min.
- **Obstructed labour:** Bandl's ring, oedematous cervix, caput, high head; **do not give oxytocin**; fluids, ampicillin 2 g q6h + gentamicin 5 mg/kg + metronidazole 500 mg q8h; CS; catheter 10-14 days.
- **Cord prolapse:** push the part up, knee-chest, fill bladder 500-750 mL, CS. **Induction:** Foley 30 mL; misoprostol 25 mcg vaginal 4-6 h or oral 2 h; oxytocin 2 mU/min doubling every 30 min to 32.
- **Oxytocin 5 IU in 500 mL** = 10 mU/mL; mL/h = mU/min ÷ 10 × 60. **Johnson EFW** = (SFH - n) × 155 (n = 13, 12, 11 by station). **Vacuum:** 3 pulls, 2 pop-offs, 20 min.
- **BEONC** seven signal functions; **CEONC** adds CS and blood transfusion. **CS:** cefazolin 1-2 g IV within 60 min before the incision; mobilise at 6-12 h.

## Chapter summary

- Abnormal labour is detected by the LCG thresholds; ask power, passenger and passage, and never give oxytocin in obstruction.
- Breech, brow, face and transverse lies need ECV, CS or a skilled breech birth; shoulder dystocia is managed with the HELPERR drill and cord prolapse by relieving pressure and a fast CS.
- Obstructed labour causes rupture, fistula, sepsis and death; resuscitate, give antibiotics, catheterise and deliver by caesarean.
- Induction uses Foley, misoprostol or oxytocin by a titrated ladder; instrumental birth needs full dilatation and an engaged head.
- Caesarean is for seven main indications; CEONC provides CS and blood, and BEONC the seven signal functions, so early referral saves lives.

**MCQs (with reasons)**

1. Oxytocin 5 IU in 500 mL (10 mU/mL) at 8 mU/min runs at: (a) 24 mL/h (b) 48 mL/h (c) 80 mL/h (d) 96 mL/h.
2. SFH 36 cm with the head at the ischial spines gives a Johnson estimated fetal weight of: (a) 3,255 g (b) 3,720 g (c) 3,875 g (d) 4,030 g.
3. Gentamicin 5 mg/kg for a 60 kg woman is: (a) 120 mg (b) 240 mg (c) 300 mg (d) 600 mg.
4. Misoprostol 200 mcg dissolved in 200 mL of water. The volume that gives 25 mcg is: (a) 5 mL (b) 10 mL (c) 25 mL (d) 50 mL.
5. Starting at 2 mU/min and doubling every 30 minutes, the maximum 32 mU/min is reached after: (a) 60 min (b) 90 min (c) 120 min (d) 150 min.

::: note Answers and rationales
**1. (b).** 8 ÷ 10 = 0.8 mL/min × 60 = 48 mL/h. 24 mL/h is the 4 mU/min rate and 96 mL/h the 16 mU/min rate.
**2. (b).** (36 - 12) × 155 = 3,720 g. Using n = 13 (head above the spines) gives 3,565 g, and 4,030 g would need an SFH of 38 cm.
**3. (c).** 5 × 60 = 300 mg once daily; 120 mg is 2 mg/kg.
**4. (c).** The solution has 1 mcg/mL, so 25 mcg is 25 mL. Discard the rest after use.
**5. (c).** Steps 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 are four doublings, 4 × 30 = 120 minutes after the start. 150 minutes counts one extra step.
:::

**Short answer (3 marks): Write the steps of management of cord prolapse.** Call for help. Relieve pressure on the cord by lifting the presenting part with two fingers in the vagina and avoid handling the cord. Place the woman in the knee-chest or exaggerated Sims position. Fill the bladder with 500-750 mL of saline through a catheter. Listen to the fetal heart and arrange the fastest birth, usually emergency caesarean section (assisted vaginal birth if fully dilated and the head is low).

**Medium answer (5 marks): Describe the management of shoulder dystocia (Figure 4.2).** Shoulder dystocia is entrapment of the anterior shoulder behind the symphysis after the head is born (turtle sign). Do not pull the head or push the fundus. Use **HELPERR**: **H** call for help and note the time; **E** evaluate for episiotomy; **L** legs, **McRoberts** position (flex the hips onto the abdomen); **P** suprapubic pressure from the side; **E** enter manoeuvres (Rubin, Woods screw, delivery of the posterior arm); **R** roll onto hands and knees; then advanced measures at a CEONC. Deliver within 5 minutes, document the head-to-body interval and examine the baby for brachial plexus injury and fractures. Prevent PPH with active management and counsel the woman for the next birth (planned CS if macrosomic or earlier dystocia).

**Long answer (10 marks): Sita Magar, 19, primigravida, 144 cm and 45 kg, has been in labour for 36 hours. T 38.4 °C, pulse 120, BP 90/60, Bandl's ring, cervix 7 cm swollen, caput 3, SFH 38 cm, FHR 150. Calculate and write the nursing care plan.**

*Calculations.* Her **shock index is 1.33** (120 ÷ 90), a sign of major compromise. Johnson's estimated fetal weight (head above the spines) is (38 - 13) × 155 = **3,875 g**. Gentamicin 5 mg/kg is **225 mg** once daily; ampicillin 2 g every 6 hours is 8 g a day; Ringer's lactate at 125 mL/h with a 15 drops/mL set is 31 drops/min.

*Management.* The diagnosis is **obstructed labour from CPD** (short stature, high head, ring, caput) with **early sepsis and dehydration**. The midwife calls the obstetrician, inserts two IV lines, sends blood for Hb, group, cross-match and malaria test and starts Ringer's lactate. She gives **ampicillin 2 g, gentamicin 225 mg and metronidazole 500 mg IV**, paracetamol for fever, catheterises, withholds oxytocin and keeps her nil by mouth. The doctor decides on an **emergency caesarean**. The fetal heart is monitored every 15 minutes; the ring is marked on the abdomen. After the operation oxytocin is given, blood loss and the uterus are monitored, the catheter is kept for 10-14 days to protect the bladder, antibiotics continue, and she is counselled on spacing (18-24 months) and giving birth in a CEONC next time. If the baby is dead, a decision on delivery is taken by the doctor; the PDR form (within 72 hours) and grief support follow.
