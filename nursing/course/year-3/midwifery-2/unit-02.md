## 2.1 Definition and size of the problem

**Postpartum haemorrhage** was long defined as blood loss of **500 mL or more** after vaginal birth (1,000 mL after caesarean). The **WHO, FIGO and ICM consolidated guideline of October 2025** now recommends **earlier diagnosis**: PPH is diagnosed when measured loss is **500 mL or more, or 300 mL or more with any sign of haemodynamic instability** (fast pulse, falling BP). Loss of **1,000 mL or more is severe PPH**. **Primary (immediate) PPH** occurs within **24 hours** of birth; **secondary (late) PPH** occurs from **24 hours to 12 weeks**. Women die because blood loss is **underestimated by eye** (which can miss half), so WHO recommends **objective measurement with a calibrated blood-collection drape**, or weighing (1 g of blood is about 1 mL).

Globally about **45,000 women die from PPH each year**, and PPH is the commonest cause of death in the first 24 hours. In Nepal haemorrhage contributes 26% of maternal deaths (NMMS 2021; MMR 151), most through **delays in recognition, in reaching the CEONC and in receiving blood**. A healthy woman has a blood volume of 100 mL/kg at term, an increased clotting factor level, and about 500-800 mL of blood flowing to the placental bed **every minute**; the uterus must contract to close it.

### Seven points of PPH prevention (antenatal, intrapartum and community)

1. **Find and treat anaemia early.** A woman with haemoglobin under 7 g/dL cannot afford to lose even 500 mL. Give iron-folic acid (180 tablets in Nepal's programme), deworm after the first trimester and treat malaria. Transfuse or refer if severe anaemia remains near term.
2. **Identify the woman at risk and plan the place of birth.** Previous PPH, praevia, twins, big baby, grand multiparity and hypertensive disease raise risk. Advise birth in a BEONC or CEONC and not at home. Use the birth preparedness plan (money, transport, blood donor, escort).
3. **Skilled birth attendant at every birth.** In Nepal 80% of births are with an SBA (NDHS 2022); the other 20% have much higher risk of delay. Train FCHVs and community members to call and escort mothers early. Use Aama free delivery and transport support.
4. **Active management of the third stage (AMTSL) for every woman.** Oxytocin 10 IU IM within 1 minute of birth, delayed cord clamping and controlled cord traction by a skilled hand are the key steps. WHO 2025 recommends **one quality-assured uterotonic: oxytocin (IM or IV) first choice, heat-stable carbetocin if the cold chain is uncertain, misoprostol if neither is available**. Ergometrine is not recommended for prevention.
5. **Community-based misoprostol where there is no SBA.** In some Nepal districts, FCHVs and health workers provide **misoprostol 600 mcg (3 tablets of 200 mcg) orally** after a home birth. The woman and family must be counselled on timing, side effects (shivering, fever) and that it is for after the baby is born. Follow with referral if bleeding continues.
6. **Avoid harmful practices.** Do not use fundal pressure, do not pull on the cord without counter-traction, do not give oxytocin before the baby is delivered, and do not do unnecessary episiotomy. Pushing the fundus before separation can cause inversion. Empty the bladder after birth, since a full bladder prevents contraction.
7. **Observe every woman for at least 2 hours and teach danger signs.** Check the uterus, bleeding, pulse and BP every 15 minutes for the first two hours and keep her in the facility for 24 hours. Teach her and her family to massage the uterus and to call at once for soaked pads, dizziness or a bleeding clot as big as a lemon.

## 2.3 Treating PPH: the MOTIVE bundle and the escalation ladder

PPH kills through **delay and one-at-a-time care**. The 2025 WHO-FIGO-ICM guideline (51 recommendations) validates the **MOTIVE bundle**, tested in the E-MOTIVE trial in four countries, where a bundle with a calibrated drape for early detection **cut severe PPH or death by more than half**. When PPH is diagnosed, the team does **all six elements at once**: **M**assage of the uterus, **O**xytocic drug, **T**ranexamic acid, **IV** fluids, **E**xamination of the genital tract, and **E**scalation if bleeding continues.

### Second-line drugs and doses (use the hospital protocol)

| Drug | Dose and route | Cautions |
|---|---|---|
| **Oxytocin** | 10 IU IM or slow IV; **infusion 20 IU in 1 L** at 60 drops/min | Avoid rapid undiluted IV push (hypotension) |
| **Ergometrine** | 0.2 mg IM | **Not** in hypertension, pre-eclampsia or heart disease |
| **Carboprost (15-methyl PGF2α)** | 250 mcg IM, repeat every 15 min, **maximum 8 doses (2 mg)** | **Not** in asthma; vomiting, diarrhoea, bronchospasm |
| **Misoprostol** | 800 mcg sublingual (4 tablets of 200 mcg) | Shivering, fever; useful where there is no cold chain |
| **Tranexamic acid** | 1 g IV over 10 min, second gram if bleeding continues after 30 min | Give within 3 hours of birth |

If bleeding continues after the bundle, climb the **escalation ladder** without waiting for the full range of drugs: **bimanual uterine compression** (one fist in the vagina, the other hand on the abdomen), **aortic compression**, **uterine balloon tamponade** (a condom, Foley catheter or Bakri balloon filled with 300-500 mL of saline), the **NASG** (to keep blood in the core while transferring), then surgery: **compression suture (B-Lynch)**, **uterine and internal iliac artery ligation** and finally **hysterectomy**.

### Eight points of nursing care during postpartum haemorrhage

1. **Call for help and say the word "PPH".** One midwife cannot do the bundle alone. Shout for the nurse, doctor and anaesthetist, and start a clock and a recorder. A named team leader prevents confusion.
2. **Massage the uterus at once.** Place one hand above the symphysis and rub the fundus in circles until it is firm. Express clots, because they stop contraction. Repeat every 15 minutes for 2 hours, and teach the mother to do it.
3. **Give the uterotonic and tranexamic acid.** Oxytocin 10 IU IM or slow IV, then an infusion of 20 IU in 1 L. Give TXA 1 g slow over 10 minutes within 3 hours. Always check the dose, route and time with a second nurse and record.
4. **Start two IV lines and replace volume.** Use 16-18 G cannulae, send blood for group, cross-match, Hb and clotting, and give warmed Ringer's lactate with a **3:1 rule** (3 mL for every 1 mL lost). Add blood early when the shock index is above 1. Warm the woman with blankets because cold worsens clotting.
5. **Empty the bladder and examine.** A catheter keeps urine measured (aim at least 30 mL/h) and lets the uterus contract. Look at the placenta for missing pieces and inspect the cervix, vagina and perineum by light. Suture tears or call the doctor.
6. **Measure loss and watch the vital signs.** Use a calibrated drape or weigh pads (1 g is about 1 mL). Record pulse, BP, respiration, oxygen saturation, skin colour and level of consciousness every 5-15 minutes, and calculate the shock index. Give oxygen 10 L/min by mask.
7. **Escalate and prepare for theatre or transfer.** If bleeding continues, apply bimanual compression, call for the balloon, apply the NASG and prepare consent, blood and transport. Send a nurse escort with fluids, oxygen and a written summary. Telephone the receiving CEONC.
8. **Support the woman and family, and document.** Tell the woman what is happening in plain Nepali, hold her hand and respect her dignity. After the crisis, keep her in high dependency, check haemoglobin and give iron. Record the timeline, and if a death occurs, complete the **MDR form within 24 hours** and notify the district MPDSR focal point (review within 72 hours).

## 2.4 Other problems of the third stage

**Retained placenta.** The placenta is **retained** when not delivered within **30 minutes** of birth with active management (60 minutes with physiological management). Do **one** gentle controlled cord traction, empty the bladder, put the baby to the breast, set up an IV line and check the bleeding. WHO 2025 advises a uterotonic for retained placenta **only if PPH is present** and gives a **single dose of prophylactic antibiotic** if **manual removal** is needed (done by a trained doctor or midwife under anaesthesia). A bulky uterus after delivery suggests retained fragments or **accreta**.

**Uterine inversion** (rare, about 1 in 3,000) is the turning inside out of the uterus, usually from **fundal pressure or cord traction before separation**. Signs: profound shock out of proportion to blood loss, a red mass at the vulva (Plate 2.1b) and no fundus felt abdominally. **Stop oxytocin, call for help, do not remove the placenta, and replace the uterus at once** with the **Johnson manoeuvre** (the palm pushes the fundus through the cervix toward the umbilicus, with the hand left in until a contraction). If this fails, use hydrostatic (O'Sullivan) replacement or surgery, then give a uterotonic.

**Genital tract trauma.** Check every woman for tears after birth.

| Degree | Structures | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| **First** | Skin and vaginal mucosa | Suture if bleeding, or leave |
| **Second** | Perineal muscles | Midwife repairs with absorbable suture (e.g. 2-0 polyglactin) |
| **3a / 3b / 3c** | External anal sphincter under 50% / over 50% / plus internal sphincter | Doctor repairs in theatre; antibiotics |
| **Fourth** | Anal mucosa also torn | Doctor repairs; antibiotics and stool softener |

**Secondary PPH** (24 hours to 12 weeks) is usually due to **retained products or endometritis**: foul-smelling lochia, fever, tender uterus and heavy bleeding. Give broad-spectrum antibiotics (for example ampicillin, gentamicin and metronidazole), a uterotonic, and evacuate retained tissue after antibiotics have begun. **Haemorrhagic shock** is graded by loss: class I under 15% (pulse under 100); class II 15-30% (pulse 100-120); class III 30-40% (pulse 120-140, low BP); class IV over 40% (collapse).

## 2.5 Calculations in PPH

**Measured loss** = drape volume + (wet pads weight - dry pads weight) in grams (1 g is about 1 mL). The **shock index** is pulse ÷ systolic BP (1.0 or more is major haemorrhage). **Blood volume** is 100 mL/kg at term. **Fluid replacement** is **3 mL of crystalloid for each 1 mL of blood lost**. An **infusion rate** in mL/min is drops per minute ÷ drop factor (15 drops/mL for a standard set), and IU per hour follows from the bag concentration. Laxmi weighs 55 kg, pulse 124, BP 84/50; the drape holds 780 mL and the pads weigh 460 g wet against 140 g dry.

```python
pulse, sbp, kg = 124, 84, 55
drape, wet, dry = 780, 460, 140
loss = drape + (wet - dry)                         # measured blood loss, mL
bv = 100 * kg
pct = loss / bv * 100
si = pulse / sbp
fluid = 3 * loss                                   # 3:1 crystalloid replacement
rate = 60 / 15                                     # mL/min at 60 drops/min, 15 drops/mL set
minutes = 1000 / rate
iu_h = 20 / minutes * 60                           # 20 IU in 1 L
txa_ml = 1000 / 100                                # 1 g from 100 mg/mL
carbo_total = 250 * 8                              # mcg, maximum 8 doses
print(f"Laxmi: loss {loss} mL = {pct:.1f}% of {bv} mL; shock index {si:.2f}; fluid {fluid} mL")
print(f"Oxytocin 20 IU/L at 60 drops/min: {rate:.0f} mL/min, lasts {minutes:.0f} min, {iu_h:.1f} IU/h")
print(f"TXA 1 g = {txa_ml:.0f} mL over 10 min = {txa_ml / 10:.0f} mL/min; carboprost max {carbo_total} mcg = {carbo_total / 1000:.0f} mg; misoprostol 800 mcg = {800 // 200} tablets")
```

```text
Laxmi: loss 1100 mL = 20.0% of 5500 mL; shock index 1.48; fluid 3300 mL
Oxytocin 20 IU/L at 60 drops/min: 4 mL/min, lasts 250 min, 4.8 IU/h
TXA 1 g = 10 mL over 10 min = 1 mL/min; carboprost max 2000 mcg = 2 mg; misoprostol 800 mcg = 4 tablets
```

## Formula and fact sheet

- **PPH (WHO 2025):** 500 mL or more, or 300 mL or more with abnormal vital signs; **severe** 1,000 mL or more; **primary** within 24 h, **secondary** 24 h to 12 weeks. **Four Ts:** Tone 70%, Trauma 20%, Tissue 10%, Thrombin 1%.
- **MOTIVE:** Massage, Oxytocic, Tranexamic acid, IV fluids, Examination, Escalation. **TXA** 1 g IV over 10 min within 3 h; repeat after 30 min if bleeding continues.
- **Prevention uterotonic:** oxytocin 10 IU IM or IV (first choice); carbetocin (heat-stable) 100 mcg IM; misoprostol 600 mcg orally. **Not** ergometrine for prevention.
- **Treatment drugs:** oxytocin 20 IU/L; ergometrine 0.2 mg IM (not in hypertension); carboprost 250 mcg q15 min × 8 (not in asthma); misoprostol 800 mcg SL.
- **Shock index** = pulse ÷ SBP; **blood volume** 100 mL/kg; **fluid** 3:1; **retained placenta** over 30 min (active); **inversion:** Johnson manoeuvre.
- **Nepal:** haemorrhage 26% of maternal deaths (NMMS 2021, MMR 151); MDR form within 24 h; committee review within 72 h; community misoprostol 600 mcg where no SBA.

## Chapter summary

- PPH is 500 mL or more, or 300 mL with unstable vital signs; measure loss with a drape or weight and calculate the shock index.
- The four Ts are tone, trauma, tissue and thrombin; tone causes 70%, so massage and a uterotonic come first.
- The MOTIVE bundle is delivered all at once: massage, oxytocic, tranexamic acid, IV fluids, examination and escalation.
- If bleeding continues, climb the ladder: second-line drugs, bimanual compression, balloon tamponade or NASG, compression suture, ligation and hysterectomy.
- Retained placenta, inversion, tears and secondary PPH each have clear steps; prevention is anaemia control, a skilled attendant, AMTSL and 2 hours of observation.

**MCQs (with reasons)**

1. The pads weigh 540 g wet and 120 g dry. The blood loss is about: (a) 120 mL (b) 420 mL (c) 540 mL (d) 660 mL.
2. A woman has lost 800 mL of blood. Using the 3:1 rule the crystalloid needed is: (a) 800 mL (b) 1,600 mL (c) 2,400 mL (d) 3,200 mL.
3. 40 IU of oxytocin in 1 L runs at 125 mL/h. The dose delivered is: (a) 2.5 IU/h (b) 5 IU/h (c) 10 IU/h (d) 40 IU/h.
4. Tranexamic acid 1 g is available as 100 mg/mL. The volume to give over 10 minutes is: (a) 1 mL (b) 5 mL (c) 10 mL (d) 20 mL.
5. The commonest cause of PPH is: (a) trauma (b) tone (c) tissue (d) thrombin.

::: note Answers and rationales
**1. (b).** 540 - 120 = 420 mL; subtract the dry weight of the pads, because only the difference is blood. 540 mL counts the pad itself.
**2. (c).** 3 × 800 = 2,400 mL; the crystalloid leaves the vessels, so three times the blood lost is needed. 1,600 mL is a 2:1 ratio and 800 mL a 1:1 ratio.
**3. (b).** 40 IU ÷ 1,000 mL × 125 mL/h = 5 IU/h. 2.5 IU/h would need 62.5 mL/h, and 40 IU/h would empty the bag in one hour.
**4. (c).** 1,000 mg ÷ 100 mg/mL = 10 mL, given slowly over 10 minutes (1 mL/min). A fast push can cause hypotension or seizures.
**5. (b).** Tone (uterine atony) causes about 70% of PPH, so massage and a uterotonic are the first actions; trauma 20%, tissue 10%, thrombin 1%.
:::

**Short answer (3 marks): List the causes of postpartum haemorrhage (the four Ts).** **Tone** (uterine atony, 70%): from overdistension, long labour, grand multiparity, infection or a full bladder. **Trauma** (20%): tears of the cervix, vagina or perineum, uterine rupture, inversion. **Tissue** (10%): retained placenta or membranes, accreta. **Thrombin** (1%): coagulopathy from abruption, pre-eclampsia, sepsis or DIC. A firm uterus with bleeding points to trauma; a soft uterus points to tone.

**Medium answer (5 marks): Describe the MOTIVE bundle for management of PPH (Figure 2.2).** The 2025 WHO-FIGO-ICM guideline recommends a **first-response bundle given at once**. **M** is uterine massage, rubbing up the fundus and expelling clots. **O** is an oxytocic: oxytocin 10 IU IM or slow IV, then an infusion of 20 IU in 1 L. **T** is **tranexamic acid 1 g (10 mL) IV over 10 minutes within 3 hours of birth**, with a second dose after 30 minutes if bleeding continues. **IV** means two wide cannulae with warmed Ringer's lactate (3 mL per mL lost) and blood when needed. **E** is examination of the cervix, vagina, perineum and the placenta for trauma or tissue, and the second **E** is **escalation**: calling senior staff, blood, theatre and CEONC transfer. The bundle is started when loss is 500 mL (or 300 mL with abnormal vital signs), is measured by a calibrated drape, and its steps are documented with times. In the E-MOTIVE trial it cut severe PPH or death by more than half. *Diagram:* a timeline with six boxes M, O, T, IV, E, E.

**Long answer (10 marks): Laxmi Tharu, 31, G5P4, 55 kg, delivers a 4.1 kg baby at a birthing centre. The placenta is complete, the uterus is soft and boggy, pulse 124, BP 84/50. The drape holds 780 mL and the pads weigh 460 g wet (140 g dry). Calculate and write the nursing care plan.**

*Calculations.* Measured loss is 780 + (460 - 140) = **1,100 mL**. Her blood volume is 100 × 55 = 5,500 mL, so she has lost **20%** (class II haemorrhage). Her **shock index is 1.48** (124 ÷ 84), a sign of major haemorrhage. The 3:1 rule gives crystalloid of 3 × 1,100 = **3,300 mL**. An infusion of 20 IU oxytocin in 1 L at 60 drops/min (15 drops/mL set) runs at 4 mL/min, lasts 250 minutes and delivers 4.8 IU/h. TXA 1 g from a 100 mg/mL vial is 10 mL over 10 minutes.

*Management.* The diagnosis is **primary PPH from uterine atony** (tone: grand multipara, big baby). The midwife calls for help and starts the **MOTIVE bundle**: she **massages the uterus** and expels clots, gives **oxytocin 10 IU IV slowly** and starts an infusion of 20 IU in 1 L, gives **tranexamic acid 1 g IV over 10 minutes**, inserts two 18 G cannulae, takes blood for group, cross-match and Hb and runs warmed Ringer's lactate, then **examines** the cervix, vagina and perineum for tears and checks the placenta (it is complete). She empties the bladder with a catheter, gives oxygen 10 L/min, covers Laxmi with a blanket, and measures pulse, BP and bleeding every 5 minutes. As the uterus stays soft after 15 minutes she **escalates**: she calls the doctor, gives **misoprostol 800 mcg sublingually** (4 tablets), starts bimanual compression, applies the NASG and arranges transfer to the CEONC with a nurse escort, blood and a referral letter. If bleeding stops, she keeps Laxmi under observation for 24 hours, checks Hb, gives iron-folic acid (180 tablets), counsels about family planning and breastfeeding, and records the MDR-style timeline for audit.
