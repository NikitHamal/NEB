## 4.1 What labour is and how it begins

**Labour** is the process by which the fetus, placenta and membranes are expelled from the uterus through the birth canal, with **regular, painful contractions that cause progressive cervical dilatation**. **Term** is 37 to 41 completed weeks. The exact trigger is not fully known; the fetal brain and adrenal gland mature and release cortisol, **oestrogen rises relative to progesterone**, the uterus becomes sensitive to **oxytocin** (more receptors), **prostaglandins** soften the cervix, and stretch of the uterus and cervix releases more oxytocin (the **Ferguson reflex**).

**Signs that labour is near:** **lightening** (the head engages, breathing eases, frequency of urine returns), **a "show"** (blood-stained mucus from the cervix), **Braxton Hicks contractions** that grow stronger, backache, loose stools and a burst of energy (nesting). **True labour** has contractions that come at regular, shorter intervals, grow stronger, are felt in the back and abdomen, are not relieved by walking or a warm bath, and **change the cervix**. **False labour** is irregular, felt in the lower abdomen, relieved by rest and **does not dilate the cervix**. **Rupture of membranes** may come before or during labour: ask about the time, colour and smell of the fluid.

## 4.2 The five Ps

| P | What it means | Normal and the midwife's concern |
|---|---|---|
| **Passage** | The bony pelvis and the soft tissues of the cervix, vagina and pelvic floor | Inlet 13 cm transverse, OC 10 cm or more (Unit 1); a scarred or tight cervix delays dilatation |
| **Passenger** | The fetus: size, lie, presentation, position and attitude; also the placenta and membranes | Cephalic, well-flexed (SOB 9.5 cm), longitudinal lie; a deflexed head (OP) or a large baby delays progress |
| **Powers** | Uterine contractions (involuntary) and maternal pushing (voluntary) | Active phase: 3-4 contractions in 10 minutes, each over 40 seconds; weak contractions need assessment |
| **Position** | The woman's position: upright, kneeling, squatting, side-lying | Upright and mobile positions shorten the first stage; avoid lying flat on the back |
| **Psyche** | Fear, support, culture and sense of control | A calm companion and respectful care lower pain and interventions |

**Uterine contractions.** The upper segment contracts, **retracts** and thickens, while the lower segment and cervix are stretched and thinned. **Fundal dominance** (the strongest contraction at the fundus, travelling downwards) and **polarity** keep the contraction effective. A contraction has an **increment, acme and decrement**; the midwife describes the **frequency** (from the start of one to the start of the next), **duration** (seconds) and **strength** (mild, moderate or strong by palpation).

## 4.3 The stages of labour

| Stage | From - to | Usual duration (WHO) | Key events |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First: latent phase** | Onset to 5 cm | Not fixed; may be long, especially in a first labour | Cervix softens and effaces; no interventions for time alone if mother and baby are well |
| **First: active phase** | 5 cm to full dilatation (10 cm) | Seldom more than **12 h** in a first labour and **10 h** in later labours; median 4 h and 3 h | Faster dilatation, descent, membranes may rupture |
| **Second** | Full dilatation to birth of the baby | Usually within **3 h** in a first labour and **2 h** in later labours | Descent, rotation and pushing, birth of head and shoulders |
| **Third** | Birth of the baby to delivery of the placenta and membranes | Normally under **30 min** with active management | Separation, expulsion and control of bleeding |
| **Fourth** (recovery) | First 1-2 hours after the placenta | Observe closely | Highest risk of postpartum haemorrhage |

**WHO 2018 and 2020: new definitions.** The WHO Labour Care Guide (LCG) replaced the old partograph with its fixed 1 cm/hour line: **the active phase starts at 5 cm, not 4 cm**, a dilatation rate of less than 1 cm an hour is **not alone** a reason to intervene, and the **alert and action lines are gone**. Instead, the LCG uses **time thresholds for each centimetre of dilatation** (Figure 4.2); when a threshold is reached the midwife **reassesses and decides**, instead of automatically intervening. The LCG is started when the woman reaches 5 cm and covers supportive care, the baby (FHR, liquor, moulding), the woman (BP, pulse, temperature, urine) and labour progress (contractions, dilatation, descent), with decisions and drugs recorded.

## 4.4 Assessing the cervix: the Bishop score

The **Bishop score** rates how ready the cervix is: it is used to predict the success of **induction of labour** and is scored from 0 to 13. Five components are scored (dilatation, effacement, station, consistency and position of the cervix). A score of **8 or more** predicts a vaginal birth that begins on its own or after induction; **6 or less** means the cervix is unfavourable and needs ripening (for example with a Foley catheter or prostaglandin) before oxytocin.

| Score | Dilatation (cm) | Effacement (%) | Station (cm from spines) | Consistency | Position |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | closed | 0-30 | -3 | firm | posterior |
| 1 | 1-2 | 40-50 | -2 | medium | mid-position |
| 2 | 3-4 | 60-70 | -1 or 0 | soft | anterior |
| 3 | 5 or more | 80 or more | +1 or +2 | | |

## 4.5 Care in the first stage: eight points

1. **Admission and respectful welcome.** Greet her by name, explain the plan, ask permission before every examination and let a chosen companion stay. Record the history, LMP and EDD, danger signs, ANC card findings and the time contractions began. Respectful maternity care is a WHO standard and also a Nepal MoHP quality indicator.
2. **Assessment of mother and baby.** Check temperature, pulse, BP and respiration, urine for protein and ketones, abdominal examination with Leopold's grips and descent in fifths, and the **FHR for a full minute after a contraction**. In the active phase FHR is recorded every 30 minutes (intermittent Doppler or Pinard) and abnormal readings are acted on. Look at the colour of the liquor after membranes rupture.
3. **Vaginal examination (VE).** Do it with consent, using sterile gloves and a no-touch technique, and **no more often than every 4 hours** in the active phase unless indicated (WHO). Assess dilatation, effacement, membranes, station, position, moulding and caput. Never do a VE in antepartum bleeding before ruling out placenta praevia.
4. **Monitoring contractions.** Palpate for 10 minutes and record the number in 10 minutes and the duration in seconds. In the active phase expect 3-4 in 10 minutes, each over 40 seconds. Fewer or weaker contractions suggest inadequate powers, a full bladder, a deflexed head or obstruction.
5. **Mobility, positions and comfort.** Encourage walking, kneeling, leaning on a birthing ball or a partner, and side-lying. Upright positions shorten the first stage by about an hour and reduce caesarean births. Provide a clean, quiet, private room, warmth and dim light.
6. **Fluids, food and bladder.** Offer water, ORS and light food (rice, soup) by choice, because labour needs energy. Remind her to pass urine every 2 hours, as a full bladder slows descent. IV fluids are only for dehydration or a complication.
7. **Pain relief.** Use non-drug methods first: a companion, massage, warm compress to the back, slow breathing, water and positions. Give drugs only on order (for example pethidine in some hospitals, or epidural in a CEONC). Routine enema, shaving and amniotomy are not recommended by WHO.
8. **Documentation and referral.** Start the **Labour Care Guide at 5 cm** and record every observation on time. When a threshold is reached, or when she shows a danger sign (fever, bleeding, fetal distress, meconium, cord prolapse, obstructed labour), reassess, call for help and **refer early with an escort** and a written note.

## 4.6 The second stage: care of the woman and delivery of the baby

The second stage begins at **full dilatation (10 cm)** and ends with the birth. The woman feels the urge to push as the head presses on the pelvic floor. **WHO advises spontaneous pushing in the position she chooses** (squatting, kneeling, side-lying, semi-sitting), not a forced "hold your breath" push, and no fundal pressure (which is harmful). The midwife checks the FHR after each contraction (or every 5 minutes), watches the perineum, offers water and keeps the room warm and private.

**Delivery of the head.** When the head crowns, the midwife keeps it **flexed** with one hand and supports the **perineum** with the other (**hands-on or hands-poised**), asks the woman to pant, and lets the head extend slowly. She checks for a **cord round the neck**: if loose she slips it over the head, and if tight she clamps and cuts. After **restitution and external rotation**, she guides the head gently down to deliver the **anterior shoulder** and then up for the **posterior shoulder**, and the body follows. She **dries the baby at once**, places him **skin to skin** on his mother's chest, covers both, and notes the time of birth (Unit 5).

**Episiotomy** is *not routine*; WHO recommends it only for **fetal distress, shoulder dystocia, a rigid perineum or an instrumental delivery**, or a history of third-degree tear. It is a **mediolateral cut at about 60 degrees** to the right of the midline, made at the peak of a contraction with the head bulging, after a local anaesthetic (1% lignocaine, up to 20 mL), and repaired after the placenta. **Perineal tears:** first degree (skin), second (perineal muscle), third (anal sphincter) and fourth (rectal mucosa); third- and fourth-degree tears need a doctor. ## 4.7 The third stage and active management (AMTSL)

The **third stage** starts after the baby is born and ends when the placenta and membranes are out. The placenta separates because the uterus shrinks (retraction) and the **retroplacental clot** forms. Signs of separation: a **gush of blood**, the **cord lengthens**, the **fundus rises and becomes firm and globular**, and the uterus feels like a rounded ball above the pubis. **Active management (AMTSL)** cuts the risk of postpartum haemorrhage (PPH) by about 60% and shortens the stage; PPH (**blood loss of 500 mL or more**) is a leading cause of maternal death in Nepal and worldwide.

### Eight steps of third-stage care

1. **Prepare before the birth.** Check oxytocin (10 IU per mL, kept cool and dark), syringe, cord clamp, gloves, a kidney dish for the placenta, a clean sheet and weighing scale before the head crowns. The midwife should know exactly where each item is. A bleeding woman has no time to look for drugs.
2. **Give oxytocin within one minute.** After the baby is born and a second baby is excluded by palpation, give **oxytocin 10 IU IM** into the outer thigh (WHO 2018). It makes the uterus contract in 2-3 minutes and is the most effective single step to prevent PPH. Misoprostol 600 mcg orally is used only if oxytocin is not available.
3. **Clamp the cord late.** Wait for **1-3 minutes** (or until the cord stops pulsating), unless the baby needs resuscitation. Delayed clamping increases the baby's iron stores and reduces anaemia in the first months. Tie or clamp 2-3 cm from the abdomen and cut with a sterile blade; apply **chlorhexidine 7.1%** gel to the stump as in the Nepal programme.
4. **Wait for signs of separation.** Do not pull the cord before the uterus is firm. Watch for the gush of blood, lengthening of the cord and the rising firm fundus. Giving the baby to the mother for skin-to-skin contact and breastfeeding releases natural oxytocin.
5. **Controlled cord traction (CCT).** When the uterus is firm during a contraction, hold the clamp with one hand and place the other hand just above the pubis, pressing **upward and backward** (counter-traction) while pulling the cord gently downward. Never pull on a soft uterus (risk of inversion). Stop and wait if there is no descent after 30-40 seconds.
6. **Deliver the placenta and membranes.** Support the placenta with both hands and rotate it slowly so that the membranes twist into a rope and do not tear. If the placenta is not delivered **within 30 minutes**, it is retained, so empty the bladder, repeat oxytocin if needed and refer; never use force.
7. **Check uterine tone.** Rub up the fundus until it is firm, and check every 15 minutes for 2 hours. WHO 2018 states that sustained massage is not needed when oxytocin has been given, but regular tone checks are. Nepali protocols still teach fundal massage to stimulate contraction, and the woman or her relative should learn to do it.
8. **Inspect and measure blood loss.** Examine the **placenta, membranes and cord** (Unit 1) for completeness and the perineum and vagina for tears; repair under local anaesthetic or refer. Estimate blood loss by **weighing**: 1 g of blood is about 1 mL. Record vital signs, bleeding and time. The **fourth stage** is the first 2 hours: observe BP, pulse, uterus and bleeding every 15 minutes, keep her warm and help her breastfeed.

```python
oxy_ml = 10 / 10                                 # oxytocin 10 IU in a 10 IU per mL ampoule

Sarita's pads weighed 400 g against a dry weight of 120 g, so she lost **280 mL**, below the 500 mL line. A second woman whose pads weigh 620 g (dry 120 g) lost 500 mL and has a PPH.

## Formula and fact sheet

- **Stages:** latent to 5 cm; active 5-10 cm (up to 12 h first labour, 10 h later); second up to 3 h (first) or 2 h (later); third under 30 min; fourth 2 h. **LCG thresholds:** 5 cm 6 h, 6 cm 5 h, 7 cm 3 h, 8 cm 2.5 h, 9 cm 2 h.
- **Cardinal movements:** engagement, descent, flexion, internal rotation, extension, restitution, external rotation, expulsion.
- **Bishop score:** dilatation, effacement, station, consistency, position; 8 or more favourable, 6 or less unfavourable.
- **FHR** 110-160; check every 30 min in active phase; VE every 4 h; **PPH** = blood loss 500 mL or more (1 g ≈ 1 mL).
- **AMTSL:** oxytocin 10 IU IM in 1 min, delayed clamp 1-3 min, CCT with counter-traction, tone check. Misoprostol 600 mcg oral if no oxytocin; tranexamic acid 1 g IV for PPH.
- **Episiotomy:** mediolateral, 60 degrees, only for indications. Chlorhexidine 7.1% to the stump (Nepal).

## Chapter summary

- Labour is regular painful contractions with cervical dilatation; the five Ps (passage, passenger, powers, position, psyche) decide the course.
- WHO's active phase begins at 5 cm; the Labour Care Guide replaces the alert and action lines with time thresholds that prompt reassessment, not automatic intervention.
- The cardinal movements are engagement, descent, flexion, internal rotation, extension, restitution, external rotation and expulsion.
- First-stage care is respectful, upright, hydrated and carefully recorded; the second stage uses spontaneous pushing and perineal support with selective episiotomy.
- AMTSL (oxytocin in 1 minute, delayed clamping, CCT, tone checks) and blood-loss measurement prevent and detect PPH in the third and fourth stages.

**1. (c).** WHO 2018 and 2020 set 5 cm as the start of the active phase; 4 cm was the old partograph.
**2. (b).** The LCG threshold for 7 cm is 3 hours; reaching it prompts reassessment of the woman, baby, contractions and descent, not automatic surgery.
**3. (c).** 60 ÷ 15 = 4 mL/min; 1,000 ÷ 4 = 250 min. 333 min applies to a 20 drops/mL set.
**4. (c).** Scores 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 6, an unfavourable cervix for induction (8 or more is favourable).
**5. (b).** WHO recommends oxytocin 10 IU IM within one minute of birth; the oral route does not work and waiting for the placenta wastes the key minutes.
:::

**Short answer (3 marks): List the cardinal movements of labour.** Engagement, descent, flexion, internal rotation, extension, restitution, external rotation (shoulder rotation) and expulsion. Flexion brings the smallest diameter (SOB 9.5 cm), internal rotation turns the occiput forward at the pelvic floor, extension delivers the head under the symphysis, and restitution and external rotation align the head with the shoulders for their delivery.

**Medium answer (5 marks): Compare the old partograph with the WHO Labour Care Guide (Figure 4.2).** The **1994 partograph** began plotting at 4 cm, used an **alert line** (1 cm/hour) and an **action line** 4 hours later, and assumed that all women dilate at 1 cm/hour; crossing a line triggered intervention. Evidence from large studies (Zhang and others) showed that normal labour is slower and more variable: dilatation often takes more than an hour per centimetre before 6 cm. The **2020 Labour Care Guide** therefore starts at **5 cm** (the active phase), has **no alert or action lines**, and uses **time thresholds for each centimetre** (5 cm 6 h, 6 cm 5 h, 7 cm 3 h, 8 cm 2.5 h, 9 cm 2 h), plus 3 hours (first labour) or 2 hours (later) for the second stage. When a threshold is reached the midwife **reassesses and decides**. It also records supportive care, the baby (FHR, liquor, moulding), the woman (BP, pulse, temperature, urine) and decisions (drugs, fluids), and is designed for respectful, woman-centred care that reduces needless oxytocin and caesareans. Nepal's guidelines are adopting it in stages, so students should be able to use both tools.

**Long answer (10 marks): Sarita Tamang, 22, primigravida at 39 weeks, is admitted at 06:00 with contractions every 3 minutes, cervix 5 cm, 80% effaced, soft, anterior, station -1, FHR 140, membranes intact. At 08:30 the cervix is 7 cm; full dilatation is at 10:45; the baby is born at 11:30 and the placenta at 11:42 after oxytocin 10 IU IM. The pads weigh 400 g (dry 120 g). Calculate and write the nursing care plan.**

*Calculations.* Her **Bishop score is 12** (5 cm = 3, 80% = 3, station -1 = 2, soft = 2, anterior = 2): favourable. The **active first stage lasted 4 h 45 min** (06:00 to 10:45), the second stage 45 minutes (within the 3-hour limit for a first labour) and the third stage 12 minutes (under 30). From 5 to 7 cm took 2.5 hours (0.8 cm/h), inside the LCG 3-hour threshold, so no intervention was needed. Blood loss by weight is 400 - 120 = **280 mL**, below the 500 mL PPH line.

*Care.* The midwife welcomes her with a companion (her sister), explains the plan, and starts the **Labour Care Guide at 5 cm**. She checks FHR every 30 minutes, contractions for 10 minutes each half-hour, BP and pulse every 2 hours, temperature every 4 hours and urine; she does a VE with consent at 06:00, 08:30 and 10:45 only. She encourages walking, a birthing ball and side-lying, offers ORS and light food, reminds her to void every 2 hours, and uses massage, a warm compress and breathing for pain. In the second stage she supports her chosen kneeling position, protects the perineum, delivers the head slowly, checks for a cord, delivers the shoulders and places the baby skin to skin, dried and covered. She gives **oxytocin 10 IU IM within a minute**, clamps the cord after 2 minutes, applies chlorhexidine, delivers the placenta by controlled cord traction, inspects it and the perineum, weighs the pads, and monitors BP, pulse, fundus and bleeding every 15 minutes for 2 hours, then starts breastfeeding.
