## 1.1 Child health in Nepal

A **child** in Nepal is a person under **18 years** (Act Relating to Children 2075, which follows the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child ratified by Nepal in 1990). Paediatric ages are grouped: **neonate** (birth to 28 days), **infant** (to 1 year), **toddler** (1-3 years), **preschooler** (3-6 years), **school-age child** (6-12 years) and **adolescent** (12-18 years). Each group has its own growth pattern, illnesses and nursing needs.

Nepal has improved child survival faster than most countries. The **NDHS 2022** reports a **neonatal mortality rate of 21**, an **infant mortality rate of 28** and an **under-5 mortality rate of 33** per 1,000 live births. About **80%** of children aged 12-23 months are fully vaccinated (BCG 95%, third dose of DPT-HepB-Hib 89%, MR 89%). **Stunting is 25%, wasting 8% and underweight 19%** in children under 5. The main killers after the newborn period are **pneumonia, diarrhoea, malnutrition, injuries and fevers**. The nurse works through the **Child Health Division** of the Department of Health Services, the **NIP**, **CB-IMNCI** and **community nutrition programmes**, and reports on **HMIS/DHIS2** indicators such as immunisation coverage and the proportion of children under 2 years whose growth is monitored.

**The nurse's roles** are caregiver, educator, advocate, counsellor and researcher. The Nepal Nursing Council Code of Ethics asks the nurse to protect the child's right to survival, protection, development and participation. Always treat the **child and the family** together, because a young child cannot decide or speak for himself; give information to the parents and, with age, to the child.

## 1.2 Growth and development

**Growth** is an increase in size (weight, length, head size) that can be measured. **Development** is the gain in skill and function (movement, speech, thinking, social behaviour) and follows a pattern. They go together, but a child can grow well and still be delayed in development, or the reverse.

**Principles.** Growth and development are continuous; the **order is the same** for all children but the **speed differs**; they proceed **head to toe (cephalocaudal)** and **centre to outside (proximodistal)**; they go from **simple to complex**; and the early years (the **first 1,000 days**) shape health for life. Factors that change them are **heredity, nutrition, infection, hormones, care and stimulation, poverty, and the mother's health**.

**Normal growth.** A newborn weighs about **3 kg** and is **50 cm** long. Birth weight **doubles at 5 months, triples at 1 year and is four times at 2 years**. Length is about **75 cm at 1 year** and **87 cm at 2 years**. **Head circumference** is about **35 cm at birth** and **46 cm at 1 year**. The **posterior fontanelle** closes by 6-8 weeks and the **anterior fontanelle** by **12-18 months**. Teeth start at **6 months**; the 20 milk teeth are complete by 2-2.5 years. Useful formulas (the child's **expected weight**):

| Age | Expected weight (kg) | Expected height (cm) |
|---|---|---|
| 3-12 months | (age in months + 9) ÷ 2 | about 75 cm at 12 months |
| 1-6 years | (2 × age in years) + 8 | (6 × age in years) + 77 |
| 7-12 years | (7 × age in years - 5) ÷ 2 | (6 × age in years) + 77 |

Maya is 9 months old, so her expected weight is (9 + 9) ÷ 2 = **9.0 kg**. Her 7.0 kg is **78%** of expected, which is **Gomez grade 1** (75-90%) underweight by this old rule. By the **WHO Child Growth Standards** the nurse plots the weight on the **weight-for-age** card and works out a **z-score = (observed - median) ÷ standard deviation**. If the reference median for her age and sex is taken as 8.2 kg with an SD of 0.9 kg, her z-score is **-1.3**; a child below **-2** is **underweight** and below **-3** is **severely underweight**. Use the official WHO tables in practice. A growth **curve that is flat or falling** matters more than one reading, so the card is plotted **every month up to 2 years** (the Nepal growth-monitoring policy).

**Development milestones** (the nurse tests them with the Denver II or the ASQ and in IMNCI by asking the mother):

| Age | Gross motor | Fine motor and language | Social |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 months | Lifts head when prone | Coos | Social smile |
| 4 months | Holds head steady, rolls | Reaches for objects, laughs | Recognises mother |
| 6 months | Sits with support | Transfers objects, babbles | Stranger awareness |
| 9 months | Sits alone, crawls, pulls to stand | Pincer grasp begins, "ba-ba" | Waves bye-bye, stranger fear |
| 12 months | Stands, walks with help | 2-3 words with meaning | Plays pat-a-cake |
| 2 years | Runs, kicks a ball | 2-word sentences | Parallel play |
| 3 years | Rides a tricycle | 3-word sentences, names colours | Group play begins |

**Theories in short.** **Erikson:** trust vs mistrust (infant), autonomy vs shame (toddler), initiative vs guilt (preschooler), industry vs inferiority (school age), identity vs role confusion (adolescent). **Piaget:** sensorimotor (0-2 y), preoperational (2-7 y), concrete operational (7-11 y), formal operational (11 y on). **Red flags** that need referral are: **no social smile by 3 months, no sitting by 9 months, no walking by 18 months, no words by 18 months, loss of a skill, or any seizure**.

## 1.3 Immunisation in Nepal

**Immunisation** gives a vaccine so that the body makes antibodies before the real disease comes. The **National Immunisation Programme (NIP)** started as the EPI in 2034 BS (1977/78) and gives vaccines free at health posts, PHCCs, hospitals, outreach clinics and campaigns. The current schedule (check the latest MoHP circular) is:

| Age | Vaccine | Dose, route and site | Protects against |
|---|---|---|---|
| Birth | **BCG** | 0.05 mL intradermal, right upper arm | Severe TB (meningitis, miliary) |
| 6, 10, 14 weeks | **DPT-HepB-Hib** (pentavalent) | 0.5 mL IM, left outer thigh | Diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, hepatitis B, Hib |
| 6, 10, 14 weeks | **bOPV** (oral polio) | 2 drops by mouth | Polio |
| 6, 10 weeks and 9 months | **PCV** | 0.5 mL IM, right outer thigh | Pneumococcal pneumonia and meningitis |
| 6, 10 weeks | **Rotavirus** | oral, whole tube, inner cheek | Rotavirus diarrhoea |
| 14 weeks and 9 months | **fIPV** (fractional inactivated polio) | 0.1 mL intradermal, right upper arm | Polio |
| 9 and 15 months | **MR** (measles-rubella) | 0.5 mL subcutaneous, left upper arm | Measles, rubella |
| 12 months | **JE** (in endemic districts) | 0.5 mL subcutaneous | Japanese encephalitis |
| 15 months | **TCV** (typhoid conjugate) | 0.5 mL IM, left outer thigh | Typhoid |
| Grade 6 girls and out-of-school girls aged 10 | **HPV** (single dose from 2025) | 0.5 mL IM, left deltoid | Cervical cancer |
| Pregnancy | **Td** | 0.5 mL IM; 2 doses in the first pregnancy, 1 dose later | Maternal and neonatal tetanus |

**Vitamin A** (100,000 IU at 6-11 months, 200,000 IU at 12-59 months, every 6 months) and **albendazole 400 mg** from 1 year are given in the national campaigns. A child who has missed vaccines is **caught up** and not restarted: give the missed dose at the first contact and keep the minimum intervals. **Rotavirus** is not started after 2 years. **Live vaccines** (BCG, bOPV, rotavirus, MR, JE) are kept dry and cold or frozen per label; **inactivated vaccines** (pentavalent, PCV, fIPV, TCV, Td, HPV) are **never frozen**.

**Cold chain.** Vaccines travel from the national store through regional and district stores to the health post at **+2 °C to +8 °C**, using ice-lined refrigerators, cold boxes and vaccine carriers with four conditioned ice packs. A **vaccine vial monitor (VVM)** shows heat damage; a **Fridge-tag** or data logger records the temperature. **Open-vial policy:** multi-dose vials of liquid vaccines (pentavalent, PCV, fIPV, Td, OPV) may be kept up to **28 days** if stored correctly and not contaminated; **reconstituted BCG and MR must be discarded after 6 hours** or at the end of the session. Dispose of syringes in a **safety box**.

**Contraindications and false contraindications.** True ones are few: a **severe allergic reaction to a previous dose or component**, and for live vaccines **severe immunodeficiency** (BCG is withheld in symptomatic HIV and in a baby with severe illness). **Not contraindications:** mild fever, cold, cough, mild diarrhoea, malnutrition, prematurity, breastfeeding, or a family history of reaction. In Maya's case, all of the 9-month vaccines are safe because she is well.

**Adverse events following immunisation (AEFI).** Common mild reactions are pain, redness, a low fever and irritability for 1-2 days (give **paracetamol 15 mg/kg** if needed). BCG makes a papule, then an ulcer and a scar at **2-12 weeks**. **Serious AEFI** (anaphylaxis, seizure, hypotonic episode, abscess, death) must be reported on the **AEFI form** within 24 hours to the district and Family Welfare Division; adrenaline 0.01 mg/kg (0.01 mL/kg of 1:1000) IM is kept ready at every session.

### Eight points of nursing responsibility in immunisation

1. **Check the child and the card first.** Ask about fever, allergy and earlier reactions, and read the card for the vaccines due. Never vaccinate without a quick health check. This avoids missed opportunities and false refusals.
2. **Keep the cold chain unbroken.** Read the refrigerator thermometer at each session, check the VVM, and keep vials in the carrier on conditioned ice packs. Discard any vaccine that has been frozen or heat-exposed. A heat-damaged vaccine gives no protection and creates a false sense of safety.
3. **Prepare the correct vaccine, dose and diluent.** Match each vaccine with its own diluent and write the time of reconstitution on BCG and MR vials. Draw the dose with a new auto-disable syringe. Mixing diluents is a serious error and can kill.
4. **Use the right route and site.** IM in the anterolateral thigh for infants, ID for BCG and fIPV, SC for MR, oral for polio and rotavirus. Hold the limb firmly and do not massage after injection. Wrong sites lower the immune response and may injure nerves.
5. **Give the injections safely.** Wash hands, clean the skin if dirty, use a new needle and syringe for every child, and drop them in the safety box without recapping. Safe disposal protects the child, the nurse and the community from hepatitis B, HIV and needlestick injury.
6. **Counsel the mother before and after.** Tell her which diseases are prevented, the expected mild reactions, how to give paracetamol, and when to come back. Give the date of the next visit on the card and in the register. A mother who is told what to expect rarely loses faith in vaccines.
7. **Watch for and manage reactions.** Keep the child for 15-30 minutes, have adrenaline, hydrocortisone and oxygen ready, and report serious AEFI at once. Quick action in anaphylaxis saves life within minutes.
8. **Record, report and trace defaulters.** Fill the card, the register and the tally sheet, enter the data in HMIS/DHIS2, and ask the FCHV to find children who miss a visit. Good records let the district see the coverage and the **dropout rate** and plan outreach.

## 1.4 IMNCI: the sick child in Nepal

It covers the **sick young infant (up to 2 months)** and the **sick child (2-59 months)** with simple colour-coded classifications: **pink (urgent referral), yellow (treat at the facility or home) and green (home care)**.

**The main IMNCI facts.** Fast breathing is **50 or more per minute at 2-11 months** and **40 or more at 12-59 months**. A child with **chest indrawing or fast breathing without danger signs** has pneumonia and gets **oral amoxicillin dispersible tablets** at home; a child with **danger signs** has severe disease and is referred with a first dose. **ORS and zinc** are given for all diarrhoea. Children with **MUAC below 11.5 cm** or oedema of both feet are **severely malnourished** and referred. The sick young infant is checked for **possible serious bacterial infection** (fast breathing 60 or more, severe chest indrawing, temperature 37.5 °C or above or below 35.5 °C, convulsions, no movement) and given the first dose of injectable antibiotics before referral.

### Seven points of growth-promotion education for a mother

1. **Breastfeed exclusively for 6 months.** Give only breast milk, no water or tea, and feed on demand day and night. Breast milk protects against diarrhoea and pneumonia and gives all the nutrition a baby needs. Continue breastfeeding up to 2 years or more.
2. **Start complementary food at 6 months.** Give thick, local, home-made foods such as **jaulo, lito, sarbottam pitho and dal with ghee**, 2-3 times a day at 6-8 months and 3-4 times at 9-23 months. Add an egg, a little oil, green leaves and fruit. Thin watery food fills the stomach but gives few calories.
3. **Come for monthly growth monitoring.** Bring the child to the clinic every month up to 2 years and every 3 months up to 5 years. Show the mother the dots on the card and explain what a flat or falling line means. She should act at the first flat line and not wait for thinness.
4. **Keep the vaccination card safe and the schedule on time.** Tell her the next date and the diseases each vaccine prevents. Remind her that mild fever after a vaccine is normal. A complete card protects the child from the biggest killers.
5. **Feed during and after illness.** Give extra fluids and one extra meal a day for 2 weeks after the illness. A sick child needs food to recover and to avoid falling into malnutrition. Never stop breastfeeding in diarrhoea.
6. **Wash hands and keep the home clean.** Wash with soap after the toilet and before preparing food or feeding, use a latrine, boil or chlorinate water and keep the child's play area free of faeces. This breaks the faecal-oral chain, the cause of most infant diarrhoea and worms.
7. **Play, talk and respond.** Talk, sing, and play with the child from birth, and watch the milestones. A loving, stimulating home builds the brain in the first 1,000 days. Report delayed milestones early to the health worker.

## 1.5 Calculations

Growth uses **expected weight** formulas and **z-scores**; immunisation planning uses **doses needed = children × doses ÷ (1 - wastage)**; and quality is checked by **coverage = vaccinated ÷ target × 100** and **dropout = (first dose - last dose) ÷ first dose × 100**. Child drug doses are **mg/kg × weight**, then changed to mL by **dose ÷ strength**. Emergency adrenaline is **0.01 mg/kg** of the 1:1000 solution (1 mg/mL).

```python
age_m, wt, birth = 9, 7.0, 3.0
exp = (age_m + 9) / 2
pct = wt / exp * 100
z = (wt - 8.2) / 0.9
gain = (wt - birth) * 1000 / age_m
print(f"Maya: expected {exp:.1f} kg; actual is {pct:.0f}% of expected; z-score {z:.2f}; gain {gain:.0f} g per month")
infants, doses, wf = 120, 3, 1 / (1 - 0.25)
need = infants * doses * wf
print(f"Pentavalent: {infants * doses} doses; wastage factor {wf:.3f}; order {need:.0f} doses = {need / 10:.0f} vials of 10 doses")
p1, p3, target = 144, 132, 150
print(f"Coverage Penta3 {p3 / target * 100:.1f}%; dropout {(p1 - p3) / p1 * 100:.1f}%")
print(f"Maya's visit: PCV 0.5 + fIPV 0.1 + MR 0.5 = {0.5 + 0.1 + 0.5:.1f} mL in three sites")
pcm = 15 * wt
print(f"Paracetamol 15 mg/kg = {pcm:.0f} mg = {pcm / 25:.1f} mL of 125 mg/5 mL syrup; adrenaline 0.01 mg/kg = {0.01 * wt:.2f} mg = {0.01 * wt:.2f} mL of 1:1000")
print(f"Expected weight at 3 years {2 * 3 + 8} kg; height {6 * 3 + 77} cm; baby of 3.2 kg doubles to {3.2 * 2:.1f} kg at 5 months")
```

```text
Maya: expected 9.0 kg; actual is 78% of expected; z-score -1.33; gain 444 g per month
Pentavalent: 360 doses; wastage factor 1.333; order 480 doses = 48 vials of 10 doses
Coverage Penta3 88.0%; dropout 8.3%
Maya's visit: PCV 0.5 + fIPV 0.1 + MR 0.5 = 1.1 mL in three sites
Paracetamol 15 mg/kg = 105 mg = 4.2 mL of 125 mg/5 mL syrup; adrenaline 0.01 mg/kg = 0.07 mg = 0.07 mL of 1:1000
```

## Formula and fact sheet

- **Expected weight:** 3-12 months (age in months + 9) ÷ 2; 1-6 years 2 × age + 8; 7-12 years (7 × age - 5) ÷ 2. **Height** after 2 years 6 × age + 77. **z-score** = (observed - median) ÷ SD; below -2 underweight, below -3 severe.
- **Birth weight doubles at 5 months, triples at 1 year, 4 times at 2 years.** Anterior fontanelle closes by 12-18 months.
- **NDHS 2022:** NMR 21, IMR 28, U5MR 33; fully vaccinated 80%; stunting 25%, wasting 8%, underweight 19%.
- **NIP:** BCG at birth; pentavalent and OPV 6, 10, 14 weeks; PCV 6, 10 weeks and 9 months; rotavirus 6, 10 weeks; fIPV 14 weeks and 9 months; MR 9 and 15 months; JE 12 months; TCV 15 months; HPV single dose. Cold chain +2 to +8 °C; BCG and MR discard after 6 hours.
- **IMNCI:** fast breathing 50 or more (2-11 months), 40 or more (12-59 months); MUAC below 11.5 cm is severe; colours pink, yellow, green.
- **Wastage factor** = 1 ÷ (1 - wastage rate). **Dropout** = (Penta1 - Penta3) ÷ Penta1.

**MCQs (with reasons)**

1. The expected weight of a healthy 4-year-old is: (a) 12 kg (b) 14 kg (c) 16 kg (d) 20 kg.
2. The expected weight of a 6-month-old baby is: (a) 6.0 kg (b) 7.5 kg (c) 9.0 kg (d) 10.5 kg.
3. A 5-year-old weighs 13.5 kg; the reference median is 17.0 kg and the SD is 1.5 kg. The z-score is: (a) -0.5 (b) -1.2 (c) -2.3 (d) -3.5.
4. Ninety children need one dose of MR in a session and the wastage rate for the 10-dose vial is 40%. The doses to issue are: (a) 90 (b) 126 (c) 150 (d) 225.
5. Penta1 was given to 200 infants and Penta3 to 170. The dropout rate is: (a) 8% (b) 15% (c) 18% (d) 30%.
6. An 8 kg child needs paracetamol 15 mg/kg using a 125 mg/5 mL syrup. The volume is: (a) 2.4 mL (b) 4.8 mL (c) 6.0 mL (d) 9.6 mL.
7. Which vaccine is given intradermally in the right upper arm at 14 weeks and 9 months in Nepal? (a) PCV (b) MR (c) fIPV (d) TCV.
8. Which is a TRUE contraindication to vaccination? (a) Mild fever (b) Mild diarrhoea (c) Anaphylaxis after the previous dose (d) Malnutrition.

::: note Answers and rationales
**1. (c).** 2 × 4 + 8 = 16 kg. 14 kg is the value for a 3-year-old and 12 kg is the weight at 2 years.
**2. (b).** (6 + 9) ÷ 2 = 7.5 kg. 6 kg is the weight at 5 months by the doubling rule, a rough check only.
**3. (c).** (13.5 - 17.0) ÷ 1.5 = -2.3, below -2, so she is underweight. -1.2 is within the normal band.
**4. (c).** 90 ÷ (1 - 0.4) = 150 doses = 15 vials. Ordering only 90 doses leaves the last 60 children unvaccinated because open vials are discarded after 6 hours.
**5. (b).** (200 - 170) ÷ 200 = 15%. Dropout above 10% is considered high and needs defaulter tracing.
**6. (b).** 15 × 8 = 120 mg; 120 ÷ 25 mg/mL = 4.8 mL. 2.4 mL is half the dose and 9.6 mL is double.
**7. (c).** fIPV is 0.1 mL intradermal in the right upper arm. PCV is IM in the thigh, MR is subcutaneous in the left arm and TCV is IM in the left thigh.
**8. (c).** A severe allergy to a previous dose is a true contraindication. Mild fever, mild diarrhoea and malnutrition are not, and refusing vaccines for them leaves the child at greater risk.
:::

**Short answer (3 marks): Differentiate growth from development.** Growth is an increase in the size of the body (weight, length, head circumference), measured in kg and cm, and is mostly physical and quantitative. Development is the gain in skill and function (motor, language, cognitive, social) and is qualitative; it follows an orderly pattern from head to toe and from the centre outward. Growth can stop when development goes on, as in a well-nourished child with delayed speech. Both are affected by heredity, nutrition, illness and stimulation, and both are checked at every child contact.

**Short answer (3 marks): List the contraindications and common adverse events following immunisation.** True contraindications are a severe allergic reaction to a previous dose or component, and for live vaccines severe immunodeficiency. Mild illness, malnutrition and prematurity are false contraindications. Common reactions are local pain, redness, mild fever and irritability, lasting 1-2 days and managed with paracetamol 15 mg/kg. Serious events are anaphylaxis, seizure and injection abscess; adrenaline 0.01 mg/kg IM is kept ready and the event is reported on the AEFI form within 24 hours.

**Medium answer (5 marks): Describe the cold chain for vaccines in Nepal (Figure 1.3).** The **cold chain** keeps vaccines at **+2 °C to +8 °C** from the manufacturer to the child. In Nepal the path is the central vaccine store in Kathmandu, regional stores, district stores, and then health posts and outreach sessions. Equipment includes ice-lined refrigerators and deep freezers for the store, cold boxes for transport and **vaccine carriers with four conditioned ice packs** for sessions. A **vaccine vial monitor** on each vial shows heat exposure, and a thermometer or data logger is read twice a day. Rules: never freeze pentavalent, PCV, fIPV, TCV, Td or HPV; keep live vaccines cold, and discard reconstituted **BCG and MR after 6 hours**; use the earliest-expiry-first rule; keep the open-vial policy of up to 28 days for liquid multi-dose vials; and never store food or drinks in the vaccine refrigerator. Report a power cut or a breach at once and use the shake test for freeze damage. A broken chain makes vaccines useless without anyone knowing, so it is the most important quality duty of the nurse.

**Long answer (10 marks): Maya Tamang, 9 months, weighs 7.0 kg (birth weight 3.0 kg) and has MUAC 12.8 cm. She is brought for her 9-month vaccines. Calculate her growth status, state the vaccines and write the nursing care plan.**

*Calculations.* Maya's **expected weight is (9 + 9) ÷ 2 = 9.0 kg**, so she is **78% of expected (Gomez grade 1)** and her **z-score is -1.3** if the median is 8.2 kg and SD 0.9 kg. She has gained 4.0 kg in 9 months, about 444 g a month. The MUAC of 12.8 cm is above 12.5 cm, so there is no acute malnutrition, but she is a **child at risk of faltering growth**. Her visit needs **PCV3 0.5 mL IM, fIPV2 0.1 mL ID and MR1 0.5 mL SC = 1.1 mL** in three different sites. Paracetamol for fever is 15 × 7.0 = 105 mg = 4.2 mL of 125 mg/5 mL syrup; adrenaline 0.01 mg/kg = 0.07 mg = 0.07 mL of 1:1000.

*Management.* The nurse welcomes the mother, asks about illness, allergy, feeding and development (she sits alone and babbles, as expected at 9 months) and checks the card. She prepares the vaccines from the carrier, checks the VVM and writes the time on the MR vial. She gives the PCV in the **right thigh**, fIPV in the **right upper arm** and MR in the **left upper arm**, holding the child securely. The mother stays for 15-30 minutes and learns the expected reactions. The nurse counsels on **complementary feeding** (3-4 meals a day of thick food with egg, oil and green leaves, plus breastfeeding), monthly growth monitoring, hand washing and play, and asks the FCHV to visit. The next dates are written on the card: **MR2 and TCV at 15 months** and JE at 12 months if in an endemic district. Data go into the register and HMIS.
